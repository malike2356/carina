# Gas Safety and Electrical Compliance: A Landlord's Legal Obligations

Safety compliance is not optional in the private rented sector. The law imposes specific obligations on landlords regarding gas and electrical installations, and the penalties for non-compliance can include fines, imprisonment, and invalidating your ability to evict tenants.

This guide covers the key requirements for gas safety and electrical compliance in 2026.

## Gas safety: the legal framework

The Gas Safety (Installation and Use) Regulations 1998 is the main legislation governing gas safety in rented properties. It applies to all landlords who let properties with gas appliances, including boilers, gas fires, and gas cookers.

### Key obligations

As a landlord, you must:

- Ensure all gas appliances, flues, and pipework are maintained in a safe condition
- Arrange an annual gas safety check by a Gas Safe registered engineer for each appliance and flue
- Keep a record of each safety check for at least two years
- Provide a copy of the current gas safety record to existing tenants within 28 days of the check
- Provide a copy to new tenants before they move in
- Ensure any gas appliance installed in the property is installed by a Gas Safe registered engineer

### The gas safety record

The gas safety record must include:

- The name and registration number of the Gas Safe registered engineer
- The date of the check
- The address of the property
- The location and description of each appliance checked
- Details of any defects found and actions taken
- Confirmation that the checks have been passed

### What happens if you do not comply

The penalties for gas safety breaches are serious. A landlord who fails to arrange annual gas safety checks, fails to keep records, or allows unsafe gas appliances to remain in a property can face:

- An unlimited fine in the magistrates' court
- A prison sentence of up to six months for certain offences
- A prison sentence of up to two years if the offence involves someone being injured or killed

Beyond the criminal penalties, failing to provide a valid gas safety record invalidates a Section 21 notice for possession. If you need to evict a tenant and cannot produce a valid gas safety record, the court will not grant possession.

For a typical Portsmouth rental property with a gas boiler and gas hob, the annual gas safety check costs approximately GBP 80 to GBP 120. This is not an area where cutting costs makes sense.

## Electrical safety: the new requirements

Since 1 June 2020, the Electrical Safety Standards in the Private Rented Sector (England) Regulations 2020 have required landlords to ensure electrical installations are inspected and tested at least every five years.

### Key obligations

You must:

- Ensure the electrical installation (wiring, sockets, fuse board) is inspected and tested by a qualified person at least every five years
- Obtain an Electrical Installation Condition Report (EICR) from the inspection
- Provide a copy of the EICR to existing tenants within 28 days of the inspection
- Provide a copy to new tenants before they move in
- Complete any remedial work identified as code C1 (danger present) or C2 (potentially dangerous) within 28 days (or sooner if specified)
- Retain the EICR until the next inspection is due

### What the EICR checks

The EICR assesses the electrical installation against current standards and identifies any defects using a coding system:

- Code C1: Danger present. Immediate action required.
- Code C2: Potentially dangerous. Urgent remedial action required.
- Code C3: Improvement recommended. Not currently dangerous but does not meet current standards.
- Code FI: Further investigation required without delay.

If the report identifies any C1 or C2 issues, you must have them remedied within 28 days and provide written confirmation of the remedial work to the tenant.

### Portable appliance testing

The regulations do not require testing of portable appliances (kettles, toasters, lamps, etc.) unless you provide them as part of a furnished let. However, it is good practice to include PAT testing for appliances you supply.

### What about HMOs

If you operate an HMO, additional electrical safety requirements apply. The HMO licence conditions typically require annual electrical safety checks rather than five-yearly ones, and you may need to provide emergency lighting and other safety measures.

The costs of an EICR vary but typically range from GBP 150 to GBP 300 for a standard three-bedroom property. Remedial work costs depend on what is found, but re-wiring a property is rarely necessary unless the installation is severely deteriorated.

## Common compliance mistakes

- Forgetting the gas safety record expires on the anniversary of the check, not at the end of the calendar year or tenancy date. Book it early.
- Assuming electrical safety checks only apply to new tenancies. They apply to all tenancies.
- Not providing the gas safety record and EICR to tenants in time. The 28-day window is tight.
- Thinking PAT testing replaces the need for an EICR. They are different requirements for different things.
- Letting a gas safety check lapse during a tenancy. It is your responsibility to arrange access.

## Final thought

Gas safety and electrical compliance are not bureaucratic hurdles. They exist because unsafe installations kill people. The legal requirements are clear, the penalties for non-compliance are severe, and there is no excuse for cutting corners.

If you own rental property in Hampshire, Portsmouth, or the Isle of Wight, make sure your safety compliance is in order. It protects your tenants, your investment, and your legal position.
