# Property sourcing: business model (UK)

**Updated:** 2026-05-03  
**Kind:** External practice guide (Carina RAG).  
**Source notes:** Property Sourcing Manual (compiled). Not legal or tax advice; verify with qualified advisers and current legislation.  
**Related (product):** `04-deals.md`, `29-deals-module-strategy.md`.

## 1. What is property sourcing?

Property sourcing is a business where you act as an agent to facilitate finding property investment deals for investors.

**Typical workflow:**

1. Find a suitable investment deal.
2. Carry out an appropriate level of due diligence.
3. Present the deal to an investor.
4. The investor pays a consultation or finder fee.
5. The investor may proceed to purchase.

## 2. Why sourcing exists

- Many people have capital to invest but lack time, knowledge, or inclination to source deals themselves.
- UK residential property is often framed as a long-term wealth asset (capital growth narratives vary by area and period).
- Investors may pay a sourcer (for example roughly £2,000 to £5,000) when the fee is small relative to time and travel saved.
- A large share of many sourcers' databases are relatively small portfolios (for example amateur investors with about one to five properties).

## 3. Ideal client profile

- **Typical investor:** Often holds about one to five properties.
- **Demographic:** Working professionals (for example doctors, corporate executives), solid earners (for example £60k+), often time-poor.
- **Mindset:** Values time more than the sourcing fee.

## 4. UK legal and compliance overview (deal sourcer)

A deal sourcer commonly falls under the **Estate Agents Act 1979**. Training materials typically list:

- A limited company (common pattern, not the only possible structure; take advice).
- A day-to-day business bank account and a **separate client account** if holding client money.
- Data protection: ICO registration where required.
- Insurance: professional indemnity (training example minimum £100k cover), public liability, employers' liability where staff exist.
- Anti-money laundering: HMRC registration and ongoing AML procedures where applicable.
- Redress: membership in a property redress scheme (required for relevant estate agency work).

## 5. Monetisation options (examples)

- **VIP mailing list:** Monthly fee for early access to deals (example £24.99/month, 24 hours early).
- **Bespoke sourcing:** Upfront fee (example £1,000 + VAT) to deliver a set number of on-brief properties (example six).
- **Finder's fee per deal:** Often cited minimum about £2,000+ with typical band roughly £2k to £5k.
- **Upsells:** Project management, staging, furnishing, tenant find, management.

## 6. Limited company formation (Companies House)

- Many founders use an online company formation service rather than paying an accountant for the basic incorporation (cost trade-off is subjective).
- Some sourcers avoid literal words like "property sourcing" in the registered name where they believe it affects how agents perceive them; this is anecdotal market behaviour, not a legal requirement.
- Abbreviated or neutral names are common in training examples.
- Formation bundles often include a registered office service to keep a home address off the public register (fee examples in training vary).
- **SIC code example:** 68310 (real estate agency activities).
- **Share capital example:** 1,000 ordinary shares at £1 nominal.
- **Identity checks:** Companies House verification (photo ID, proof of address, selfie via approved flow) is part of standard incorporation.
