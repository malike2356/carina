# Development Finance Interest: Calculating the Cost of Borrowing

Published: Wed, 12 May 2027 09:00:00 +0000
Category: Calculations
Slug: development-finance-interest-calculating-the-cost-of-borrowing

---

Development finance interest is calculated on the drawn balance, not the full facility. Because you draw in stages, the average loan balance is roughly 60% of the total facility for the duration of the build. This is the 60% drawdown rule.\n\nFor a £334,875 facility at 7.5% over 18 months, the average drawn balance is £200,925. The total interest is £200,925 times 7.5% times 1.5 years, which equals £22,604.\n\nIf the build runs over by three months, the interest increases to £22,604 plus three more months at the average rate. That extra £3,767 in interest eats into your profit.\n\nThe faster you build, the less interest you pay. A fast, well-managed build can save 20% to 30% of your finance costs compared to a slow build with delays.\n\nSome developers structure their drawdown schedule to minimise the average balance. They draw the minimum needed at each stage rather than the maximum available. This keeps the average balance lower and saves interest.

**The formula**

```
Average Drawdown = Development Loan × 60%
Interest = Average Drawdown × Annual Finance Rate × (Project Months / 12)
```

**Why this matters**

Development finance interest at the 60% average drawdown rate is a significant cost. Build fast to reduce interest. Every month the project is delayed adds roughly 1/18th of your total interest cost.
