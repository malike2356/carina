# Mortgage Interest Tax Relief: How Section 24 Affects Your Net Income

The net impact of Section 24 is that heavily mortgaged properties are less tax-efficient for individual landlords. The higher your mortgage, the more tax you pay relative to your actual profit.\n\nFor a property with high mortgage costs, the effective tax rate can exceed 100% of the actual profit. If your rental profit after costs but before interest is £2,000 and your mortgage interest is £5,000, you pay tax on £2,000 (minus the 20% credit on £5,000). Your actual cashflow after tax might be negative.\n\nThe solution is to own BTL properties through a limited company, where mortgage interest is fully deductible. But company ownership has its own costs: annual accounts, corporation tax, and higher SDLT on purchase. The break-even point where a company becomes tax-efficient depends on your income level and portfolio size.\n
For most higher rate taxpayers with more than one or two BTLs, a limited company is worth considering. Run the numbers both ways to see which structure gives you a better post-tax return.

**The formula**

```
Extra Tax vs Pre-2017 = (Monthly Mortgage Interest × 12) × 20%
```

**Why this matters**

Section 24 makes BTL less tax-efficient for individual landlords, especially higher rate taxpayers. For portfolios of three or more BTLs, a limited company structure is worth serious consideration.
