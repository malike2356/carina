# Commercial NIY: The Industry Standard for Valuing Income Property

Published: Wed, 09 Jun 2027 09:00:00 +0000
Category: Calculations
Slug: commercial-niy-the-industry-standard-for-valuing-income-property

---

Commercial Net Initial Yield is the equivalent of residential net yield. It is the passing rent minus purchaser's costs, divided by the purchase price. The inclusion of purchaser's costs makes it more conservative than gross yield.\n\nFor a £417,000 commercial unit with £25,000 rent, the NIY calculation includes SDLT of £11,350, legal fees of £2,500, and survey of £3,000. Total purchaser's costs: £16,850. The adjusted purchase price is £433,850. The NIY is £25,000 divided by £433,850, which is 5.8%.\n\nNIY is the most quoted metric in commercial property. When a commercial agent says a property is trading at a 7% yield, they mean NIY. It is the standard that allows investors to compare properties across different price points and sectors.\n\nNIY movements are the primary driver of commercial capital values. If yields harden (go down), capital values go up. If yields soften (go up), capital values fall. Over the past decade, commercial yields have compressed significantly, meaning capital values have risen.\n
If you plan to invest in commercial property, learn to think in NIY terms. It is the language that agents, valuers, and lenders all speak.

**The formula**

```
NIY (%) = ((Passing Rent - Operating Costs) / Purchase Price) × 100
```

**Why this matters**

Commercial NIY is the standard valuation metric. It includes purchaser's costs, making it more conservative than gross yield. Movements in NIY drive commercial capital values.
