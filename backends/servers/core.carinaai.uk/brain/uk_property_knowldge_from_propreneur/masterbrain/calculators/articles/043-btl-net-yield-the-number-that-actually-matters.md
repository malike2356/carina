# BTL Net Yield: The Number That Actually Matters

Published: Mon, 12 Oct 2026 09:00:00 +0000
Category: Calculations
Slug: btl-net-yield-the-number-that-actually-matters

---

Gross yield is what agents quote. Net yield is what you earn. And in today's BTL market, the gap between them is larger than most investors realise.

For a typical £250,000 BTL in Portsmouth with £1,100 a month rent, your gross yield is 5.3%. Your annual rent is £13,200. Your operating costs are roughly: management at 10% = £1,320, insurance = £300, maintenance reserve at 10% = £1,320, letting fees = £600, void allowance at one month = £1,100. Total costs: £4,640.

Your net yield is (£13,200 minus £4,640) divided by £250,000, which equals 3.4%. Your gross yield was 5.3%. Your net yield is 3.4%. The difference of 1.9% represents the cost of owning and managing the property.

Now factor in your mortgage. At 75% LTV and 5%, your annual interest is roughly £9,375. Subtract that from your £8,560 net income and your cashflow is negative £815 a year. You are subsidising the mortgage by £68 a month.

This is the reality of BTL on the South Coast in 2025 and 2026. Many properties do not cashflow at current prices and rates. The bet is on capital growth. That is a valid strategy, but you should know you are making it.

If your net yield after costs is below 3%, the property is unlikely to cashflow at a 75% LTV with current mortgage rates.

**The formula**

```
Annual Operating Costs = Management + Insurance + Maintenance + Void Allowance + Letting Fees + Other

Net Yield (%) = ((Annual Rent - Annual Operating Costs) / Purchase Price) × 100
```

**Why this matters**

BTL net yield is what you actually earn after costs. On the South Coast, typical net yields are 3% to 4.5%. If your net yield is below 3%, the property will struggle to cashflow at current mortgage rates.
