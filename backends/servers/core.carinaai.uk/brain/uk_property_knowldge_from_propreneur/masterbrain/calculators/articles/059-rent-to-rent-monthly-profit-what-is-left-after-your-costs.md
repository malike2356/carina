# Rent-to-Rent Monthly Profit: What Is Left After Your Costs

Published: Mon, 07 Dec 2026 09:00:00 +0000
Category: Calculations
Slug: rent-to-rent-monthly-profit-what-is-left-after-your-costs

---

If spread is your gross margin, monthly profit is what remains after you subtract your operating costs. And those costs can eat a surprising amount of your spread.

Using the same four-bed example with a £600 spread, your monthly costs might be: management (your time or an agent) at 15% of tenant income = £270, utilities = £250, council tax = £180, insurance = £50, maintenance reserve = £100, letting fees for new tenants = £100. Total costs: £950.

Your monthly profit is £600 spread minus £950 costs, which equals negative £350. You are losing money.

This is the trap of rent-to-rent. The spread looks attractive, but the costs are higher than you expect. Utilities alone can be £250 to £400 a month for a shared house.

To make rent-to-rent work, you need either a wider spread or lower costs. A wider spread means finding a better deal with the landlord. Lower costs mean managing more efficiently, using bills-inclusive packages, or targeting properties with lower utility costs.

The break-even point varies by property. But as a rule of thumb, your monthly costs will be roughly 40% to 60% of your tenant income. If your spread does not cover 60% of your tenant income, the numbers will not work.

**The formula**

```
Monthly Profit = Spread - Management Fee - Utilities - Insurance - Council Tax - Other Fixed Costs
```

**Why this matters**

Rent-to-rent monthly costs are typically 40% to 60% of tenant income. If your spread does not cover 60% of your income, the deal is unlikely to work. Run the costs before you sign the lease.
