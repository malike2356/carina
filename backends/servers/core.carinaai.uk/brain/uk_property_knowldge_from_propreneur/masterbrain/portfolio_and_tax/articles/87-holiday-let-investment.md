# Holiday Let Investment on the South Coast

The South Coast is one of the UK's strongest markets for holiday lets. From the beaches of Bournemouth to the Isle of Wight's coastal paths, demand for short-term holiday accommodation remains robust. For property investors, holiday lets offer higher income potential than long-term lets, but with different risks, tax treatment, and management requirements.

## Holiday Let vs Assured Shorthold Tenancy vs Serviced Accommodation

It is important to distinguish between three operating models.

A **holiday let** is a property let for short periods, typically a few days to a few weeks, to holidaymakers. The key distinction for tax purposes is the Furnished Holiday Letting (FHL) regime, which requires the property to be available for at least 210 days per year and actually let for at least 105 days.

A **standard assured shorthold tenancy (AST)** is the typical long-term tenancy, usually six or twelve months. The tenant has security of tenure, and the landlord has predictable income.

A **serviced accommodation** sits between the two. The property is let for short periods but may include corporate lets, contractors, and longer stays. It does not qualify for FHL tax treatment but offers flexibility.

## Taxation Differentials

Furnished holiday lets benefit from significantly more favourable tax treatment than standard buy-to-lets:

- Mortgage interest is deductible from rental income in full (not restricted to 20% tax credit)
- Capital gains tax reliefs are available, including business asset roll-over relief and entrepreneurs' relief
- Allowances for fixtures and fittings can be claimed as capital allowances
- The property qualifies as a business for inheritance tax purposes, potentially attracting business property relief

However, the government has announced the abolition of the FHL tax regime, effective from April 2025. From this point, holiday lets will be taxed under the same rules as standard rental income, removing these advantages. The exact implementation date and transitional provisions should be confirmed with an accountant.

## Coastal Property Focus: Isle of Wight and South Coast

The Isle of Wight is a prime holiday let market. Its key selling points include the Jurassic Coast, the Needles, Cowes Week, walking and cycling holidays, and proximity to Portsmouth via regular ferries. Popular holiday let locations include:

- **Ventnor** - Victorian seaside town with charm, good restaurants, and a microclimate
- **Shanklin and Sandown** - traditional bucket-and-spade holidays, family friendly
- **Cowes** - sailing and yachting, premium market
- **Freshwater and the West Wight** - walking, nature, quiet getaways

A typical three-bedroom Victorian cottage in Ventnor might command £700-£1,200 per week in peak summer, dropping to £400-£600 in shoulder season and £300-£400 in winter. With an average occupancy of 60-70%, gross annual income might reach £28,000-£38,000.

On the mainland, holiday lets in Southsea, Old Portsmouth, and the Hampshire coast also perform well, particularly properties near the Historic Dockyard, Gunwharf Quays, and the seafront.

## Income Comparison: Holiday Let vs Long-Term Let

Consider a two-bedroom period cottage on the Isle of Wight purchased for £250,000.

As a long-term let, it would achieve £750-£900 per month (£9,000-£10,800 per year). As a holiday let with 65% occupancy at an average of £150 per night, the gross income might reach £28,000 per year. After higher management costs (typically 20-25% for a holiday let management company against 10-12% for a letting agent), cleaning fees, utilities, and increased maintenance, the net may be £14,000-£18,000 - still significantly ahead of the long-term let.

## Management of Holiday Lets

Holiday lets demand active management. Guests arrive and depart on different days. Cleaning, laundry, check-in, and maintenance are constant operational tasks. Many owners use a specialist holiday let management company, particularly if they do not live locally. For a three-bedroom cottage, management fees typically range from 15-25% of gross income.

Self-managing is possible if you live nearby, but be prepared for the time commitment - guest communications, emergency call-outs, and turnover management are weekly demands.

## Seasonality and Income Stability

The holiday let market is seasonal. Summer months generate 60-70% of annual income on the Isle of Wight. Winter occupancy drops significantly, though Christmas and New Year can be strong. Many holiday let investors reduce rates in winter to attract long weekend stays or short breaks, or use the winter months for maintenance and refurbishment.

To stabilise income, some operators target non-holiday demand: contractors working on the island, visiting academics at the university, nurses needing short-term accommodation.

## Regulations and Compliance

Holiday lets must comply with fire safety regulations (smoke alarms on each floor, carbon monoxide detectors where required, fire extinguishers), gas safety checks (annual), electrical safety (EICR every five years), and the Tourism Accommodation Rating scheme if you use certain booking platforms. Planning permission for change of use may be required in some areas, though many residential properties have permitted development rights for holiday letting.

The Isle of Wight Council has introduced additional licensing and regulation for holiday lets in response to concerns about housing availability for local residents. Check current local requirements before purchasing.

## Is It Right for You?

Holiday let investment works best for investors who can tolerate seasonal cash flow, are prepared for active management, and own properties in strong leisure destinations. It is not a passive income strategy. But for those who get it right, the financial returns can significantly exceed what a standard buy-to-let delivers.
