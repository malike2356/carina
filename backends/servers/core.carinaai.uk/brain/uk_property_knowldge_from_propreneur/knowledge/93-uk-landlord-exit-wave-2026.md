# UK Landlord Exit Wave 2026 - Opportunity for Buyers

**Source:** PIN Virtual Property Exhibition, 16 May 2026. Speaker: Simon Zutshi (PIN founder).
**Additional context:** Savills research cited by Simon Zutshi.

---

## The scale of the exit wave

Savills research (cited May 2026): **247,000 rental properties** are coming to market from landlords who are selling up. This is not normal market turnover - it represents a structural exit driven by policy changes rather than the natural end of landlord careers.

Some of these landlords are naturally retiring and have held for decades. Many, however, are selling because the economics and regulatory environment have deteriorated to the point where continuing to let is no longer worthwhile for them.

---

## Why landlords are exiting

### Taxation - Section 24

Section 24 of the Finance Act 2015 (fully phased in by April 2020) removed the ability for landlords to deduct mortgage interest as a business expense. Instead, interest is now added back to taxable income, and a 20% basic-rate tax credit is applied. For higher-rate taxpayers, this means:

- Rental income is taxed at 40-45% before interest costs
- Interest is only offset at 20%, leaving a 20-25% net tax cost on interest paid
- Many landlords with mortgaged portfolios are now technically profitable but tax-negative: rental income covers costs and mortgage payments, but the resulting tax liability leaves them with less than the mortgage interest they paid out

This is why landlords describe their properties as "costing them money" even when occupancy is high. The economics have fundamentally changed.

### Renters Rights Act (2025)

The Renters Rights Act abolished assured shorthold tenancies and Section 21 "no-fault" evictions. Landlords can no longer:
- Recover a property for personal use without a court order (now requires a specific ground)
- Use the short fixed-term tenancy as a simple exit mechanism
- Quickly end a tenancy when they want to sell or redevelop

This dramatically increases the perceived risk of taking on a tenant. Many smaller landlords (1-3 properties) who bought as a retirement strategy are unwilling to navigate mandatory grounds-only eviction through a court system with long backlogs.

### Increased compliance burden

EPC upgrade requirements, licensing schemes, HMO regulation, and deposit protection rules have all increased the time and cost of being a landlord. For older landlords managing their own properties, the compliance burden has become a deterrent.

## What this means for BMV buyers

The exit wave creates a pool of potential motivated sellers that is larger than at any point since 2008. Crucially:

1. **Many landlords want close to full market price but are flexible on timing** - they may accept delayed completion or instalment structures (lease option / vendor finance) rather than a heavy cash discount
2. **Some are genuinely motivated** - void pressure, an upcoming renewal (knowing the property will be vacant for months), a bailiff threat from non-paying tenants, or a desire to exit before EPC compliance deadlines
3. **Most have significant equity** - landlords who bought before 2010 have appreciated values many times their original purchase. A 20-30% discount still represents a meaningful cash profit for them
4. **Tax awareness varies** - landlords selling with CGT implications may be more flexible on price if the buyer can complete before April 5 (end of the tax year) or structure the deal in a way that spreads their gain

## Where to find exiting landlords

| Channel | Notes |
|---|---|
| **Council HMO registers** | FOI request to local council for licensed HMO addresses. Landlord details are often public record. Direct mail to these addresses is legal and effective. |
| **Rightmove landlord listings** | Search "available to let" in your target area. Contact landlords with multiple listings who may be over-leveraged or struggling with voids. |
| **Independent letting agents** | When a landlord decides to sell, the first person they tell is usually their letting agent. Independent (not estate agent-linked) lettings businesses don't sell properties and will often refer landlords to you for a fee. |
| **Networking events** | PIN meetings and property investor groups. Other investors hear about landlords looking to exit before listings appear. |
| **Direct mail to HMO register addresses** | Letter-based campaigns to licensed HMO landlords. Subject line approach: "I buy properties in [area] - are you considering selling?" |
| **Property Filter** | Propertyfiltter.co.uk (~£97/month) aggregates on-market properties with signals (repeated price reductions, time on market, chain breaks). Many of these are landlords. |
| **Solicitors** | Probate and divorce solicitors handle landlord estates. A relationship with a local probate solicitor can surface properties before public listing. |

## How to approach an exiting landlord

The conversation with an exiting landlord is different from a distressed homeowner. Key principles:

- **Acknowledge their position first**: "I understand the Section 24 situation has made it harder - a lot of landlords I speak to are in a similar position."
- **Don't lead with price**: Lead with the fact that you can move quickly, that you understand the process, and that you won't waste their time.
- **Ask open questions about their timeline**: "Do you have tenants in place? Do you need to wait for their notice period?" Understanding the timeline reveals urgency.
- **Understand their tax situation** (carefully, not intrusively): If they bought decades ago, they may have significant CGT exposure. A phased purchase (instalment sale) or a deal completed before April 5 may be worth more to them than a slightly higher headline price.
- **Don't assume they want a big discount**: Many landlords with equity will accept market value with speed and certainty rather than the highest offer with a protracted process. Offer to match their price and exchange quickly. Only negotiate on price if they volunteer that they need to sell below market.
- **Offer a path to exit with tenants in situ**: Many landlords cannot easily evict (Renters Rights Act grounds apply); some buyers are willing to purchase with tenants. This removes the landlord's problem entirely.

## The intersection with the same-day remortgage strategy

For landlords selling a well-maintained property at a genuine discount (typically because of urgent financial need or void pressure), the same-day remortgage strategy applies directly. See `brain/knowledge/92-bmv-motivated-seller-and-100-finance.md` for the full mechanics.

The landlord exit wave increases the supply of properties where:
- The property is in reasonable condition (the landlord has maintained it for letting)
- The seller is distressed by circumstances (financial, regulatory, tax), not by the property itself
- A 20-40% discount may be achievable if the seller is urgently motivated

This is the optimal combination for the same-day remortgage no-money-down structure.

## Carina guidance notes

When a user mentions landlord exits, exiting landlords, Section 24, or the Renters Rights Act:
1. Acknowledge the scale of the exit wave (247,000 properties, Savills data)
2. Explain the distinction between a landlord who wants market value + speed versus one who is genuinely distressed and will accept a discount
3. Point to the sourcing channels - especially independent letting agents, council HMO registers, and direct mail
4. If the user is looking to buy, prompt them to assess whether the landlord's property qualifies for the same-day remortgage structure (distressed seller, not distressed property)
5. Flag that many exiting landlords have significant equity and may be more flexible on timing/structure than on price
