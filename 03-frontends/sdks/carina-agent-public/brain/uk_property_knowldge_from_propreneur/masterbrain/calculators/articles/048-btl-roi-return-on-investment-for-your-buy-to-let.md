# BTL ROI: Return on Investment for Your Buy-to-Let

Published: Wed, 28 Oct 2026 09:00:00 +0000
Category: Calculations
Slug: btl-roi-return-on-investment-for-your-buy-to-let

---

Gross yield and net yield tell you about the property. ROI tells you about your money. And for BTL investors, ROI is the number that separates a good investment from a mediocre one.

Your total cash invested in a £250,000 BTL might be £62,500 deposit plus £15,000 stamp duty plus £3,000 legal and survey fees plus £2,000 mortgage fees. Total cash: £82,500.

Your annual net cashflow after all costs and mortgage payments is roughly negative £816. But that ignores capital growth. If the property appreciates at 3% a year, that is £7,500 in year one. Your ROI based on total return is (£7,500 minus £816) divided by £82,500, which equals 8.1%.

That is a decent return. But notice the capital growth is doing all the heavy lifting. If growth slows to 1% and rates stay at 5%, your ROI drops to roughly 0.2%.

BTL ROI in the current market is heavily dependent on capital growth assumptions. If you are buying for cashflow alone, you need a higher yield than the South Coast typically offers. If you are buying for total return, the growth assumption needs to be realistic.

Run your ROI with both an optimistic and conservative growth assumption. If the property underperforms on both, it is not a good investment.

**The formula**

```
Total Cash Invested = Deposit + SDLT + Legal Fees + Survey Fees + Refurb Budget
ROI (%) = (Annual Net Cashflow / Total Cash Invested) × 100
```

**Why this matters**

BTL ROI in the current South Coast market is heavily dependent on capital growth. If your ROI drops below 5% in a conservative scenario, the property needs to offer something special to justify the risk.
