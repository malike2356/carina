# BRR Minimum Discount: The Bare Minimum You Need for BRR to Work

Published: Wed, 03 Mar 2027 09:00:00 +0000

Not every property can be a BRR. You need a minimum discount between the purchase price and the after-refurb value to make the numbers work. This is the BRR minimum discount.\n\nThe calculation works backwards from your target cash left in. If you want to leave £32,000 in the deal and you estimate refurb costs of £30,000 and purchase costs of £8,000, your total cost is purchase price plus £38,000. At 75% LTV on the ARV, your refinance proceeds are 75% of ARV.\n\nYou need ARV such that 75% of ARV equals your total cost minus £32,000. If you estimate the ARV at £220,000, your refinance proceeds are £165,000. Your total cost is £150,000 purchase plus £38,000 = £188,000. You are left with £23,000 in the deal, which is better than your target of £32,000.\n\nBut if the ARV is only £200,000, your refinance proceeds drop to £150,000 and your cash left in jumps to £38,000. The deal is marginal.\n\nThe minimum discount (ARV minus purchase price) needed for a viable BRR is typically 30% to 50% of the purchase price. On a £150,000 purchase, you need an ARV of at least £195,000 to £225,000. Anything less and the refinance will leave too much cash tied up.

**The formula**

```
Required ARV = Total Cost / 0.75
Minimum Uplift = Required ARV - Purchase Price
```

**Why this matters**

The minimum BRR discount between purchase price and ARV should be 30% to 50% of the purchase price. Below 30%, the cash left in will be too high for the strategy to work effectively.
