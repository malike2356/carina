# NPV: Net Present Value -- The Most Honest Way to Evaluate a Property Investment

NPV is the gold standard for investment appraisal. It projects all future cashflows, discounts them back to today at your required rate of return, and sums them up. If the NPV is positive, the investment beats your required return. If it is negative, it does not.\n\nFor a BTL held for ten years with £3,000 annual cashflow and a £250,000 sale at the end, discounted at 8%, the NPV calculation looks like this. Year one cashflow of £3,000 discounted at 8% is worth £2,778 today. Year two is worth £2,572. And so on. The terminal value of £250,000 in year ten is worth £115,800 today. Sum it all up, subtract your initial investment, and you have the NPV.\n\nIf the NPV is positive, the property generates more than your 8% required return. If it is negative, the property underperforms a simple investment returning 8%.\n\nThe beauty of NPV is that it forces you to be explicit about your assumptions. You cannot fudge the numbers. If the NPV is negative at 8%, you either need to pay less for the property, increase the rent, or accept a lower return.\n
Most individual investors do not use NPV because it requires a spreadsheet. Professional investors use it for every deal. Building a simple NPV model will transform your investment decisions.

**The formula**

```
NPV = Σ[CF(t) / (1 + r)^t]  for t = 0 to n
```

**Why this matters**

NPV tells you whether a property beats your required rate of return. A positive NPV means the investment is worth pursuing. It is the most honest and comprehensive valuation method available.
