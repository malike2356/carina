# HMO Net Yield: The Real Return on Your Multi-Let Investment

Published: Wed, 18 Nov 2026 09:00:00 +0000
Category: Calculations
Slug: hmo-net-yield-the-real-return-on-your-multi-let-investment

---

If the gross yield on an HMO looks spectacular, the net yield is what you actually keep. And the gap between them is larger than for standard BTL because the costs are higher.

Using the same five-bed Portsmouth HMO, the numbers look like this. Gross rent: £36,000. Effective rent after voids: £33,120. Total operating costs: £18,436. NOI: £14,684. If the property is worth £300,000, your net yield is £14,684 divided by £300,000, which equals 4.9%.

A single let on a £300,000 property might deliver a net yield of 3% to 3.5%. The HMO premium is roughly 1.5% to 2% at the net level. That premium is the reward for the additional management work.

Is 1.5% to 2% extra yield worth the hassle? That depends on your situation. If you manage the HMO yourself and have the skills to do it, the extra yield can be significant. If you pay full management and the costs eat the premium, the maths becomes marginal.

The question every HMO investor should ask is: after all costs, am I earning enough extra to justify the extra work? If the net yield premium is below 1.5%, consider whether a simpler single let strategy would serve you better.

**The formula**

```
Net Yield (%) = (NOI / Purchase Price) × 100
```

**Why this matters**

HMO net yield is typically 1.5% to 2% higher than a single let on the same property. If the premium is smaller than that, the extra management might not be worth it.
