# Flip Net Profit: What You Walk Away With After Everything

---

Net profit is the number that matters. It is sale price minus total acquisition cost minus refurb costs minus holding costs minus selling costs. Everything else is noise.\n\nFor our Portsmouth flip, the full calculation looks like this. Sale price: £220,000. Acquisition cost: £160,100. Refurb: £30,000. Holding costs (mortgage, council tax, utilities over six months): £5,500. Contingency (10% of refurb): £3,000. Selling costs: £5,280. Total costs: £203,880. Net profit: £16,120.\n\nThat is a net profit of £16,120 on a £220,000 sale. Your net margin is 7.3%. That is the reality of a typical South Coast flip. The TV shows make flipping look like a 30% margin business. In practice, on the South Coast with current costs, 5% to 10% net margin is the realistic range.\n\nThe £16,120 profit represents a return on your cash invested. If you put in roughly £70,000 of your own cash (deposit plus refurb plus costs), your ROI is 23%. That is decent but not spectacular.\n\nThe key to improving net profit is reducing the gap between gross and net. Every cost you can cut, every day you can save, and every pound you can negotiate on the purchase price goes straight to the bottom line.

**The formula**

```
Net Profit = Sale Price - Total Cost - Selling Costs
```

**Why this matters**

Flip net profit on the South Coast is typically 5% to 10% of the sale price. If your net margin is below 5%, the risk is not worth it. Above 10%, you have a strong deal.
