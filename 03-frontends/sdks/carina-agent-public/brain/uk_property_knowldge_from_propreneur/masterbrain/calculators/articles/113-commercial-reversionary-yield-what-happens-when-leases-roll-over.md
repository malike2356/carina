# Commercial Reversionary Yield: What Happens When Leases Roll Over

Published: Mon, 14 Jun 2027 09:00:00 +0000
Category: Calculations
Slug: commercial-reversionary-yield-what-happens-when-leases-roll-over

---

Reversionary yield in commercial property answers the same question as residential: what would the yield be if the property were let at its full market rent? It represents the potential upside.\n\nIf a commercial unit has a passing rent of £25,000 but the estimated rental value is £35,000, the reversionary yield is based on £35,000. At the same purchase price of £417,000, the reversionary yield is 8.4% compared to the NIY of 5.8%. That 2.6% gap is the value opportunity.\n\nProfessional commercial investors look for properties where the reversionary yield is significantly higher than the NIY. This gap represents the value they can unlock through active asset management. A well-timed rent review or a new letting at market rent can deliver a significant capital gain.\n\nBut the gap is also a risk indicator. If the reversionary yield is much higher than NIY, it means the property is under-rented. When the tenant leaves or the lease expires, the rent could jump significantly. But that assumes the market can support the higher rent. If the local market has declined, the rent might actually fall.

**The formula**

```
Reversionary Yield (%) = ((ERV - Operating Costs) / Purchase Price) × 100
```

**Why this matters**

A wide gap between NIY and reversionary yield means value can be unlocked through asset management. But it also means the market may be overestimating the achievable rent. Assess the gap with a critical eye.
