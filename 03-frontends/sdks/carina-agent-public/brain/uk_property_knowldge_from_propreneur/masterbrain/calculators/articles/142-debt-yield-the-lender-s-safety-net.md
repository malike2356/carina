# Debt Yield: The Lender's Safety Net

Published: Wed, 22 Sep 2027 09:00:00 +0000
Category: Calculations
Slug: debt-yield-the-lender-s-safety-net

---

Debt yield is the ratio of a property's net operating income to the loan amount. It is calculated as NOI divided by the loan balance. A debt yield of 10% means the property generates 10% of the loan amount in net income each year.\n\nLenders use debt yield as a safety metric. If the property generates strong income relative to the loan, the lender is confident the loan will be repaid. A debt yield of 8% to 10% is considered healthy for most commercial lenders. Below 5%, the loan is high risk.\n\nUnlike LTV, debt yield does not depend on the property value. It depends only on income and loan size. This makes it useful in markets where valuations are uncertain. Even if valuations are inflated, a strong debt yield means the lender has coverage.\n
If you are buying a commercial property or a large BTL portfolio, know your debt yield. If it is below 8%, the lender may require more equity or charge a higher rate.

**The formula**

```
Debt Yield (%) = (NOI / Loan Amount) × 100
```

**Why this matters**

Debt yield of 8% to 10% is the lender's comfort zone. Below 5% is high risk. It is a useful metric because it depends on income, not potentially inflated valuations.
