# GDV: Gross Development Value -- What Your Finished Project Will Be Worth

Published: Wed, 21 Apr 2027 09:00:00 +0000
Category: Calculations
Slug: gdv-gross-development-value-what-your-finished-project-will-be-worth

---

GDV is the most important number in any development project. It is what the finished scheme will sell for or be worth when complete. Get this wrong and nothing else matters.\n\nGDV is not what you hope it will be worth. It is what comparable properties have recently sold for, adjusted for location, specification, and market conditions. A valuer will assess it using comparable evidence, not developer optimism.\n\nFor a small development in Portsmouth, converting a commercial unit into two two-bed flats, the GDV might be £280,000 for each flat, giving a total GDV of £560,000. For a larger scheme of four three-bed houses on the outskirts, the GDV might be £380,000 each, totalling £1.52 million.\n\nGDV determines everything else in your development appraisal. Your maximum build cost, your finance requirement, your profit margin, and your residual land value all flow from the GDV. If the GDV is wrong, every downstream number is wrong too.\n\nThe safest approach is to be conservative. Use recent comparable sales for similar properties, not the most optimistic sale in the area. Add a 5% to 10% buffer. If the development still works at the lower GDV, you have a robust project.\n
On the South Coast, where sale prices have been flat or declining in real terms, being conservative with GDV is particularly important. A project that works at GDV of £560,000 might fail at GDV of £510,000.

**The formula**

```
GDV = Number of Units × Average Unit Value
```

**Why this matters**

GDV is the foundation of every development appraisal. Be conservative. Use recent comparable sales, not optimistic projections. If the numbers do not work at a conservative GDV, the project is too risky.
