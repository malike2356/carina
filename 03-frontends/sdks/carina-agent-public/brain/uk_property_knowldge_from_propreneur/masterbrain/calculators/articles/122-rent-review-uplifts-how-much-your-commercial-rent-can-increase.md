# Rent Review Uplifts: How Much Your Commercial Rent Can Increase

Published: Wed, 14 Jul 2027 09:00:00 +0000
Category: Calculations
Slug: rent-review-uplifts-how-much-your-commercial-rent-can-increase

---

Most commercial leases include rent review clauses that allow the landlord to increase the rent at specified intervals. The most common type is an upward-only rent review to the higher of the current rent and the market ERV.\n\nAt the review date, the rent is compared to the market ERV. If the market has gone up, the rent increases to the new ERV. If the market is flat or down, the rent stays the same. The landlord never loses ground.\n\nThe uplift calculation is the new rent minus the old rent. If the old rent was £25,000 and the ERV at review is £30,000, the uplift is £5,000. That is a 20% increase. For the tenant, that is a significant cost. For the landlord, it is the reward for holding the property through the lease term.\n\nSome leases use index-linked reviews based on RPI or CPI. These produce smaller but more predictable uplifts. A 3% annual RPI increase over five years gives a cumulative uplift of roughly 16%. That is lower than a market review in a strong market but higher in a weak market.\n
When underwriting a commercial purchase, assume conservative rent review uplifts. If the market is strong, be pleasantly surprised rather than overly optimistic.

**The formula**

```
New Rent = max(Passing Rent, Market ERV at Review Date)
Rent Uplift = max(0, ERV - Passing Rent)
```

**Why this matters**

Rent review uplifts are the primary mechanism for income growth in commercial property. Upward-only reviews protect the landlord. Index-linked reviews provide predictable growth. Model both scenarios in your underwriting.
