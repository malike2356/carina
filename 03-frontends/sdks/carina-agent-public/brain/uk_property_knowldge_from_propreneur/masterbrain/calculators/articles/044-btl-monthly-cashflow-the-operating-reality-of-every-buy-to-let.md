You have already seen the monthly cashflow calculation in section 3. But for BTL specifically, the numbers deserve their own spotlight because the margin between profit and loss is so thin.

For a £250,000 Portsmouth BTL with a £187,500 mortgage at 5%, the monthly picture looks like this. Rent: £1,100. Mortgage (interest-only): £781. Management at 10%: £110. Maintenance reserve at 10%: £110. Insurance: £25. Void allowance: £92. Ground rent and service charges (if relevant): £50. Monthly cashflow: negative £68.

That is the reality for many South Coast BTLs bought at current prices. You are paying £68 a month to keep the property. Over a year, that is £816 out of your pocket.

How do you fix it? You can increase the deposit to lower the mortgage. A 40% deposit on the same property reduces the mortgage to £150,000 and the monthly payment to £625. That makes your monthly cashflow positive by roughly £88.

You can increase the rent. Every £50 a month in extra rent adds £600 a year to your cashflow. In Portsmouth, where rental demand is strong, aggressive but fair rent increases can make a significant difference.

Or you can buy cheaper properties. A £180,000 property in a commuter town renting for £900 a month will likely cashflow better than a £250,000 city centre flat at £1,100.

**The formula**

```
Monthly Cashflow = Rent - Mortgage - Management - Maintenance - Insurance - Council Tax - Utilities - Void Allowance
```

**Why this matters**

BTL monthly cashflow on the South Coast is often tight or negative at current prices. Every £50 in extra rent or reduced mortgage cost can flip a deal from negative to positive.
