# R2R Break-Even Occupancy: How Many Rooms Must Be Full to Avoid Losing Money

This is the most important number in rent-to-rent. Break-even occupancy tells you the minimum occupancy you need to cover your landlord rent and fixed costs. Anything above that is profit. Anything below is a loss.

Using the four-bed example, your fixed costs are the landlord rent of £1,200 plus fixed operating costs of roughly £500 a month for insurance, council tax, and management. Total fixed costs: £1,700. Your variable costs per fully occupied room are roughly £150 for utilities and maintenance.

Your tenant income per occupied room is £450. The contribution per room after variable costs is £450 minus £150, which equals £300. To cover your fixed costs of £1,700, you need £1,700 divided by £300, which equals 5.67 rooms. But you only have four rooms. You cannot break even.

This is the fundamental maths problem with many R2R deals. The fixed costs are too high relative to the contribution per room. The only solution is either a lower landlord rent, higher tenant rents, or lower fixed costs.

A healthy R2R deal has a break-even occupancy of 65% to 80%. Below 65%, the deal is robust. Above 80%, one empty room could put you in loss.

**The formula**

```
Break-even Occupancy (%) = (Landlord Rent + Fixed Costs) / Subtenant Income × 100
```

**Why this matters**

Break-even occupancy is the single most important R2R metric. If you need more than 80% occupancy to break even, the deal is too risky. Look for break-even below 75%.
