# Property sourcing: social media and content creation

**Updated:** 2026-05-03  
**Kind:** External practice guide (Carina RAG).  
**Source notes:** Social media training compilations (multiple internal skill docs). Platform algorithms change; treat timing and format advice as heuristic.

## 1. Writing engaging posts

- Write as conversation, not lecture.
- **Ask questions:** prompts about refurbs, margins, obstacles.
- **Calls to action:** DM, comment, link in bio, newsletter signup.
- **Length:** training band often cited around 30 to 50 words plus context when needed.
- Use stories, testimonials, concrete examples.
- Frame benefits from the reader perspective (WIIFM).

## 2. Commenting and reciprocity

- Reply to comments on your posts.
- Before posting, spend roughly 10 to 15 minutes engaging on others' content where appropriate (community visibility).

## 3. Short-form vertical video (example: Instagram Reels)

**Typical creation flow:**

1. Create new reel; pick clip or record in-app.
2. Trim to target length (example 20 to 30 seconds if retention drops late).
3. Caption with clear CTA and hashtags (examples: `#property` `#business`).
4. Tag relevant accounts where appropriate.
5. Publish.

**Heuristics:**  
Post at a consistent daily slot for a trial window (example 30 days); review retention curves and shorten if viewers drop off early. Consistency beats waiting for perfection.

## 4. On-camera basics

- Video carries tone, expression, and body language better than text-only posts for trust building.
- A modern smartphone is sufficient for many use cases.
- **Types:** pure value clips; direct CTA clips; friendly educational or day-in-the-life.
- **Eye line:** look at the lens, not your own preview, where possible (some creators cover the preview to force lens contact).
- **Delivery:** clear speech, friendly directness, one-take preference where editing capacity is low.
- **Framing:** arm's length, lens height near eye line, avoid extreme high or low angles.

## 5. Touchpoints before high-ticket purchase

- Training material cites order of magnitude **seven** meaningful interactions before some prospects buy higher fees (for example low thousands GBP).
- Do not only appear when selling; add value on a schedule.
- **Example cadence (illustrative):** Monday afternoon VIP-only email; Tuesday morning general list; additional value emails in the week; ongoing social engagement.

**Principle:** Prospects tend to convert when value, frequency, and trust compound.
