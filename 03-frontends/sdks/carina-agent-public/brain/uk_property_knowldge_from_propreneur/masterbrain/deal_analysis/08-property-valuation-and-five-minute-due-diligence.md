# Property Valuation Methods and Five-Minute Due Diligence

## Five-Minute Property Due Diligence - Pre-Viewing Analysis

Before booking a viewing, conduct a rapid initial assessment of any property. Do not spend hours on deep due diligence before viewing - the property may have structural issues you only discover in person.

**Purpose of quick analysis:** Confirm the deal is viable on paper before committing to a viewing. Filter shortlist candidates quickly.

**Five questions to answer in 5 minutes:**
1. What is the market value of this property? (based on comparable sold prices nearby)
2. What can I buy it for? (asking price vs realistic negotiated price)
3. What is the likely rental income? (from your prior area due diligence - you should already know the local rental range)
4. What is the demand like? (you know your goldmine patch - is this a high-demand area?)
5. What is a rough refurb cost? (from visual inspection of listing photos - know your area's standard rates)

**Pre-viewing property analysis should take 5 minutes maximum.** Save deep due diligence for after viewing.

### Building Your Rapid Refurb Cost Reference (Know Your Patch)

Once you know your goldmine area well, you should have mental benchmarks for common refurb costs. Examples:

| Work | Example Reference Cost |
|---|---|
| Carpets, 3-bed | £1,200 |
| Carpets, 4-bed | £1,500 |
| Internal doors (standard, fitted) | £150 each including fitting and door lining |
| Internal fire doors (fitted) | £300 each |
| Full repaint, 3-bed | £1,000-£1,500 |
| Plaster one room | £250 |

These benchmarks come from repeatedly getting quotes in your specific area. They vary by city and region - know your area's costs, not a generic national average.

### Steps Before Booking a Viewing

1. Check the listing on Rightmove/Zoopla - flick through photos while asking "what does this need?"
2. Note the asking price
3. Pull up comparable sold prices on Rightmove or Land Registry (same street, same type, similar size)
4. Cross-reference your known rental rate for this property type in this area
5. Roughly estimate the refurb cost from photos (decoration, kitchen, bathroom, structural)
6. Calculate a quick ROI sanity check - is this deal viable on paper?
7. If yes: book the viewing; if no: skip

## How to Value a Property - RICS and Comparable Methods

### The Gold Standard: RICS Valuation

For lending purposes in the UK, mortgage lenders only accept RICS (Royal Institution of Chartered Surveyors) approved valuations. Estate agent valuations are opinions, not formal valuations.

**RICS valuation features:**
- Conducted by a RICS-qualified surveyor
- Adheres to the "Red Book" - the professional standards for UK property valuations
- Valid for 3 months
- The surveyor can be held accountable if the valuation is significantly wrong
- Required by every mortgage lender before releasing funds

**Factors RICS valuers consider:**
- Size (floor area in square metres/feet)
- Location (proximity to amenities, transport, employment)
- Condition and structural integrity
- Comparable sold prices for like-for-like properties
- Rebuild cost (insurance replacement value)

**Key rule:** Always use like-for-like comparables. A 3-bed terrace cannot be compared to a 3-bed semi. Same structure, same type, similar size, similar condition.

**When you get a RICS down-valuation:**
A down-valuation occurs when the RICS surveyor values the property below the agreed purchase price. Under the standard reservation agreement (see legal/20), if the down-valuation exceeds 10% of the agreed purchase price, a replacement property is offered. This is a key deal risk to manage.

### Pre-Sale Valuation Methods (Without RICS)

For sourcing purposes (before your investor gets a formal RICS survey), use these methods to estimate a realistic property value:

#### Method 1: Rightmove Sold House Prices

1. Go to Rightmove → "Sold House Prices"
2. Enter the postcode of the property
3. Filter by: same street or close radius, same property type (terrace, semi, detached), similar number of bedrooms
4. Note the sold prices and the sale dates (recent data is more accurate - prefer last 12 months)
5. Calculate the average of 3+ comparables

**Critical rule:** Only compare like for like. A 4-bed comparable for a 4-bed property. Same structure. Same road or within 1 mile.

#### Method 2: PropertyData.co.uk

- Enter the postcode
- PropertyData aggregates valuation estimates based on square footage and comparable data
- The estimate is a range - not a guaranteed valuation
- Can be downloaded as a branded PDF to present to investors
- Also shows asking rent range for comparable properties in the area

**Important:** All online tools give estimates only. The RICS surveyor's opinion is the only one that matters for lending.

#### Method 3: Land Insight

- Satellite view tool for assessing properties and streets
- Shows ownership history (who bought it, for how much, when)
- Useful for identifying under-valued or long-held properties with room for negotiation
- Can confirm your purchase price vs what was paid historically

#### Method 4: Call Local Estate Agents (Buyer Positioning)

Instead of asking agents "how much is this worth to sell?" (they'll inflate to win the listing), call as a buyer:

> "I'm looking to buy a 3-bed terrace in [area]. What would I expect to pay for a property in good condition?"

This gets a more accurate market price from the buyer's perspective.

### BRR Valuation Strategy - After Refurbishment

For BRR (Buy-Refurbish-Refinance) deals, the post-refurb valuation is critical:

**Before viewing:** Use online comparables to estimate the After Refurbishment Value (ARV)

**After viewing:** Confirm the ARV estimate with:
1. Rightmove sold prices (same type, area, beds, good condition)
2. PropertyData estimate for the post-refurb specification
3. A conversation with 2 local agents (acting as a buyer)
4. Ideally: a RICS desktop valuation before purchasing (costs £200-£400)

**BRR valuation worked example:**
- Property bought: £201,000 (Bedford, July 2020, 4-bed semi)
- Converted to 5-bed HMO post-refurb
- PropertyData estimated value: £410,000
- RICS formal valuation: £400,000
- Refinance LTV (75%): £300,000 - capital recovered and reinvested

**Key insight:** The valuation figure must be supported by actual sold comparables, not just online estimates. Present comparables to the investor as part of the DD pack.

## How to Present Valuations to Investors

Your opinion on value is not enough. Present evidence:

1. Download PropertyData PDF report (branded with your company logo if account allows)
2. Screenshot 3+ Rightmove comparable sold prices with addresses, sizes, dates, prices
3. Note the average comparable sale price
4. Present the range: lowest comparable / average / highest comparable
5. State clearly: "Our estimated ARV is £[X] based on [N] sold comparables within [radius] of the property. The formal RICS valuation will be conducted by the mortgage lender and must confirm this before the investor can refinance."

**Rule:** Never guarantee a valuation. Present it as an estimate backed by evidence. The RICS survey is the only guaranteed figure.

## Seller Pricing Tactics - What to Watch For

### The Running HMO Premium Problem

Vendors who sell an already-running HMO often price it above bricks-and-mortar value, incorporating the rental income stream into their asking price.

**Example:**
- Bricks and mortar RICS value: £250,000
- Vendor asking: £300,000 (claiming rental income justifies the premium)
- Lender mortgage: based on bricks and mortar only - maximum loan at 75% LTV = £187,500
- Investor still needs to fund the £112,500 difference from their own capital

**Unless:** The property is mortgaged on a commercial basis (which uses income-based valuation rather than bricks and mortar). This is less common for residential HMOs.

**Sourcing rule:** Never base your valuation on rental income for residential lending purposes. Residential mortgages are always bricks-and-mortar valued.

## Strategy-Specific Deal Price Ranges

Based on where the most investor interest concentrates:

| Strategy | Typical Price Range | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| BRR to BTL | £80,000-£200,000 | Widest investor audience, manageable deposit |
| BRR to HMO | £100,000-£300,000 | HMO mortgage minimum values; highest ROI tier |
| Single let BTL | £80,000-£175,000 | First-time investor range |
| Below £80,000 | Reduced interest | Insufficient profit margin for investors |
| Above £200,000 | Reduced audience | Requires larger investor capital base |

**Note:** These are general patterns. Specific area market pricing overrides these ranges - a £250,000 property in Manchester may be equivalent to a £150,000 property in Hull for the same strategy.
