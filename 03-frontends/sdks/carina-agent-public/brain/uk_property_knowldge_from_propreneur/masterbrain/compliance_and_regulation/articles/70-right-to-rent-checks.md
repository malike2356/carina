# Right to Rent Checks: What Landlords Need to Know

Right to Rent checks have been a legal requirement for landlords in England since February 2016. The rules have evolved over time, and getting them wrong can result in significant penalties.

This guide covers the current requirements, the digital checking process, and how to stay compliant.

## What Right to Rent means

The Immigration Act 2014 introduced a requirement for landlords to check that tenants have the legal right to occupy a property in England. This applies to all residential tenancies granted on or after 1 February 2016.

If you let a property to someone who does not have the right to rent, and you have not carried out the required checks, you can face a civil penalty of up to GBP 3,000 per occupier for a first offence and up to GBP 5,000 for subsequent offences. In serious cases involving deliberate non-compliance, criminal prosecution is possible.

The requirement applies to all landlords, whether you manage the property yourself or use a letting agent. If you use an agent, you should confirm in your agreement who is responsible for carrying out the checks. The legal responsibility ultimately sits with the landlord, but a well-drafted agency agreement can transfer it.

## When to carry out checks

Checks must be carried out before a tenancy begins. You must see the original documents and verify them before the tenant moves in.

The check applies to all prospective tenants aged 18 and over who will occupy the property as their main home. It does not apply to:

- Children under 18
- Local authority tenants
- Social housing tenants
- Holiday lets (where the tenancy is for less than three months)
- Student accommodation provided by the university or college
- Tenancies granted before 1 February 2016

## The checking process

### Step 1: Obtain original documents

You need to see the tenant's original documents. The Home Office provides a list of acceptable documents, divided into List A and List B.

List A documents (permanent right to rent):

- UK passport
- EU, EEA, or Swiss passport or national identity card (for those with settled or pre-settled status)
- Biometric residence permit or card
- Certificate of registration or naturalisation as a British citizen
- Permanent residence card issued by the Home Office

List B documents (time-limited right to rent):

- Valid passport endorsed with a visa (showing leave to remain)
- Biometric residence permit (for those with time-limited leave)
- Home Office letter confirming the right to rent

### Step 2: Check the documents

You must check that the documents:

- Are genuine and have not been tampered with
- Belong to the person presenting them (check the photo looks like the person)
- Are in date and have not expired
- Are valid for the UK

Take copies of all documents and keep them securely for the duration of the tenancy plus one year.

### Step 3: Record the check

Record the date of the check and keep a copy of the documents on file. If a follow-up check is needed, record the date of that check too.

## Digital Right to Rent checks

Since April 2022, digital checks have been available as an alternative to physical document checks. The digital process uses the Home Office's online checking service.

For tenants with a biometric residence permit, biometric residence card, or status under the EU Settlement Scheme, you can obtain a share code from the Home Office website. You enter this code on the Home Office checking service to verify the tenant's right to rent.

The digital check is considered more secure than physical document checks because it verifies the information directly against Home Office records. It also removes the risk of accepting counterfeit documents.

## Follow-up checks

For tenants with a time-limited right to rent, you must carry out a follow-up check shortly before the right expires. This is an ongoing obligation. If the tenant's right to rent expires and you do not follow up, you can be liable for a penalty from that point onwards.

The follow-up check involves:

- Asking the tenant to provide evidence that their right to rent has been extended
- Checking the Home Office online service for an updated share code
- If the tenant cannot demonstrate an ongoing right to rent, you must report this to the Home Office

You are not expected to evict the tenant immediately if their right to rent expires, but you must contact the Home Office using the landlord reporting service.

## Common mistakes

- Relying on the letting agent to do everything without checking their process
- Accepting a check that was done before the tenancy was confirmed (checks must be current)
- Not keeping copies of documents for the required period
- Assuming the tenant looks British so no check is needed
- Messing up follow-up checks for tenants with time-limited rights
- Not checking every adult occupier
- Not doing a follow-up check on a tenant whose right to rent expires partway through a tenancy

## Data protection

The copies of documents you hold for Right to Rent purposes contain sensitive personal data. You must:

- Store them securely
- Only keep them for as long as required (duration of tenancy plus one year)
- Destroy them securely after that period
- Comply with GDPR in how you handle and store the data

## Final thought

Right to Rent checks are straightforward when you do them properly and a legal risk when you do not. The cost of a penalty far outweighs the few minutes it takes to check documents or use the Home Office online service. Build it into your standard process for every tenancy and you will never have a problem.
