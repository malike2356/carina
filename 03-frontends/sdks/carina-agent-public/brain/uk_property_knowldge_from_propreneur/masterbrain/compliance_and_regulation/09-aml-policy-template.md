# Anti-Money Laundering (AML) Policy Template

## Legal Basis

Property sourcing businesses are classified as **Estate Agents** under the Estate Agents Act 1979 if they:
- Deal with people wanting to buy or sell freehold or leasehold property
- Do this as part of a business
- Act on instructions from a client

This classification brings the business under the **Money Laundering Regulations 2007** (as updated by the Money Laundering, Terrorist Financing and Transfer of Funds Regulations 2017).

---

## Policy Statement

The UK property industry is regularly targeted by domestic and international criminals to launder corrupt money through high-value transactions. This policy commits the firm to the highest standards of integrity, transparency, and accountability.

**Legal obligations:**
- Obtain and hold personal information for all clients entering into business relationships
- Report suspicious activity to the Money Laundering Reporting Officer (MLRO)
- Submit Suspicious Activity Reports (SARs) to the National Crime Agency (NCA) where required

---

## Staff Obligations

All staff must:

- Be wary of unusually large cash transactions
- Be aware of the possibility of money laundering when faced with clients or potential clients
- Be wary of the absence of a legitimate source of funds
- Be wary of the absence of legitimate proof of ID and/or proof of address and/or source of funds
- Be wary of delay or reluctance to provide proof of ID and/or address and/or source of funds

**Important:** Under the Proceeds of Crime Act 2002, it is a criminal offence to suspect money laundering and do nothing about it.

All suspicious activity must be reported to the MLRO via an Internal Suspicion Report (ISR). The MLRO is obliged to submit a Suspicious Activity Report (SAR) to the NCA if they deem it appropriate.

## Identification Procedure

Verification is required before conducting business when the firm:
1. Forms an ongoing business relationship with a client
2. Undertakes a one-off transaction involving payment
3. Undertakes a series of linked one-off transactions

### Acceptable ID Documents

#### List A - Identity Document (one required)
- Current signed passport
- Resident permit issued to EU nationals by the Home Office
- Current UK/EU photo driving licence
- Inland Revenue tax notification
- State pension or benefits book/notification letter

#### List B - Proof of Address (one required, dated within past 3 months)
- Current UK/EU photo driving licence (if not used in List A)
- Current local authority tax bill
- State pension or benefits book/notification letter
- Local authority rent card or tenancy agreement
- Most recent mortgage statement
- Bank, building society or credit union statement
- Utility bill (not mobile) from the last three months
- Home or motor insurance certificate
- NHS medical card

#### List C - Proof of Ownership (for landlords/property owners)
- Most recent mortgage statement
- Home or motor insurance certificate
- Solicitors letter confirming house purchase/deeds/land registration

### Face-to-Face Verification
- One document from List A
- One document from List B
- Optionally: check against fraud, deceased, sanctions, and PEP lists

### Electronic Verification (Remote Clients)
- Scanned copies of original documents
- One or two forms of photo ID depending on risk level
- Proof of address and address history for the last 3 years
- Bank statement within the last 3 months
- Utility bill within the last 3 months
- Checks against fraud, deceased, sanctions, and PEP lists
- For non-UK clients: check against PRADO (Public Register of Authentic Travel and Identity Documents Online)

### Corporate Clients
- Certificate of Incorporation
- Articles of Association
- Memorandum of Association
- Latest Annual Return or Confirmation Statement
- Identity documents for all individuals holding 25% or more shares/voting rights

### Offshore Companies
- All UK company documents plus
- Certificate of Incumbency or equivalent
- Nominee director declaration and/or Power of Attorney (if applicable)

### Trusts
- Trust deed
- List of trustees
- List of beneficiaries
- ID documents for all individuals with 25%+ interest in capital or control

### Probate Representatives
- Grant of Probate (if a will was left)
- Letter of Administration (if no will)
- Personal ID from List A and List B for the executor/administrator

## Enhanced Due Diligence (EDD)

EDD is required when:
- The client has been identified as high risk in the initial risk assessment
- The client has not been met face to face
- The client is from a country deemed high risk by the EU, FATF, or HMRC
- False or stolen documents are used (a SAR must also be submitted)
- The client has been identified as a Politically Exposed Person (PEP)
- The transaction is complex or unusual

EDD comprises:
- More rigorous identification checks
- Wider internet searches (social media, LinkedIn, news) to confirm identity
- Checks against HM Treasury Sanctions List and OFSI (Office of Financial Sanctions Implementation)
- Senior management approval before proceeding

## Ongoing Monitoring

All clients are subject to ongoing monitoring to ensure transactions are consistent with known client profile.

| Client Risk Level | CDD Review Frequency | ID Review Frequency |
|---|---|---|
| Low risk | Annually | Every 3-4 years |
| High risk | Every 6 months | Annually |

---

## Record Keeping

All records must be maintained for **5 years** from:
- The end of the business relationship, OR
- The completion of the one-off transaction

Records must include:
- Identification evidence
- Details of all relevant transactions
- Sufficient information to provide an audit trail for any subsequent investigation
