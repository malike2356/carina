# Managing Commercial Property: A Guide for New Investors

Managing commercial property is not like managing residential lettings. The legal framework, tenant relationships, and maintenance expectations are all different. For investors transitioning from residential to commercial, understanding these differences is essential before taking on your first asset.

Commercial management is not necessarily more difficult, but it demands a different skillset. Get it right, and commercial property offers more stable, longer-term income with less hands-on involvement than residential.

## The Landlord and Tenant Act 1954

The most important legal framework in commercial property management is the Landlord and Tenant Act 1954. This governs the relationship between commercial landlords and tenants, including the tenant's right to renew the lease at the end of the term.

Key provisions include:

- Tenants with "inside the Act" leases have a statutory right to renew their lease when it expires
- The landlord can only oppose renewal on specific grounds, such as redevelopment or persistent rent arrears
- If the landlord successfully opposes renewal, they must pay compensation to the tenant
- Leases can be granted "outside the Act," which removes the tenant's automatic renewal rights

Understanding whether a lease is inside or outside the Act is critical. If you acquire a commercial property with sitting tenants, their lease terms dictate your rights and obligations.

## Service charges and recoverable costs

Commercial leases typically allow the landlord to recover the cost of building insurance, maintenance of common areas, and other services through a service charge. This is one of the key differences from residential, where the landlord absorbs most costs within the rent.

The service charge must be:

- Clearly defined in the lease
- Reasonable in amount
- Properly accounted for, with annual statements provided to tenants

Poorly managed service charges are a common source of tenant disputes. Keep clear records, spend only on what the lease permits, and communicate openly with tenants about costs.

## Maintenance responsibilities

Commercial leases are usually "full repairing and insuring," meaning the tenant is responsible for all repairs and maintenance to the property. The landlord retains responsibility for the structure and exterior, but internal repairs fall to the tenant.

This is a significant advantage over residential, where the landlord is responsible for most repairs. However, it also means that at the end of a lease, the tenant must return the property in good repair. Disputes about the condition of a property at lease end are common.

Schedule regular inspections so you know the condition of your asset. A five-yearly survey of a Portsmouth commercial property might cost GBP 2,000 to GBP 3,000, but it prevents the shock of discovering structural issues only when the tenant leaves.

## Rent collection and arrears

Commercial tenants are generally more reliable than residential tenants when it comes to paying rent. Businesses depend on their premises and understand the consequences of non-payment. However, when a commercial tenant does fall into arrears, the amounts are larger and the process for recovery is different.

The Commercial Rent (Coronavirus) Act 2022 introduced new rules for pandemic-related rent arrears, but for standard arrears, the landlord's remedies include:

- Forfeiture of the lease (re-entering the property)
- Debt recovery through court proceedings
- Commercial Rent Arrears Recovery (CRAR), which allows the landlord to seize the tenant's goods

CRAR has specific notice requirements. It is not a DIY process. Always instruct a solicitor experienced in commercial property when dealing with arrears.

## When to use a managing agent

Commercial property management is less intensive than residential, but it does require specialist knowledge. Many commercial investors manage their own portfolio until it reaches five to ten units, then engage a managing agent.

A good commercial managing agent on the South Coast will charge 8% to 12% of rent collected. They handle rent collection, service charge accounting, maintenance coordination, and tenant liaison.

The key is finding an agent who understands commercial property, not a residential letting agent who offers commercial management as an add-on.

## Final thought

Commercial property management is a different discipline from residential, but it is not more difficult. The legal framework is clearer, the tenants are fewer in number, and the income is more stable. Learn the basics of the Act, invest in good documentation, and you will find commercial management to be a rewarding and largely hands-off experience.
