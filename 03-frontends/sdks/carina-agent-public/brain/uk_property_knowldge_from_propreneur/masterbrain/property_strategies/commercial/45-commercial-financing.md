# Financing Commercial Property: What Investors Need to Know

Commercial property finance is different from residential buy to let lending. The application process, the criteria, and the products all reflect the different risk profile of commercial assets.

If you are moving into commercial property for the first time, understanding how the finance works is as important as understanding the property itself. A good deal badly financed can become a bad deal very quickly.

## How commercial mortgages differ from residential

The most significant difference is that commercial mortgages are assessed on the property's income rather than the borrower's personal income. The lender wants to know what the rent roll is, who the tenants are, and whether the income covers the loan payments.

Key differences include:

- Loan to value ratios of 65% to 75% are typical, compared to 75% to 85% for residential BTL
- Interest rates are higher, typically 4% to 7% depending on the loan size and property type
- Arrangement fees are larger and calculated differently
- Personal guarantees are almost always required
- The borrower is often expected to have commercial property experience

On the South Coast, a well-let commercial property in Portsmouth or Southampton with a strong tenant covenant can secure competitive finance. A vacant or semi-vacant property will attract higher rates and lower LTVs.

## Types of commercial finance

**Commercial mortgage.** The standard product for buying a commercial property. It works like a residential mortgage but with a commercial assessment. Terms range from 10 to 25 years, with repayment or interest-only options.

**Bridging finance.** Short-term lending secured against the property, typically used for purchases that need to complete quickly, for auction purchases, or for properties that do not yet have a tenant. Bridging loans run for 6 to 18 months and attract higher interest rates, typically 0.6% to 1.2% per month.

**Development finance.** Used when the property requires significant refurbishment or conversion. The lender releases funds in stages as the work progresses. Development finance is more expensive than standard commercial mortgages but includes the construction element.

**Commercial refinance.** After value has been added through refurbishment, lease restructuring, or tenant improvement, refinancing allows you to release equity and move on to the next deal.

## What lenders look for

A commercial lender will typically want to see:

- An interest cover ratio of at least 1.25 to 1.5 times the loan payments
- A strong tenant covenant (government, plc, or established local business)
- A lease term of at least 5 years remaining
- The borrower's commercial experience (or a plan to gain it)
- A clear business plan for the property

The interest cover ratio is the key metric. If the property's rental income is GBP 50,000 per annum and the loan payments are GBP 30,000, the ICR is 1.67, which most lenders will accept.

## Deposit requirements and costs

Expect to put down a minimum 25% deposit on a commercial purchase. For properties that are vacant, in poor condition, or in sectors lenders perceive as higher risk, 30% to 40% may be required.

In addition to the deposit, budget for:

- Lender arrangement fee: 1% to 2% of the loan amount
- Valuation fee: GBP 1,500 to GBP 5,000 for commercial
- Legal fees: GBP 2,000 to GBP 5,000
- Survey costs: GBP 1,000 to GBP 3,000

On a GBP 500,000 commercial purchase with a 25% deposit, total upfront costs including fees might be roughly GBP 145,000 to GBP 160,000.

## South Coast commercial finance market

Commercial property on the South Coast is well understood by lenders. Portsmouth, Southampton, and Bournemouth have active commercial markets with adequate lender appetite. Industrial units, offices, and retail properties in these cities are all financeable with the right tenant profile.

The Isle of Wight is a smaller market, which some high street lenders treat with caution. Specialist lenders and local building societies are often more accommodating.

## Final thought

Commercial property finance is more demanding than residential lending, but the returns can justify the higher costs. The key is preparing properly: knowing your ICR, understanding your tenant covenant, and having a clear plan for the property. Lenders reward preparation.
