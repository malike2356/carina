## Interest Rates - How They Work

### The Bank of England Base Rate
The Bank of England Monetary Policy Committee (MPC) sets the base rate. This drives all lending rates in the UK economy.

**Factors the MPC considers:**
- Inflation (target: 2%)
- GDP growth and economic activity
- Employment rates
- Financial system stability

**Impact on property:**
- Base rate rises → mortgage rates rise → monthly costs increase → fewer buyers can afford property → prices cool
- Base rate falls → mortgage rates fall → affordability improves → demand rises → prices increase

### Key Formula
```
Monthly Mortgage Cost (Interest-Only) = Mortgage Balance × Annual Rate / 12

Example:
£150,000 mortgage × 5% / 12 = £625/month
```

## Loan-to-Value (LTV) Ratios

```
LTV (%) = (Loan Amount / Property Value) × 100

Example:
Loan: £240,000
Property: £300,000
LTV = (240,000 / 300,000) × 100 = 80%
```

### LTV Benchmarks for Investment Property
| Product | Standard LTV | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Buy-to-Let mortgage | 75% | Standard investor LTV |
| HMO mortgage | 75% | Specialist lender required |
| Commercial mortgage | 65-70% | Lower LTV due to higher risk |
| Bridging finance | 65-75% | Based on current or GDV |

**Impact of LTV on cost:**
- Higher LTV → more borrowed → higher monthly payments → lower cashflow
- Lower LTV → less borrowed → lower monthly payments → higher cashflow

### LTV and Mortgage Insurance
Borrowers with LTV >75% may face higher rates or additional requirements. Standard investment LTV is 75% - do not exceed this without specific justification.

## Capital Repayment vs Interest-Only

### Interest-Only Mortgage
- Monthly payment covers interest charges only
- Loan balance remains unchanged throughout the term
- **Pro:** Lower monthly payment → higher cashflow
- **Con:** No equity built through repayments; must repay principal at end of term
- Used by: almost all property investors for buy-to-let

### Capital Repayment Mortgage
- Monthly payment covers interest + part of principal
- Loan balance decreases over time
- **Pro:** Builds equity, loan fully repaid at end of term
- **Con:** Higher monthly payment → lower cashflow
- Used by: homeowners or investors who want to own outright

### Comparison Summary

| Factor | Interest-Only | Capital Repayment |
|---|---|---|
| Monthly payment | Lower | Higher |
| Cashflow | Better | Lower |
| Equity building | Through asset appreciation only | Through repayments + appreciation |
| End of term | Must refinance or sell | Property owned outright |
| Best for | Maximising short-term cashflow | Long-term ownership without debt |

**Standard investor choice:** Interest-only for investment properties. This maximises monthly cashflow and allows capital to be deployed across multiple properties.

## Buy-to-Let Mortgage Detail

### Eligibility Criteria (Typical Lender Requirements)
- Property must be rentable (meet minimum EPC and habitability standards)
- Rental income must cover 125%-145% of mortgage payment (stress-tested at higher rate)
- Minimum personal income: £25,000 (some lenders)
- Minimum property value: £50,000-£75,000
- No more than a defined number of mortgaged properties (varies by lender - specialist lenders allow more)

### Buy-to-Let Mortgage Costs
| Cost | Amount |
|---|---|
| Arrangement fee | 0-3% of loan amount |
| Valuation fee | £150-£500 |
| Legal / conveyancing fees | £1,000-£3,000 |
| Broker fee | ~1% of loan amount |

### Loan Term
- Minimum: 5 years
- Maximum: 35 years
- Longer terms = lower monthly payment (useful for cashflow) but more total interest paid

## Bridging Finance

### What Is Bridging Finance?
A short-term loan (1-18 months) used to:
- Purchase quickly (e.g. auction)
- Fund refurbishment before refinancing to a longer-term mortgage (BRR strategy)
- Bridge the gap when a property is unmortgageable in its current state

### Bridging Finance Terms
| Feature | Details |
|---|---|
| Interest rate | 0.4%-1.5% per month |
| LTV | Up to 75% (can include GDV for development) |
| Term | 1-18 months |
| Repayment | Monthly (serviced) or rolled up (added to loan, paid at end) |
| Speed | Can complete in 5-14 days vs 6-12 weeks for a mortgage |

### Bridging Finance Monthly Cost Example
```
Bridge loan: £100,000
Rate: 0.75%/month
Monthly interest: £750
6-month total interest: £4,500

(If rolled up, this is added to the outstanding balance)
```

### Bridging Finance - Key Rules
1. **Always have your exit strategy before taking bridging finance** - the exit is your refinance, sale, or investment sale
2. Going over the agreed loan term triggers extension/default fees and higher rates
3. Budget for both the bridging period AND the refinance costs
4. Work only with regulated or reputable bridging lenders; use a specialist broker

## Private Finance

### What Is Private Finance?
Borrowing from individuals (not banks) - friends, family, high-net-worth contacts, or private investors who want a return on their capital.

### Typical Structure
- Lender provides capital (e.g. £50,000 for a deposit)
- Borrower uses it to purchase/refurbish a property
- Borrower pays agreed interest rate (e.g. 6-10% pa) or fixed monthly return
- Capital returned at end of agreed term (e.g. when refinanced or sold)

### How to Mitigate Risk - Private Finance
1. **Formalise in writing** - solicitor-drafted loan agreement covering term, rate, repayment terms, security
2. **Conduct due diligence on lender** - confirm source of funds (AML obligation)
3. **Maintain open communication** - regular updates on project progress
4. **Plan contingencies** - what happens if the deal is delayed?
5. **Consider a legal charge** - lender may want a charge on the property as security

### Benefits of Using Private Finance
- Access to capital without bank criteria or credit scoring
- Faster to arrange than mortgages
- Flexible terms negotiated directly
- Builds long-term investor relationships

## Joint Ventures (JVs)

### Types of JV Structure

**1. Equity Joint Venture**
- Two parties form a new company or partnership
- Each contributes equity; profits and control shared by agreed percentage
- Best for: long-term partnerships

**2. JV Loan Agreement**
- One party loans capital to the other for a project
- Borrower completes purchase/refurb, repays investor with agreed return on completion
- Best for: one-off deals, simpler structure

**3. Development JV**
- One party provides land/property, other provides development expertise and finance
- Profits from completed development shared
- Best for: complementary skills where each party contributes what the other lacks

### What a JV Proposal Must Include
1. Property details: location, condition, strategy
2. Capital required and how funds will be used
3. Expected returns and timeline
4. Exit strategy (refinance, sale, hold)
5. Structure: how profits or interest are distributed
6. Roles and responsibilities of each party
7. Legal documentation (partnership agreement or loan agreement)

### The Five Killer Questions for Raising Private Finance

These are conversation starters - not a sales pitch. Ask in natural conversation at networking events:

1. "What are you currently investing in?" - let them talk, listen for opportunities
2. "Have you considered the long-term potential of the property market?" - discuss resilience and growth
3. "What do you look for in a partnership when considering investments?" - understand their ideal involvement
4. "Are you open to leveraging your capital to maximise returns?" - introduce the concept of deploying capital in property
5. "We have this deal and are looking for [X]. We're offering [Y] in return. Would you know anyone who would be interested?" - FOMO trigger; you're not asking them directly

**Key principle:** Never position yourself as desperate. You are a professional investor helping others build wealth.

## Finance Decision Matrix

| Scenario | Recommended Finance |
|---|---|
| Straightforward BTL, standard property | Buy-to-let mortgage |
| Property needing refurb before mortgageable | Bridge → refurb → mortgage (BRR) |
| Auction purchase (short completion window) | Bridging finance |
| Want to recycle capital across multiple deals | BRR with bridging |
| Limited personal capital, investor with cash | JV loan agreement or equity JV |
| Building relationship with private investor | Private finance loan agreement |
| Maximum cashflow, property unencumbered | Cash purchase (no mortgage) |
