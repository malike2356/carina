# Raising Private Capital for Property Investment

Bank lending is the traditional route for funding property purchases, but it is not the only route. Private capital, raised from individual investors, family offices, or joint venture partners, can be a more flexible and faster way to fund deals.

This guide explains how to raise private capital for property investment.

## Why raise private capital

Private capital offers several advantages over traditional bank finance:

- **Speed:** Private investors can move in days rather than the weeks or months a bank needs.
- **Flexibility:** Terms can be negotiated directly rather than fitting a lender's standard product.
- **Bespoke structures:** You can agree profit shares, interest rates, and exit terms that suit both parties.
- **Access to deals:** Some deals are too small or too complex for banks but are perfect for private capital.
- **Relationship-based:** A private investor who knows and trusts you can fund repeat deals without starting from scratch each time.

The trade-off is that private capital is usually more expensive than bank finance, and it requires strong relationships and trust.

## Where to find private investors

### Friends and family

The most common source of private capital. Friends and family who have savings earning negligible interest in a bank account may be attracted by the prospect of a 6% to 10% return secured against property.

The key is to treat the arrangement professionally. Have a written agreement. Be clear about the risks. Make the payments on time.

### Professional contacts

Accountants, solicitors, financial advisors, and other professionals often know people with capital to deploy. A referral from a trusted professional carries significant weight.

Build relationships with professionals who work with high-net-worth individuals. Attend networking events where investors gather.

### Property networking groups

Property networking groups are full of investors with capital and investors looking for deals. The challenge is that many people in these groups are looking for the same thing.

To stand out, you need a track record, a clear deal pipeline, and the ability to explain why you are a safe pair of hands.

### Angel investors and family offices

Family offices manage the wealth of wealthy families and are increasingly looking at property as an asset class. They typically invest larger amounts and want professional deal packaging and reporting.

Angel investors may invest smaller amounts but are often willing to take more risk if the potential returns are clear.

### Online platforms

Property crowdfunding and peer-to-peer lending platforms connect investors with property deals. These platforms have their own due diligence processes and fee structures.

While they provide access to capital, you lose the flexibility and relationship benefits of direct private funding.

## Structuring private capital deals

The structure of the deal depends on what the investor wants and what the deal supports.

### Fixed return loans

The simplest structure. The investor lends you capital at a fixed interest rate, secured by a legal charge on the property. You pay the interest monthly or at the end of the term, and repay the capital when the property is sold or refinanced.

Typical terms:

- Interest rate: 6% to 12% per year
- Term: 6 to 24 months
- Security: first or second legal charge
- LTV: up to 75% of the purchase price or 65% of the refurbished value

### Joint ventures

A joint venture where the investor provides the capital and you provide the deal finding, project management, or operational expertise. Profits are shared according to a pre-agreed split, commonly 50:50 or 60:40 in favour of the capital provider.

Typical terms:

- Capital provider: 100% of purchase and refurbishment costs
- Operator (you): deal sourcing, project management, tenant finding
- Profit split: 50:50, 60:40, or 70:30 depending on who does what
- Exit: property is sold or refinanced within an agreed timeframe

### Equity partnerships

The investor takes an equity stake in the property, either through a company structure or as a co-owner. They share in the rental income and capital appreciation.

Typical terms:

- Investor: 100% of capital
- Operator: 25% to 50% equity share for sourcing and managing
- Income and capital gain: divided according to equity shares
- Exit: agreed trigger events (sale, refinance, time limit)

## Building investor trust

Private investors are lending you their money because they trust you. That trust is earned, not assumed.

- Start small: Raise capital for one deal, deliver the promised return, and use that track record to raise for the next one.
- Be transparent: Show investors the full deal analysis, including the risks. Do not sugar-coat the numbers.
- Report regularly: Send monthly or quarterly updates showing the property performance, financial position, and any issues.
- Pay on time: Interest payments that arrive late erode trust faster than almost anything else.
- Have an exit plan: Every deal should have a clear, documented plan for returning investor capital.

## The legal side

Private capital arrangements need proper documentation. A handshake deal is not acceptable when someone's savings are at stake.

You will typically need:

- A loan agreement or joint venture agreement drafted by a solicitor
- A legal charge or other security document
- A clear explanation of the investor's rights if the deal goes wrong
- Compliance with financial promotion regulations if you are marketing to the public

The Financial Conduct Authority rules on financial promotions can apply if you are raising capital from people who are not professional investors. Take legal advice before you approach anyone.

## Common mistakes

- **Overpromising returns:** If you promise 15% and deliver 8%, the investor is unhappy even if 8% is a good return.
- **Underestimating costs:** If the deal costs more than expected, your profit drops and the investor's return suffers.
- **Poor communication:** Investors hate silence. If there is a problem, tell them early.
- **No exit plan:** If you cannot repay the capital, the investor's trust is broken and they are unlikely to work with you again.

## Final thought

Raising private capital is a powerful way to scale your property portfolio without relying on bank lending. The key is to treat every investor relationship as a long-term partnership, deliver on your promises, and be transparent at every stage.

A reputation for delivering what you promise is the most valuable asset you can build in private capital markets.
