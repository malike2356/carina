# Awaab's Law: Damp, Mould and Private Sector Extension

**Source:** The HMO Operator's Compliance Bible 2026 (Propreneur/Verlox Ltd)
**Status:** Expected extension to private rented sector by 2030
**Purpose:** Carina can advise HMO operators on the upcoming duty to investigate and remediate damp and mould on a defined timeline

---

## Background

Awaab's Law is named after the tragic death of two-year-old Awaab Ishak in social housing in Rochdale in 2020. His death was directly attributable to prolonged damp and mould exposure that was repeatedly reported to, and ignored by, the landlord. The legislation was initially applied to the social housing sector.

## Extension to Private Rented Sector

The extension to the private rented sector -- including HMOs -- is **expected by 2030**. Under the private sector provisions:

- Landlords will be required to **investigate any report of damp or mould** within a defined period.
- Landlords must **remediate** the issue within a further defined period.
- **'Condensation -- tenant lifestyle' will no longer be an acceptable first response** to a damp report.
- The landlord must demonstrate that the property's heating, ventilation, and insulation are adequate before attributing moisture to occupant behaviour.

## Practical Steps to Take Now

The 2030 deadline is approaching. Retrofitting ventilation is significantly more expensive than installing it correctly during development.

**Recommended actions:**
1. Install adequate mechanical extraction in kitchens and bathrooms.
2. Install trickle vents in windows.
3. Document all installed systems, including maintenance schedules.
4. Provide tenants with written guidance on ventilating their rooms.
5. Keep thorough records of ALL damp reports, inspections, and actions taken.
6. When a tenant reports damp, investigate immediately and document everything.
7. Before concluding that condensation is caused by tenant behaviour, verify that heating, ventilation, and insulation are adequate.

**The key regulatory shift:** the burden of proof moves from the tenant to the landlord. You must prove you investigated properly, not blame the tenant by default.
