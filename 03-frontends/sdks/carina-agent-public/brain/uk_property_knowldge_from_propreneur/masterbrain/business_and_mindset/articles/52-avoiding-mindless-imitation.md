# Avoiding Mindless Imitation: Finding Your Edge in Property Investment

Warren Buffett once warned investors about the danger of imitation. When everyone is buying the same stocks, in the same sectors, at the same prices, the opportunity has already passed. The same principle applies to property.

In the current market, most investors are chasing the same deals. One-bedroom city centre flats for service accommodation. Three-bedroom semis in commuter towns for buy to let. The same properties, in the same locations, using the same strategy. This is mindless imitation, and it leads to average results.

## The problem with following the crowd

When a strategy becomes widely adopted, the returns compress. More buyers compete for the same type of property in the same location, driving prices up and yields down. The investor who entered early did well. The latecomer buying the same product on the same assumptions inherits lower returns.

Consider the rush to city centre one-bedroom flats for Airbnb in 2021 and 2022. Early adopters achieved strong returns. By 2025, the same strategy in the same cities faced saturation, lower occupancy, and price competition. The returns had compressed because everyone was doing the same thing.

On the South Coast, the same pattern is visible in certain student-dominated areas of Portsmouth. The HMO market in these areas has attracted so much investment that yields have compressed for standard conversions. The easy money has been made.

## How to find your edge

The alternative to imitation is to find a less contested entry point. This does not mean doing something exotic. It means understanding what drives demand and positioning yourself differently from the majority.

The professional approach involves four steps:

1. Identify a genuine source of demand that others are overlooking
2. Find a location where that demand exists but investor attention is low
3. Build a product or strategy that serves that demand better than the competition
4. Execute with discipline

A practical example comes from service accommodation. Most investors target city centre one-bedroom flats because they are easy and familiar. The professional operator identifies a different demand driver, such as proximity to a football stadium with 400,000 annual tours, and builds a larger property in a less fashionable area with boutique execution. The result is less competition, higher prices, and stronger occupancy.

## The commercial opportunity

Commercial property is a natural off-market for avoiding imitation. Most residential investors do not consider commercial because it feels unfamiliar and requires different knowledge. That unfamiliarity is exactly what creates the opportunity.

A retail unit in a secondary Portsmouth location with a flat above, bought at a commercial yield of 9%, is a deal that most residential investors will not even evaluate. The competition is fewer, and the returns can be significantly higher.

The same logic applies to industrial units, mixed-use buildings, and properties in smaller towns like Fareham and Gosport that receive less attention from the mainstream investor community.

## Capital efficiency over status

Another form of mindless imitation is chasing prestige postcodes. Investors buy in expensive areas because that is what others do, even though the returns are lower and the capital requirement is higher.

A GBP 129,000 property in an area with strong demand drivers, generating GBP 50,000 per year in revenue, outperforms a GBP 500,000 property in a prestigious location generating GBP 30,000. The first is capital efficient and underserved. The second is popular but mediocre.

Buffett's lesson applies directly: the highest returns come from understanding what drives value and finding a less contested way to access it.

## Practical steps to avoid imitation

- Review every deal for the herd factor. If ten other investors are bidding on the same type of property, ask whether the return justifies the competition.
- Look at smaller markets. The Isle of Wight, secondary areas in Portsmouth like Paulsgrove or Leigh Park, and smaller Hampshire towns offer less competition.
- Consider strategies the majority ignores. Commercial property, mixed-use, and residential conversions using permitted development rights are all less contested than standard buy to let.
- Develop an independent thesis. Before looking at properties, decide what you believe about the market and why. Be able to articulate it without reference to what others are doing.

## Final thought

The property market rewards independent thinking. Following the crowd is comfortable, but it rarely produces exceptional returns. Find your edge, be prepared to go where others are not looking, and execute better than the competition. That is how portfolios are built that outperform the average.
