# Commercial to Residential Permitted Development Rights

Permitted development (PD) rights have transformed the UK property development landscape. Since 2013, successive governments have expanded the ability to convert commercial buildings into residential use without full planning permission. For property investors on the South Coast, understanding these rights opens up opportunities to acquire and convert commercial properties at attractive prices.

## What Are Permitted Development Rights?

Permitted development rights are a national grant of planning permission for specific types of development. They allow certain changes of use or building works without needing to submit a full planning application to the local authority. The rights are set out in the Town and Country Planning (General Permitted Development) Order 2015 (as amended).

For investors, the most significant PD rights concern the conversion of commercial buildings to residential use.

## Class MA: Commercial, Business, and Service to Residential

Class MA of Part 3 of the GPDO allows the change of use from Class E (commercial, business, and service) to Class C3 (residential). This is the most widely used PD right for residential conversions.

**Class E uses include:** shops, restaurants, cafes, offices, health and fitness centres, nurseries, indoor sports, and certain light industrial uses. If you own or acquire a building in Class E, you have a PD right to convert it to residential use.

**Prior approval requirements.** Despite being permitted development, Class MA conversions require prior approval from the local planning authority. The authority considers:

- Transport impacts and highways safety
- Contamination risk
- Flood risk
- Noise impacts on future occupiers
- Provision of natural light in all habitable rooms
- Impact on the character and sustainability of the area (from 2024, this was strengthened)

Prior approval is a lighter process than full planning, but it is not automatic. Local authorities can refuse where they have legitimate concerns.

## Key Constraints on PD Rights

**Floor space limit.** There is currently no upper floor space limit for Class MA, but individual units must not exceed 1,500 square metres.

**Article 2(3) land.** PD rights are more restricted in conservation areas, Areas of Outstanding Natural Beauty, and other designated areas. Much of the South Coast - including parts of Old Portsmouth, the Southsea seafront, and large areas of the Isle of Wight - falls within conservation areas. In these locations, you need full planning permission for residential conversions.

**Vacancy requirement.** For Class MA, the building must have been vacant for at least three continuous months before the date of the prior approval application. This applies to conversions from shops and offices under the current rules.

**Size and space standards.** The resulting dwellings must comply with the nationally described space standards (minimum floor areas for different unit sizes). A one-bedroom flat must be at least 37 square metres; a two-bedroom flat must be at least 50 square metres.

## Financial Case for Commercial Conversions

Commercial buildings can often be acquired below residential values because they have a more limited buyer pool. An investor who can see a conversion route can unlock significant value.

Consider a ground-floor shop with a studio flat above in a Portsmouth parade purchased for £180,000. The shop element (Class E) is vacant. Through a Class MA conversion, the shop can be turned into a one-bedroom flat, and the upper flat can be upgraded. Total conversion costs of £25,000-£40,000 might result in a property valued at £250,000-£280,000 as two residential units. The profit margin - after costs, finance, and stamp duty - could reach £30,000-£50,000.

On the Isle of Wight, former retail units in Ryde, Newport, and Shanklin are regularly available at prices well below residential values. A former estate agent office or gift shop bought for £100,000-£150,000 and converted to a one or two-bedroom flat can represent outstanding value.

## Other PD Rights Worth Knowing

**Class Q - Agricultural to residential.** Permits conversion of agricultural buildings to up to ten dwellings. Each dwelling must be no larger than 465 square metres, and the total floor space must not exceed 1,000 square metres across the development. This right is relevant on the Isle of Wight, where agricultural buildings are common, but prior approval from the council is required and can be challenging.

**Class R - Flexible agricultural uses.** Permits change of use from agricultural to a range of commercial and community uses, including shops, cafes, offices, and nurseries.

**Class B - Commercial to mixed use.** Allows certain commercial buildings to operate as mixed commercial and residential.

## South Coast Opportunities

Portsmouth has a significant stock of former retail and office space in secondary locations. As high street retail continues to decline, many of these properties are becoming available at attractive prices. The city's conservation areas limit PD rights in some locations, but large areas of Fratton, Cosham, and the city centre fall outside conservation designations.

Southampton City Council has been relatively permissive with prior approval applications, recognising the need for residential development. The city's former office blocks in the centre and retail parades in the suburbs are strong candidates for PD conversion.

The Isle of Wight offers particular opportunity because of lower purchase prices and a less complex planning environment. However, the island's transport links and ferry costs must be factored into the viability calculations.

## Getting Professional Advice

PD rights are technically complex and subject to frequent changes. The government revised the GPDO substantially in 2024 and 2025. Always instruct a planning consultant or architect experienced with PD prior approval applications before committing to a commercial conversion. The cost of professional advice - typically £1,000-£3,000 for a prior approval application - is small relative to the risk of buying a property that cannot be converted.
