# BMV: Motivated Seller Psychology, Sourcing, and 100% Finance via Same-Day Remortgage

**Source:** PIN Virtual Property Exhibition, 16 May 2026. Speaker: Simon Zutshi (founder, Property Investors Network; founder, Crowd Property; author, "Property Magic").

---

## The core principle: distressed seller, not distressed property

There are two entirely different reasons a property might trade at a discount:

1. **Distressed property**: structural defects, damp, subsidence, planning issues, extensive refurb required. The discount reflects the cost and risk of the work. This is a development/refurb deal.
2. **Distressed seller**: the property is in reasonable or good condition but the seller has personal circumstances that make speed and certainty more important than maximum price.

The BMV (below market value) motivated-seller strategy targets scenario 2 exclusively. When the seller is distressed but the property is not, a same-day remortgage at full market value becomes possible - because lenders will value the property at its true market price.

---

## Who is a motivated seller?

Approximately 5% of sellers at any given time are genuinely motivated (per Simon Zutshi's 20+ years of experience). They share common characteristics:

- **They have a problem**: divorce/separation, bereavement/probate, financial difficulty, impending bailiff action, a chain that has collapsed, a tight deadline to purchase somewhere else
- **Their priority is speed and certainty, not price**: they have had deals fall through and fear it happening again. A buyer who will commit, move fast, and not renegotiate at the last minute is worth more than a buyer offering the highest price
- **Time pressure amplifies motivation**: the closer to a deadline, the more willing to accept a deeper discount
- **They often sell at a profit anyway**: many have held their property for decades and bought for far less than today's value, so in their mind they are still selling at a substantial gain even at a discount

**Case study (Steve and Roshan, Staffordshire, January 2026):**
- Property originally listed at £325,000. Reduced to £300,000. Reduced again to £275,000.
- Sale fell through on 15 December. Seller (elderly man, recently widowed, daughter in Scotland) needed to complete by end of January to secure his dream retirement bungalow.
- Two offers from cash-buying companies: £240,000 and £225,000.
- Steve visited next day, built genuine rapport, understood the seller's actual need. Offered £190,000 with a guaranteed completion date. Seller accepted.
- Discount: £110,000+ below true market value of approximately £300,000 (36% below market value).
- Seller was not exploited - he secured his retirement home on time. His daughter left a video testimonial describing the experience as stress-free and professional.

## The ethics of BMV buying

BMV buying has a bad reputation because some operators act unethically: making a low offer verbally, then dropping it further at exchange ("gazundering"). Ethical practice means:

- Never make an offer you intend to reduce later
- Spend time understanding the seller's real situation
- Offer what they genuinely need (certainty, speed, no fall-through)
- Do not misrepresent your intentions or capabilities
- If you cannot complete to the timeline you promised, withdraw early so the seller has time to find another buyer

When done ethically, BMV buying is genuinely helpful to sellers in difficult situations. The seller values certainty over maximum price.

## Where to find motivated sellers

| Source | Notes |
|---|---|
| Estate agents | Most agents don't distinguish motivated from ordinary sellers. Build relationships. Ask for properties that have been reduced multiple times or that have had buyers pull out. |
| Failed auction lots | Properties entered in auction signal a desire for speed. A lot that fails to sell means a seller who has publicly failed - their motivation usually increases after auction. |
| Property Filter | Paid software (~£97/month, 8-day free trial) that aggregates deals by reduction history, time on market, and other signals. One of the most effective screening tools in the UK market. |
| Off-market letters | Write to landlords at addresses obtained from HMO registers, landlord lists (via council FOI), or Rightmove landlord listings. Many landlords who want to sell tell their letting agent long before they list publicly. |
| Letting agents | Independent letting agents (not linked to estate agents) often know when landlords want to sell but don't know what to do. A relationship with a local lettings-only agent can produce off-market leads. |
| Word of mouth / networking | Many top BMV deals come from relationships: investors, solicitors, accountants, mortgage brokers who know someone in difficulty. PIN meetings and property networks are specifically built for this. |
| Quick-sale franchises | "Sell my house fast" websites (e.g., Sell My House Quickly franchise - a network of franchise operators) attract motivated sellers directly. Franchise owners earn introduction fees. |
| Solicitors | Probate and divorce solicitors regularly encounter clients who need to sell quickly. A relationship with one or two local specialists in these areas can be a reliable source. |

## The 100% Finance method: same-day remortgage

### The fundamental distinction: mortgage vs remortgage

- **Mortgage** (at purchase): lenders calculate loan on the lower of purchase price or market value. You buy at £190,000 in a property worth £300,000 - the lender offers a mortgage of 75% of £190,000 = £142,500. You still need to fund the remaining £47,500 + costs.
- **Remortgage** (after you own it): lenders calculate based on current market value, not what you paid. You own a property worth £300,000 - lender offers 75% of £300,000 = £225,000. Purchase price is irrelevant to a remortgage.

This distinction is the legal basis of the same-day remortgage strategy.

### How the same-day remortgage works

**Step 1 - Agree the BMV purchase** (at a significant discount, minimum approximately 32% for a true no-money-down result at 75% LTV)

**Step 2 - Arrange bridging finance** covering 100% of the purchase price. As of mid-2026, a new private bridging lender is entering the market that will lend 100% of the purchase price (previously capped at 90%) specifically for same-day remortgage transactions. Cost: 2% arrangement fee, payable once, no monthly interest (it is not held for more than a day).

**Step 3 - Simultaneously apply for the remortgage** (before completing the purchase). Use the same solicitor for both transactions. The remortgage offer is based on a RICS valuation at market value.

**Step 4 - Complete**. On completion day:
1. Solicitor registers transfer of ownership
2. Remortgage funds (e.g., £225,000) are drawn down
3. Bridging loan (e.g., £190,000) is repaid from remortgage funds, plus 2% fee (£3,800)
4. Remaining funds (e.g., £31,200) cover SDLT, legals, valuation - any surplus stays with the investor

**Step 5 - Hold as BTL / HMO / SA** with a standard buy-to-let mortgage in place and ongoing rental income.

### The maths (Steve and Roshan example)

| Item | Amount |
|---|---|
| Market value (RICS valuation) | £300,000 |
| Purchase price (36% BMV) | £190,000 |
| Bridging loan (100% of purchase) | £190,000 |
| Remortgage at 75% of market value | £225,000 |
| Bridging fee (2% of purchase) | £3,800 |
| Net proceeds after bridging repayment | £31,200 |
| SDLT + legals + valuation (approx.) | ~£10,000 |
| **Net cash out** | ~£21,200 |

The investor completed with zero of their own money and received approximately £21,000 in cash on completion.

### The 32% rule

At 75% LTV (standard BTL remortgage), approximately 32% below market value is required to achieve true no-money-down (covering bridging fee, SDLT, legals, and valuation). At shallower discounts (e.g., 20%), some cash is still required but it is significantly less than a standard 25% deposit.

Historical note: When mortgages were offered at 85% LTV (pre-2008), a 17% discount was sufficient for no-money-down. The GFC tightened LTV ratios, making the strategy less viable for many years. As of mid-2026, the strategy is live again.

### Current limitations (as of May 2026)

- **England and Wales only** (and Northern Ireland): the specific remortgage exit lenders do not currently offer products in Scotland due to Scottish property law differences
- **BTL / HMO / block of flats only**: this is not available for owner-occupied residential, where affordability (income) assessment drives the mortgage, not rental yield
- **Reasonable credit required**: no active CCJs or defaults; credit does not need to be excellent
- **Timeline**: 4-6 weeks minimum (searches, RICS valuation, remortgage offer must all run simultaneously)
- **Works in limited company or personal name**: structure does not affect the mechanics
- **SDLT**: same-day remortgage proceeds usually cover SDLT, but some lenders require SDLT to be paid on the day from available funds before remortgage draws down

### Who coordinates this

A specialist broker (such as Edge Finance / Jamie Gillespie) can arrange both the 100% bridging and the remortgage in parallel. Standard solicitors may not be familiar with the simultaneous transaction structure - use a solicitor experienced in same-day remortgage completions.

## The second method: development finance + private lender

When the property requires significant refurbishment (distressed property, not distressed seller), the same-day remortgage is not available (the valuation will reflect the as-is condition, not end value). Instead:

- **Development/bridging finance** covers the majority of purchase + works (first charge lender, e.g., Crowd Property)
- **Private lender** covers the remainder as a second charge, at a fixed return (e.g., 15-20% per annum for the duration of the build) rather than 50% profit share (JV)

Why private lending is preferable to JV partnerships:
- JV: partner takes 50% of all profit and all capital growth in perpetuity
- Private lending: partner takes a fixed return only (e.g., 20% for 6 months = 10%), then exits. Full upside stays with the investor.
- Once the development is complete and refinanced, the private lender is repaid in full and the investor retains 100% of the asset

Private lending requires: a clear loan agreement, first or second charge security, personal guarantee (for higher-risk lenders), a project plan with cost and GDV schedules, and a lender who understands property development risk.

## Carina guidance notes

When a user asks about BMV deals, Carina should:
1. First establish whether they mean distressed seller or distressed property - two different strategies
2. For distressed seller: explain the empathy-first sourcing approach, sourcing channels, and the same-day remortgage calculator
3. For distressed property (refurb): explain development finance + private lending structure
4. Remind users of the 32% rule for no-money-down qualification
5. Flag that the strategy currently applies in England, Wales, and Northern Ireland only
6. Always recommend a specialist broker for the bridging + remortgage arrangement (not a high street bank)
7. Emphasise the ethical dimension: motivated sellers are helped, not exploited, when the process is transparent and commitments are honoured
