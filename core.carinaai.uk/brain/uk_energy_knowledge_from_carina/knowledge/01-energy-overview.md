# UK Energy Industry

## Overview

The UK energy sector contributes approximately £95 billion annually to the economy and employs approximately 700,000 people. The UK is a global leader in offshore wind and has legally binding net-zero targets (net zero by 2050, with intermediary carbon budgets). The energy mix has transformed dramatically: coal has been virtually eliminated (last coal power station closed September 2024), while renewables, particularly offshore wind, have grown to over 40% of electricity generation.

The sector splits into: electricity generation, transmission and distribution, gas supply, oil and gas extraction (declining North Sea), nuclear, and the fast-growing renewables and clean tech segments.

## Structure

### Electricity Generation (2023 mix)
- Gas: approximately 32%
- Renewables: approximately 42% (wind 29%, solar 5%, biomass 6%, hydro 2%)
- Nuclear: approximately 14%
- Imports (interconnectors): approximately 11%
- Coal: <1% (ended September 2024)

### Renewables
- Offshore wind: UK is world leader. Capacity approximately 14 GW (2024), target 40 GW by 2030.
- Major offshore wind farms: Dogger Bank (3.6 GW, under construction, world's largest), Hornsea (2.6 GW), Seagreen (1.1 GW)
- Onshore wind: approximately 15 GW capacity. De facto ban in England (2015) lifted 2024.
- Solar: approximately 18 GW capacity. Rapid growth in utility-scale and rooftop.
- Major developers: Orsted, SSE Renewables, RWE, ScottishPower Renewables, Vattenfall, Equinor

### Nuclear
- Current fleet: 5 operational stations generating approximately 5.9 GW. All Advanced Gas-Cooled Reactors (AGRs) nearing end of life. Last AGR closure expected 2028.
- New build: Hinkley Point C (EDF, 3.2 GW, £32.7 billion budget, operational target 2031). Sizewell C (3.2 GW, investment decision pending, £20+ billion).
- Small Modular Reactors (SMRs): Government competition launched. Rolls-Royce SMR frontrunner.
- Nuclear decommissioning: NDA (Nuclear Decommissioning Authority) manages 17 legacy sites. Total estimated clean-up cost £132 billion (over 120 years).

### Oil and Gas
- North Sea production declining at approximately 5% per year
- UK Continental Shelf has produced approximately 47 billion barrels of oil equivalent since 1970s
- Remaining reserves: approximately 10-20 billion barrels equivalent (depending on price and technology)
- North Sea operators: BP, Shell, TotalEnergies, Harbour Energy, Ithaca Energy
- NSTA (North Sea Transition Authority): regulator for oil, gas, and carbon storage
- Windfall tax (Energy Profits Levy): 35% on North Sea profits (total tax rate 75%). Extended to 2029.

### Networks
- National Grid: owns and operates the high-voltage transmission network in England and Wales. Also operates the electricity system (ESO; to become independent NESO from 2024).
- Distribution Network Operators (DNOs): 14 licensed DNOs operating the lower-voltage distribution networks. Largest: UK Power Networks, Western Power Distribution (National Grid), Scottish and Southern Electricity Networks.
- Gas transmission: National Gas (sold by National Grid in 2023). Gas distribution: 4 gas distribution networks (Cadent, SGN, Northern Gas Networks, Wales & West).

### Retail Supply
- "Big Six" (now reduced): British Gas (Centrica), E.ON Next, EDF Energy, OVO Energy (acquired SSE retail), ScottishPower, Octopus Energy. Octopus has grown to second-largest supplier through innovation.
- Approximately 20 million domestic electricity customers and 18 million gas customers
- Price cap: Ofgem energy price cap introduced 2019, reviewed quarterly. Typical household bill approximately £1,928 (Jan-Mar 2024 cap).

## Key Regulation

| Body | Role |
|------|------|
| Ofgem | Economic regulator for electricity and gas markets. Price cap, network price controls (RIIO), supplier licensing. |
| DESNZ (Department for Energy Security and Net Zero) | Government department for energy policy (created 2023 from BEIS) |
| NSTA (North Sea Transition Authority) | Oil, gas, and carbon storage regulator |
| Environment Agency | Environmental permits for power stations, emissions, water abstraction |
| ONR (Office for Nuclear Regulation) | Nuclear safety and security regulator |
| National Grid ESO (becoming NESO) | Electricity system operator. Balances supply and demand in real time. |
| Climate Change Committee (CCC) | Independent advisory body on climate targets and carbon budgets |
| HSE | Health and safety across energy infrastructure |

## Key Legislation
- Climate Change Act 2008; legally binding net zero by 2050. Five-yearly carbon budgets.
- Energy Act 2013; Electricity Market Reform. Contracts for Difference (CfD) scheme, Capacity Market.
- Energy Act 2023; framework for carbon capture, hydrogen, heat networks, and energy security
- Electricity Act 1989; foundational electricity regulation
- Petroleum Act 1998; oil and gas licensing regime
- Nuclear Energy (Financing) Act 2022; RAB (Regulated Asset Base) model for nuclear financing

## Pain Points (Agent Opportunity)
1. **CfD (Contracts for Difference)**: Application process for renewable projects. Complex bid documentation, financial modelling, supply chain plans.
2. **Grid connection applications**: 40+ GW of projects in queue. Connection dates in 2030s. Complex application and modification process.
3. **Planning applications (DCOs)**: Nationally Significant Infrastructure Projects. Development Consent Orders for major energy projects. Environmental statements, consultation.
4. **REGO and green certificates**: Renewable Energy Guarantees of Origin. Compliance tracking and reporting.
5. **Energy Performance Certificates (EPCs)**: Residential and commercial. MEES (Minimum Energy Efficiency Standards) require EPC E or above for lettings.
6. **Heat network regulation**: New regulatory framework from Energy Act 2023. Notification, metering, pricing rules.
7. **Supply chain compliance**: Modern slavery statements, local content reporting (CfD requirement), carbon footprint of supply chain.
8. **Customer switching**: Ofgem faster switching programme. Debt blocking, erroneous transfers.
9. **Smart meter rollout**: 35 million+ installed. DCC (Data Communications Company) manages data. SMETS1 to SMETS2 migration issues.
10. **Carbon reporting**: SECR (Streamlined Energy and Carbon Reporting), ESOS (Energy Savings Opportunity Scheme), TCFD-aligned disclosures.

## Key Terminology
- **CfD (Contract for Difference)**: Renewable support mechanism. Generator receives difference between strike price and market price.
- **RIIO**: Revenue = Incentives + Innovation + Outputs. Ofgem price control framework for networks.
- **NESO**: National Energy System Operator (from 2024). Independent from National Grid plc.
- **DNO**: Distribution Network Operator.
- **ESO**: Electricity System Operator.
- **Capacity Market**: Auction to ensure sufficient generating capacity for future winters.
- **DCC**: Data Communications Company. Smart meter data infrastructure.
- **SMETS1/SMETS2**: Smart meter specifications. SMETS2 is current standard.
- **ESOS**: Energy Savings Opportunity Scheme. Mandatory 4-yearly audit for large undertakings.
- **SECR**: Streamlined Energy and Carbon Reporting. Mandatory for large companies and LLPs.
- **MEES**: Minimum Energy Efficiency Standards. Minimum EPC E for lettings.
- **EPC**: Energy Performance Certificate. Scale A (best) to G.
- **REGO**: Renewable Energy Guarantee of Origin. Certificate proving electricity is from renewable sources.
- **CCS (Carbon Capture and Storage)**: Government target to capture 20-30 million tonnes CO2 annually by 2030.
- **RAB (Regulated Asset Base)**: Infrastructure financing model now applied to nuclear.
