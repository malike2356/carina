# UK Hospitality Industry

## Overview

The UK hospitality sector contributes approximately £73 billion annually to the economy (2023) and employs approximately 2.5 million people, making it the third-largest employer in the UK. The sector encompasses hotels, restaurants, pubs, bars, cafes, contract catering, events, and nightclubs.

Post-pandemic recovery has been hampered by labour shortages, energy costs, and inflation. Approximately 140,000 hospitality businesses operate in the UK.

## Structure

### Hotels
- Approximately 45,000 hotels and guesthouses
- Major groups: Whitbread (Premier Inn, 850+ hotels), InterContinental Hotels Group (IHG; Holiday Inn, Crowne Plaza, headquartered in UK but global), Travelodge (600+ hotels), Accor (Ibis, Novotel, Mercure)
- Boutique/luxury: Firmdale Hotels, Soho House, The Pig Hotels
- Budget: easyHotel, Yotel, Z Hotels
- Short-term rentals: Airbnb (approximately 300,000 UK listings)

### Restaurants
- Approximately 85,000 restaurants
- Casual dining: Nando's, PizzaExpress, Wagamama, The Restaurant Group (Frankie & Benny's, Chiquito, Wagamama)
- Premium: D&D London, The Ivy Collection, Gaucho
- Fast food: McDonald's (1,400+ UK locations), KFC, Burger King, Subway
- Delivery: Deliveroo, Uber Eats, Just Eat
- Fine dining: Approximately 190 Michelin-starred restaurants in the UK (2024)

### Pubs and Bars
- Approximately 44,000 pubs (down from 50,000 in 2010)
- Pub groups: Greene King, Mitchells & Butlers (All Bar One, Toby Carvery, Harvester), Wetherspoon (800+ pubs), Stonegate (Slug and Lettuce, Yates)
- Wet-led vs food-led split; trend toward food-led venues
- Average pub closure rate: approximately 500 per year

### Events and Contract Catering
- Contract caterers: Compass Group (world's largest, headquartered UK), Sodexo, Elior, BaxterStorey
- Events market: approximately £42 billion (conferences, exhibitions, weddings, festivals)
- Wedding industry: approximately £15 billion, approximately 250,000 weddings per year

## Key Regulation

| Body | Role |
|------|------|
| Food Standards Agency (FSA) | Food safety regulation and enforcement |
| Environmental Health Officers (EHOs) | Local authority food hygiene inspections. Scores on the Doors (0-5 rating). |
| HSE | Health and safety enforcement |
| HMRC | Alcohol duty, VAT, National Minimum Wage enforcement |
| Local authorities | Licensing (alcohol, entertainment, late-night refreshment) |
| National Minimum Wage | NMW and National Living Wage apply. Hospitality is the most-enforced sector (25% of all NMW breaches). |

## Market Data
- Average restaurant spend per head: £20-25
- Hotel average daily rate (ADR): £92 (London £155, regions £72)
- Hotel occupancy: 78% (London 82%)
- Tipping: Service charge typically 12.5% (discretionary). New legislation from October 2024 requires 100% of tips to go to workers.
- VAT: 20% on hospitality (temporarily reduced to 5% during pandemic, returned to 12.5% then 20%)

## Pain Points (Agent Opportunity)
1. **Allergen management**: Natasha's Law (2021) requires full ingredient labelling. Compliance is complex and high-stakes.
2. **Staff scheduling/rotas**: High turnover (30% annually), zero-hour contracts, seasonal demand.
3. **Food hygiene compliance**: EHO inspection preparation, HACCP documentation, temperature logs.
4. **Supply chain**: Multi-supplier ordering, invoice reconciliation, food cost percentage management.
5. **Reservation management**: No-shows, deposit handling, table optimisation.
6. **Licensing applications**: Premises licence variations, temporary event notices, designated premises supervisor changes.
7. **Menu engineering**: Costing, allergen annotation, nutritional information, seasonal updates.
8. **VAT and tipping compliance**: Complex rules around service charges, tronc systems, and distribution.
9. **Guest communication**: Booking confirmations, pre-arrival, post-stay follow-up, complaint handling.
10. **Energy management**: Hospitality is energy-intensive. £6 billion annual energy spend. Sustainability reporting.
