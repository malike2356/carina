# UK Finance & Banking Industry

## Overview

The UK financial services sector is the largest in Europe and second globally after the US. It contributed approximately £173 billion to the UK economy in 2023 (8.3% of GDP) and employs over 1.1 million people. London is the world's leading financial centre alongside New York, with the largest foreign exchange market (38% of global FX turnover) and the leading centre for cross-border bank lending.

The sector generated £75.6 billion in tax revenue (2023), representing 10.5% of total UK tax receipts.

## Structure

### Banking

- **Retail banking**: Current accounts, savings, mortgages, personal loans. Dominated by the "Big Four": HSBC, Barclays, Lloyds Banking Group (including Halifax, Bank of Scotland), NatWest Group (including RBS, Ulster Bank).
- **Challenger banks**: Monzo (9 million customers), Starling Bank (4 million), Revolut (10 million UK customers), Metro Bank, Virgin Money. Digital-first, branchless or light-branch.
- **Investment banking**: M&A advisory, capital markets, trading. Major players: Goldman Sachs, JP Morgan, Morgan Stanley, Barclays, HSBC.
- **Private banking/wealth management**: Coutts, C. Hoare & Co, UBS Wealth Management. Approximately £1.4 trillion assets under management.
- **Building societies**: Nationwide (largest, 16 million members), Yorkshire, Coventry, Skipton. Mutual ownership structure. Approximately 43 building societies remain.

### Insurance

- UK insurance market is the largest in Europe and fourth globally
- Total premium income: approximately £227 billion (2023)
- London Market (Lloyd's of London): unique insurance and reinsurance marketplace. Approximately £50 billion gross written premiums.
- Major insurers: Aviva, Legal & General, Prudential, RSA, Admiral, Direct Line Group
- Life insurance: approximately £6.7 trillion liabilities managed by UK insurers
- Motor insurance: mandatory, approximately £13 billion market
- Health insurance: Bupa (42% market share), AXA PPP, Aviva, VitalityHealth

### Asset Management

- UK is the second-largest asset management centre globally
- Assets under management: approximately £11 trillion (2023)
- Major firms: Schroders, Legal & General Investment Management, abrdn (formerly Standard Life Aberdeen), M&G, Baillie Gifford
- Pension funds: approximately £3 trillion assets

### Fintech

- UK fintech sector: approximately £22 billion revenue (2023)
- Over 2,500 fintech firms
- London is the global fintech capital alongside San Francisco
- Major segments: payments (Stripe UK, Checkout.com, Wise, GoCardless), digital banking (Monzo, Starling, Revolut), regtech (Onfido, ComplyAdvantage), insurtech (Zego, ManyPets), wealthtech (Nutmeg, Moneybox), lending (Funding Circle, Zopa)
- Open Banking: UK pioneered open banking regulation in 2018. Over 7 million active users of open banking services.

### Capital Markets

- London Stock Exchange: approximately 2,000 companies listed. Market cap approximately £3.8 trillion.
- AIM (Alternative Investment Market): approximately 700 smaller companies
- Bond markets: UK government bonds (gilts) approximately £2.5 trillion outstanding
- FX: London handles 38% of global foreign exchange turnover ($3.6 trillion daily)

## Key Regulation

| Body | Role |
|------|------|
| Bank of England | Central bank. Monetary policy, financial stability, prudential regulation via PRA |
| PRA (Prudential Regulation Authority) | Prudential regulation of banks, insurers, and major investment firms |
| FCA (Financial Conduct Authority) | Conduct regulation of all financial services firms (approximately 50,000 firms). Consumer protection, market integrity. |
| FPC (Financial Policy Committee) | Systemic risk monitoring, macroprudential policy |
| PSR (Payment Systems Regulator) | Regulates payment systems |
| FOS (Financial Ombudsman Service) | Resolves disputes between consumers and financial firms |
| FSCS (Financial Services Compensation Scheme) | Protects consumers when firms fail (up to £85,000 per person per firm) |
| ICO (Information Commissioner's Office) | Data protection enforcement in financial services |
| The Pensions Regulator | Regulates workplace pension schemes |

### Key Legislation

- **Financial Services and Markets Act 2000 (FSMA)**; foundational regulatory framework. Created the FSA (now split into PRA and FCA).
- **Financial Services Act 2012**; abolished FSA, created PRA and FCA (twin peaks model)
- **Financial Services and Markets Act 2023**; post-Brexit regulatory reforms, repeals retained EU law
- **Consumer Duty (2023)**; FCA requirement. Firms must deliver good outcomes for retail customers. Major implementation programme for all firms.
- **Payment Services Regulations 2017**; implements PSD2, open banking
- **Senior Managers and Certification Regime (SMCR)**; individual accountability for senior financial services staff
- **Money Laundering Regulations 2017**; AML requirements for financial institutions
- **UK GDPR**; governs processing of personal financial data
- **Mortgage Market Review (MMR, 2014)**; stricter affordability checks for mortgage lending

## Pain Points (Agent Opportunity)

1. **KYC/AML compliance**: Customer due diligence, PEP screening, sanctions checking. High volume, high stakes.
2. **Consumer Duty compliance**: Fair value assessments, customer communications reviews, management information production.
3. **Mortgage application processing**: Document collection, verification, and assessment. Average time: 4-6 weeks.
4. **Claims handling**: Insurance claims triage, document review, and settlement calculation.
5. **Regulatory reporting**: COREP, FINREP, and other regulatory returns. Quarterly/annual submissions. Prone to errors.
6. **Complaint handling**: FCA-regulated complaint process (acknowledge within 3 days, resolve within 8 weeks). High volume.
7. **Financial crime detection**: SARs (Suspicious Activity Reports) preparation. Approximately 500,000 SARs filed annually.
8. **Contract review**: ISDA agreements, loan documentation, vendor contracts. Heavy document review.
9. **Wealth management suitability**: Assessing investment suitability, producing suitability reports. Regulated process.
10. **Open banking data aggregation**: Reconciling transactions across multiple accounts and providers.

## Key Terminology

- **PRA**: Prudential Regulation Authority. Part of Bank of England.
- **FCA**: Financial Conduct Authority. Conduct regulator.
- **SMCR**: Senior Managers and Certification Regime.
- **AML**: Anti-Money Laundering.
- **KYC**: Know Your Customer. Identity verification and risk assessment.
- **PEP**: Politically Exposed Person. Enhanced due diligence required.
- **SAR**: Suspicious Activity Report. Filed with the National Crime Agency.
- **MiFID II**: Markets in Financial Instruments Directive. EU regulation, largely retained in UK law.
- **PRIIPs**: Packaged Retail and Insurance-based Investment Products. Disclosure requirements.
- **COREP**: Common Reporting framework. Regulatory capital reporting.
- **FINREP**: Financial Reporting. Standardised financial reporting for supervisory purposes.
- **PSD2**: Payment Services Directive 2. Enabled open banking.
- **Open Banking**: Requirement for banks to share customer data with authorised third parties (with customer consent).
- **BTL**: Buy-to-Let mortgage.
- **LTV**: Loan-to-Value ratio.
- **APRC**: Annual Percentage Rate of Charge. Total cost of mortgage including fees.
- **FOS**: Financial Ombudsman Service.
- **FSCS**: Financial Services Compensation Scheme. Protects deposits up to £85,000.
- **Ring-fencing**: Requirement since 2019 for large UK banks to separate retail banking from investment banking.
