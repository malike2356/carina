# UK Manufacturing Industry

## Overview

The UK manufacturing sector contributes approximately £224 billion annually (2023), representing approximately 9.7% of GDP. The UK is the 8th-largest manufacturing nation globally. Despite the narrative of decline, UK manufacturing output has grown in real terms; the decline is in employment share, not output value.

The sector employs approximately 2.6 million people. Key strengths: aerospace (2nd globally after US), pharmaceuticals, automotive, food and drink (largest sub-sector), chemicals, and precision engineering.

## Structure

### Food and Drink
- Largest manufacturing sub-sector: approximately £35 billion turnover
- Employs approximately 450,000 people
- Major players: Associated British Foods, Unilever UK, Nestle UK, Diageo (spirits), Coca-Cola Europacific Partners, Britvic, Premier Foods
- Export strength: Scotch whisky (£6.2 billion exports, 2023), chocolate, cheese

### Aerospace
- Second-largest aerospace industry globally (after US)
- Annual turnover: approximately £35 billion
- Major players: Airbus (wings designed and manufactured at Broughton and Filton), Rolls-Royce (aero engines, Derby and Bristol), BAE Systems, GKN Aerospace, Bombardier (Belfast, now Spirit AeroSystems)
- Approximately 120,000 direct employees
- Key products: wings, engines, landing gear, avionics, satellites

### Automotive
- Approximately 905,000 vehicles produced in the UK (2023, up from 775,000 in 2022 but below pre-pandemic 1.3 million)
- Major manufacturers: Jaguar Land Rover (JLR, Tata-owned), Nissan (Sunderland; largest UK car plant), Toyota (Burnaston), BMW MINI (Oxford), Rolls-Royce Motor Cars (Goodwood, BMW-owned), Bentley (Crewe, VW-owned), Aston Martin, McLaren
- Engine manufacturing: Ford (Bridgend closed 2020, now Dagenham diesel only), BMW Hams Hall
- Supply chain: approximately 2,500 automotive suppliers
- F1 cluster: Motorsport Valley in Oxfordshire/Northamptonshire; 8 of 10 F1 teams based in UK

### Pharmaceuticals and Life Sciences
- See Healthcare domain for details
- Annual turnover: approximately £38 billion
- GSK and AstraZeneca are the two UK-headquartered global pharma companies

### Chemicals
- Annual turnover: approximately £55 billion
- Major players: INEOS, BASF UK, Croda International, Johnson Matthey
- Teesside and Humber are major chemical clusters

### Steel and Metals
- UK steel production: approximately 6 million tonnes (2023)
- Major producers: Tata Steel UK (Port Talbot; transitioning from blast furnaces to electric arc), British Steel (Scunthorpe, owned by Jingye Group), Liberty Steel
- Aluminium: Liberty British Aluminium (Fort William smelter; Scotland's last)

## Pain Points (Agent Opportunity)
1. **Supply chain resilience**: Post-pandemic and post-Brexit, supply chain mapping and risk assessment are priorities.
2. **Regulatory compliance**: UKCA marking, CE marking, ISO 9001/14001/45001 audits, REACH (chemicals), COMAH.
3. **R&D tax credit claims**: Complex documentation requirements. HMRC increasing scrutiny and compliance checks.
4. **Production scheduling**: MES/ERP integration, capacity planning, materials requirement planning.
5. **Quality control documentation**: Inspection records, non-conformance reports, corrective action tracking.
6. **Health and safety**: Risk assessments, method statements, COSHH assessments, permit to work systems.
7. **Customs and rules of origin**: Post-Brexit, proving UK origin for preferential trade under UK-EU TCA. Complex supply chain tracing.
8. **Energy management**: Manufacturers are energy-intensive. ESOS, CCA (Climate Change Agreements), SECR reporting.
9. **Skills and apprenticeships**: Apprenticeship levy management (0.5% of payroll for large employers). Engineering skills shortage.
10. **Maintenance scheduling**: Predictive maintenance, CMMS (Computerised Maintenance Management Systems), spare parts inventory.
