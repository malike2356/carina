## 1. Deal Sourcing Principles (Residential and Commercial)

### Filter by motivation, not by property
The beginner approach is scrolling Rightmove/Zoopla and booking viewings on anything interesting. The professional approach:
- Focus on motivated seller signals: price reductions, days on market, relisting, selling below purchase price
- Only engage with properties where the vendor demonstrably wants to sell
- Result: 10x fewer viewings, 10x more effective deal finding

### Automated direct vendor letters: qualify before contacting
Mass mailings to all landlords ("I want to buy your property") are routinely ignored. Effective targeting:
- Identify specifically motivated vendors (price-reduced, relisted, distress signals)
- Write targeted letters to those qualified prospects
- Response rates on unqualified mailings are negligible; qualified targeting produces dramatically higher results

### Remote viewings work
Using local agents, sourcing partners, or paid viewers to conduct viewings is normal for experienced scale investors. This allows UK-wide sourcing without travel and scales through team leverage.

### AI deal packs
Once a deal is analysed, preparing an investor-facing deal pack can be generated by AI (Claude, ChatGPT) in under 60 seconds from the property data and financial analysis. This is integrated into sourcing tools like Property Filter. For sourcers charging £3,000-£5,000 per deal, this is a significant competitive advantage.

### Off-market deals
Off-market properties can be found through direct-to-vendor letter campaigns targeted at motivated sellers identified via Land Registry purchases, planning applications, and other circumstantial data. This is standard practice, not a rare technique.

## 2. Service Accommodation Tactical Additions

These complement the full SA guide at `83-service-accommodation-strategy.md`.

### High-end positioning as a problem filter
Pricing at the premium tier of your market automatically eliminates the vast majority of problem guests. People looking to book cheaply for parties, damage, or refund exploitation will not book the most expensive, visibly well-managed property on the street. Premium positioning removes 90% of nightmare guest scenarios before they start.

### How to handle the inevitable bad review
- Every SA property will eventually receive a 4-star or 3-star review regardless of quality
- The defence is volume and consistency - a portfolio of 5-star reviews makes one outlier irrelevant
- Never argue with guests publicly in review responses
- For harassment or blackmail attempts: contact Airbnb directly with evidence, document everything, respond calmly and factually
- Airbnb Guest Favorites (top 20% of listings) capture nearly 50% of all bookings - at that level, one negative review does not derail placement

### Dynamic pricing is non-negotiable
Setting fixed prices for a whole calendar year is a costly mistake. Dynamic pricing software (PriceLabs, AirDNA) adjusts nightly rates in real time based on demand signals, events, competitor pricing, and seasonal patterns. Without it, revenue is left on the table every week.

### Regulation is a quality filter
Counter-intuitive but consistent: the most regulated SA market in the UK (Scottish Highlands and Edinburgh) has the highest nightly rates and highest occupancy. Regulation filters out amateur operators, reduces supply, and raises the quality floor. Professional operators thrive when amateurs exit the market.

## 3. Commercial Property Tactical Additions

These complement the full commercial investment guide at `84-commercial-property-investment.md`.

### Approach motivated sellers with empathy
Do not lead with a lowball offer. First: understand why they are selling. What has changed? Motivation drives negotiation, not your opening price. Common triggers: time pressure, estate/succession, tenant problems, vacancy frustration.

### Adding value in commercial
The best commercial deals have a gap between current passing rent and estimated rental value (ERV). Buy at a yield based on current income, close the gap through active management, and capital value rises as income rises. Value is also created through:
- Planning gains (change of use, extending, subdividing)
- Lease restructuring
- Filling vacancy

### Mixed-use: underwrite on commercial first
When evaluating retail/commercial with residential above, underwrite the deal on the commercial income alone. The residential upside (planning, conversion) is bonus value, not the primary thesis. If planning falls through, the commercial yield must still justify the acquisition.

### Scotland
Different law (contract, landlord and tenant statutes), same investment principles. Scottish commercial lawyers are required. Deals happen at all price points.

## 4. Investor Mindset

### Buffett's warning: avoid mindless imitation
Copying what competitors do ("everyone builds 1-bed City Centre SA, so I will too") produces average results because you compete for the same tenants with the same product in the same market. The highest returns come from understanding what drives demand and finding a less-contested way to access it. Example: proximity to a demand driver (football stadium + 400,000 annual tours) in an area with below-average property prices, with boutique execution in a non-boutique area.

### Capital efficiency over headline price
A £129,000 property in an area with strong SA demand generating £50,000/year revenue outperforms a £500,000 property generating £30,000/year. Entry price, local competition, and demand-driver proximity are the key inputs - not the prestige of the postcode.

### Fewer viewings, better decisions
Experienced SA investors viewed 10 properties in 14 months and bought 2 with confidence. Most new investors view 50-100 and struggle because they have not pre-validated demand. Pre-validate demand at macro and micro level before viewings, then view only properties that meet the criteria.
