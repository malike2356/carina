## 1. Client Journey & Boundary Management

### Why Boundaries Matter

Clients push boundaries to see what they can get away with. Your job is to hold the line professionally but firmly. Professionalism protects the deal. If something feels wrong, it probably is wrong.

### Boundary Enforcement Rules

| Scenario | Action |
|---|---|
| Client asks for something outside scope | Politely decline and restate what was agreed |
| Client challenges pricing or terms | Do not negotiate - stand by your published terms |
| Client requests work outside payment tier | Require upgrade before delivering |
| Client becomes demanding or rude | Call it out calmly and offer to continue when they're ready |

### Red Flags - Fire Early

- Client haggles on £24.99 VIP access
- Client pays late once
- Client repeatedly asks for exceptions
- Client ignores your communication system

**Rule:** A client who pays late once will pay late forever. Fire bad clients early. Not every client is worth having.

## 2. Effective Communication Systems

### Scheduled Engagement Cadence

| Day | Time | Group | Content |
|---|---|---|---|
| Monday | 2:00 PM | VIP database (paid) | Early deals, priority insights, exclusive opportunities |
| Tuesday | 9:00 AM | General database (unpaid) | General market updates, educational content, soft offers |

### Why This Works

- Trains investors to expect and watch for your communications at specific times
- Creates routine and anticipation
- Engagement builds Know Like Trust (KLT)
- VIPs feel prioritised, general database sees the value of upgrading

### Communication Protocol

1. Send exactly on schedule - same day, same time, every week
2. Do not send off-schedule communications unless urgent
3. Make every communication value-adding - no spam
4. Track open rates and engagement per contact

## 3. Business Systems & CRM Automation

### CRM Requirements (Non-Negotiable)

Use a proper CRM from day one. Spreadsheets are not an option.

| What to avoid | Why |
|---|---|
| Excel/Google Sheets | Leads get lost in the abyss. No automation. No tracking. |
| Paper records | Unsearchable. Unsortable. Unrecoverable. |
| Your inbox as a CRM | Impossible to scale. No pipeline visibility. |

### What the CRM Must Track

Every single interaction with every lead and client:

- **Calls** - date, duration, outcome, next action
- **Emails** - sent, received, opened, clicked, replied
- **Viewings** - property shown, date, feedback, objections
- **Offers** - amount, status (submitted/accepted/rejected/withdrawn), timeline
- **Follow-ups** - automated sequences triggered by behaviour (open, click, reply, no action)
- **Notes** - free-text notes from every conversation

### Automation Rules

| Trigger | Automated Action |
|---|---|
| Lead signs up (free) | Welcome sequence: 5 educational emails over 10 days |
| Lead opens 3 emails in a row | Tag as "warm" - move to nurture sequence |
| Lead attends a viewing | 24-hour follow-up: "How was the property?" |
| Lead makes an offer | Enter deal pipeline - daily status updates until exchange |
| Lead goes cold (no open in 30 days) | Re-engagement sequence: 3 emails over 2 weeks, then archive |

### The Database Is Your Business Asset

- Your lead and client database is the single most valuable asset in your business
- Back it up. Protect it. Never lose it.
- A spreadsheet is not a database. A CRM is.
- Every lead you lose because of bad systems is money you are throwing away.

## 4. Know Like Trust (KLT) Reinforced

### The 7-Touch Rule

People need **7 interactions** before they will trust you enough to buy high-value items (property, investments, retainer services).

### The 7-Touch Sequence (Example)

| Touch | Channel | Content | Sell? |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Email | Welcome - "Who I am and how I help investors" | No |
| 2 | Email | Educational - "3 things every investor must check before buying" | No |
| 3 | Email | Case study - "How [Client Name] found their ideal property" | No |
| 4 | Email | Market insight - current trends in their area | No |
| 5 | Email | Social proof - testimonials, success stories | No |
| 6 | Email | Soft offer - "Want early access to next week's deals?" | Soft |
| 7 | Email | Direct invitation to a conversation or viewing | Yes - open sales conversation |

### KLT Principles

- Each touch must add value without selling
- Do not skip touches - rushing the trust-building process breaks it
- Use the exact same cadence every week so the database learns to trust your reliability
- Reliable operators attract reliable clients

## 5. The Client Upgrade Path

### From Free → VIP (£24.99)

- Once someone buys £24.99 VIP, they have proven they are serious
- This is the first step in qualification

### Upgrade Conversation Script

> "You're already getting deals early as a VIP. But if you want **first refusal on everything I find** - before anyone else sees it - let's talk about setting up a retainer agreement."

### Upgrade Path

| Tier | Price | Access |
|---|---|---|
| General database | Free | Market updates, educational content |
| VIP | £24.99 | Early deals, exclusive opportunities |
| Retainer client | Bespoke | First refusal on every deal, dedicated sourcing, priority negotiation support |

## 6. Saying No

### When to Say No

| Situation | Response |
|---|---|
| Client haggles on £24.99 | "This is the price. It's not negotiable." If they push, walk away. |
| Client asks for off-schedule contact | Restate your communication schedule. Stick to it. |
| Client demands work beyond their tier | "That's available on the retainer plan. Let me know when you're ready to upgrade." |
| Client is consistently late paying | Fire them. Send a professional termination notice. |

### Why Saying No Matters

- A client who haggles on £24.99 will haggle on £5K deals
- Setting boundaries early prevents bigger problems later
- Professionalism protects the deal - not the relationship
- The right clients respect boundaries. The wrong ones get filtered out.

### The Rule

**"A client who pays late once will pay late forever."** - Fire bad clients early. Set boundaries and stick to them.

## 7. Building Trust Through Consistency

### The Consistency Framework

| Element | Rule |
|---|---|
| Day | Same day every week (Monday for VIP, Tuesday for general) |
| Time | Same time every week (2:00 PM Monday, 9:00 AM Tuesday) |
| Format | Same structure, same header style, same signature |
| Length | Concise - respect their time |
| Value | Every message contains at least one actionable insight |

### Why It Works

- Your database should know when to expect your email
- This builds trust through reliability
- Reliability attracts reliable clients
- Inconsistent communication = unreliable operator in their mind

### Enforcement

- Do not deviate from the schedule
- If you must miss a slot, communicate it in advance
- Never send unscheduled marketing emails
- Log every send and verify delivery

## Summary: The Golden Rules

1. **Boundaries protect the deal** - hold the line professionally
2. **Your communication schedule trains your database** - same day, same time, every week
3. **CRM from day one** - every interaction tracked, never lose a lead
4. **7 touches minimum** before a sales conversation
5. **Upgrade the serious** - VIP proves intent, retainer proves commitment
6. **Fire bad clients early** - they will only cause more pain at higher tiers
7. **Consistency builds trust** - reliable operators attract reliable clients
