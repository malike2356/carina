# Flip Profit Margin: The Percentage That Tells You If the Deal Was Worth It

Published: Wed, 17 Mar 2027 09:00:00 +0000
Category: Calculations
Slug: flip-profit-margin-the-percentage-that-tells-you-if-the-deal-was-worth

---

Net profit in pounds is useful. Profit margin as a percentage tells you how efficient the deal was. It is your net profit divided by the sale price, multiplied by 100.\n\nA net profit of £16,120 on a £220,000 sale gives a 7.3% profit margin. That is not great but it is realistic for a South Coast flip. For comparison, a good flip should aim for 10% to 15% net margin.\n\nThe profit margin varies significantly by market. In parts of the North, where purchase prices are lower and refurb costs are a higher proportion of the total, margins of 15% to 20% are achievable. On the South Coast, where purchase prices are high and sale prices are constrained by affordability, 7% to 10% is more realistic.\n\nThe profit margin determines whether your flip is worth the risk. A 7% margin on a six-month project annualises to roughly 14%. That is decent but not exceptional. A 12% margin on a four-month project annualises to 36%, which is excellent.\n\nAim for a net profit margin of at least 10%. If the numbers show less than 7%, the deal is probably not worth the risk of cost overruns, delays, or market changes.

**The formula**

```
Profit Margin (%) = (Net Profit / Sale Price) × 100
```

**Why this matters**

Flip profit margin should be at least 10%. Below 7%, the risk of cost overruns makes the deal unattractive. South Coast flips typically achieve 7% to 12%.
