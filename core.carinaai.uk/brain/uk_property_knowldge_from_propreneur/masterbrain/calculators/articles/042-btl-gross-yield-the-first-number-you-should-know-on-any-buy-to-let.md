# BTL Gross Yield: The First Number You Should Know on Any Buy-to-Let

Published: Wed, 07 Oct 2026 09:00:00 +0000
Category: Calculations
Slug: btl-gross-yield-the-first-number-you-should-know-on-any-buy-to-let

---

Every BTL conversation starts with the same question: what is the yield? Gross yield for BTL is calculated the same way as for any property. Annual rent divided by purchase price, multiplied by 100. But in a BTL context, gross yield carries extra weight because the rental income is your primary return.

On the South Coast, BTL gross yields are typically 4% to 7%. A £250,000 property renting at £1,100 a month gives you a 5.3% gross yield. That is about average for Portsmouth. In cheaper parts of the country, yields of 7% to 9% are common. The trade-off is capital growth, which has historically been stronger in the south.

The challenge with gross yield on BTL is that it ignores your mortgage. If you have a 75% LTV mortgage at 5%, your interest-only payment is roughly 3.75% of the property value per year. That leaves only 1.5% to 3% of the property value for all your other costs.

A 5% gross yield on a £250,000 Portsmouth terrace gives you £12,500 in annual rent. Your mortgage at 5% on a 75% LTV is roughly £7,031. After mortgage costs, you have £5,469 left for management, maintenance, insurance, voids, and your profit. That is a tight margin.

Use gross yield as your first filter. If it is below 5% in the current rate environment, the deal will struggle to cashflow.

**The formula**

```
Gross Yield (%) = (Monthly Rent × 12 / Purchase Price) × 100
```

**Why this matters**

BTL gross yield on the South Coast is typically 4% to 7%. In the current rate environment, anything below 5% will be tough to make cashflow positive. Use gross yield as your first filter, then dig deeper.
