# HMO NOI: Net Operating Income for a Multi-Let Property

Published: Mon, 16 Nov 2026 09:00:00 +0000
Category: Calculations
Slug: hmo-noi-net-operating-income-for-a-multi-let-property

---

HMO Net Operating Income is your effective gross rent minus all your operating costs. And those costs are significantly higher than a single let.

For a five-bed HMO in Portsmouth with effective gross rent of £33,120, your operating costs might include: HMO licence at £800 a year, council tax (split across rooms but you pay during voids) at roughly £2,500, utilities at £3,600, insurance at £600, management at 15% of gross rent = £4,968, maintenance at 15% = £4,968, and letting fees per room change at £200 each, roughly £1,000 a year. Total costs: £18,436. Your NOI is £14,684.

Compare that to a single let in the same area with a NOI of roughly £7,000. The HMO NOI is double. That is why investors tolerate the extra management.

But here is the risk. If costs increase, the HMO NOI drops faster than a single let because you have more variable costs. A 10% rise in utility costs hits the HMO harder. A 10% rise in insurance hits the HMO harder. The higher yield comes with higher operational leverage.

Professional HMO investors track NOI monthly. If it drops below 40% of gross rent, something is wrong. Either costs are too high or rents are too low.

The HMO NOI is also the number you use when applying for HMO-specific mortgage products, which are available from some lenders who understand the multi-let model.

**The formula**

```
Operating Costs = HMO Licence + Council Tax + Utilities + Insurance + Management + Maintenance
NOI = Effective Gross Rent - Operating Costs
```

**Why this matters**

HMO NOI is typically double that of a single let on the same property. But the costs are higher and more variable. Track it monthly. If NOI drops below 40% of gross rent, your HMO is underperforming.
