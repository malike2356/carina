# Break-Even Ratio: What Occupancy You Need Just to Survive

Published: Mon, 04 Oct 2027 09:00:00 +0000

Slug: break-even-ratio-what-occupancy-you-need-just-to-survive

The Break-Even Ratio is your total operating expenses plus debt service divided by your gross potential income. It tells you what percentage of your property's potential income is consumed by costs and debt. Any remaining percentage is your profit margin.\n\nIf your total costs (operating expenses of £4,000 plus mortgage payments of £7,000) are £11,000 and your gross potential income is £12,000, your BER is 91.7%. That means 91.7% of your income goes to costs, leaving only 8.3% as profit. This is a tight margin.\n\nA BER of 85% or lower means you have a healthy buffer. Anything above 90% is dangerously thin. A small increase in costs or drop in occupancy could push you into negative territory.\n
The BER is useful for stress testing. If interest rates rise by 1% and your mortgage payment increases by £2,000, your BER jumps to 108.3%. The property stops generating positive cashflow.

**The formula**

```
BER (%) = (Operating Expenses + Debt Service) / Gross Income × 100
```

**Why this matters**

BER above 90% means dangerously thin margins. Aim for 85% or lower. Stress test your BER at higher interest rates. A small rise in rates can push a property from profit to loss.
