# HMO Gross Potential Rent: How Much a House Share Could Earn

Published: Mon, 09 Nov 2026 09:00:00 +0000
Category: Calculations
Slug: hmo-gross-potential-rent-how-much-a-house-share-could-earn

---

HMO maths is different from BTL maths. Instead of one rent for one property, you calculate room by room. The gross potential rent is the total rent from every room if every room is full.

For a five-bed HMO in Portsmouth with rooms renting at £550 to £650 each, your gross potential rent is roughly £3,000 a month, or £36,000 a year. Compare that to a standard BTL in the same area that might rent for £1,200 a month for the whole house. The HMO earns 2.5 times as much from the same building.

But that extra income comes with extra costs. Higher management, more wear and tear, higher utility bills, and more frequent voids. The HMO yield is higher, but the work is higher too.

The gross potential rent gives you the ceiling. Your actual income will be lower because of voids and bad debts. But it is the starting point for every HMO calculation.

In Portsmouth, where there is strong student and professional demand, a well-located HMO can achieve gross annual rents of £30,000 to £50,000 depending on the size and quality of the property. The gross yield can look spectacular compared to BTL. But the net yield after all the additional costs tells the real story.

**The formula**

```
Gross Potential Rent (Annual) = Rent per Room × Number of Rooms × 12
```

**Why this matters**

HMO gross potential rent is your starting point. Calculate it room by room. A well-run HMO can earn two to three times the rent of a single let in the same property, but the costs are proportionally higher.
