# First-Time Buyer SDLT Relief: How Much You Actually Save

Published: Mon, 21 Sep 2026 09:00:00 +0000
Category: Calculations
Slug: first-time-buyer-sdlt-relief-how-much-you-actually-save

---

First-time buyers get a significant stamp duty discount. After April 2025, you pay 0% on the first £300,000 and 5% on the portion between £300,001 and £500,000. If the property costs more than £500,000, the relief does not apply and you pay standard rates.

On a £300,000 property, a first-time buyer pays zero stamp duty. A non-first-time buyer pays £5,000. That is a £5,000 saving for FTBs. On a £400,000 property, the FTB pays 5% on £100,000, which is £5,000. A non-FTB pays £10,625.

The relief is designed to help people get onto the property ladder. For a young couple buying their first home in Portsmouth, where average prices are around £280,000 to £320,000, the stamp duty saving could be significant.

But the cliff edge at £500,000 matters. If you are buying a property for £501,000, you lose the relief entirely and pay standard rates on the full amount. That leap from £500,000 to £501,000 could cost you an extra £15,000 or more in stamp duty.

If you are a first-time buyer looking at properties near the threshold, consider whether you can negotiate the price down to £500,000 or below. The stamp duty saving might make the difference between being able to afford the property and not.

**The formula**

```
If price > £500,000: use standard residential rates
If price ≤ £300,000: SDLT = £0
If £300,001 ≤ price ≤ £500,000: SDLT = (price - 300,000) × 5%
```

**A worked example**

£400,000 FTB: (400,000 - 300,000) × 5% = **£5,000**

**Why this matters**

First-time buyers save thousands in stamp duty. But the relief cuts off at £500,000. If you are buying near that threshold, negotiate hard. Crossing it costs you far more than the extra pound in purchase price.
