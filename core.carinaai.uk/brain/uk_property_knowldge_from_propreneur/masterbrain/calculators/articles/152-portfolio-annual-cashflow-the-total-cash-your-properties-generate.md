# Portfolio Annual Cashflow: The Total Cash Your Properties Generate

Published: Wed, 27 Oct 2027 09:00:00 +0000
Category: Calculations
Slug: portfolio-annual-cashflow-the-total-cash-your-properties-generate

---

Portfolio annual cashflow is the sum of your before-tax cash flow across all properties. It is what you actually have in your bank account at the end of the year after all costs and mortgage payments.\n\nIf you have four properties with individual BTCFs of £1,500, -£500, £2,200, and £800, your portfolio annual cashflow is £4,000. One property is draining cash, but the portfolio as a whole is positive.\n\nThe portfolio cashflow view helps you see the big picture. A property that is negative individually might be worth keeping if it has strong capital growth or tax advantages. But if the portfolio as a whole is negative, you have a structural problem.\n
Aim for portfolio cashflow that covers your living expenses by at least 1.5 times. That gives you a buffer for voids, repairs, and interest rate rises. If your portfolio cashflow is below your target, consider selling or refinancing underperformers.

**The formula**

```
Portfolio Annual Cashflow = Σ(Individual Property Annual Cashflows)
```

**Why this matters**

Portfolio cashflow should be positive and growing. If the portfolio as a whole is negative, you have a structural problem. Aim for cashflow that covers living expenses with a 1.5x buffer.
