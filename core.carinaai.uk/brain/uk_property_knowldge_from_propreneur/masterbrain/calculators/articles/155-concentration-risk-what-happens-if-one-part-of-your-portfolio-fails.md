# Concentration Risk: What Happens If One Part of Your Portfolio Fails

Published: Mon, 08 Nov 2027 09:00:00 +0000
Category: Calculations
Slug: concentration-risk-what-happens-if-one-part-of-your-portfolio-fails

---

Concentration risk measures how dependent your portfolio is on a single property, sector, or location. If one tenant in one property accounts for 40% of your income and that tenant leaves, you lose 40% of your portfolio income overnight.\n\nConcentration risk is calculated as the percentage of your total portfolio income coming from any single source. If a single property generates 30% of your total NOI, your concentration risk for that property is 30%.\n\nA portfolio with concentration risk below 10% per property is well diversified. Between 10% and 20% is manageable. Above 20%, a single property failure could destabilise your entire portfolio.\n
Diversification is the solution. Buy properties in different locations, different sectors, and different price ranges. A portfolio of ten £100,000 properties in different towns is safer than a single £1 million property.

**The formula**

```
Concentration % = (Single Asset Value or Single Area Value / Total Portfolio Value) × 100
```

**Why this matters**

Concentration risk above 20% per property is dangerous. A single property failure could destabilise your portfolio. Diversify across properties, locations, and sectors.
