# WAULB: The Break Clause Version of WAULT

Published: Wed, 30 Jun 2027 09:00:00 +0000
Category: Calculations
Slug: waulb-the-break-clause-version-of-wault

---

Many commercial leases include break clauses that allow the tenant or landlord to exit early. WAULB accounts for these break options. It measures the weighted average time until the earliest break date in each lease.\n\nIf a tenant has a ten-year lease with a break at year five, WAULT would show ten years but WAULB would show five years. The difference between WAULT and WAULB is the risk that the tenant could exit at the break, reducing the actual lease term.\n\nThe WAULB is often more important than WAULT for risk assessment. A property with a ten-year WAULT and a two-year WAULB is riskier than a property with a five-year WAULT and no break options. The break clause could be exercised at any time, making the nominal lease length misleading.\n\nLenders increasingly look at WAULB rather than WAULT when assessing commercial loan applications. If the WAULB is less than three years, the loan is considered higher risk and may be priced differently.\n
If you are buying a commercial property with break clauses, calculate both WAULT and WAULB. The gap between them is a measure of income uncertainty.

**The formula**

```
WAULB (years) = Σ(Years to Break × Annual Rent) / Σ(Annual Rent)
```

**Why this matters**

WAULB is often more important than WAULT. If there is a significant gap between WAULT and WAULB, the leases look safer than they are. Breaks can be exercised sooner than expiry.
