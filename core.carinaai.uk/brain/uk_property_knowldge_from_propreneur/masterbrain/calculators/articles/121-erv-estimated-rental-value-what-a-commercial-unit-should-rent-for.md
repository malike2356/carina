# ERV: Estimated Rental Value -- What a Commercial Unit Should Rent For

Published: Mon, 12 Jul 2027 09:00:00 +0000
Category: Calculations
Slug: erv-estimated-rental-value-what-a-commercial-unit-should-rent-for

---

ERV is the rent a valuer believes a property would achieve if it were let on the open market at the valuation date. It is not what the current tenant pays. It is what a new tenant would pay.\n\nERV is the foundation of commercial valuation. The investment method uses ERV to calculate reversionary value. Lenders use ERV to assess loan security. Investors use ERV to identify value-add opportunities.\n\nERV is determined by comparable lettings of similar properties in the same area. A valuer will look at recent lease transactions for comparable space and adjust for differences in location, condition, and lease terms.\n\nThe gap between passing rent and ERV is the reversionary potential. If passing rent is £20 per square foot and ERV is £25 per square foot, there is 25% upside. If the gap moves the other way, with passing rent above ERV, the property is over-rented and faces potential rental decline.\n
ERV is an opinion, not a fact. Different valuers can arrive at different ERVs for the same property. Use multiple sources and be conservative.

**The formula**

```
ERV = Market Rent Rate (£/sqft) × Lettable Area (sqft)
```

**Why this matters**

ERV is the market rent a property could achieve. The gap between current passing rent and ERV determines whether there is reversionary upside or downside risk. Be conservative with ERV estimates.
