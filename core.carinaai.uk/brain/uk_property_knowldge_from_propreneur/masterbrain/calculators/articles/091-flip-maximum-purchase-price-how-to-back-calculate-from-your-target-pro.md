# Flip Maximum Purchase Price: How to Back-Calculate from Your Target Profit

Published: Mon, 29 Mar 2027 09:00:00 +0000
Category: Calculations

The most disciplined flippers do not start with a property. They start with a target profit and work backwards to find the maximum purchase price they can pay.\n\nIf you want a net profit of £25,000 on a house that would sell for £220,000 after refurb, your calculation works backwards from the sale price. Sale price £220,000 minus target profit £25,000 minus selling costs £5,280 minus refurb £30,000 minus holding costs £5,500 minus contingency £3,000 minus stamp duty £2,500. That leaves a maximum purchase price of £148,720.\n\nIf you cannot buy the property for £148,720 or less, the deal does not meet your profit target. You walk away. This discipline prevents you from overpaying in a competitive market.\n\nThe maximum purchase price is your buying discipline. If the seller wants £160,000 and your maximum is £148,720, the numbers do not work. You cannot negotiate your way to a good deal from a bad starting price.\n\nExperienced flippers have a minimum profit per project (£20,000 to £30,000) and a minimum annualised return target (20% to 30%). They calculate the maximum purchase price for every property they look at. If the asking price is above their maximum, they do not make an offer.

**The formula**

```
Max Purchase = Sale Price - Selling Costs - Target Profit - Refurb - Legal - Survey - Finance - SDLT
```

**Why this matters**

Maximum purchase price is your flip discipline. Calculate it from your target profit before you look at any property. If the asking price is above your maximum, walk away.
