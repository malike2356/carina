## 1. Capital Allocation - Launching with 15,000 GBP

### The Setup Budget

A property business can be launched for **100-500 GBP**. Here is exactly where that money goes:

| Item | Cost | Notes |
|------|------|-------|
| Company registration (UK) | 95 GBP | Companies House, standard online incorporation |
| Business bank account | Free | Monzo, Starling, Tide, Mettle - all free to open |
| Simple website | Free - 200 GBP | WordPress, Carrd, or a single-page lead capture |
| Basic CRM / spreadsheet | Free | Google Sheets, Notion, or free tier of Pipedrive/HubSpot |
| Legal templates (AST, etc.) | Free - 50 GBP | OpenProperty or basic template packs |
| Domain name | 10 GBP | .co.uk or .com |
| Professional email | Free | Google Workspace trial or Zoho free tier |
| **Total setup** | **100 - 500 GBP** | |

### The Mistake

**Spending 8,000 GBP on brand, compliance, and marketing before the first deal.** This is the fastest way to fail.

### Case Study: 14,000 GBP Burned Before a Single Deal

A bootcamp participant spent:
- 3,000 GBP on a professional branding package (logo, stationery, website)
- 4,000 GBP on legal compliance setup (full solicitor-drafted documents, compliance software)
- 5,000 GBP on marketing (Google Ads, flyers, paid listings)
- 2,000 GBP on miscellaneous (phone system, CRM subscription, co-working desk)

**Total: 14,000 GBP. Zero deals. Zero revenue.**

The business was effectively dead before it started because the capital that should have funded deal-acquisition activity was spent on things that don't generate revenue.

### The Correct Order

1. Register the company (95 GBP)
2. Open the bank account (free)
3. Set up a basic spreadsheet CRM (free)
4. **START DEAL FINDING** - calls, viewings, offers
5. Upgrade tools only when revenue justifies it

### Checklist: Minimum Viable Setup

- [ ] Company registered at Companies House
- [ ] Business bank account opened
- [ ] Simple spreadsheet for tracking deals and contacts
- [ ] Free phone number (Google Voice or similar)
- [ ] Email signature with company name
- [ ] Ready to make calls today

## 2. The Sell-or-Keep Decision

### Stage 1: Sell Deals (First 6-12 Months)

In the early phase of the business, **sell every deal as a sourcing fee** rather than keeping it as a rent-to-rent or lease option.

**Typical sourcing fee: 3,000 GBP**

Benefits of selling deals early:
- **Zero upfront cost** - no deposit, no refurb, no furnishing
- **Zero operational risk** - no tenants, no voids, no maintenance
- **Immediate capital** - paid within days of completion
- **Proof of concept** - validates that you can find deals
- **Cash flow** - funds the next round of marketing and deal finding

### Stage 2: Keep Deals (After Systems Are in Place)

Once the deal-finding engine is proven and operational systems exist:
- Reliable contractor network established
- Property management processes documented
- Tenant sourcing pipeline working
- Cash reserve built (3+ months operating expenses)

Only then should a deal be kept as a rent-to-rent (or later, a lease option/refurb).

### The Two-Stage Framework

| | Stage 1: Sell | Stage 2: Keep |
|---|---|---|
| **Focus** | Prove deal-finding ability | Build portfolio |
| **Risk** | Minimal | Operational |
| **Capital required** | Almost none | Moderate |
| **Cash flow** | Immediate (3K per deal) | Delayed (monthly rent) |
| **Systems needed** | None | Documented processes |
| **Duration** | First 6-12 months | Ongoing |

**Rule:** Stage 1 proves you can find deals. Stage 2 builds the portfolio. Do not skip to Stage 2 until Stage 1 is proven.

## 3. The Volume Question

### The One Question You Must Ask Every Landlord

**"Do you have other properties?"**

This single question multiplies every transaction. A landlord with a block of 4 apartments = 4 potential transactions.

### Maths of Pipeline Thinking

- 1 landlord with 4 apartments = 4 sourcing fees at 3,000 GBP each = **12,000 GBP potential**
- 1 landlord with 8 apartments = 8 sourcing fees = **24,000 GBP potential**
- 1 repeat landlord sending referrals = unlimited pipeline

### The Mindset Shift

Stop thinking about individual deals. Think about **landlord relationships** and **portfolio unlocking**.

When you find a landlord who needs help with one property:
- Find out what else they own
- Ask who else they know in the same position
- Ask if their portfolio properties need the same solution
- Follow up every 90 days - circumstances change

### Pipeline Tracking

Track in your CRM / spreadsheet:
- Landlord name
- Number of properties owned
- Number of properties discussed
- Number of deals closed
- Potential pipeline value (remaining properties x 3,000 GBP)
- Follow-up dates

## 4. Business Failure Statistics

### The Hard Numbers

**Year 1: 25% fail** (75% survive)

**Year 2: 48% of survivors fail** (approximately 39% of original cohort fail in year 2)

**Year 3: 49% of remaining fail** (approximately 20% of original cohort remain)

**Year 5+: Less than 5% of original businesses remain**

### Primary Cause

**Cash flow is the number one killer of property businesses.** Not lack of deals. Not lack of knowledge. Not bad market conditions. **Cash flow.**

### Why Cash Flow Kills

- Expenses come every month (software, phone, travel, marketing)
- Revenue is lumpy (not every month produces a deal)
- The gap between outlay and income widens
- Without forecasting, the business runs out of money before the next deal closes

### Survival Checklist

- [ ] Monthly cash flow forecast maintained and reviewed
- [ ] Minimum 3 months personal runway saved before starting
- [ ] Expenses kept below 500 GBP/month in first year
- [ ] No significant tool/software investment before first deal
- [ ] Side income or part-time work maintained until deal-finding is consistent
- [ ] Every deal sold for cash in Stage 1 (not kept)
- [ ] Sourcing fees set at minimum 3,000 GBP

## 5. The Renegotiation Mindset (Ninja Approach)

### Core Principle

**Every supplier/contract is negotiable.** There is no such thing as a fixed price in business. The price is whatever you negotiate it to be.

### Case Study: The 118-Property Utility Renegotiation

A property assistant called every utility provider across a portfolio of 118 properties. Each renegotiation saved approximately 15 GBP per month per property.

**The maths:**
- 15 GBP/month x 118 properties = 1,770 GBP/month
- 1,770 GBP/month x 12 months = **21,240 GBP/year**

**Total time invested:** Approximately 2 weeks of phone calls.

**ROI:** Over 20,000 GBP saved for two weeks of work. That is a 10,000 GBP/week pay rate.

### Key Renegotiation Targets

| Area | Typical Saving | Strategy |
|------|---------------|----------|
| Utility bills | 10-20 GBP/month per property | Call and ask for retention/best rate |
| Insurance | 15-30% | Get 3 quotes, ask current provider to match |
| Council tax → Business rates | 70-80% | Convert to business rates and apply relief |
| Furniture/appliances | 10-20% | Buy used / ask for bulk discount |
| Maintenance contractors | 10-15% | Get 3 quotes, negotiate on scope |
| Property management fees | 2-5% of rent | Negotiate percentage, especially for multiple properties |
| Software subscriptions | 10-30% | Annual payment, ask for startup/educational discount |
| Phone/internet | 15-25% | Call retentions, mention competitor offer |

### Council Tax to Business Rates - The Detail

This is the single biggest cost saver for many property entrepreneurs.

**Before:** 900 GBP/month council tax per property
**After:** 180 GBP/month business rates (with small business rate relief)

**Savings: 720 GBP/month per property = 8,640 GBP/year per property**

**Process:**
1. Check if property qualifies for business rates (used for business purposes, e.g., HMO, serviced accommodation)
2. Apply to local council for business rates assessment
3. Apply for Small Business Rate Relief (properties with rateable value under 15,000 GBP)
4. Monitor and renew relief annually

### The Renegotiation Script

**Basic structure for any call:**

1. "I'm reviewing our supplier costs and would like to discuss our current rate."
2. "I've received a competitor quote for [amount]. Can you match or beat it?"
3. "We have multiple properties. Is there a portfolio/multi-property discount?"
4. "If you can do [target price], I'll sign/pay today/annual upfront."
5. "Thank you. Please confirm this in writing."

### Renegotiation Tracker

Track every negotiation in a spreadsheet:
- Supplier name
- Service/utility
- Previous cost
- New cost
- Monthly saving
- Annual saving
- Renewal date
- Next review date

## 6. Cash Flow Forecasting

### The Tracking Framework

**What you measure, you can change.**

Track everything in a single spreadsheet. Every week, update the numbers. Every month, review the trends.

### The Core Tracks

| Metric | Tracking Method | Review Frequency |
|--------|----------------|------------------|
| Calls made | Tally per day | Weekly |
| Viewings conducted | Calendar entries | Weekly |
| Offers made | Deal sheet | Weekly |
| Deals closed | Bank account / contract | Monthly |
| Revenue | Bank account | Monthly |
| Expenses | Bank account / receipts | Weekly |

### Zero Is a Number

**Put zeros in your spreadsheet.** Do not leave blanks. Zeros show you where you are falling short and force you to confront the gap between activity and targets.

**Example weekly tracking:**

| Week | Calls | Viewings | Offers | Deals Closed | Revenue |
|------|-------|----------|--------|-------------|---------|
| W1 | 50 | 5 | 2 | 0 | 0 |
| W2 | 45 | 3 | 1 | 1 | 3,000 |
| W3 | 60 | 8 | 4 | 0 | 0 |
| W4 | 55 | 6 | 3 | 2 | 6,000 |
| W5 | 0 | 0 | 0 | 0 | 0 |

Week 5 shows zero activity. That is an alarm bell. The zero forces the question: "What happened? And how do we fix it?"

### Cash Flow Forecast Template

A simple three-column rolling forecast:

| Month | Projected Revenue | Projected Expenses | Net Cash Flow | Running Balance |
|-------|-------------------|-------------------|---------------|-----------------|
| Month 1 | 0 | 500 | -500 | -500 |
| Month 2 | 0 | 500 | -500 | -1,000 |
| Month 3 | 3,000 | 500 | 2,500 | 1,500 |
| Month 4 | 3,000 | 600 | 2,400 | 3,900 |
| Month 5 | 0 | 600 | -600 | 3,300 |
| Month 6 | 6,000 | 600 | 5,400 | 8,700 |

**Key rules:**
- Be conservative on revenue - only count what is in contract or highly likely
- Be realistic on expenses - account for everything, not just the obvious items
- Update weekly
- If running balance goes negative for more than 2 months, cut costs immediately

### Dashboard Metrics

Keep these visible at all times:
- **Conversion rate:** Offers ÷ Viewings (target >50%)
- **Viewing-to-deal rate:** Deals closed ÷ Viewings (target >30%)
- **Cost per deal:** Total expenses ÷ Deals closed
- **Average fee:** Revenue ÷ Deals closed
- **Pipeline value:** All potential deals x average fee
- **Cash zero date:** When current cash runs out at current burn rate

## 7. Profit First Implementation

### The Formula Switch

**Traditional accounting:**
Sales - Expenses = Profit

**Profit First:**
Sales - Owner's Profit = Expenses

### Why It Matters

The traditional formula treats profit as whatever is left over. In practice, that means profit is often zero (or negative). Profit First reverses the equation so that profit is taken first, and expenses must fit within what remains.

### Implementation Checklist for Property Entrepreneurs

**Step 1: Set up separate bank accounts**

- **Income account** - all revenue lands here
- **Owner's Profit account** - profit allocation
- **Operating Expenses account** - business costs
- **Tax account** - VAT/corporation tax reserve
- **Growth account** - savings for scaling

**Step 2: Define allocation percentages**

For a Stage 1 (deal-selling) property business:

| Allocation | Percentage | Purpose |
|------------|-----------|---------|
| Owner's Profit | 10% | Your income |
| Tax | 15% | HMRC reserve |
| Operating Expenses | 50% | Running costs |
| Growth | 25% | Marketing, systems, scaling |

**Step 3: The rhythm**

1. Revenue lands in Income account
2. Immediately transfer allocations to each account
3. Spend only from the Operating Expenses account
4. If Operating Expenses runs out before next revenue, **cut costs** - do not raid profit, tax, or growth

**Step 4: Take profit monthly**

- Transfer from Owner's Profit account to personal account
- This is your salary from the business
- If there's nothing in the account, the business can't afford to pay you
- Forces discipline: either close more deals or cut expenses

### The Effect

- Forces creativity - when there is no budget, you find free/cheap ways
- Builds tax reserves - no surprise tax bills
- Builds growth capital - funds expansion without external investment
- Creates consistent owner income - even when revenue is lumpy

## 8. The Apprentice Lesson

### The Story

On BBC's *The Apprentice*, two teams were given a car washing task.

- **Team A:** 9,500 GBP on equipment (pressure washers, premium products, branded buckets, signage)
- **Team B:** 200 GBP on buckets, sponges, and basic detergent

**Both teams washed cars equally well.**

The customer did not care about the equipment. The customer cared about the result: a clean car.

### The Lesson for Property Entrepreneurs

You do not need premium tools before your first deal. You need:
- A phone
- A spreadsheet
- The willingness to make calls

### The "200 GBP Before 9,500 GBP" Rule

Before spending any significant amount on tools, ask:
- Is there a free version of this that works for 80% of the need?
- Will spending this money directly generate a deal?
- Can I do this manually until I have revenue?

### Common "9,500 GBP" Traps in Property

| Trap | Cheap Option | Premium Option | Saving |
|------|-------------|---------------|--------|
| CRM | Google Sheets | Pay monthly CRM (50 GBP/month) | 600 GBP/year |
| Website | Carrd / free domain landing page | Custom-built site (2,000 GBP) | 2,000 GBP |
| Legal documents | Template pack (50 GBP) | Solicitor-drafted (500 GBP) | 450 GBP |
| Phone system | Mobile phone | VOIP phone system (30 GBP/month) | 360 GBP/year |
| Marketing | Free directories, cold calling | Google Ads (1,000 GBP/month) | 12,000 GBP/year |
| Co-working | Local library / cafe | Premium co-working (300 GBP/month) | 3,600 GBP/year |
| Branding | Simple Canva logo | Full agency branding (3,000 GBP) | 3,000 GBP |

**Total potential saving: 22,010 GBP in the first year** by choosing the 200 GBP approach over the 9,500 GBP approach.

## Quick Reference: Cost-Cutting Checklist

Run through this checklist every 90 days:

- [ ] Council tax → business rates conversion checked for all properties
- [ ] Utility providers renegotiated or switched in last 6 months
- [ ] Insurance policy reviewed - 3+ quotes obtained
- [ ] All subscription services audited - unused ones cancelled
- [ ] Marketing spend reviewed - what is the cost per lead?
- [ ] Maintenance contractors - are prices still competitive?
- [ ] Property management fees - are they still market rate?
- [ ] Phone/internet - retentions call made this year
- [ ] Software licenses - students/startup discounts applied?
- [ ] All supplier contracts reviewed for renewal terms

## Monthly Cash Flow Routine

**Week 1:** Update income/revenue from previous month
**Week 2:** Pay allocations (Profit First)
**Week 3:** Review expenses - cut anything unnecessary
**Week 4:** Update cash flow forecast for next 3 months
**End of month:** Print dashboard, review goal progress, set next month targets

## The Two Numbers That Matter

1. **Monthly burn rate** - How much does the business cost to run per month?
2. **Days of cash remaining** - Current cash ÷ daily burn rate

If days of cash remaining drops below 90, cut costs immediately. If it drops below 30, the business is in critical condition.
