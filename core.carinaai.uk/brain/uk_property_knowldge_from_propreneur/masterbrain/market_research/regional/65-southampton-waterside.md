# Southampton and the Waterside: A Major City Investment Guide

Southampton is the largest city in Hampshire, with a population of over 250,000. It is a major port city, a regional economic centre, and home to two universities. For property investors, it offers a depth of stock and tenant demand that is hard to match elsewhere on the South Coast.

This article covers the Southampton property market and the waterside areas that surround it, including the western shore of Southampton Water.

## The Southampton market in numbers

Southampton's property market is distinct from Portsmouth. Prices are slightly higher on average, but the city offers a bigger rental pool, a more diverse economy, and a wider range of property types.

- Average house price: roughly GBP 290,000
- Average rent for a two-bedroom property: GBP 950 to GBP 1,200 per month
- Gross rental yields: 5% to 7% for standard buy-to-let, up to 10% for HMOs
- Student population: over 40,000 across the University of Southampton and Solent University
- Major employers: Southampton Port, the B&Q head office, Ordnance Survey, Carnival UK, and the NHS

The city's rental market is underpinned by three main tenant groups: students, young professionals, and families. Each group has distinct preferences and budget ranges, which creates opportunities across multiple property types.

## Student property

Southampton has one of the strongest student property markets in the South East. Over 40,000 students attend the city's two universities, and while both have increased their purpose-built accommodation stock, there is still significant demand for private rented housing.

The key student areas are:

- Portswood and Highfield: The traditional student heartlands near the University of Southampton main campus. High demand, reasonable prices (GBP 250,000 to GBP 350,000 for a terraced house), and HMO yields of 8% to 10%.
- Bitterne and St Denys: More affordable areas within walking distance of the city centre and university. Good for HMO conversion.
- City centre: Studio and one-bedroom apartments popular with postgraduate and international students. Higher capital values but consistent demand.

Student property in Southampton requires active management. Turnover is concentrated in June and September, and properties need to be maintained to a standard that attracts tenants competing with purpose-built accommodation. However, the returns can be excellent for operators who get it right.

## Professional lets

Young professionals form the second major tenant group. The city's economy has diversified significantly, with growing technology, creative, and financial services sectors alongside the traditional maritime and logistics industries.

Areas popular with young professionals include:

- Bedford Place and London Road: The city centre fringe, close to restaurants, bars, and shops. High demand for one and two-bedroom apartments.
- Ocean Village: Premium waterside apartments popular with higher-earning professionals. Higher capital values but strong tenant demand.
- Shirley and Freemantle: More affordable options for professionals who want to be close to the city centre without paying the premium.

Professional lets in Southampton tend to offer more stable cash flow than student properties, with longer tenancies and lower management intensity.

## The waterside areas

The western side of Southampton Water includes some of the most attractive residential areas in the region, and some of the most interesting investment opportunities.

### The Western Docks and Weston Shore

This area has seen significant regeneration. The former industrial land along the western shore is gradually being redeveloped with new homes, riverside walks, and improved amenities. Early investors in these regeneration zones have seen strong capital growth.

### Hythe and Marchwood

Across the water from Southampton, these villages offer a different lifestyle. Property prices are lower (averaging GBP 250,000 for a family home), and the community feel is stronger. The Hythe Ferry provides direct access to Southampton Town Quay, making the area viable for commuters.

### Netley and Hamble

Further south along the eastern shore, these waterside villages are premium locations. Prices are higher (GBP 350,000 to GBP 500,000) and stock is limited, but the lifestyle appeal drives strong demand from families and professionals.

## Family rental areas

Southampton has strong family rental demand in the suburbs:

- Bassett and Chilworth: Popular with families, good schools, larger properties. Yields are lower but tenant quality is excellent.
- Thornhill and Weston: More affordable suburbs with higher yields. Tenant demand is consistent but property condition needs careful management.
- Lordshill and Millbrook: Established suburbs with a mix of housing stock and good access to the M271.

## What to watch for

Southampton's property market has a few challenges. The city's student housing stock is significant, and some areas can feel dominated by the academic calendar. Parking can be an issue in student-heavy areas. The city also has pockets of deprivation that affect rental demand and capital growth in specific postcodes.
