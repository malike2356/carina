# Deal Analysis 101 - Fundamentals

## The Core Principle

You must be able to determine what a great property deal looks like. Estate agents are there to sell - they will use phrases like "Great Potential" and "Deceptively Spacious" to trigger emotional attachment. Property investing requires logical decisions based on facts and figures, never emotions.

---

## The 10 Factors to Analyse Every Deal

### 1. Location
- High demand area with good transport links and a strong local economy
- Major UK investment hotspots: Manchester, Birmingham, Leeds (northern cities offer stronger yields)
- Use the Area Identification Framework - see `algorithms/04-area-investment-scoring-metrics.md`
- Never invest in an area you haven't visited and researched in person

### 2. Property Condition
- The condition directly impacts refurbishment costs, which directly impact ROI
- Getting refurb costs wrong by even £5,000-£10,000 can destroy a deal
- Always use the refurbishment calculator - see `algorithms/02-refurbishment-cost-calculator.md`
- Arrange a thorough inspection before making an offer

### 3. Return on Investment (ROI)
```
ROI (%) = Annual Net Cashflow / Total Cash Invested × 100
```
- Minimum benchmark: 15% ROI for a worthwhile deal to present to investors
- 20%+ is a strong deal
- Factor in ALL costs: deposit, legals, SDLT, refurbishment, finders fee
- See `algorithms/01-property-roi-calculator-formulas.md` for strategy-specific calculations

### 4. Rental Demand
- Research local rental market before committing
- Use SpareRoom to check supply vs demand for rooms
- Use Rightmove/OpenRent for BTL/SA rental rates
- Use Propertydata.co.uk for rental demand analysis
- Look for areas with high occupancy rates and low vacancy rates

### 5. Capital Growth Potential
- Property values can appreciate over time - UK average doubles approximately every 10 years
- Research local market for planned development, infrastructure investment
- Capital growth is a bonus - never make cashflow decisions based on speculative growth

### 6. Market Trends
- Stay up to date with property values, rental rates, and the local economy
- Identify potential risks and opportunities from macro trends
- Understand current interest rate environment - affects mortgage costs

### 7. Financing Options
Available finance types:
- **Cash** - no mortgage costs, highest cashflow, limited by available capital
- **Buy-to-Let mortgage** - typical LTV 75%, interest rates 3.75%-6%
- **Bridging finance** - short-term 1-18 months, rates 0.4%-1.5%/month
- **Private finance** - from individuals/investors, flexible terms
- **Joint venture** - partner capital for share of deal profit/equity

### 8. Property Management
- Decide whether to self-manage or use an agent before analysing the deal
- Management companies: 8-15% of monthly rent for BTL, 12-15% for HMO
- Factor management costs into your ROI calculation from day one

### 9. Tax Implications
- **Stamp Duty Land Tax (SDLT):** Additional 3% surcharge for investment properties
- **Income tax:** Rental profits taxed as income (check your tax band)
- **Capital Gains Tax:** On disposal of investment property
- **Allowable deductions:** Mortgage interest (limited since Section 24), maintenance, management fees, insurance
- Always consult a property accountant before purchasing

### 10. Exit Strategy
**Define your exit before you enter:**
- Hold long-term for rental income and capital appreciation
- Refinance after adding value (BRR strategy) to recycle capital
- Sell after refurbishment (flip) to realise immediate profit

**Consequences of no exit strategy:**
- May be forced to sell at a loss if unexpected cash needs arise
- Miss opportunities to take profit or recycle capital at the right time
- Cannot effectively manage risk exposure

## Common Mistakes Beginner Investors Make

| Mistake | Consequence | How to Avoid |
|---|---|---|
| Insufficient research | Unexpected costs destroy returns | Research area, rentals, and demand before viewing |
| Overestimating returns | Cash negative deals | Use conservative rental figures, not best-case |
| No exit strategy | Trapped in poor investment | Define exit before purchase |
| Ignoring cost of ownership | Profit eaten by ongoing costs | Budget maintenance 5-10%, management 10-12% |
| Poor location choice | Low demand, high vacancy | Area research first - always |
| Undervaluing a good PM | Operational chaos | Budget for management from day one |
| Insufficient cash reserves | Unable to survive voids or repairs | Keep 3-6 months' costs in reserve |
| Emotional attachment | Overpay or miss red flags | Decisions from numbers only, never feelings |
| Inadequate inspection | Hidden costs discovered post-purchase | Thorough viewing checklist, professional survey |
| Impatience | Exit prematurely at a loss | Property investing is a long-term game |

## Spotting a Money Pit - How Sellers Hide Problems

Sellers use these tactics to conceal issues:

| Tactic | What It Hides |
|---|---|
| Fresh paint (walls, ceilings) | Cracks, dampness, water staining |
| Strategically placed furniture/rugs | Floor damage, wall cracks |
| Strong air fresheners / scented candles | Damp, mould, musty odours |
| Clever lighting (dim or bright) | Defects, uneven surfaces, poor condition |

### How to Counter These Tactics
1. **Look beyond the surface** - check under rugs, behind furniture, in corners
2. **Ask direct questions** - recent renovations? Known issues? Why selling?
3. **Bring a professional** - surveyor or experienced property person on every viewing
4. **Use your nose** - dampness and mould have a distinctive smell even when masked
5. **Arrive early** - inspect exterior before the agent arrives

## Deal Analysis Quick Reference Formulas

```
Gross Yield = (Annual Rent / Purchase Price) × 100

ROI = (Annual Net Cashflow / Total Cash In) × 100

Total Cash In = Deposit + SDLT + Legals + Refurb + Finders Fee

Monthly Cashflow = Monthly Rent - All Monthly Costs

Monthly Costs = Mortgage + Management + Maintenance + Utilities + Insurance + Council Tax
```

### Worked ROI Example
```
Purchase Price:     £200,000
Deposit (25%):      £50,000
Stamp Duty:         £8,000
Legals:             £1,800
Refurb:             £15,000
Total Cash In:      £74,800

Monthly Rent:       £1,500
Annual Rent:        £18,000
Annual Expenses:    £7,000
Net Income:         £11,000

ROI = £11,000 / £74,800 × 100 = 14.7%
```

## Minimum Deal Criteria for Presenting to Investors

| Criterion | Minimum Threshold |
|---|---|
| ROI | 15% |
| Monthly cashflow | £200 (BTL) / £500 (HMO) |
| Gross yield | 6% (BTL) / 8% (HMO) |
| Local rental demand | Confirmed by letting agent and SpareRoom |
| Comparable evidence | 3+ sold comps within 1 mile, same property type |
| Refurb quote | Obtained from at least 1 contractor |
| Legal due diligence | Planning checks, no restrictive covenants identified |
| Viewing completed | In person, with photos and video |
