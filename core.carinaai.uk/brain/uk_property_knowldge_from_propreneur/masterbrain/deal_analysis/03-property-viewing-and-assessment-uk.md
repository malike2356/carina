# Property viewing and assessment (UK field manual)

**Updated:** 2026-05-03  
**Kind:** External practice guide (Carina RAG).  
**Source notes:** Viewing guide (compiled). Cost figures are training examples, not quotations.

## Golden rule

Arrive at least **10 minutes** early to inspect the exterior before the agent attends.

## 1. External assessment

### Roof

- Replacement on a terraced house often cited **£10,000+**; repairs still costly once scaffolding and labour are included.
- Look for **straight lines** on planes and tile courses.
- **Bowing or dipping:** may mean major roof work or structural movement.
- **Chimney:** brick defects, pot damage, bird ingress.
- **Lead flashings:** missing or lifted strips precede leaks.
- **Tiles/slates:** slipped, cracked, uneven courses.

### Gutters

1. Present and complete after weather events.  
2. Damaged (holes, misaligned joints).  
3. Blocked (vegetation, stain lines on walls below).

Faulty rainwater goods drive **penetrating damp** and fabric loss.

### Brickwork and pointing

- Straight faces; stepped or widening cracks need professional diagnosis (costs can reach tens of thousands if structural).
- Repointing a whole elevation or house often cited around **£2,000** example order of magnitude.

### Gardens and invasive species

- Overgrown plots reduce value and deter tenants; budget clearance.
- **Japanese knotweed:** can damage hard surfaces and complicate lending until professional treatment and guarantees exist. Specialist bills often cited **£5,000+**. Do not self-treat and assume lender acceptance.

## 2. Internal assessment

### Windows

- Outside: frame condition, lintel presence and soundness.
- Inside: single glazing implies upgrade path; **blown double glazing** shows mist between panes; damp at reveals can mean seal or detailing failure.
- Frame types: timber (higher maintenance), PVC common, sash variants exist.

### Doors

**External (thermal and security rough ranking in training material):** timber (often weakest), PVC common, composite premium (do not budget composite if you priced PVC).

**Internal:** standard timber doors may be fine unless very old.

**Fire doors:** intumescent strips and closers; mandatory on HMO bedroom doors; wider BTL fire safety rules evolve (verify current Approved Document B and licensing).

### Boilers

- Replacement order of magnitude about **£2,000** in training examples.
- Brands often cited for parts availability: Viessmann, Alpha, Vaillant, Worcester Bosch, Ideal, Baxi (not exhaustive).
- Typical **service life** cited around **10 years**; older units can be inefficient and parts-scarce.
- **Hot tap test:** if hot water takes well over **20 seconds** to arrive at a nearby outlet, investigate (symptom not diagnosis).

### Consumer unit (fuse box)

- Old rewireable or obsolete plastic boards in many cases imply upgrade path toward metal consumer units with modern protection; **partial rewire** examples **£2,000+**; unit swap examples around **£600** (site-specific).

### Socket layout

- Skirting-mounted sockets or very low counts per room suggest obsolete electrics; **full rewire** examples around **£3,000**.
- Modern practice often cites minimum socket height about **450mm** from finished floor (confirm against current wiring rules and building control).

### Interior decor signals

Heavy dated patterns (for example 1980s to 1990s wallpaper) used as quick signal of long-deferred modernisation.

## 3. Damp types

### Rising damp

Ground moisture and salts drawn up masonry. Signs: tide marks, efflorescence, crumbling plaster, rotten skirtings; failed or bridged DPC suspected. Specialist specification and retrofit DPC systems are common responses.

### Penetrating damp

Localised wet patches from defects: gutters, downpipes, roof, failed pointing, cracked render, failed seals. Fix the source path first.

### Condensation

Vapour production exceeds ventilation and heating balance. Signs: droplets on cold surfaces, black mould on finishes, musty air. Improve extraction, heating balance, occupant habits; early mould clean with appropriate products.

## 4. Loft

- Inspect when safe: straight timbers, intact felt, no daylight streaks through cover, sound joists, adequate insulation without blocking required ventilation paths.

## 5. Cracks

- **Diagonal stair-step in brickwork:** investigate for structural movement.
- **Above openings:** possible lintel issues.
- **Vertical or horizontal:** may be benign shrinkage or may not; do not assume without advice.
