# Property Sourcing: Property Inspection Checklist

**Source:** TENX Property Sourcing Manual Module 6
**Purpose:** Complete physical property inspection guide -- what to check, how to diagnose, what it costs
**Audience:** Carina can guide a sourcer or investor through every aspect of viewing and assessing a property

---

## Walk-Through Protocol

Viewings should last 10-15 minutes maximum. Be methodical:
- Check every room: walls, ceiling, floors, windows, doors, plug sockets.
- Check: boiler, consumer unit/electric board, water pressure.
- Look in cellar, loft if accessible.
- Bring: laser/tape measure, damp reader, phone for video/photos, floorplan, viewing checklist.
- Ask lots of questions. Note everything down.
- If you can't access something, book a second viewing.

## Damp Diagnosis

### Rising Damp
**Cause:** Moisture from ground travels up walls by capillary action. Usually caused by missing or failed Damp Proof Course (DPC).

**Signs:** Tide marks on internal walls, salt blooms on internal surface, deteriorating plaster/paint, loose wallpaper. Mortar may crumble on external walls.

**Treatment:** Damp proofing injection cream. Injected into mortar course at intervals. Creates a chemical DPC barrier. Affordable and effective.

### Penetrating Damp
**Cause:** Water enters through external wall defects.

**Sources:** Overflowing gutters, leaking pipes, damaged pointing or flashing, poorly installed windows, missing roof tiles, porous or spalled bricks, cracked render.

**Signs:** Localised damp patches that appear after rain. Can worsen over time.

**Treatment:** Identify and remove moisture source. Let brickwork dry. Replaster. Repair timbers. Remove black mould.

### Condensation (Most Common)
**Cause:** Excess moisture in the air combined with poor ventilation and cold surfaces.

**Signs:** Moisture droplets on walls, mirrors, windows. Black mould growth on walls, behind furniture, in corners.

**Treatment:** Improve ventilation (extractor fans, dehumidifiers). Lifestyle changes: close bathroom doors when showering, open windows, use pan lids while cooking, dry clothes outdoors, keep furniture away from external walls.

**Prevention:** Treat with anti-fungal spray, dry area thoroughly, apply mould-resistant paint.

## Roof and Loft Inspection

**Check roof lines:** Look for STRAIGHT lines in roof tiles and along the pitch. Bowing or dipping can mean:
- Possible new roof needed (cost: £10,000+ for a small 2-bed terrace).
- Structural movement (needs further investigation).

**Check:** Tiles present and not broken. Chimney stack condition. Lead flashing present and in good condition.

**In loft (if accessible):** Check joists condition, roof felt (look for daylight = holes), straight lines in roof structure. If can't access on first viewing, book a second viewing to check.

## Interior Condition

Watch for **fresh paint hiding issues** (damp, structural cracks). Especially suspicious if only one room painted while the rest is original.

Standard expected condition: reasonable living condition for the price range.

**Typical refurb items:** Painting, plastering, lighting, carpets/flooring, furniture, interior doors.

## Exterior Condition

**Check:** Gardens (maintenance required), parking (permit? private?), outbuildings, trees near property (root damage risk). Scope for extension or further development.

**Japanese Knotweed:** SERIOUS issue. Eats through mortar and concrete. Makes property unmortgageable. Treatment costs £5,000+ from qualified professionals. If suspected, get a specialist survey. Lenders will refuse mortgages on properties with knotweed within 7 metres.

General rule: exterior condition reflects interior and structural condition.

## Structural Issues

| Sign | Likely Issue |
|---|---|
| Diagonal cracks (stair-step pattern) | Subsidence / foundation movement |
| Cracks above doors/windows | Failed lintel |
| Sticking windows or doors | Subsidence or movement |
| Cabinet doors opening on their own | House settling |
| Bulging or leaning walls | Structural issue |
| Sagging roof | Roof timbers moving, mis-sized framing, removed load-bearing wall |
| Uneven floors | Improper construction, settling foundation, cracked joists, dry rot, wet rot |

If unsure, get a structural engineer. Structural issues can make property unmortgageable.

## Flooring and Joists

- Buckling or bending floorboards = structural issue or joist damage.
- Joists hold up the roof. Damaged joists risk roof collapse.

**Dry Rot:** Brown rot decay from fungi. Spreads through wood and porous masonry. Treatment: remove and replace all affected wood, treat nearby timber with fungicide. Lenders won't lend where dry rot is present.

## Windows and Doors

**Window ranking (best to worst):** Triple glazed > double glazed > single glazed. uPVC frames most common and efficient.

**Door ranking:** Composite (most efficient) > uPVC (mid) > wooden (least efficient).

**Fire doors (critical for HMOs):**
- Minimum 30-minute burn time.
- Have a closer mechanism on top or built-in.
- Required in most/all interior doors of HMOs.
- BTLs: all bedrooms should have fire doors.
- Projected: by 2025, UK legislation may require fire doors throughout (not just bedrooms).
- Cost: ~£300 fitted (vs ~£150 for standard door, including frame and labour).
