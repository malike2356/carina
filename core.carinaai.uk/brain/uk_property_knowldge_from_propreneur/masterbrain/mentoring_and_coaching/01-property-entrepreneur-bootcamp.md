## 1. Business Systems & Infrastructure

### 1.1 The Four-Department Business Structure

Every property business must be organised into four core departments:

| Department | Function | Lead Role |
|------------|----------|-----------|
| **Leads/Lead Generation** | Generating interest, attracting investors, building database | Head of Marketing |
| **Sales** | Closing deals, prescriptive selling, collection | Closer/Salesperson |
| **Operations** | Deliverables, viewings, staging, project management | COO |
| **Systems & Processes** | CRM, automations, SOPs, finance/accounting | Systems Architect |

**Key principle:** In the beginning, the founder wears all four hats. The goal is to systematically delegate each hat as the business grows.

### 1.2 Systems Before Scale

- You cannot scale without systems, processes, and automations
- "If it solely relies on you, it's a job, not a business"
- Systems = CRM, finance/accounting software, project management tools, chat/email automations
- Standard Operating Procedures (SOPs) for every task

### 1.3 Building to Sell

- Design the business to operate without you from day one
- Build infrastructure that can be systemized, outsourced, and delegated
- A proper systemized property sourcing business can be sold for ~5x annual profit
- Example: Build to £100K/year profit → sell for ~£500K → invest proceeds into passive assets

### 1.4 Systems from the Bootcamp Provider (Capex)

- The bootcamp provider (Alistar) has built "Capex" - a complete marketing and CRM system
- Capex includes: landing pages, lead capture forms, CRM, email automations, SMS automations
- The provider claims: "We built the systems for you. You do not need to reinvent this. You need to just use the stuff we're giving you."

### 1.5 The "Don't Reinvent the Wheel" Principle

- Case study: Adam spent £15K on adverts to a poor landing page because he tried to reinvent the system instead of using what was provided
- "You either pay for your ignorance, or you pay for the systems"
- "Don't ever go cheap when it comes to marketing - if you pay cheap you pay twice (the redo cost + the lost revenue)"

## 2. Financial Management & Cash Flow

### 2.1 Business Failure Statistics

| Stage | Failure Rate | Survivors (from 100) |
|-------|-------------|---------------------|
| Year 1 | 25% fail | 75 |
| Year 2 | 48% of survivors fail | ~39 |
| Year 3 | ~49% of survivors fail | ~20 |
| Year 5+ | <5% of original remain | <5 |

**Primary cause of failure:** Cash flow problems.

### 2.2 The Money Jar System (T. Harv Eker - Millionaire Mind Intensive)

Six bank accounts/pots for every person:

| Account | Purpose | Suggested % |
|---------|---------|-------------|
| **Day-to-Day Expenses** | Living costs, bills | 80% |
| **Savings** | Emergency fund, rainy day | 4% |
| **Investment** | Growth assets, property deals | 4% |
| **Education** | Personal development, courses | 4% |
| **Fun** | Guilt-free spending, treats | 4% |
| **Charity/Giving** | Donations, helping others | 4% |

**Rules:**
- Every time money hits your account, deploy it immediately to these pots
- Do not steal from investment/education to cover day-to-day shortfalls
- Start with percentages that work for you (even 90/2/2/2/2/2)
- The habit matters more than the number
- Make these accounts invisible/separate bank - "out of sight, out of mind"
- Automate the distribution

**Real result:** Alistar's investment account hit 6 figures without him realising - automated saving over 3 years.

### 2.3 Profit First Method (Mike Michalowicz)

**Traditional accounting model:**
Sales − Expenses = Profit

**Profit First model:**
Sales − Owner's Profit = Expenses

**Key concepts:**
- The owner should not be the last to get paid
- "Smaller plates" - give yourself a budget (smaller plate) and force resourcefulness
- Every 3-4 months, take profit out of the business entirely for personal enjoyment (reward)
- Find accountants who understand this model

### 2.4 Practical Money Management Rules

- **Money thermometer:** Set a base line. If below it, treat yourself as broke - all efforts go to making money
- **Start with £10K minimum cash** before doing passive investments
- **Cut expenses ruthlessly:** Upload bank statements to AI, ask "where am I losing money? Where are leakages?"
- **Negotiate everything:** Mobile phone, Sky, utilities - use retention departments
- **Credit cards:** Cut them up don't cancel them. Keep utilisation under 50%. Pay in full every month. Avoid minimum payments
- **Credit file:** Check with Experian (£7.99/month). Fix errors (case study: Mercedes double-listed on credit file). Remove incorrect linked associates
- **Electoral roll:** Must be registered for mortgage applications

### 2.5 Cost Reduction Examples

- AI workforce replacing 2 staff at £1K/month each → AI costs £2.78/day (saving ~£1,700/month)
- Moving council tax to business rates (£300/property → £180 total for block)
- Negotiating utility contracts across portfolio (Joe's assistant Christina saved £15/month × 118 properties)
- Every 3-4 months, renegotiate supplier contracts

### 2.6 Cash Flow Forecasting

- "What you measure, you can change"
- Use spreadsheets to track daily/weekly numbers
- Zero is a number - put zeros in to see where you're falling short
- The spreadsheet doesn't lie - consistent zeros = consistent lack of results

### 3.2 Lead Magnets (Creation via AI)

**Types of lead magnets for a property sourcing business:**
1. Local intelligence reports (e.g., "Warrington Buying Hotspot Maps 2026")
2. Deal analysis tools (calculators, ROI sheets)
3. Case studies (deal breakdowns, investor readiness reports)
4. Free reports / ebooks
5. Deal alert subscriptions
6. Area guides and market reports
7. Viewing checklists
8. Joint venture partnership guides
9. Refurbishment cost guides
10. Tenant demand reports

**Process:** Use AI to generate 20 different lead magnets → run them at £10/day for 5 days → analyse which converts best → fund the winners.

**Cost per lead:** ~£12/lead at £10/day ad spend.

### 3.3 Know Like Trust (KLT) Framework

**Three stages:**
1. **Know** - They know you exist (lead magnet, ad, social media)
2. **Like** - They like what they see (value content, helpful, authentic)
3. **Trust** - They trust you to deliver (testimonials, case studies, results)

**52-week nurture campaign:**
- One email per week at the same time/day
- Content: property education, local market insights, inspiration
- Goal: Move them down the funnel to a sales call
- Can be fully automated with AI-generated content

### 3.4 The VBE Framework (Visible, Valuable, Vocal)

- **Visible** - Show up consistently (Zoom with camera on, proper lighting, 80% screen fill)
- **Valuable** - Provide genuine value (education, insights, tools)
- **Vocal** - Speak up, command the room, be authoritative

### 3.5 Social Media & Content Strategy

- Use AI to create scripts, avatars, videos (KG.AI or similar)
- Upload a selfie → AI creates avatar → AI writes script → AI generates video with any background
- "You don't need to be on camera yourself"
- Post consistently on LinkedIn, Instagram, Facebook groups

### 3.6 Networking

- Local networking events, property meetups
- Relationship building with local estate agents
- "Speed of implementation wins" - the faster you follow up, the more deals you close
- Go to events and collect contacts (Alistar got 27 leads from one night networking)

## 4. Sales Processes & Product Offerings

### 4.1 Multiple Product Lines for a Sourcing Business

| Product/Service | Price Point | Description |
|----------------|-------------|-------------|
| **VIP Investor Database** | £24.99/month | Priority access to deals, 5% off sourcing fee |
| **Sourcing Service (Retainer)** | £1,000 upfront | Find 4 deal opportunities over 3 months |
| **Sourcing Fee (Per Deal)** | £3,000-£5,000 | Per successful deal closed |
| **Discovery Day** | £3,000-£10,000 | Full day of viewings with investor, first refusal |
| **Staging Service** | Markup on cost (2x) | Furniture, decoration, turnkey setup |
| **Project Management** | 10% of builder quote | Overseeing refurbishment |
| **Property Management** | 10-15% of monthly rent | Ongoing management |
| **12-Month Coaching/Programme** | £12,000+ | Full mentoring and systems access |
| **Custom Partnership** | £25,000+ | Bespoke weekly calls, strategy sessions |

### 4.2 Prescriptive Selling

- Don't list products - diagnose needs
- "Ask them what they need and what they want, then give them the solution"
- Example: "I can find that for you" → retainer → then sourcing fee
- Create products on the spot if needed (Alistar created a £25K custom package in one meeting)

### 4.3 The Sales Funnel/Product Stack

- The lifetime value of a client going through the full funnel = ~£18K+
- Some companies have clients worth £150K through full funnel progression
- Low-ticket (£24.99) → Mid-ticket (£3K-£10K) → High-ticket (£12K+)
- Each level naturally filters the number of clients

### 4.4 Closing Principles

- "It's not been sold until you've seen the gold" - payment must be received
- Speed of follow-up is critical
- Personalised offers - create bespoke packages for specific client needs
- Money collection is the salesperson's responsibility

### 4.5 Discovery Days (Revenue Model)

- Organise a full day of property viewings for an investor
- They get first refusal on all deals found
- Charge £3K-£10K for the day
- Case study: Alistar charged £10K, took an investor from Bahrain around HMOs, sold 3 deals that day
- "I didn't even do, I didn't even find anything. Julie just gave us a list."

## 5. Operations & Standard Operating Procedures

### 5.1 What Operations Covers

- Deal progress tracking
- Viewings management
- Staging delivery
- Admin (website updates, ad tweaks)
- Staff management
- Stock/inventory management
- Ordering (books, marketing materials)
- Postage and logistics
- Making payments
- Travel arrangements

### 5.2 Creating SOPs (Standard Operating Procedures)

**Three methods:**

1. **AI-generated:** Voice-record yourself explaining a process → AI formats it into SOP
2. **Screen recording:** Use Loom or Glitch to record your screen while doing the task
3. **Written document:** Step-by-step instructions

**Organisation:** Store all SOPs in Google Drive with a clear folder structure.

**SOP example - Finding deals in an area:**
```
1. Open Rightmove
2. Select area (e.g., Warrington, 3-mile radius)
3. Filter: £70K-£200K, 3-bed, houses only
4. Look for properties in bad condition (no carpets, needs work)
5. Save to Rightmove folder
6. Copy Rightmove link to spreadsheet
```

### 5.3 Delegation Playbook

- For every task, record/document how you do it (SOP)
- Give SOP to VA/freelancer/new team member
- Slowly build out SOPs for every task in every department
- This process takes months - start with one

### 5.4 The Eisenhower Method for Task Management

|  | **Urgent** | **Not Urgent** |
|---|---|---|
| **Do** | Money-making activities (leads, sales, collections) | Strategy, planning, compliance |
| **Delegate** | Email, accounts, admin, cleaning | - |
| **Delete** | Negative people, TV, unnecessary subscriptions | - |

**Key actions:**
- Anything money-related = urgent
- Compliance is important but not urgent (plan for it)
- Delegate anything that costs <£20 to outsource vs. your hourly rate
- Delete activities and people that drain energy

## 7. Compliance & Legal

### 7.1 Company Setup Requirements

- Companies House registration (~£50)
- Business bank account
- Compliance documentation (can be partnered/co-sourced initially to share costs)
- Registered address and service address management

### 7.2 The CCJ Disaster - Real Case Study

**The problem:** Alistar had a CCJ for £187 (Companies House filing fee) because:
1. He changed his registered address online (confirmed changed in Companies House records)
2. Companies House continued sending notifications to the old address
3. He missed the penalty notices, solicitor letters, court summons
4. Default CCJ issued without his knowledge

**The cost:**
- £18,000 in legal fees to get it fast-tracked and removed
- £9,000 extra bridging interest (1% → 2% for 9 months)
- Blocked mortgage refinance during the period
- Total cost: £27,000

**Lessons:**
- Always pay a professional to handle Companies House filings
- Verify your accountant is doing their job
- Check your credit file regularly (Experian £7.99/month)
- Companies House admitted liability but still cost £18K to fix

### 7.3 Credit File Management

- Check credit report monthly
- Fix errors immediately
- Remove incorrect linked associates (ex-partners, old flatmates)
- Get on the electoral roll
- Keep credit utilisation under 50%
- Pay credit cards in full every month
- Avoid minimum payments

### 7.4 Business Compliance Requirements

- Anti-money laundering (AML) registration
- Property ombudsman membership
- Client money protection insurance
- Data protection/GDPR compliance for CRM databases
- Professional indemnity insurance
- Public liability insurance

### 7.5 Business Rates vs Council Tax

- Short-term let/Airbnb properties should be moved from council tax to business rates
- Significantly cheaper
- Requires application to Valuation Office Agency
- Takes effort but saves substantial money across a portfolio

## 8. Money Mindset & Psychology

### 8.1 Core Beliefs to Develop

- "Property makes money" - believe it at a soul level
- Money is a tool, not an end
- Money = opportunity, growth, investment
- "Money brings out the real version of you" - if you're a jerk when broke, you'll be a bigger jerk with money
- "Rich people don't wear designer brands - broke people trying to look rich do"

### 8.2 Overcoming Limiting Beliefs

**Common limiting beliefs identified:**
- "I'm not good enough"
- "There's no space for me in the market"
- "I'm too old / too young"
- "I've been doing X for 30 years, I can't change"
- "What if I fail?"
- "I don't trust myself with money"

**The arrow-breaking exercise:**
1. Write your biggest fear/limiting belief on an arrow
2. Place the arrow base against your neck
3. Say your name and "I'm no longer going to let [fear] control me"
4. Push forward with full commitment until it snaps
5. The snap doesn't happen at 50% - it requires 100% commitment

### 8.3 The Pain-Fuel-Growth Cycle

- Use pain as fuel, not as an anchor
- "The pain's big enough that you can create change"
- Take the emotional hat off → put business hat on
- "Drop the sob stories. Nobody cares." (In business meetings, focus on numbers and solutions)

### 8.4 The Money Association Exercise

- When given free money (£15K in the exercise), participants had different reactions:
  - "What do you want from me?" (distrust)
  - "I don't deserve it" (unworthiness)
  - "It won't go very far" (scarcity)
  - "How can I multiply it?" (abundance)
- Your relationship with money = your money ceiling
- Past bad experiences (lending, losing) create negative associations that repel money

### 8.5 Thoughts → Feelings → Actions → Results

- Positive thoughts → positive feelings → productive actions → good results
- "If you keep telling yourself you're tired, tired is what you'll be"
- Ask better questions: Not "I haven't got time" but "How can I make time?"

## 9. Property Business Models

### 9.1 Property Sourcing Business

**Core model:** Find deals → package them → sell to investors → collect sourcing fee

**Revenue streams (expanded):**
1. Sourcing fee per deal (£3K-£5K)
2. VIP investor database (£24.99/month passive)
3. Sourcing retainer (£1K upfront)
4. Discovery days (£3K-£10K)
5. Staging service (2x material cost)
6. Project management (10% of builder quote)
7. Property management (10-15% monthly)

**Key principle:** "Focus on one area, one strategy, one business model"

**Case study - Alistar's start:**
- Sourced HMOs in Hull only
- First month: sold 9 deals at £3K each = £27K in one month
- Went from £2.5K/month as a mechanic to £27K/month

### 9.2 Rent to Rent (R2R)

- Rent a property from a landlord → sublet for profit
- No capital required (operational model)
- Profit margin: typically £200-£500/month per deal (can go to £4K+ per deal)
- Can start with small capital pool
- **Case study - Joe (young entrepreneur):**
  - £12K upfront for 3 months' rent on a 4-bed house at £4K/month
  - Makes £4K/month profit on it
  - Has a 12-month contract
  - Got the deal in 2 days (speed of implementation)

### 9.3 HMOs (House in Multiple Occupation)

- Higher yield per property
- Requires licensing and compliance
- Best done in specific target areas
- Can be sourced for investors (sourcing fee) or self-operated

### 9.4 BRRR (Buy, Refurb, Refinance, Repeat)

- Buy discounted property → refurbish → refinance → release capital → repeat
- Requires good local knowledge of area
- Power team (builders, agents, brokers) needed locally
- UK-wide is difficult - stay in one area

### 9.5 Short-Term Lets / Airbnb

- Higher revenue but more management
- Move from council tax to business rates
- Example: Joe has 118 properties, £2.5M turnover in Airbnb
- Started 4 years ago with £8K-£10K

### 9.6 Joint Ventures (JV)

- Find someone with money but no time
- You find/manage the deal → they fund it → split profits 50/50
- Low-risk path for new entrepreneurs
- "Find somebody who's got money but not time"

## 10. Personal Development & Productivity

### 10.1 Diary Management

- Plan your diary from 7am to 7pm (at minimum)
- No white spaces - everything should be allocated including downtime
- "Start saying no to meetings" - if there's no clear purpose, don't take it
- Reduce meetings to 15 minutes by cutting small talk
- Every day: allocate 15-50 minutes for "deep strategic thinking" about your business

### 10.2 Daily Strategic Thinking Routine

**How to execute:**
1. Set aside 15-50 minutes daily (same time)
2. No distractions - phone off/away, no notifications
3. Use the Life Coach AI if available, or just journal
4. One action step must come from each session
5. Play ambient/binaural music if it helps focus

### 10.3 Income Generating Tasks (IGT)

- First thought every morning: "What am I going to do to make money in my business today?"
- Every task should be evaluated: Does this move the needle?
- "Stop sweating the small stuff"

### 10.4 Energy Management

- "Energy is emotion in motion"
- Your energy level affects your results
- Show up with 10/10 energy even when tired
- Business, the economy, and competitors don't care that you're tired

### 10.5 Delegation Mindset

- Anything not making you money is wasted energy
- Pay people to do what you're not good at or don't enjoy
- Example: Cleaner (£20 per visit) frees up 2 hours/week → worth it
- "If you can't afford a cleaner, get the cheapest cleaner" - outsourcing pays for itself
- Time is your most limited resource

### 10.6 The "Act Like You're Broke" Principle

- Even when successful, maintain the hunger
- Don't buy luxury brands to look rich - buy assets that pay you while you sleep
- Alistar's philosophy: "I don't have luxury watches. I don't drive Lamborghinis. I have assets."

## 11. CRM & Automation Systems

Auto-Email Day 1 → Auto-Email Day 7 → ...

## 20. Decision-Making Framework - Best Case / Worst Case / Likely Case

### 20.1 The Three-Question Framework

For every decision (business, investment, JV, partnership, purchase):

1. **Best case** - What's the absolute best that can happen?
2. **Worst case** - What's the absolute worst that can happen?
3. **Likely case** - What's the realistic, most probable outcome?

**Decision rule:** If you can live with the worst case and want the best case, execute.
"I'll do it for every deal, every joint venture, every partnership. That's how I make decisions quickly. That's how I stop analysis paralysis."

### 20.2 Application Examples

**Joining the bootcamp (investing £2K-£25K):**
- Best case: Build a successful business, make significant money, gain freedom
- Worst case: Lose the investment, no change in life, potentially lose some time
- Likely case: Grow, learn, make some money, build network

**The 40-foot jump:**
- Best case: Land safely, feel exhilarated
- Worst case: Serious injury or death
- Likely case: Land in water, be uncomfortable for a moment
- Result: Still jumped because all evidence showed nobody had ever died

### 20.3 The "One, Two, Three, Go" Execution Method

- After assessing the three cases, count down and execute immediately
- "Broke people do not make decisions. They need to consult with everybody about everything. Get 101 different opinions from people who don't know anything."
- Successful people make decisions quickly, even multi-million pound decisions
- Speed of decision = speed of implementation = speed of results

### 20.4 Overcoming Procrastination

- People leave things open-ended because they're scared to say no
- "Your job in business is to say: I understand you may be nervous or anxious. What do you need from me to make a decision?"
- If a client says "I want to think about it," they're usually too scared to say no
- Push for a clear decision - yes or no are both acceptable

### 20.5 The 40-Foot Jump Leadership Lesson

- Alistar took his group cliff jumping (10ft → 20ft → 40ft)
- At 40ft, he hesitated. The instructor called him out: "You've spent all day telling them to jump. Do you really want to be that person right now?"
- He jumped. Everyone else followed.
- "You can do this in every aspect of your life. If you're procrastinating on making decisions: one, two, three, go. I'm doing it."

## 21. VBE Framework - Visible, Valuable, Vocal

### 21.1 The Three Pillars

The core personal branding and authority framework. Property Entrepreneurship Training is built on "courage" - having the courage to be:

| Pillar | Meaning | Action |
|--------|---------|--------|
| **Visible** | Show up consistently online and offline | Social media, networking, website, speaking |
| **Valuable** | Provide genuine value to your audience | Education, insights, tools, solutions |
| **Vocal** | Speak up, command rooms, be authoritative | Elevator pitch, events, calls, meetings |

### 21.2 Building Visibility

**Online:**
- Social media presence (pick one platform and go all-in)
- Website with blog posts for SEO credibility
- Lead magnets (free reports, calculators, area guides)
- Get featured on local radio, podcasts, business shows

**Offline:**
- Attend networking events and speak
- Interview local business owners for content
- Write a book (AI-generated from your story) for instant authority
- Get reviews on Trustpilot and Google with real names

**Search presence:**
- "If somebody Googles me, what are they going to see?"
- Blog posts = Google ranking = credibility
- AI can write 20+ SEO-optimised blog posts in one session for location-based keywords
- Example: "I am a property sourcer in Warrington. Write 20 SEO blog posts for my Warrington property sourcing website."

### 21.3 Being Vocal - The Elevator Pitch

**Structure (value-first, not name-first):**
```
"I help [target audience] achieve [specific outcome] in [specific area]. 
My name is [name]. Come and see me in the break."
```

**Networking event rule:** Don't start with your name - start with the value you provide. By the third person saying their name, everyone has switched off.

**Example - Accountant at a property event:**
"How many of you hate dealing with numbers and accountants? (Nobody responds). Great. My name is Mike. I've been an accountant for a long time. I actually love numbers and I can help you understand yours very quickly. Come see me in the break."

**Example - Roofer at a property event:**
"One of the biggest mistakes people make is ignoring the most expensive part of a property - the roof. My name's Wade. I've put together a complete inspection pack. Come grab it from me."

**What NOT to do:**
- Don't lead with personal trauma or sob stories - "I've been physically and mentally abused for 20 years. I'm here to help you get over that." - This makes people switch off.
- Don't lead with victim narratives. Lead with what you can do for them.

### 21.4 Speaking at Events

- Start with 3-minute presentations, build up to 8 → 12 → 15 → 90 minutes → full day
- Stay away from PowerPoint slides - "death by PowerPoint"
- Only use slides for big images or one key point
- Don't put full text on slides - people read instead of listening
- Walk around the room to engage all sections of the audience
- Make eye contact, check in with the back row
- People remember those who made them laugh or connected authentically
- Energy matters more than content in how you're remembered

### 21.5 Value Creation

- "Your bank account is a direct correlation to the value you bring to other people's lives."
- Find rich people → understand their problems → provide solutions
- Wealthy clients care less about cost and more about results
- Case study: Marketing agency approached Alistar by auditing his ad library and funnel → showed him the leakages → justified their £6K/month fee by demonstrating how they'd cover it through improvements

## 23. Team Building, Leadership & Culture

### 23.1 The Leadership Framework

**Three pillars:**
1. **Trust** - Trust your team to do their job (no micromanaging)
2. **Train** - Teach them the right way, then train them on execution
3. **Clear Expectations** - Document what you expect and what they can expect from you

### 23.2 Command Authority Without Dictatorship

- "If you have to tell somebody you're their boss, you're not a boss. Authority is demonstrated, not talked about."
- Lead by example - show up on time, work hard, keep commitments
- People should know you're in charge by how you act and behave
- Demonstrate firmness when needed (e.g., addressing chronic lateness directly)

**Real example - Adam (team member, chronic lateness):**
After being nice about it several times:
"I'm going to tell you right now. If you miss one more meeting, don't even come. You have a 9am meeting every day Monday-Friday. If you miss one more, assume you no longer have a job." - He was fine after that.

### 23.3 The Shit Sandwich Feedback Method

1. **Good news** (opening) - Acknowledge what they're doing well
2. **Bad news** (middle) - Address the issue directly
3. **Good news** (closing) - Reaffirm their value, end on a positive note

**Example - Coaching a team member on call quality:**
"Hey, clients love you. You've had great feedback come in, you're doing a great job. By the way, I noticed in a few calls you were a bit short with a particular client. I think you need to be more aware of that. Is that fair? Look, I think you did an amazing job, your clients love you, keep doing what you're doing - we're just working on these small areas."

### 23.4 Accountability Culture

- The highest level of accountability sits with the leader - if you can't hold yourself accountable, you can't expect your team to
- Hold team members accountable to what they agreed to
- Be prepared to call people out and be called out
- "Don't be ashamed to call people out if they're not doing what they said they would."
- Not holding people accountable = no accountability in the business

### 23.5 Empowering vs Managing

- Empower team members to be forward thinkers
- Let them find ways to use AI to make their jobs easier
- Brainstorm together - involve them in strategy and planning
- "Somebody that feels empowered and part of the business will be around for a long time."
- "You work with me, not for me" - a simple word change that changes the relationship
- Goal: Team members believe in the business and the mission

### 23.6 Supporting Your Team

**Go above and beyond:**
- Pay for training and development
- Act as guarantor for rental applications
- Give them their first investment property with no deposit and rent-free period
- Take them on business trips (Las Vegas, Los Angeles)
- Invest in their skills and growth

**Trust principle:**
- If you don't trust them, they'll second-guess themselves
- If they make mistakes, don't punish them - use it as training
- "If you came to this event and I constantly berated you for not doing something, how would you leave? Discouraged."

### 23.7 Partnership Dynamics

- Start business relationships "as enemies" - with proper contracts, clear expectations, written agreements
- Then you can part company as friends if needed
- Different agendas cause breakdowns - get alignment early
- Be prepared to terminate relationships when they no longer serve both parties
- "People come into your life and leave your life at the right time."

**Real example - The marketer who promised too much:**
- Hired a marketer who claimed to do 8 things (social media, Facebook ads, video, photography, design, etc.)
- After 6 months, only doing 3 things (photography, videography, design) and quality was dropping
- Asked for a pay rise. Alistar showed him the original contract and said "You're doing 25% of what you promised."
- Marketer responded with "You don't value me" (gaslighting).
- Result: Let go. Was unemployed for 6 months.
- Tried to damage reputation with lies - Alistar's network knew better.

### 23.8 Hiring Principles

- Have a contract with clear expectations from day one
- Interview for character and alignment, not just skills
- Watch how they behave in informal settings (e.g., offering to split a taxi fare)
- "The people that get the best of me are the ones who show up, take action, offer value."
- Don't promise friendship - promise support, training, and clear working relationship
