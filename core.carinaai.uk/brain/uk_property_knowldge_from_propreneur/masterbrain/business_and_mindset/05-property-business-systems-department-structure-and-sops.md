> Source: 3-Day Property Entrepreneurship Bootcamp

## The Four-Department Business Structure

Every property business must be organised into four core departments. In the beginning, the founder wears all four hats. The goal is to systematically delegate each hat until the founder works **on** the business, not **in** it.

### 1. Lead Generation Department

**Purpose:** Fill the pipeline with opportunities (both deals and investors/partners).

**Key functions:**
- Marketing (advertising, social media campaigns)
- Ads management (Facebook, Google, Instagram, TikTok)
- Cold outreach (calling, texting, LinkedIn)
- Networking (events, property meetups, referrals)
- Landing page management and opt-in funnels
- Lead magnets and content marketing
- Partner/JV sourcing (estate agents, solicitors, surveyors)

**KPIs to track:**
- Number of leads generated per week/month
- Cost per lead
- Lead-to-discovery conversion rate

---

### 2. Sales Department

**Purpose:** Convert leads into clients, deals, and closed revenue.

**Key functions:**
- Discovery calls
- Closing calls (deals, investor commitments)
- Collection / fee negotiation
- Follow-up sequences
- Objection handling
- Contract signing and onboarding

**KPIs to track:**
- Number of discovery calls per week
- Close rate (% of leads that convert)
- Average deal value / fee collected
- Time from lead to close

---

### 3. Operations Department

**Purpose:** Deliver the product - viewings, project management, and client deliverables.

**Key functions:**
- Property viewings (arranging, conducting, reporting)
- Photography and floor plans
- Staging and refurbishment oversight
- Refurb project management
- Admin and documentation
- Deliverables management (reports, offers, surveys)
- Diary management and scheduling

**KPIs to track:**
- Viewings booked vs completed
- Average days from instruction to delivery
- Client satisfaction / feedback score
- Project delivery on time and within budget

---

### 4. Systems & Processes Department

**Purpose:** The engine room. Keeps everything running, compliant, and optimised.

**Key functions:**
- CRM management and data hygiene
- Automations (chat, email, workflows)
- Standard Operating Procedures (SOPs) documentation
- Finance and accounting
- Bookkeeping, invoicing, and tax compliance
- Reporting and dashboard creation
- Software/techstack management
- Compliance and regulatory checks

**KPIs to track:**
- SOP coverage (% of tasks documented)
- Automation rate (% of repetitive tasks automated)
- Data accuracy in CRM (duplicates, completeness)
- Invoice-to-collection time

## Founder Role Evolution

The founder moves through four distinct stages:

| Stage | Description | Key Action |
|-------|------------|------------|
| Stage 1 | Founder wears all 4 hats | Document everything you do |
| Stage 2 | Create SOPs and delegate tasks | Hire a VA or part-time assistant |
| Stage 3 | Department heads hired | Manage managers, not tasks |
| Stage 4 | Founder works ON the business | Strategy, growth, systems improvement |

**Transition triggers:**
- Stage 1 → Stage 2: When you are consistently busy for 8+ hours a day
- Stage 2 → Stage 3: When VAs need a manager and you're still the bottleneck
- Stage 3 → Stage 4: When department heads can run their teams without daily input

## Systems Before Scale

"If it solely relies on you, it's a job, not a business."

### Why systems matter
- **Scalability**: You can grow without burning out
- **Sellability**: A properly systemised property sourcing business can be sold for approximately **5x annual profit**
- **Reliability**: Consistent output regardless of who is doing the work
- **Time freedom**: Founder can step away without everything collapsing

### Must-have systems

| System | Tool Examples | Purpose |
|--------|--------------|---------|
| CRM | Pipedrive, HubSpot, Salesforce, or custom | Track leads, deals, and client communication |
| Finance & Accounting | Xero, QuickBooks, FreeAgent | Invoicing, expenses, tax, profit tracking |
| Project Management | Asana, Trello, ClickUp, Notion | Task assignment, deadlines, status tracking |
| Communication | Email, WhatsApp Business, Slack, SMS | Client contact, team updates, automations |
| Document Management | Google Drive, Dropbox | SOPs, contracts, templates, checklists |

### Build to sell from day one

Every system decision should pass this test: "If someone new walked in tomorrow, could they run this process based on what's documented?"

## Standard Operating Procedures (SOPs)

### Three creation methods

Choose the method that works best for the task and your personal preference.

#### Method A: AI-generated from voice recording

1. Record yourself explaining the task step-by-step (use phone voice memo or Loom)
2. Upload recording to an AI transcription tool (or use ChatGPT/Claude directly)
3. Paste the transcript with this prompt: _"Turn this into a clear step-by-step SOP with numbered steps, screenshots suggestions, and a checklist at the end."_
4. Review, edit, and format the output
5. Save to the SOP folder with a clear file name

**Best for:** Founders who think faster than they type. Quick documentation of existing workflows.

#### Method B: Screen recording (Loom)

1. Open Loom or any screen recorder
2. Start recording and narrate the task as you do it
3. Walk through every step - do not assume the viewer knows anything
4. Stop recording and save the video
5. Upload to Google Drive SOP folder or embed in a written SOP document

**Best for:** Visual/software-heavy tasks where seeing is easier than reading.

#### Method C: Written step-by-step document

1. Open Google Docs (or your document tool)
2. Title: `[Department] - [Task Name] - SOP`
3. Write each step as a numbered instruction
4. Include: who does it, when, with what tool, what the output looks like
5. Add screenshots where helpful
6. Save as PDF in the SOP folder for version control

**Best for:** Simple processes, legal/compliance tasks, and tasks where screenshots are sufficient.

### Example SOP: Finding Deals in a Target Area

```
SOP: Deal Sourcing - Rightmove Filtering
Department: Operations
Version: 1.0
Last Updated: [Date]

Steps:
1. Open Rightmove.co.uk in a browser
2. Enter the target postcode or area name in the search bar
3. Set search radius to 1 mile from centre
4. Filter by property type: Houses only (exclude flats/apartments)
5. Set minimum price: £50,000
6. Set maximum price: £120,000
7. Select "Sold Subject to Contract" and "Under Offer" statuses
8. Sort by "Newest Listed" (most recently reduced listings are priority)
9. Scan for properties that match these criteria:
   - Bad condition from photos (dated kitchen, old bathroom, no central heating)
   - Chain-free or probate indicated
   - No allocated parking (adds value)
   - Multiple images suggest empty property
10. Save any promising listing to the shared "Deals Pipeline" folder
11. Copy the listing URL into the CRM spreadsheet under "Potential Deals" tab
12. Add notes: asking price, condition score (1-5), vendor situation if visible
13. Repeat weekly - refresh search at least once per week

Checklist:
[ ] Rightmove search filters configured
[ ] Properties screened against criteria
[ ] Promising listings saved to folder
[ ] URLs and notes added to CRM spreadsheet
[ ] Search refreshed weekly
```

## The "Don't Reinvent the Wheel" Principle

**Case study - Adam's £15K mistake:**
Adam spent £15,000 on adverts directing traffic to a poor landing page because he tried to reinvent the system instead of using proven templates. The campaign failed. The money was wasted.

**The rule:**
> "You either pay for your ignorance or you pay for the systems."

- If a competitor or industry standard has a system that works, **use it**
- Don't waste time and money building from scratch when battle-tested templates exist
- Pay for systems, SOPs, and templates - they are cheaper than ignorance
- The most expensive thing in business is learning the hard way when someone else already figured it out

**Practical application:**
- Use proven deal-sourcing scripts (don't write your own from scratch)
- Use industry-standard CRM pipelines (customise later if needed)
- Buy done-for-you SOP templates and adapt them
- Follow established property investing frameworks

## Action Checklist for Implementation

### Week 1: Document
- [ ] List every recurring task you do (brain dump)
- [ ] Group tasks into the four departments
- [ ] Identify which tasks have zero documentation

### Week 2: SOP Creation
- [ ] Pick the 5 most frequent tasks
- [ ] Document each using one of the three SOP methods
- [ ] Save to Google Drive with correct folder structure

### Week 3: Systems Audit
- [ ] Audit your current CRM - is it clean and usable?
- [ ] Check what automations exist (or don't)
- [ ] Identify the biggest time-wasting manual task

### Week 4: First Delegation
- [ ] Hand off the most documented task to someone else
- [ ] Use the SOP as your training manual
- [ ] Shadow them for 2 cycles, then let go

### Ongoing
- [ ] Review SOPs quarterly - update when processes change
- [ ] Track which department you spend the most time in each week
- [ ] Move toward spending 80%+ of time in your zone of genius
- [ ] Target: Founder works ON the business (Stage 4) within 12-18 months
