# Building Your Property Team: Who You Need and When

Property investment is not a solo endeavour. The most successful investors build a team of professionals who bring expertise that the investor does not possess themselves. Knowing who you need, and when to engage them, is a skill that separates growing portfolios from stalled ones.

On the South Coast, where the property market is active but fragmented, a good team can be the difference between finding great deals and missing them.

## The core team

**Solicitor or conveyancer.** Every property transaction needs legal representation. A property-savvy solicitor who understands the local market and can move quickly is essential. For commercial purchases, this becomes even more important because the lease documentation and legal framework are more complex.

Do not use a high-street conveyancer who primarily handles residential sales for a first-time buyer. Use a solicitor who works regularly with property investors and understands lease structures, company purchases, and the Landlord and Tenant Act.

**Mortgage broker.** A good broker who understands property investment is worth their weight in gold. They will know which lenders accept limited company borrowers, which products work for HMOs, and how to structure finance for commercial purchases.

On the South Coast, local brokers often have relationships with building societies and smaller lenders that are more accommodating to portfolio landlords.

**Accountant.** Property tax is complex. Section 24, stamp duty land tax on additional properties, capital gains tax, and the decision to hold in a personal name or a limited company all have significant financial implications. A property-focused accountant will save you more in tax than they cost in fees.

**Letting agent.** A good letting agent handles viewings, tenant referencing, tenancy agreements, and deposit protection. They also deal with the headaches. The best agents know their local market intimately and can advise on achievable rent levels and tenant profiles.

Portsmouth and Southampton have strong letting agents who specialise in investment properties. The key is finding one who treats investors as clients, not just as a source of stock.

## The specialist team

As your portfolio grows, you will need additional specialists.

**Surveyor.** A chartered surveyor provides valuations for mortgage purposes and can assess building condition, particularly for older properties or those requiring refurbishment. A full building survey on a Victorian terrace in Southsea costs around GBP 600 to GBP 1,000 and is money well spent.

**Architect or planning consultant.** If you are converting a property, adding a storey, or changing use, an architect or planning consultant familiar with the local planning authority is essential. Portsmouth City Council and the Isle of Wight Council have different planning policies. A local consultant saves time and frustration.

**Building contractor.** A reliable builder who understands the pace and quality expectations of property investors is worth developing a long-term relationship with. The cheapest quote is rarely the best value. Look for a contractor who completes work on time and to budget, and who understands that delays cost you mortgage payments.

**Sourcing agent.** If you do not have the time or network to find off-market deals, a sourcing agent can find them for you. A good sourcing agent on the South Coast will have relationships with local agents, knowledge of motivated sellers, and the ability to negotiate below market value.

## When to build your team

Start building your team before you need them. The worst time to find a solicitor is when you are about to exchange contracts. The worst time to find a mortgage broker is when the auction is next week.

For a first-time investor, the minimum team is:

- A solicitor who understands investment property
- A mortgage broker who can place investment deals
- An accountant for your first tax return

As you complete your first deal, add a letting agent and a reliable builder. At five properties plus, bring in a tax specialist and consider a sourcing agent.

## How to find the right people

Referrals from other investors are the best source. Join local property investor networks in Portsmouth and Southampton. Ask at meetups which solicitors, brokers, and agents other investors use. A personal recommendation from someone with a similar portfolio is worth more than any online review.

Interview potential team members before engaging them. Ask how many property investor clients they have. Ask about their experience with your specific strategy. A solicitor who has never handled an HMO purchase may not be the right fit for your HMO portfolio.

## Final thought

Your team is your infrastructure. The quality of the people around you directly affects the quality of your deals, the speed of your transactions, and the resilience of your portfolio. Invest time in finding good people and treat them well.
