# Risk Management in Property Investment: Protecting Your Portfolio

Every property investment carries risk. The successful investor is not the one who avoids risk entirely, but the one who understands, measures, and manages it. The goal is not to eliminate risk, which is impossible, but to ensure that no single failure destroys your portfolio.

On the South Coast, where property prices are moderate but not immune to market shifts, risk management is a practical discipline that separates sustainable portfolios from fragile ones.

## Types of property investment risk

**Void risk.** The period between tenancies when your property generates no income. A rental void of two months on a GBP 1,200 per month property costs you GBP 2,400 in lost income, plus any letting fees for finding the next tenant.

Mitigation: Target locations with consistently high tenant demand. Portsmouth, with its university and hospital employment base, has shorter average void periods than many other markets. Maintain a reserve fund equivalent to three months of rental income across your portfolio.

**Interest rate risk.** If your mortgage is on a variable rate or approaching the end of a fixed term, rising rates increase your costs significantly.

Mitigation: Stress-test your deals at 1% and 2% above your current rate. If the deal does not work at the higher rate, you are exposed. Consider fixing rates for longer terms on properties where the margin is tight.

**Regulatory risk.** Changes to tax rules, licensing requirements, or energy efficiency standards can affect the profitability of your properties. Section 24, the increase in EPC requirements, and additional HMO licensing schemes have all changed the economics of certain strategies.

Mitigation: Stay informed about regulatory changes. Join a landlord association. Build flexibility into your strategy so you can adapt when rules change.

**Tenant risk.** Problem tenants cause damage, fail to pay rent, and create stress. The worst cases lead to possession proceedings that can take months.

Mitigation: Thorough referencing before accepting a tenant. Use a letting agent or referencing service that checks employment, previous landlord references, and credit history. Consider rent guarantee insurance for higher-risk profiles.

**Concentration risk.** Having all your properties in one area, one type of property, or one strategy exposes you to localised market downturns or regulatory changes that affect that specific sector.

Mitigation: Diversify across locations and property types. A Portsmouth landlord with three properties might hold two in different Portsmouth neighbourhoods and one on the Isle of Wight, with a mix of BTL and HMO.

## The risk management framework

A simple approach to risk management uses three questions for every deal:

1. What could go wrong with this investment?
2. How likely is that scenario?
3. What is my plan if it happens?

If you cannot answer the third question satisfactorily, the deal is riskier than you think.

For example, an HMO in Fratton might carry the risk of a licensing scheme change. The likelihood is moderate, given the expansion of additional HMO licensing across UK cities. The plan might be to have the property configured so it can be converted to a single let if licensing becomes uneconomical.

## Insurance that matters

Standard landlord insurance covers buildings, contents, and liability. Consider adding:

- Rent guarantee insurance for void and non-payment cover
- Legal expenses insurance for eviction and tenant dispute costs
- Commercial property insurance if you hold commercial assets
- Public liability cover appropriate to your property type

The cost is modest relative to the protection it provides. A typical landlord insurance policy for a GBP 280,000 Portsmouth terrace might cost GBP 300 to GBP 500 per year. Rent guarantee insurance adds roughly GBP 100 to GBP 200.

## When to take professional advice

Some risks require professional input. A solicitor should review all tenancy agreements and lease documents. An accountant should advise on tax structuring, particularly for higher-rate taxpayers and limited company owners. A mortgage broker should confirm financing terms before you exchange contracts.

Saving a few hundred pounds on professional fees is false economy if it leads to a costly mistake.

## Final thought

Risk management is not about being cautious to the point of inaction. It is about knowing what you are exposing yourself to and having a plan for when things do not go as expected. Every investor will face a void, a bad tenant, or a regulatory change at some point. The question is whether you are prepared for it.
