# Building a Property Investment Strategy That Works

Most new investors start with the property. They browse Rightmove, find something that looks interesting, and try to make the numbers work. This is backwards. The property should be the last thing you consider, not the first.

A proper investment strategy starts with understanding what you want to achieve, defining the criteria that will get you there, and only then looking for properties that match.

## Define your destination

Every investment strategy needs a target. Without one, you cannot evaluate whether a deal is good for you or not. The target should be specific and measurable.

Ask yourself:

- What income do I need this portfolio to generate in five years?
- What is my total capital available for investment now and in the next twelve months?
- How much time can I realistically dedicate to property each week?
- What is my risk tolerance? Can I sleep through a void period of three months?
- Do I want capital growth to build wealth, or income to replace my salary?

A Portsmouth-based investor targeting GBP 36,000 per year in passive income has a different strategy from someone wanting to build a GBP 5 million portfolio for inheritance purposes. The first might focus on high-yielding HMOs in secondary locations. The second might favour long-lease commercial property or strategic residential development.

## Choose your vehicle, then your location, then your property

The professional approach is to decide on an investment vehicle before looking at any properties. Your options include buy to let, houses in multiple occupation, rent to rent, service accommodation, commercial property, or a blend.

Each vehicle has different requirements for capital, time, and expertise. A buy to let in a suburban area of Southampton requires less management than a holiday let on the Isle of Wight. A commercial property in Portsmouth requires more capital but less day-to-day involvement.

Once you have chosen your vehicle, you select the location based on the vehicle's requirements. And only then do you look for a specific property.

This is the opposite of the amateur approach, which is to find a property first and then try to invent a strategy around it.

## Build in buffers

A good strategy accounts for things going wrong. Property investors who succeed over the long term are not those who never make mistakes. They are those who structure their investments so mistakes are survivable.

Build buffers into your numbers:

- Assume two months of vacancy per year, not one
- Budget 10% of rent for repairs and maintenance, not 5%
- Allow for interest rate rises of 1% to 2% above your initial rate
- Factor in letting agent fees of 10% to 12% even if you manage yourself at first

An investor who bought a Portsmouth terrace at GBP 280,000 with a 75% mortgage at 4% interest, assuming GBP 1,500 per month rent but with no buffer, might find that a two-month void and a boiler replacement wipe out a year's profit. The same deal with proper buffers remains comfortable.

## Review and adjust

A strategy is not a one-time decision. Markets change, personal circumstances change, and what made sense three years ago may no longer be optimal.

Schedule a review of your strategy every six months. Ask:

- Is my portfolio performing against the targets I set?
- Have interest rates or tax rules changed in ways that affect my strategy?
- Are my properties still in the right vehicle for my goals?
- Do I need to adjust my risk tolerance?

For example, the changes to Section 24 and buy to let tax relief caused many landlords to incorporate their portfolios or shift to commercial property. Those who reviewed their strategy early and adjusted were better positioned than those who continued regardless.

## Final thought

Building an investment strategy is not complicated, but it requires discipline. Define your destination, choose your vehicle, build in buffers, and review regularly. A good strategy does not guarantee you will never lose money, but it dramatically improves your odds of achieving what you set out to do.
