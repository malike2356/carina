# Property Team Building, Delegation & Time Management

## 1. Building a Team - Key Principles

- **Trust your team** - Let them make mistakes. That's how they learn and grow. Micromanaging stifles development and creates dependency.
- **Train them properly** - Invest time upfront in proper training. A well-trained team member is an asset; an untrained one is a liability.
- **Clear expectations in writing** - Every team member needs written clarity on their role. That means contracts, defined roles, and measurable KPIs. Verbal agreements breed confusion.
- **Command authority without dictatorship** - You're the leader, not a tyrant. Authority is earned through competence and respect, not demanded through title.
- **"If you have to tell somebody you're their boss -- you're not a boss."** - True leadership is recognised, not declared. If you need to pull rank, you've lost their respect already.
- **"Work with me, not for me."** - Build a culture of collaboration, not hierarchy. People perform better when they feel part of something, not like they're working for someone.

## 2. The Feedback Sandwich

A structured approach to giving feedback that keeps morale high while addressing areas for improvement:

- **Layer 1: Positive** - Start with genuine praise. "You're doing a great job." "I really appreciate how you handled that." Sets a constructive tone.
- **Layer 2: Constructive** - Address the area needing improvement. "On a few calls you were a bit short with clients. Let's work on that together." Specific, actionable, not personal.
- **Layer 3: Positive** - End on encouragement. "Keep doing what you're doing - it's just polishing the small stuff to get you from good to great."

**Why it works:** People are more receptive to criticism when it's sandwiched between affirmations. They leave the conversation feeling motivated rather than demoralised.

## 3. Delegation Playbook

- **Document everything you do** - For every task, write down exactly how you do it. That's your Standard Operating Procedure (SOP).
- **Give the SOP to the VA, freelancer, or new team member** - They follow the system, not your brain. This makes you replaceable (in a good way).
- **Build SOPs systematically** - Over months, create SOPs for every task in every department. Start with one. Then another. Consistency beats intensity.
- **Start with one SOP** - Don't try to document everything at once. Pick the task you hate doing most or the one that takes up most of your time. Document that first.

**The goal:** A business that runs without you. Every task documented. Every process repeatable. Every team member empowered.

## 4. The Eisenhower Method for Task Management

A four-quadrant grid for prioritising everything that crosses your desk:

| Quadrant | Action | What Goes Here |
|----------|--------|----------------|
| **Urgent + Do** | Do it yourself, now | Money-making activities: leads, sales, collections, deal-closing |
| **Not Urgent + Do** | Schedule it | Strategy, planning, compliance, system building, learning |
| **Urgent + Delegate** | Hand it off | Email management, accounts/bookkeeping, admin tasks |
| **Not Urgent + Delete** | Eliminate it | Negative people, TV, unnecessary subscriptions, time-wasting meetings |

**The golden rule:** Anything money-related is urgent. Full stop. If it makes you money or loses you money, it's in the top-left quadrant.

## 5. Income Generating Tasks (IGTs)

- **The first thought every morning:** "What am I going to do today to move the needle?"
- **Examples of IGTs:** Phone calls to landlords, property viewings, negotiating deals, converting leads, follow-ups with warm prospects.
- **Support activities are not IGTs:** Emails, organising files, meetings about meetings, social media posting. These keep the wheels on but don't move the car forward.
- **Rule of thumb:** If it doesn't directly generate income today, it's a support task. Do IGTs first, support tasks second (or delegate them).

## 6. No White Spaces in Your Diary

- **Plan every hour** - Including breaks and downtime. A fully planned day is a productive day.
- **Cut meetings ruthlessly** - Default meeting length: 15 minutes, not 1 hour. Most meetings can be done in a fraction of the time if you're prepared.
- **"Most of the noise is just noise."** - 80% of what fills your day is distraction. Learn to identify and cut it.
- **Batch similar tasks** - Group calls together, admin together, deep work together. Context switching is the enemy of productivity.

## 7. Sleep & Energy Management

- **Show up on time, regardless of the night before** - "I don't care what time you got in. You still show up on time." Discipline beats motivation.
- **Change your state** - When energy dips, shift your physiology. 10 press-ups. A brisk walk. Cold water on your face. Physical state change drives mental state change.
- **Use music strategically** - Music triggers positive memories and emotional states. Build playlists that shift you into focus mode, energy mode, or calm mode.
- **Micro-breaks for brain fog** - When you feel mentally fatigued, take 2-5 minutes. Step away. Reset. Don't push through fog -- you'll produce poor work slowly.

## 8. The Hourly Rate Rule

- **Know your hourly rate** - Calculate it. If you're doing deals worth £500/hour in your sleep, you need to know that number cold.
- **Delegate anything below your rate** - If you can pay someone £15/hour to handle admin while you do £500/hour deals, you're leaving money on the table by doing the admin yourself.
- **The leverage principle:** Your time is your most finite resource. Every hour spent on low-value work is an hour you can't spend on high-value work.
- **Outsource everything that makes economic sense** - VA for admin, bookkeeper for accounts, cleaner for housework. If the maths works, do it.

## 9. Partnership Dynamics

### Choosing a Business Partner

- **Complementary over similar** - Don't pick someone who thinks like you, does what you do, and brings what you bring. Pick someone whose weaknesses are your strengths and vice versa.
- **Skills audit:** What do you lack? Sales? Operations? Finance? Find a partner who fills those gaps, not mirrors your strengths.

### Written Agreement Essentials

- **Roles** - Who does what, day to day.
- **Responsibilities** - Who owns which outcomes.
- **Equity split** - Clear percentages, including vesting schedules.
- **Exit clause** - How do you part ways? Buy-sell agreement. Valuation method. Right of first refusal.

### Ongoing Partnership Health

- **Regular review meetings** - Weekly or monthly. Structured. Agenda-driven. Not just a chat.
- **The 5% rule:** 95% of arguments between partners are actually about money. Only 5% are genuinely about people. When conflict arises, look for the money issue first.

**Bottom line:** A good partnership is a force multiplier. A bad one is a slow-motion car crash. Get the fundamentals in writing before you start.
