# Stamp Duty for Property Investors in 2026

Stamp duty land tax (SDLT) is one of the largest upfront costs in any property purchase. For investors buying additional properties, the tax landscape is more complex and significantly more expensive than for owner-occupiers. This guide explains how stamp duty works for investors in 2026, including surcharges, corporate structures, and practical examples relevant to the Portsmouth and South Coast markets.

## The Basics: Residential SDLT Rates

For a standard residential purchase in England and Northern Ireland, SDLT is charged on a sliding scale. As of 2026, the rates for a single property purchase (not an additional home) are:

- Up to £250,000 - 0% (first-time buyers benefit from a higher threshold on purchases up to £625,000)
- £250,001 to £925,000 - 5%
- £925,001 to £1.5 million - 10%
- Over £1.5 million - 12%

## The Additional Property Surcharge

If you already own a residential property and buy an additional one - whether as a buy-to-let, a second home, or a holiday let - you must pay an extra 3% on each band. This additional rate applies to each slice of the purchase price, not just the amount above the threshold.

For practical purposes, this means an investor buying a property worth more than £250,000 pays effectively:

- Up to £250,000 - 3%
- £250,001 to £925,000 - 8%
- £925,001 to £1.5 million - 13%
- Over £1.5 million - 15%

## Real-World Examples for South Coast Investors

**Example 1: Buying a three-bedroom terrace in Portsmouth.**

A Victorian terrace in Fratton purchased for £195,000 would cost nothing in SDLT for an owner-occupier. For an investor with existing properties, the additional rate applies on the entire purchase price: 3% of £195,000 equals £5,850.

**Example 2: Buying a four-bedroom HMO in Southampton.**

A four-bedroom HMO bought for £280,000. An investor pays 3% on the first £250,000 and 8% on the remaining £30,000. That works out at £7,500 plus £2,400, totalling £9,900.

**Example 3: Buying an apartment in Southsea for £165,000.**

For a buy-to-let investor, the 3% surcharge on £165,000 amounts to £4,950. This sum must be factored into the investment appraisal from day one.

## When the Surcharge Does Not Apply

There are important exceptions. If you are replacing your main residence and have not yet sold it, you can claim a refund of the surcharge if you sell your previous main home within 36 months. You must pay the higher rate upfront and reclaim the difference later.

If you own fewer than three UK and overseas residential properties and are buying a main residence for a family member who cannot buy it themselves - for example, a disabled adult child - relief may be available. Professional advice is essential in these situations.

## Buying Through a Corporate Structure

A common question is whether buying through a company avoids the surcharge. Since 2016, companies and other non-natural persons purchasing residential property worth more than £500,000 face an even higher rate: 15% flat SDLT. Properties below £500,000 purchased by companies are subject to the standard rates plus the 3% surcharge.

However, there are exceptions. Companies that own rental properties as part of a qualifying property trading business, or that buy property for development or redevelopment, may pay the standard rates with the surcharge but without the 15% rate. Professional advice from a specialist property tax accountant is essential before purchasing through a corporate vehicle.

## SDLT Planning for Portfolio Investors

**Phasing purchases.** Because SDLT is charged on the entire property price, there is no advantage to splitting a purchase into smaller lots through a single transaction. However, if you are buying a portfolio of properties over time, staggering purchases can help manage cash flow.

**Mixed-use properties.** Properties with both residential and commercial elements - such as a shop with a flat above - are treated as non-residential for SDLT purposes. This means the surcharge may not apply. Mixed-use rates start at 0% up to £150,000, then 2% to £250,000, and 5% above that. For South Coast investors looking at properties with commercial elements, this can represent a significant saving.

**Refunds for replacing a main residence.** If you have owned investment properties and then sell your main home while buying a new one, ensure you claim the surcharge refund within 36 months.

## SDLT and the Isle of Wight

Properties on the Isle of Wight tend to have lower average prices than the mainland. A two-bedroom cottage in Ryde might sell for £160,000-£200,000. At these price points, the 3% surcharge amounts to £4,800-£6,000 - a manageable cost that should always be included in your acquisition budget.

## Stamp Duty as Part of Your Total Cost

SDLT should never be treated as an incidental expense. On a £250,000 buy-to-let, stamp duty of £7,500 represents 3% of the total investment before you start. Factoring this into your yield calculations from the outset ensures your investment appraisal is accurate.
