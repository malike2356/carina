# How Property Auctions Work: A Complete Guide for Investors

Property auctions offer one of the fastest routes to acquiring investment properties, often at prices below market value. For investors in Portsmouth, Hampshire and the South Coast, understanding the auction process can unlock opportunities that never appear on the open market. This guide explains how property auctions work, the different auction types, and what you need to prepare before raising your paddle.

## Types of Property Auctions

There are three main auction formats in the UK property market, each with distinct procedures and risks.

**Traditional auction (room auction).** This is the classic format where bidders gather in a physical venue - often a hotel conference room or auction house - and bid live against each other. The auctioneer manages the pace, and the highest bidder wins provided the bid meets or exceeds the reserve price. Once the hammer falls, both buyer and seller are legally committed. Exchange happens immediately, and completion typically follows 28 days later. This format creates urgency and finality, which can work in favour of decisive investors.

**Online auction.** Platforms such as Auction House, IAMSold and SDL Auctions host properties for timed bidding over several days or weeks. Bidders register online, place bids remotely, and can track activity in real time. Online auctions have grown significantly since 2020 and now account for a substantial share of UK auction sales. They offer convenience and a longer bidding window, but the same legal commitment applies once the reserve is met and the auction closes.

**Modern method of auction (conditional auction).** Introduced as a hybrid approach, this method sees the property sold subject to contract. The winning bidder pays a non-refundable reservation fee (typically 4-5% of the purchase price, capped at a fixed amount) and then has 28 days to exchange contracts, with a further 28 days to complete. This approach gives buyers slightly more breathing room for mortgage approval, though the reservation fee is lost if you pull out.

## Before You Bid

Preparation is everything in property auctions. The auction catalogue is published several weeks in advance, giving you time to research each lot. Order a legal pack for any property you are serious about. The legal pack contains the title register, special conditions of sale, searches and any planning documentation. Your solicitor should review this before you bid.

**Arrange financing.** If you plan to use a mortgage, speak to a broker experienced with auction purchases. Many lenders will not lend on auction properties, and those that do require a much tighter timeline. Have your deposit funds ready - typically 10 per cent of the purchase price, payable on the day of the auction if you are the winning bidder. For cash buyers, proof of funds may be required before you can register to bid.

**View the property.** Never bid on a property you have not viewed. Photographs in the catalogue can be misleading. Book a viewing and take a builder or surveyor with you if possible. Auction properties are often sold as seen, with no opportunity for further negotiation once the gavel falls.

## The Bidding Process

On auction day, whether in a room or online, set your maximum bid in advance and stick to it. It is easy to get caught up in the excitement of competitive bidding and overpay. Remember that your maximum bid should include the purchase price plus all associated costs - stamp duty, auction fees, legal fees and any refurbishment budget.

If you win, you will typically need to pay the deposit immediately and sign the memorandum of sale. In a traditional auction, exchange of contracts happens on the spot, making the sale legally binding. In a conditional auction, you have a period to exchange, but the property is taken off the market.

## Costs to Consider

Beyond the purchase price, auction buyers should budget for:

- **Buyer's premium** - an additional fee charged by the auctioneer, usually a percentage of the hammer price
- **Administration fee** - a fixed charge to cover auction processing
- **Reservation fee** (conditional auctions only) - typically 4-5% plus VAT
- **Legal fees** - conveyancing for the purchase
- **Search fees** - if not included in the legal pack
- **Stamp duty land tax** - based on the purchase price
- **Refurbishment costs** - most auction properties require work

## Portsmouth and South Coast Auction Market

Properties regularly come to auction across the South Coast. Portsmouth, Southampton, Bournemouth and the Isle of Wight all have active auction markets. On the Isle of Wight, auction properties often include period terraces needing modernisation in Ryde or Ventnor, and ex-local authority flats in need of refurbishment. In Portsmouth, you will frequently find Victorian and Edwardian terrace houses, many in need of updating, offered through firms such as Clive Emson and Fox & Sons auctions.

For example, a three-bedroom Victorian terrace in Fratton requiring full refurbishment might be guided at £90,000-£120,000 at auction, compared to £180,000-£220,000 for a similar property in good condition on the open market. The discount reflects the work required and the certainty of the auction process.

## Risks to Be Aware Of

Auction purchases carry specific risks. The legal pack may reveal restrictive covenants, rights of way, or planning issues that affect the property's value or intended use. You cannot make your offer conditional on survey results or mortgage approval in a traditional auction. If you fail to complete on time, you forfeit your deposit and may be liable for the seller's costs and any shortfall on resale.

For investors new to auctions, a conditional auction or starting with lower-value lots can reduce exposure while you learn the process.
