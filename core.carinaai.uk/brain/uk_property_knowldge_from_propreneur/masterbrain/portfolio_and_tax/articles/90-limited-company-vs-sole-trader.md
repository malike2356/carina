# Limited Company vs Sole Trader for Property Investment

One of the most consequential decisions a property investor makes is choosing the legal structure for their portfolio. The choice between holding properties in your own name (as a sole trader) or through a limited company affects your tax bill, borrowing options, inheritance planning, and administrative burden. This article compares the two approaches with practical examples relevant to South Coast investors.

## The Current Tax Landscape

Before comparing structures, it is important to understand the tax rules driving the decision.

Since 2017, mortgage interest relief for individual landlords has been restricted. If you hold properties in your own name, you cannot deduct mortgage interest from your rental income. Instead, you receive a 20% tax credit on your interest costs. This means higher-rate taxpayers (40% or 45%) effectively pay tax on their rental income without deducting their largest expense - the mortgage interest.

Landlords holding properties through a limited company can deduct mortgage interest as a business expense before corporation tax is applied. This is the single biggest factor driving the trend towards incorporation.

## Sole Trader (Individual Ownership)

**Advantages.** Setting up is straightforward - you simply buy properties in your own name and declare rental income on a self-assessment tax return. There are no company formation costs, no annual filing requirements to Companies House, and no corporation tax returns. Borrowing is easier because lenders have a long history of lending to individuals and offer more competitive rates for personal buy-to-let mortgages.

Capital gains tax on sale is charged at 18% or 24% (depending on your income tax band), which is lower than the corporation tax plus dividend tax that applies through a company structure.

**Disadvantages.** The restriction on mortgage interest relief is the main drawback. A higher-rate taxpayer with a 75% loan-to-value mortgage pays 40% income tax on rental income that may be largely consumed by interest costs, resulting in negative cash flow after tax despite positive cash flow before tax.

You are personally liable for all debts and legal claims against the property portfolio. Inheritance tax planning is more limited.

## Limited Company Ownership

**Advantages.** Full deduction of mortgage interest before tax. Corporation tax rates are currently 19-25% depending on profit levels - significantly lower than the 40-45% income tax rates for higher earners. This structure is particularly advantageous for investors building a portfolio with significant mortgage debt.

Profit extraction through dividends gives flexibility. You pay no National Insurance on dividends, and your dividend allowance covers the first £1,000 of dividend income (as of 2026-27).

**Disadvantages.** Setting up and maintaining a limited company involves costs: Companies House registration, annual accounts, corporation tax returns, and potentially higher accountancy fees. A simple property company with one property might cost £600-£1,200 per year in accounting fees.

Mortgage rates for limited companies are typically 0.5-1.5% higher than personal buy-to-let rates, reflecting lenders' perception of higher risk.

When you sell a property within the company, you pay corporation tax on the gain (19-25%) and then dividend tax when extracting the proceeds - effectively double taxation on capital gains.

## Practical Examples

**Example 1: Higher-rate taxpayer building a portfolio.**

Laud (fictional example) is a 40% taxpayer earning £75,000 from his day job. He buys a three-bedroom terrace in Fratton for £220,000 with a 75% interest-only mortgage at 5.5%. Monthly interest cost: £756. Monthly rent: £1,050.

As an individual: Net rental income (£1,050 × 12) is £12,600. Interest cost (not deductible) is £9,072 per year. Taxable profit is £12,600. Tax at 40% is £5,040. After tax and interest: £12,600 - £9,072 - £5,040 = -£1,512. Negative cash flow.

Through a limited company: Rental income of £12,600 minus interest of £9,072 gives profit of £3,528. Corporation tax at 20% = £706. Retained profit: £2,822. Positive cash flow.

The difference is £4,334 per year in favour of the company structure on a single property.

**Example 2: Basic-rate taxpayer with a small portfolio.**

Sarah is employed earning £25,000 and owns one buy-to-let in Southsea worth £185,000 with a small mortgage. Her rental income is £10,000 per year with £3,000 interest. As an individual, she receives the 20% tax credit and pays relatively little tax. The costs of running a company would outweigh the benefit. Individual ownership is the right choice here.

## Inheritance Tax and Succession Planning

Properties held individually form part of your estate for inheritance tax purposes. Through a company, shares can be transferred more easily, and business property relief may apply in certain circumstances. Professional advice is essential for estate planning.

## Practical Decision Framework

Use a company structure if:

- You are a higher or additional-rate taxpayer
- You plan to build a portfolio with substantial mortgage debt
- You reinvest rental profits into further acquisitions
- You have a long-term (10+ year) investment horizon

Use individual ownership if:

- You are a basic-rate taxpayer
- You own only one or two properties with low or no mortgage debt
- You plan to sell properties within a few years
- Administration simplicity matters more to you than tax optimisation

## The South Coast Context

For investors targeting Portsmouth, Southampton, and the Isle of Wight, where typical buy-to-let properties range from £170,000 to £300,000, the tax advantage of incorporation becomes significant once you own more than two or three mortgaged properties. Many successful South Coast investors operate through limited companies, holding three to ten properties under a single corporate structure.

Transferring existing properties into a company triggers stamp duty and capital gains tax. It is almost never worth doing. Instead, begin holding new acquisitions in a company from the outset.
