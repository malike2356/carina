# Rent Guarantee Insurance for Landlords

Every landlord worries about the tenant who stops paying rent. The arrears that start as a one-week delay, stretch to a month, then two months, and turn into a costly eviction process. Rent guarantee insurance exists to protect against exactly this scenario. But it is not always the right product for every landlord. This article explains how rent guarantee insurance works, what it covers, what it costs, and when it makes sense.

## What Is Rent Guarantee Insurance?

Rent guarantee insurance (sometimes called rent protection insurance) covers the loss of rental income when a tenant defaults on their rent. Most policies cover unpaid rent for a specified period - typically six to twelve months - and may also cover the cost of eviction proceedings.

It is important to distinguish rent guarantee insurance from tenant default insurance, which is a different product more commonly focused on landlord possessions or property damage.

## How It Works

You take out a policy at the start of a tenancy or at renewal. If the tenant stops paying rent, you notify the insurer. After a standard non-payment period - usually 30 days - the insurer begins paying the rent directly to you. The policy will either cover rent arrears from the date of default or from the trigger point, depending on the terms.

Cover continues until one of the following happens: the tenancy ends, the property is vacated, the cover limit is reached (typically six to twelve months' rent), or the policy term expires.

**What is typically covered:**

- Loss of rental income up to the policy limit (usually £2,500-£5,000 per month)
- Legal costs for eviction proceedings (up to a capped amount, often £50,000-£100,000)
- Some policies include property damage caused by the tenant

**What is typically excluded:**

- Deliberately caused damage (as opposed to accidental)
- Loss of rent during refurbishment or void periods between tenancies
- Rental income above the policy limit
- Tenants already in arrears when the policy was taken out
- Tenants who have previously defaulted on you

## Cost of Rent Guarantee Insurance

Premiums vary depending on the level of cover, the rent amount, the tenant profile, and the insurer. Typical costs are:

- **Basic rent guarantee (up to £2,500 per month rent):** £100-£250 per year
- **Comprehensive cover (rent guarantee plus legal expenses):** £150-£400 per year
- **Premium cover (higher rent limits, shorter excess periods):** £200-£500 per year

As a percentage of rent, you are looking at roughly 2-5% of the annual rent. For a £950 per month property, that is £114-£285 per year. When you compare this to the cost of even a single month of non-payment, the premium is modest.

Some landlords add rent guarantee insurance as a bolt-on to their existing landlord insurance policy, which can reduce costs compared to a standalone policy.

## When Does Rent Guarantee Insurance Make Sense?

**It makes sense when:**

- You have high mortgage payments and limited cash reserves. A tenant default that leaves you without rent for three months could result in mortgage arrears and repossession. Insurance protects against that tail risk.
- Your property is in a higher-risk tenant market. Properties near universities with a high proportion of student tenants, or areas with transient populations, carry higher default risk.
- You are a new landlord without the cash reserves to weather a prolonged void or eviction.
- You have scaled up and cannot afford the concentration risk of multiple tenancies. If your portfolio produces £5,000 per month in rent, one default is manageable. If it produces £15,000, one default represents a significant income gap.

**It may not make sense when:**

- Your portfolio is highly leveraged and every pound of income is committed. In this scenario, you cannot afford the premium either, and you should be building cash reserves instead.
- You have significant cash reserves. If you can comfortably cover six months of rent from savings, the insurance premium may be an unnecessary expense.
- You let to tenants with strong guarantors. A parent guarantee for a student tenant is often as effective as insurance, at no cost.
- You already have comprehensive tenant referencing that filters high-risk applicants.

## Practical Example: Portsmouth Buy-to-Let

Consider a two-bedroom terrace in Buckland, Portsmouth, purchased for £210,000. The mortgage is £825 per month on an interest-only basis at 5.5%. The rent is £950 per month. Your net cash flow before costs is £125 per month.

If the tenant defaults and you lose three months' rent without insurance, you lose £2,850 in income. If the eviction takes six months - not uncommon - you lose £5,700 plus legal costs of £1,000-£3,000. That is enough to wipe out a full year's profit.

A rent guarantee policy for this property costs approximately £150 per year. Over five years, the total cost is £750. If it prevents even one default, it has paid for itself many times over.

## Choosing an Insurer

Not all rent guarantee policies are equal. Key factors to consider:

- **Excess period:** How long must the tenant be in arrears before cover kicks in? 14 days is better than 30 or 60.
- **Cover limit:** Does the policy cap at £2,500 per month? Will that cover your rent?
- **Eviction cover:** Does the policy include legal expenses for eviction? This is often the most valuable element.
- **Tenant vetting requirements:** Some insurers require specific referencing processes. Make sure you can comply.
- **Claims process:** Read reviews of the claims experience. A policy that is difficult to claim on is worth less than the paper it is printed on.

## Alternatives to Insurance

If you decide against rent guarantee insurance, build a cash buffer. A reserve of three to six months' rent per property provides the same protection without the premium cost. Direct tenant rent guarantees (from employers or parents) can also substitute for insurance, particularly for student or young professional tenants.
