# New Build vs Period Property: Which Is Better for Investment?

One of the most persistent debates among UK property investors is whether to buy new builds or period properties. Each has distinct advantages and drawbacks, and the right choice depends on your strategy, budget, and target market. For investors operating on the South Coast, where Victorian and Edwardian housing stock sits alongside modern developments, understanding these differences is essential.

## What Defines a Period Property

In the context of UK property investment, period properties typically refer to Victorian (1837-1901) and Edwardian (1901-1914) homes. These properties dominate much of Portsmouth, Southampton, and the older parts of the Isle of Wight. They are characterised by high ceilings, bay windows, original fireplaces, and solid brick construction. Many have been subdivided into flats or used as HMOs for decades.

## New Builds: The Modern Option

New build properties are constructed by developers and sold either off-plan or upon completion. In Portsmouth, key new build areas include Gunwharf Quays, the Northern Quarter development, and several regeneration sites around the city. On the Isle of Wight, new build estates continue to appear on the outskirts of Newport and Ryde.

## Capital Appreciation

Period properties historically outperform new builds in capital appreciation. Because new builds carry a premium - the developer's margin - they often depreciate in the first few years after completion before recovering. This is known as the new build premium. A period property bought at a fair market price does not carry this premium, so any value you add through refurbishment is pure upside.

However, location matters. A Victorian terrace in a sought-after area of Southsea that is within walking distance of the seafront and universities will likely appreciate faster than a new build on the outskirts of the city, simply because the land value is higher and supply of period homes in desirable locations is fixed.

In contrast, new builds in regeneration areas can benefit from wider area improvements that drive capital growth in the medium term.

## Rental Yields

For rental income, new builds often perform well initially because they attract tenants willing to pay a premium for modern finishes, energy efficiency, and low maintenance. A new build two-bedroom apartment in Portsmouth's city centre might command £950-£1,100 per month, whereas a Victorian conversion flat of similar size might rent for £800-£950.

But period properties can deliver stronger net yields because the purchase price is typically lower. A Victorian terrace bought for £180,000 in need of light refurbishment could rent for £1,100 per month as three beds, yielding 7.3%. A new build of similar size at £280,000 renting for £1,300 yields just 5.6%. The gap in net yield is even wider after factoring in service charges on new build apartments.

## Maintenance and Repairs

This is where the difference becomes stark. New builds come with warranties - typically a 10-year NHBC cover. The builder will rectify structural defects within the first two years, and the warranty covers major issues for a decade. For a landlord, this predictability is valuable.

Period properties require ongoing maintenance. The roof, damp proofing, electrics, plumbing, and windows all have existing service lives that may be nearing expiry. A sensible rule is to budget 1-2% of the property value annually for maintenance on a period property, compared to 0.5% or less for a new build during the warranty period.

But many period homes have already been updated. A Victorian house with a new roof, rewired electrics, and modern heating installed in the last ten years may require little more than cosmetic upkeep for years.

## Tenant Demand on the South Coast

In Portsmouth, tenant demand is driven by two major universities (Portsmouth and Southampton), the naval base, and the city's growing tech and service sectors. Students and young professionals tend to prefer period properties in areas like Southsea, with its character, proximity to the seafront, and established social scene. Families often prefer new build estates on the outskirts, but these tend to be in higher supply.

On the Isle of Wight, the holiday let market favours period properties. A Victorian cottage near Ventnor or a period farmhouse in the countryside attracts premium holiday rates precisely because of its character and original features. A new build chalet near Sandown may appeal less to the holiday market.

## The Economics of Refurbishment

One of the strongest arguments for period properties is the refurbishment opportunity. A tired Victorian terrace purchased at a discount, fully renovated for £30,000-£50,000, and refinanced at a higher valuation can unlock substantial equity. This strategy - sometimes called the BRRRR method - is far harder to execute with new builds, where there is limited scope to add value through refurbishment.

## When New Builds Make Sense

New builds are the better choice for investors prioritising low management intensity. If you live remotely, have limited time for project management, or prefer a predictable cash flow with minimal maintenance surprises, a new build managed by a letting agent can be a sound approach. New builds also tend to be more energy efficient - EPC rating B or above - which matters as minimum energy efficiency standards continue to tighten.

## Our View

For most investors on the South Coast, a well-located period property offers better long-term capital appreciation and higher net yields. New builds are a valid choice for those prioritising simplicity over optimisation, particularly in strong rental markets with limited housing supply.
