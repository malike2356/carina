# Finding Commercial Property Deals: Where to Look and What to Look For

Commercial property investment is often perceived as the preserve of institutions and high-net-worth individuals. The reality is different. With the right approach to deal sourcing, individual investors can find commercial opportunities that generate strong returns across the South Coast.

The key difference between residential and commercial sourcing is the information landscape. Residential deals are everywhere on Rightmove and Zoopla. Commercial deals require more effort to uncover, but that effort is rewarded with less competition and better value.

## The motivated seller opportunity

A generational shift is underway in UK commercial property. Baby Boomers who built commercial portfolios over decades are now reaching retirement age and looking to exit. This creates a window of opportunity where motivated sellers are more common than the market realises.

The signs of a motivated seller include properties that have been on the market for extended periods, price reductions, relistings with different agents, and properties held by older owners or estates. These are the same signals that drive residential sourcing, but in commercial the seller pool is smaller and more concentrated.

On the South Coast, we see this most often with retired shopkeepers who own the freehold of their former premises, or landlords who have held a parade of small units for thirty-plus years and simply want to cash out.

## Where to find commercial deals

**Local commercial agents.** Building relationships with two or three commercial agents in Portsmouth, Southampton, and Bournemouth gives you early access to listings before they hit the portals. Agents prefer buyers who can move quickly and demonstrate proof of funds.

**Direct outreach.** Identify commercial properties that appear vacant or underused. Look for retail units with no clear tenant, upper floors that seem empty, or industrial units with weeds growing in the yard. Write to the owner directly. The Land Registry will tell you who holds the freehold for a small fee.

**Networking.** Property investor groups, local business forums, and commercial property networking events are where off-market opportunities surface. A deal shared at a Propreneur meetup in Portsmouth may never reach Rightmove.

**Auctions.** Commercial property auctions are a reliable source of motivated sellers. Properties at auction are typically being sold because the owner wants a quick exit. The auction catalogue will also tell you the guide price and any tenancy information.

## What to look for in a commercial deal

The criteria are different from residential. You are not looking for good kerb appeal or a modern kitchen. You are looking for:

- Properties where the current rent is below market levels, creating scope for a rent review uplift
- Vacant units in an otherwise let building, where filling them adds value
- Buildings with unused space (storage rooms, upper floors) that can be repurposed
- Leases approaching a break clause or expiry, where the tenant is likely to renew at a higher rent

For example, a parade of three shop units in Portsmouth where two are let at GBP 12,000 per annum each and one is vacant: buy at a yield on the two incomes, fill the vacant unit, and the property value increases immediately as the rent roll grows.

## The numbers that matter

In commercial, the yield is king. But yield must be based on real income, not hypothetical. Calculate your return on the passing rent (what the tenant actually pays), not the estimated rental value. The ERV is your upside, not your underwrite.

A typical commercial investment on the South Coast might offer a net initial yield of 7% to 9% on a fully-let property. A value-add deal with vacancy or below-market rent might buy at a higher yield and improve from there.

## Final thought

Commercial property sourcing is not mysterious or inaccessible. The techniques are the same as residential: find motivated sellers, understand the numbers, and act decisively. The difference is that fewer investors are doing it, which means less competition and better opportunities for those who do.
