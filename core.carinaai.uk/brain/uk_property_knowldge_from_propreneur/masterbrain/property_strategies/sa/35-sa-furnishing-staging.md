# Service Accommodation Furnishing and Staging: Design for Commercial Return

In service accommodation, furnishing is not decoration. It is the primary commercial variable that drives your revenue. The difference between a generic flat with standard furniture and a professionally staged boutique property can be a 34% higher occupancy rate and nearly double the nightly rate.

This article covers how to approach furnishing and staging for commercial return, not aesthetic preference, with practical guidance for South Coast properties.

## The Commercial Case for Quality Furnishing

Consider two identical three-bedroom properties in the same Southsea street. One is furnished with flat-pack furniture, grey walls, and basic bedding. The other has boutique furnishings, thoughtful styling, and a cohesive design scheme.

The first property might achieve a GBP 95 nightly rate at 50% occupancy, generating approximately GBP 17,300 per year. The second property could achieve GBP 160 per night at 70% occupancy, generating approximately GBP 40,900 per year.

The difference is GBP 23,600 per year from the same asset in the same location, driven entirely by the quality of the furnishing and staging. The setup cost difference between the two approaches is typically GBP 10,000 to GBP 15,000, which is recovered within the first six to eight months of operation.

## Design Principles for Service Accommodation

### Win the Scroll

Guests make an emotional booking decision within three seconds of seeing your hero image. Humans process images 60,000 times faster than text. Your listing photographs are your primary sales tool, and the quality of your furnishing determines whether those photographs generate bookings.

Choose furnishings that photograph well. Light colours, clean lines, and uncluttered spaces perform better in photographs than dark, busy interiors. Statement pieces such as an interesting headboard or an unusual light fitting can differentiate your listing in search results.

### Boutique Over Generic

Grey walls with yellow accent cushions from a high street retailer is the default choice for amateur operators everywhere. If your property looks like every other listing, guests choose based on price, which drives your rates down.

Boutique design means making deliberate choices that differentiate your property. A coastal property on the Isle of Wight could incorporate local materials and maritime influences. A city centre Portsmouth flat might use industrial design elements that reference the naval heritage. The goal is to create a look that is recognisable and memorable.

### Longevity Over Trends

Do not furnish to current trends. What looks fashionable today will look dated in three years, and your furniture needs to last five to seven years in service accommodation use. Choose classic pieces in neutral tones and add interest through accessories that are easier and cheaper to replace.

A property that needs complete refurnishing every two years is not commercially viable. Every furnishing choice should be made with a minimum five-year lifespan in mind.

## Room-by-Room Guidance

### Living and Dining Area

This is where guests spend most of their waking time and where your photographs will focus. Invest in a comfortable sofa, a dining table that seats your maximum occupancy, and good lighting. A comfortable, well-lit living space photographs well and creates a positive impression.

### Bedrooms

The bed is the most important piece of furniture in the property. Invest in a quality mattress, good quality sheets, and comfortable pillows. A GBP 200 mattress from a budget retailer will not deliver the sleep quality that generates five-star reviews. Budget GBP 500 or more per bed.

Provide adequate storage. A wardrobe or chest of drawers, a bedside table with a lamp, and a mirror are minimum requirements for a bedroom.

### Kitchen

A well-equipped kitchen is disproportionately valued by guests. Provide good quality cookware, sharp knives, proper glassware, and the small appliances guests expect: a kettle, toaster, and cafetiere or coffee machine. Check and replace items regularly as they wear out.

## Budgeting for Furnishing

A professional SA fit-out costs more than furnishing for a standard let but less than most new operators expect. Typical budgets for a professional fit-out on the South Coast are:

- One-bedroom apartment: GBP 12,000 to GBP 18,000
- Two-bedroom property: GBP 18,000 to GBP 28,000
- Three-bedroom property: GBP 25,000 to GBP 35,000
- Larger properties: GBP 35,000 to GBP 50,000

These budgets cover furniture, soft furnishings, kitchen equipment, linens, towels, and decorative items. They do not cover structural work, redecorating, or new flooring.

## The Staging Process

Professional staging follows a process:

1. Define your target guest profile and their preferences
2. Create a mood board and design scheme before buying anything
3. Purchase all items before installation begins to ensure consistency
4. Install everything at once and conduct a full quality check
5. Hire a professional photographer before the first guest arrives

Do not furnish piecemeal. A property that looks half-finished during early bookings will generate mediocre reviews that are hard to recover from.

## Making Your Budget Work

You do not need to spend the most money to achieve the best results. The key is spending money on the items that guests notice and saving on items they do not.

Spend more on: beds, mattresses, bedding, towels, sofas, and dining tables. These directly affect the guest experience and generate positive reviews.

Spend less on: decorative accessories, wall art, side tables, and occasional chairs. These matters less to guests and can be sourced more affordably.

Furnishing for service accommodation is a commercial decision, not a personal one. Every item you buy should justify itself through its contribution to revenue and reviews.
