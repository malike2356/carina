# Drilling Methods in Ghana

## Rotary Drilling (Mud Rotary)

Most common method for deep community and commercial boreholes in Ghana.
Uses drilling fluid (bentonite polymer mix) to stabilise the hole and carry cuttings.
Suitable for: mixed formations, depths 40-150m, uPVC cased production boreholes.
Typical penetration: 15-25m per day in moderate formations; slower in granite.

## Air Rotary / DTH (Down-The-Hole Hammer)

Uses compressed air to drive a hammer at the bit face. Fast in hard rock.
Common on Massenza and similar rigs with onboard compressors.
Suitable for: granite, schist, hard Birimian formations.
Risk: lost circulation in fractured zones; requires experienced operator.

## Cable Tool (Percussion)

Traditional method; still used by smaller operators for shallow wells under 60m.
Slower but lower equipment cost. Common in northern Ghana for hand-pump wells.
Limited use for deep municipal or industrial boreholes.

## Auger Drilling

Shallow holes only (typically under 30m) in soft alluvial or laterite.
Not suitable for deep aquifer development in crystalline basement.

## Method Selection Guide

| Condition | Recommended method |
|---|---|
| Depth over 80m, community water | Mud rotary with uPVC casing |
| Hard granite, Eastern Region | Air rotary / DTH |
| Shallow hand-pump well under 40m | Cable tool or auger |
| Alluvial river valley | Mud rotary; watch for collapse |
| Artesian zone | Mud rotary; blowout prevention required |

## CWSA Preference

Community Water and Sanitation Agency projects typically specify rotary drilling
with full uPVC casing, gravel pack, sanitary seal, and 24-hour pump test.
