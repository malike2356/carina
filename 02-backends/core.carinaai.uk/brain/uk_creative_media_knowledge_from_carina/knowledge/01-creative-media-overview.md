# UK Creative & Media Industry

## Overview

The UK creative industries contributed approximately £126 billion to the UK economy in 2023 (GVA), representing approximately 5.8% of GDP; larger than the automotive, aerospace, life sciences, and oil and gas sectors combined. The sector grew at more than twice the rate of the wider economy pre-pandemic and employs approximately 2.3 million people.

The UK is a global leader in advertising, film and TV production, video games, publishing, music, and design. London is the world's third-largest film production centre (after Los Angeles and Mumbai) and Europe's largest.

## Structure

### Film and TV
- UK film and TV production spend: approximately £6.3 billion (2023)
- Major studios: Pinewood (007, Star Wars, Marvel), Warner Bros Studios Leavesden (Harry Potter), Shepperton, Elstree, Sky Studios Elstree
- Broadcasters: BBC (licence fee funded, largest broadcaster in the world by employees), ITV, Channel 4, Channel 5, Sky (Comcast-owned)
- Streamers investing heavily in UK production: Netflix, Amazon Prime, Apple TV+, Disney+
- High-end TV tax relief: 25% rebate on qualifying UK spend
- BFI (British Film Institute): lead body for film

### Advertising
- UK is Europe's largest advertising market. Approximately £36 billion annual spend.
- WPP (London, world's largest advertising group), Publicis, Omnicom, Interpublic all have significant UK presence
- Digital advertising: approximately 75% of total ad spend (Google, Meta dominate)
- Adtech cluster in London: approximately 700 adtech companies

### Video Games
- UK is the largest video game market in Europe (6th globally)
- Market value: approximately £7.8 billion (2023)
- Approximately 2,200 games companies
- Major: Rocksteady (Batman: Arkham series), Creative Assembly (Total War, Sega-owned), Rare (Microsoft-owned, Sea of Thieves), Codemasters (EA-owned, F1), Rockstar North (GTA, Edinburgh), Ninja Theory (Microsoft-owned), Media Molecule (Sony-owned), Sumo Group (acquired by Tencent)
- Video Games Tax Relief: 25% on qualifying UK expenditure

### Music
- UK is the second-largest music market globally (after US)
- Recorded music revenue: approximately £2.2 billion (2023)
- Major labels: Universal Music UK, Sony Music UK, Warner Music UK
- Independent labels: Beggars Group, Domino, Warp, XL Recordings, Ninja Tune
- Live music: approximately £2.5 billion. Major festivals: Glastonbury, Reading/Leeds, Wireless, Download, Latitude. Major venues: O2 Arena, Wembley Stadium.
- PRS for Music / PPL: collective management organisations collecting approximately £1 billion annually

### Publishing
- UK publishing revenue: approximately £6.9 billion
- Major: Pearson (education, world's largest education publisher), RELX (STM/legal, FTSE 100), Informa (academic publishing, events), Bloomsbury (Harry Potter), HarperCollins UK, Penguin Random House UK, Hachette UK
- Academic publishing: UK dominant globally through RELX, Informa (Taylor & Francis), Springer Nature, Oxford University Press, Cambridge University Press
- Newspaper groups: News Corp UK (The Sun, The Times), Daily Mail and General Trust (Daily Mail, Metro, i), Reach plc (Daily Mirror, Daily Express, Daily Star, 100+ regional titles), Guardian Media Group, Financial Times (Nikkei-owned)
- Magazine sector: Future plc (Bath, global specialist media), Hearst UK, Condé Nast, Bauer Media

### Design and Fashion
- Design economy: approximately £97 billion GVA (wider definition includes in-house design in manufacturing)
- Fashion: approximately £29 billion contribution. London Fashion Week. Major brands: Burberry, Alexander McQueen (Kering), Vivienne Westwood, Stella McCartney, Paul Smith, Jimmy Choo (Capri Holdings). Retailers: ASOS, Boohoo, Net-a-Porter, Farfetch.

### Architecture
- UK architecture sector: approximately £4.8 billion
- Major: Foster + Partners, Zaha Hadid Architects, Rogers Stirk Harbour + Partners (RSHP), Heatherwick Studio, Grimshaw, BDP
- RIBA (Royal Institute of British Architects): professional body. 38,000 chartered members.

### Museums, Galleries and Heritage
- Approximately 2,500 museums and galleries
- Major: British Museum (5.8m visitors), Natural History Museum (5.7m), Tate Modern (4.7m), National Gallery, V&A, Science Museum, National Museum of Scotland
- Free entry to permanent collections at national museums (since 2001)
- Arts Council England: distributes public funding. Approximately £450 million annually.

## Key Regulation

| Body | Role |
|------|------|
| Ofcom | Broadcasting, video-on-demand, content standards |
| BFI | Film. Administers tax relief certification. |
| IPO (Intellectual Property Office) | Copyright, trade marks, designs, patents |
| ASA (Advertising Standards Authority) | Advertising regulation. CAP Code. |
| BBFC (British Board of Film Classification) | Film age ratings |
| ICO | Data protection, age-appropriate design code |
| Gambling Commission | Regulates gambling advertising and loot boxes (under consultation) |
| Arts Council England / Creative Scotland / Arts Council of Wales / Arts Council of Northern Ireland | Public arts funding |

## Key Legislation
- Copyright, Designs and Patents Act 1988; foundational IP legislation
- Broadcasting Act 1990 / Communications Act 2003; broadcasting regulation
- Digital Economy Act 2017; IP enforcement, age verification (not enacted)
- Online Safety Act 2023; platform duties, harms to children
- Films Act 1985; film certification for tax relief
- Video Recordings Act 1984; physical media classification (extended to online content in certain contexts)
- Equality Act 2010; diversity and inclusion obligations relevant to creative sector

## Pain Points (Agent Opportunity)
1. **Rights clearance and IP management**: Music sync licensing, sample clearance, archive footage, image rights. Very complex, multi-stakeholder.
2. **Tax relief certification**: Film, high-end TV, animation, children's TV, video games, orchestra, theatre, museums and galleries exhibition tax reliefs. All require cultural test and qualifying expenditure calculation.
3. **Broadcast compliance**: Ofcom Broadcasting Code. Fairness, privacy, harm and offence, due impartiality. Compliance logs.
4. **Talent contracting**: Actor agreements (Equity), music producer agreements, writer agreements, option agreements for IP. Rightsholder chain of title.
5. **Music publishing and royalties**: PRS, PPL, MCPS. Split sheets, royalty statements, cue sheets for broadcast. Complex distribution chains.
6. **Film/TV production**: Call sheets, daily progress reports, health and safety (production safety passports), location agreements, insurance.
7. **Video games age rating**: PEGI rating process. IARC for mobile/digital. Loot box and gambling regulation evolving.
8. **Advertising clearance**: CAP Code compliance. Substantiation of claims. Influencer disclosure (#ad). HFSS advertising restrictions.
9. **Arts Council funding applications**: National Lottery Project Grants, Arts Council England. Complex application process, reporting requirements.
10. **Right to work for performers**: International casting. Visa sponsorship (Creative Worker visa, 3 months). Withholding tax for non-resident performers.
