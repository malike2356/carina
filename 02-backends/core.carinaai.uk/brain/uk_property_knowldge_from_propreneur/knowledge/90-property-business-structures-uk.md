# 90 - UK Property Business Structures

## Purpose
A complete guide to the business structures available to UK property investors. Covers own name vs limited company, group structures, Family Investment Companies, and when each is appropriate. Written for Carina to guide investors in understanding structural options - not as tax advice, but as educational context to prompt them to seek qualified guidance.

**Important:** Carina does not give tax advice. This knowledge is for educational context only. Always recommend the investor speak to a qualified property tax specialist before making any structural decisions.

---

## 1. The Core Question: What Is Your "Why"?

Before choosing any structure, the most important question is: **what is your long-term destination?**

Choosing a structure based only on short-term tax efficiency is "the tax tail wagging the dog." The right structure depends on:
- Why you are investing in property
- What you want the portfolio to look like in 10-20 years
- Whether you want to pass wealth to children/grandchildren
- Whether you will invest actively or passively
- Whether you plan to stay UK-resident or become non-resident
- What strategies you plan to use (investing, developing, operating as an agent)

Different destinations require different structures. Designing the wrong structure now and changing it later is expensive - you may trigger capital gains tax, stamp duty land tax, and professional fees running to tens of thousands of pounds. Get the destination right first.

## 2. Buying in Your Own Name (Personal Ownership)

**When this is appropriate:**
- You are a basic rate (20%) taxpayer and plan to remain so throughout the investment period
- You are investing for income that you need in your personal bank account immediately (not for long-term accumulation)
- You plan to retire from employment within a short period and your rental income will become your main income at a lower rate
- You have no intention to pass wealth to future generations
- You are testing whether you enjoy being a landlord before committing to a company structure

**Advantages:**
- Simpler administration (personal self-assessment tax return)
- No accountancy costs for company accounts
- Cash from rental income is directly accessible (no dividend extraction needed)
- No complications with mortgage lenders (who sometimes charge higher rates for companies)

**Section 24 disadvantage:**
Since 2020, personal landlords can no longer deduct mortgage interest as a cost against rental income. Instead, they receive a 20% tax credit on the interest paid.

Example:
- Mortgage interest: £12,000/year
- Before Section 24: higher rate taxpayer would deduct £12,000 from profits, saving £4,800 in tax (40%)
- After Section 24: they receive only a £2,400 tax credit (20%), regardless of their tax bracket
- Net extra tax cost for a 40% taxpayer: £2,400/year per £12,000 of interest

This makes personal ownership increasingly inefficient for higher rate taxpayers holding mortgaged property.

## 3. Limited Company Ownership (SPV or Trading Company)

A Special Purpose Vehicle (SPV) is a limited company created specifically to hold property. It is the most common structure for UK property investors building a portfolio.

**When this is typically appropriate:**
- You are a higher rate (40%) or additional rate (45%) taxpayer
- You intend to accumulate and reinvest profits within the portfolio rather than extract all income personally
- You are building a portfolio for the long term (10+ years)
- You have multiple strategies (e.g., investing and developing)
- You want to involve family members as shareholders to enable income splitting

**Corporation tax advantages:**
- Mortgage interest is fully deductible as a business expense
- Corporation tax: 19-25% (lower than personal income tax for higher earners)
- Retained profits reinvested inside the company are not subject to personal income tax until extracted
- Easier to grow the portfolio using pre-tax cash

**SIC codes to use:**
- 68100 (buying and selling own real estate)
- 68209 (other letting and operating of own or leased real estate)
- Use the right SIC code for the primary activity of the company

**Disadvantages:**
- Higher mortgage rates: specialist lenders typically charge 0.5-1% higher for limited companies vs personal
- Annual accountancy costs: £800-£2,500/year for company accounts, confirmation statement, corporation tax return
- Cash accessibility: profits must be extracted as salary or dividends - both are taxable. Not as flexible as drawing from a personal bank account
- No capital gains tax annual exempt amount (£3,000/year for individuals, unavailable for companies)

## 4. Group Structures (Holding Company)

When an investor has multiple strategies - for example, both holding investment properties AND developing (buying to sell) - a group structure becomes valuable.

**The three company types:**

| Company type | What it does |
|---|---|
| Investment company | Holds long-term assets (buy-to-let, HMO, commercial) - generates passive income |
| Trading company | Active buying and selling - flips, developments, deal sourcing |
| Service company | Professional fee income - letting agent fees, management consultancy |

**Without a group structure, problems arise:**
- Moving money between separate companies triggers tax events (loans to be repaid, dividend tax on transfers)
- If one company has a legal or tax problem, it can affect funds in other entities
- Losses in one company cannot be offset against profits in another

**How a holding company solves this:**

```
[You as shareholder]
       |
[Holding Company] - 100% owner of all subsidiaries
       |
  _____|_____
  |         |
[Investment] [Trading]
 Company    Company
```

**Key advantages of the holding company structure:**
1. **Dividend flow without dividend tax:** When the trading company earns profit, it pays a dividend to the holding company. Company-to-company dividends within a group are not subject to dividend tax. The holding company now has clean cash it can deploy into the investment company.
2. **Asset transfer between companies:** The trading company can transfer properties to the investment company at nil gain, no Corporation Tax, and no SDLT - within the same group.
3. **Group loss relief:** Losses in one subsidiary can be offset against profits in another.
4. **Ring-fencing risk:** Legal or HMRC action against the trading company does not automatically reach the investment company.

**When NOT to set up a group structure immediately:**
- Group structures are more expensive (more companies = more accountancy costs, more compliance)
- Not appropriate for a new investor with one or two properties
- Build toward the group structure - design the first company now to mirror what the holding company will eventually look like, so restructuring is minimal when the time comes

## 5. Family Investment Company (FIC)

A Family Investment Company is a specific type of limited company designed to transfer wealth down generations while the founder retains control and income.

**How it works:**
- The founder sets up a private limited company with specially drafted articles of association
- Different classes of shares are created: one class for the founder (control/voting shares, usually no growth), another class for children/grandchildren (growth shares with dividend rights)
- The founder loans money into the company to fund property purchases
- Company makes money; growth in value accumulates in the children's/grandchildren's shares
- Founder retains director control (decision-making) but wealth passes to the next generation via share value
- Inheritance Tax (IHT) planning benefit: the growth accrues outside the founder's estate

**HMRC clearance:**
HMRC investigated FICs extensively during 2019-2021. In 2021 they confirmed FICs are legitimate tax planning - no changes required. FICs have been used by wealthy families for generations.

**When FICs are appropriate:**
- Portfolio value of £2-3 million or above (legal setup costs of £20,000-£25,000 are proportionate at this scale)
- Clear intention to pass wealth to children or grandchildren
- Long planning horizon (10-30 years)

**FIC setup costs:**
- Specialist legal fees: £20,000-£25,000 (bespoke articles of association, shareholders agreements, director service contracts - standard templates are not suitable)
- Accountancy: ongoing company accounting plus specialist IHT/estate planning advice
- Do not attempt a cheap FIC: one known case where a client spent £10,000 on a poorly drafted FIC found everything was wrong, including the share capital calculation. The corrective work plus SDLT and CGT on share movements cost approximately £100,000.

## 6. Choosing Your Strategy Category First

Mark Barrett's framework: every property activity falls into one of three categories.

**Investment strategy** - buy and hold. Assets sweat with minimal ongoing effort. Rental income and capital growth. This is the lowest-effort, lowest-return approach.

**Duty/service strategy** - you provide a service. Letting agent work, deal sourcing, rent-to-rent operations, consultancy. You are earning fees for your time and expertise, not building an asset base.

**Trading strategy** - dirty hands. Buy, add value, sell. Flips, developments, BRR (Buy-Refurb-Refinance). Capital intensive but highest profit margins.

Before choosing a structure, know which of these categories (or combination) you are pursuing. Each has different tax treatment:
- Investment: CGT on disposal, rental income taxed at income tax/corporation tax
- Service: income tax or corporation tax on fees
- Trading: corporation tax on profits (if company); income tax/NI if sole trader

Mixing trading and investment in one company is usually inefficient. A group structure separates them cleanly.

## 7. Non-Resident Considerations

UK non-residents can own UK property but face different rules:
- Non-Resident Landlords Scheme (NRLS): letting agents or tenants must withhold 20% basic rate tax on rent unless HMRC gives approval to receive gross rents
- Non-Resident Capital Gains Tax (NRCGT): non-residents pay CGT on UK residential property since 2015 and commercial property since 2019
- Statutory Residence Test: complex rules determine UK tax residency - number of days in UK, ties to UK, activity in UK (even transit stays can count)
- Company structure: a UK company is UK-tax resident regardless of where the shareholders live

If there is any intention to become non-resident, structure advice must include the Statutory Residence Test and the implications for the property holding structure.

## 8. The Compliance Environment Is Getting Tighter

HMRC is increasingly sophisticated and aggressive:

**HMRC Connect:** A massive computer system introduced by Gordon Brown that collects and connects data from:
- Banks and credit cards
- Letting agents and estate agents
- Land Registry (every property transaction)
- Companies House
- Social media (scraping for lifestyle inconsistencies)
- International data sharing partners

Connect automatically flags inconsistencies - for example, a landlord who has not filed rental income returns despite the Land Registry showing property ownership. Filing as required removes risk.

**Making Tax Digital (MTD):**
- From April 2026, individuals with property income over £50,000 must use MTD-compatible software to report quarterly to HMRC
- MTD is currently a "push" system (you send to HMRC)
- HMRC's stated long-term objective is a "pull" system (HMRC pulls data directly from your accounting software)
- Get digital accounting systems set up now - not as a compliance burden, but because good records are the foundation of sound decisions

**Director/Company transactions:**
Tax returns for 2025/26 include an expanded section asking about transactions between directors and their companies:
- Money taken out of the company (director loans - Section 455 tax if not repaid within 9 months)
- Expenses paid by the company for personal benefit
- Company-funded assets used personally

**Cheap compliance is expensive:** The annual accountant who just files a return is not giving you value. You want an accountant who understands property, advises on structure, and helps you avoid problems - not just one who adds up numbers.

## 9. Getting Lenders to Accept Your Structure

Group structures have historically been less well understood by mortgage lenders. This is changing:
- Specialist BTL lenders like Paragon now publish acceptable group structure templates
- Show lenders a clear diagram of the structure and ownership
- Ensure articles of association allow for proper decision-making (sole director companies with model articles have caused problems - lenders have refused to lend where a sole director using model articles has made decisions that were legally challengeable as ultra vires)
- Amendment: if your company uses model articles (the government template), ensure the articles are updated to clarify decision-making authority - especially if you are the only director

## 10. The Practical Guidance

For new investors:
- Basic rate taxpayer, one or two properties, want income: buy personally
- Higher rate taxpayer, building a portfolio, reinvesting: use a limited company from the start
- Multiple strategies (invest AND develop): design for a group structure now, even if you only create one company today

For existing investors:
- Already hold property personally and want to move into a company: get specialist tax advice first. Transfer triggers CGT and SDLT - costs can run to tens of thousands. Only worth it if the long-term benefit outweighs the cost.
- Already have two or more companies: investigate whether a group structure (holding company + HMRC clearance for amalgamation) makes sense

The structural decision is important enough to warrant a consultation with a qualified property tax specialist before taking any action.
