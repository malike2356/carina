# 87 - First Deal: The UK Property Buying Process

## Purpose
A complete step-by-step guide to buying an investment property in the UK. Written for beginners who have never been through a purchase. Covers all 14 stages, who is involved at each stage, realistic costs, timeline, and what can go wrong.

---

## Overview

A UK residential property purchase takes 8-16 weeks from having an offer accepted to receiving the keys. During this time you will interact with at least 6-8 different professionals and move money multiple times. Understanding the process in advance removes most of the fear and prevents expensive mistakes.

This guide covers residential investment property (houses, flats, terraces, semi-detached). Commercial property and auction purchases have different processes covered separately.

---

## Stage 1: Define your criteria (before you look at a single property)

**Who is involved:** Just you.

**What to do:**
- Decide on your strategy (BTL, HMO, R2R, SA, BRR, flip)
- Define your target area (within 1 hour of you, or in a high-yield belt remotely)
- Set your maximum purchase price based on available capital and mortgage capacity
- Establish your minimum acceptable yield (e.g. 7% gross for BTL)
- Decide on property type (terraced, semi, flat, HMO-suitable)
- Write these down as non-negotiables BEFORE you start viewing

**Why this matters:** Without criteria, every property looks appealing. With criteria, 90% of listings are immediately eliminated, saving weeks of wasted viewings.

**Time:** 1-2 weeks of research.
**Cost:** Nothing.

---

## Stage 2: Get a mortgage in principle (Decision in Principle / DIP)

**Who is involved:** Mortgage broker, credit reference agencies.

**What to do:**
- Find a whole-of-market mortgage broker who specialises in buy-to-let (not a high street bank)
- Provide: income evidence (last 3 months payslips or 2 years of accounts if self-employed), bank statements, list of existing credit commitments, details of any existing properties
- The broker will do a soft credit check and confirm how much you can borrow
- They will issue a Decision in Principle (DIP) - a conditional offer to lend subject to a formal application and property valuation
- The DIP usually lasts 60-90 days

**Why this matters:** Estate agents take you more seriously if you can show a DIP. You also know your exact budget before falling in love with a property you cannot afford.

**Time:** 1-5 days for DIP once you provide documents.
**Cost:** Some brokers charge a fee (£300-£500). Others take lender commission only. Clarify upfront.

---

## Stage 3: Source the deal

**Who is involved:** You, estate agents, deal sourcers, auction houses, letting agents (who sometimes know of off-market sales).

**Ways to find deals:**
- Rightmove and Zoopla (public listings - mostly at full market value)
- Direct approaches to landlords via lettings agents ("do you want to sell?")
- Deal sourcers (professionals who find BMV deals for a fee - typically £2,000-£5,000 per deal)
- Property auctions (legal and survey packs released before the auction; full payment within 28 days of the gavel falling)
- Probate sales (properties from estates - often sold quickly, sometimes below market)
- Repossessions (via lenders' asset managers, slightly below market value)
- Direct mail to specific streets (letters to homeowners asking if they want to sell)
- Networking events (vendors who want a discreet sale approach investors directly)

**What makes a good deal:**
- Purchase price at or below market value
- Gross yield meeting your target (use the gross yield formula: annual rent / purchase price × 100)
- In an area with strong tenant demand (low void rates, near employment)
- Structurally sound or with only cosmetic work needed (unless BRR/flip strategy)
- Freehold or leasehold with long remaining lease (if leasehold, check: service charge, ground rent, lease length - anything under 80 years is problematic)

**Time:** 2-12 weeks depending on how actively you search.
**Cost:** Deal sourcer fees if used (£2,000-£5,000). No cost if self-sourcing.

---

## Stage 4: View the property

**Who is involved:** You, estate agent, possibly a builder or surveyor if you want a pre-offer inspection.

**What to check during a viewing:**
- Roof condition (look for missing or slipped tiles, visible sagging)
- Gutters and fascias (broken gutters cause damp - cheap to fix but easy to spot)
- Windows (single or double glazed, condition, draught)
- Damp signs (tide marks on walls, peeling plaster, musty smell)
- Structural movement (diagonal cracks above door frames are a warning sign; hairline vertical cracks in plaster are normal)
- Electrical consumer unit age (old fuseboard = full rewire possibly needed - £3,000-£7,000)
- Boiler age and condition (over 12 years = factor replacement cost into offer)
- Room sizes (measure if needed - important for HMO room viability)
- Loft (is there one? Is it insulated?)
- Parking (does the property have dedicated parking or on-street only?)

**Bring with you:** Tape measure, torch, camera, notepad.

**Never:** Do a viewing without a mental list of everything that needs spending. Before making an offer, estimate refurb costs.

**Time:** 1 hour per viewing.
**Cost:** Nothing.

---

## Stage 5: Make an offer

**Who is involved:** You, estate agent.

**How it works:**
- In England and Wales (not Scotland - Scotland uses a different "offers over" sealed bid system), offers are verbal or written to the estate agent
- There is no legal commitment at this stage - either party can walk away
- Offers are typically 5-15% below asking price on investment properties (you have room to negotiate)
- Support your offer with: evidence of your DIP, proof of funds for the deposit, a short covering letter explaining you are a cash-ready investor with no chain
- You can include conditions: subject to survey, subject to satisfactory lease review, subject to planning permission

**Negotiation tactics:**
- Start lower than you are willing to pay (you can always go up, never down)
- Offer speed and certainty over price if the vendor needs a quick sale
- Point out specific refurb costs to justify a lower price
- If the vendor lives in the property, be respectful and personal - they are leaving a home

**Time:** Offer to acceptance is usually 1-5 days.
**Cost:** Nothing.

---

## Stage 6: Instruct solicitors (conveyancing)

**Who is involved:** Your solicitor (conveyancer), vendor's solicitor.

**What happens:**
- Once offer accepted, you and the vendor each instruct a solicitor
- Your solicitor requests a "draft contract pack" from the vendor's solicitor
- The pack includes: title documents, property information form (TA6), fittings and contents form (TA10), leasehold information (if applicable), management company details
- Your solicitor reviews the pack and raises enquiries (questions) back to the vendor's solicitor
- This legal process runs in parallel with your mortgage application

**What to look for in the contract pack:**
- Title is clean (no charges, disputes, or restrictions that would prevent you renting the property)
- Lease length (if leasehold - under 80 years causes mortgage problems)
- Ground rent and service charges (excessive ground rent over £250/yr can cause issues)
- Building regulations certificates for any extensions or conversions
- Planning permissions if the property has been altered

**Time:** 6-12 weeks for the full legal process.
**Cost:** Solicitor fees £800-£2,500 (for a residential investment purchase). Varies by property value and complexity.

---

## Stage 7: Full mortgage application

**Who is involved:** Mortgage broker, lender's underwriter, lender's surveyor.

**What happens:**
- Your broker submits a full mortgage application to the chosen lender
- You provide full documentation: ID, proof of address, income evidence, bank statements, tenancy schedule (if you have existing properties), details of the subject property
- The lender sends a surveyor to value the property - this is a basic "mortgage valuation" (not a structural survey) just confirming the property is worth the price you are paying
- The underwriter checks all documents and either approves, declines, or requests more information
- If approved, you receive a Formal Mortgage Offer - this is valid for 3-6 months

**Important:** The mortgage valuation is NOT a structural survey. If the surveyor down-values the property (says it is worth less than you offered), the lender will only lend based on the lower value. Either renegotiate the price with the vendor or pay the difference from your own funds.

**Time:** 2-4 weeks from application to formal offer.
**Cost:** Mortgage application fee £150-£300. Arrangement fee (product fee) added to mortgage or paid upfront: £0-£2,000 depending on product.

---

## Stage 8: Survey (independent, not lender's)

**Who is involved:** RICS-registered surveyor you instruct separately.

**Types of survey:**
- **RICS Level 1 (Condition Report):** Basic, traffic light system, for newer properties in good condition. £250-£400.
- **RICS Level 2 (HomeBuyer Report):** Detailed inspection of accessible areas, flags defects, market valuation, most commonly used for investment properties. £400-£700.
- **RICS Level 3 (Building Survey / Full Structural Survey):** Comprehensive, recommended for older properties (pre-1900), HMOs, commercial, properties with obvious defects. £600-£1,500.

**What the survey does:**
- Identifies defects the vendor did not disclose
- Estimates repair costs
- Provides an independent valuation

**If the survey flags major issues:**
- You can renegotiate the price downward
- Request the vendor fixes the issue before completion
- Walk away (you lose solicitor and survey costs but avoid a money pit)

**Time:** Survey booked within 1-2 weeks, report within 1 week.
**Cost:** £250-£1,500 depending on type and property size.

---

## Stage 9: Exchange of contracts

**Who is involved:** Your solicitor, vendor's solicitor, you.

**What exchange means:**
- Both sides sign identical contracts
- Contracts are exchanged by post or electronic means
- You pay the deposit (typically 10% of purchase price, transferred to your solicitor's client account then to vendor's solicitor)
- **This is legally binding.** If you pull out after exchange, you lose your deposit. If the vendor pulls out, they owe you the deposit back plus potentially compensation.
- A completion date is agreed at exchange - typically 2-4 weeks after exchange

**What to check before exchanging:**
- Mortgage offer is received and valid
- All solicitor enquiries have been answered satisfactorily
- Survey issues have been resolved
- Buildings insurance is arranged from exchange date (you are legally responsible from this point)
- You have confirmed your solicitor has received your deposit funds

**Time:** Exchange to completion is typically 1-4 weeks (can be done on the same day - "simultaneous exchange and completion" - if both sides agree).
**Cost:** Deposit (10% of purchase price, minus any initial reservation fee).

---

## Stage 10: Completion

**Who is involved:** Your solicitor, vendor's solicitor, lender (releases mortgage funds).

**What happens:**
- Your solicitor receives mortgage funds from the lender
- They transfer the full balance (purchase price minus deposit already transferred) to the vendor's solicitor
- The vendor's solicitor confirms receipt of funds and authorises the agent to release the keys
- The title is transferred to you
- You get the keys

**What you now own:**
- A property with a mortgage against it
- Responsibility for all costs from this moment (insurance, mortgage payments, council tax if empty, utilities)

**Time:** Completion day is typically 10am-2pm. Keys released once funds confirmed.
**Cost:** The remaining purchase price balance is transferred on this day.

---

## Stage 11: Post-completion formalities

**Who is involved:** Your solicitor, HMRC, Land Registry.

**What happens:**
- Your solicitor registers the new ownership at Land Registry (takes 2-12 weeks, longer if there is a queue)
- Stamp Duty Land Tax (SDLT) is calculated and paid to HMRC within 14 days of completion
- Your solicitor provides you with a completion statement showing all costs and balances

**SDLT for investment property (as of 2025):**
Investment (second property or non-main-residence) purchases attract a 3% surcharge on top of standard SDLT rates:
- Up to £125,000: 0% + 3% surcharge = **3%**
- £125,001-£250,000: 2% + 3% = **5%**
- £250,001-£925,000: 5% + 3% = **8%**
- £925,001-£1.5m: 10% + 3% = **13%**
- Over £1.5m: 12% + 3% = **15%**

Example: £200,000 investment property SDLT = (£125,000 × 3%) + (£75,000 × 5%) = £3,750 + £3,750 = **£7,500**

**Time:** SDLT must be paid within 14 days of completion. Land Registry registration: 2-12 weeks.
**Cost:** SDLT as calculated above. Land Registry fee: £20-£910 depending on property value.

## Stage 12: Refurbishment (if needed)

**Who is involved:** Builder or trades team, project manager (you or a professional).

**Typical timelines for common work:**
- Light decoration and new carpets: 1-2 weeks, £1,000-£5,000
- Kitchen replacement: 1-2 weeks, £3,000-£8,000
- Bathroom replacement: 1 week, £2,500-£6,000
- Full rewire: 1-2 weeks, £3,000-£7,000
- New boiler: 1-2 days, £2,500-£4,500
- Full renovation (all of the above): 6-12 weeks, £10,000-£30,000+
- HMO conversion (new rooms, fire doors, kitchen, bathroom, alarm): 8-16 weeks, £15,000-£50,000+

**Key rule:** Get at least 3 quotes from different contractors before starting any work over £1,000. Have a written scope of works and a payment schedule (avoid paying more than 20-30% upfront to any contractor).

**Cost:** Budget an additional 10-20% contingency on top of any refurb estimate. Unexpected issues (hidden damp, asbestos, structural problems) are common in older properties.

---

## Stage 13: Compliance and legal obligations

**Who is involved:** You, local council (licences), Gas Safe engineer, NICEIC electrician, Energy Performance Certificate (EPC) assessor.

**Before you let the property, you must have:**
- **Gas Safety Certificate:** Annual inspection by a Gas Safe registered engineer. £60-£120. Legal requirement before any tenant moves in.
- **Electrical Installation Condition Report (EICR):** Must be done by a qualified electrician. Valid for 5 years. £150-£300. Legal requirement since 2020.
- **Energy Performance Certificate (EPC):** Must be at least grade E (as of 2025) to let legally. Grade D or above recommended. £60-£120. Valid 10 years.
- **HMO Licence** (if HMO with 5+ people): Applied to your local council. Fees vary (£400-£1,200). Required before any HMO tenant moves in.
- **Deposit Protection:** Tenant deposits must be held in a government-approved scheme (DPS, MyDeposits, TDS) within 30 days of receiving them. Failure to do so means fines of up to 3× the deposit.
- **Right to Rent checks:** You or your agent must verify every tenant's right to live in the UK (passport, visa, settled status). Failure is a criminal offence.

**Cost:** All compliance certificates: £300-£600 for a standard BTL property.

---

## Stage 14: Find and move in tenants

**Who is involved:** Letting agent or you (self-managed), referencing company.

**The letting process:**
1. List on Rightmove and Zoopla (only possible via a letting agent - direct landlord listings are not permitted on these platforms) or SpareRoom (for individual rooms)
2. Conduct viewings
3. Reference the chosen tenant: credit check, employment verification, previous landlord reference, right to rent check
4. Sign a tenancy agreement (Assured Shorthold Tenancy for residential single let; separate agreements for HMO licences)
5. Collect first month's rent + 5 weeks deposit (maximum under the Tenant Fees Act 2019)
6. Protect deposit in a government scheme within 30 days
7. Provide tenant with: EPC, Gas Safety Certificate, How to Rent guide (government booklet - must be provided at the start of every tenancy)
8. Register the property for council tax

**Letting agent fees (typically):**
- Let only: 8-12% of first year's rent or fixed fee (£400-£800)
- Fully managed: 10-15% of rent monthly, ongoing
- Tenant find only: fixed fee (£300-£600)

**Time:** Empty property to paying tenant: 2-6 weeks in a normal market.
**Cost:** Letting agent fees as above.

## What can go wrong (and how to avoid it)

**Gazumping:** Another buyer makes a higher offer after yours is accepted. The vendor accepts it, leaving you out. Prevention: exchange as fast as possible. Consider a "lock out agreement" (vendor agrees not to accept other offers for a defined period - costs £200-£500 to document but is legally enforceable).

**Down valuation:** Lender's surveyor values the property at less than you paid. Prevention: do your own comparable analysis before making an offer. Do not overpay.

**Survey surprises:** Major structural issue discovered. Prevention: always get a Level 2 or 3 survey. Budget a contingency. Be prepared to renegotiate or walk away.

**Chain collapse:** In a chain purchase (you are buying from someone who is buying elsewhere), any link in the chain can collapse and pull you all down. Prevention: buy from a vacant property, probate sale, or investor - no chain, no risk.

**Tenant problems:** Bad references, non-payment of rent, property damage. Prevention: thorough referencing, guaranteed rent insurance, use a reputable letting agent.

**Planning/licensing issues:** Property needs HMO licence you cannot get, or is in an Article 4 area requiring planning permission for HMO conversion. Prevention: check with the council before making an offer, not after.

**Leasehold traps:** Short lease (under 80 years), high and escalating ground rent, major works charges, unresponsive freeholder. Prevention: solicitor checks these during conveyancing. Walk away from problematic leaseholds.
