# Why Waterlooville is a Growing Property Investment Hotspot

Waterlooville often flies under the radar for property investors. It is not a city, it is not on the coast, and it does not have a university. What it does have is solid fundamentals: affordable property prices, strong family tenant demand, and a growing population driven by the continued expansion of the north Hampshire area.

For investors looking beyond the obvious city centre markets, Waterlooville deserves a closer look.

## Where Waterlooville sits in the Hampshire market

Waterlooville is located roughly eight miles north of Portsmouth, just off the A3(M). It is effectively a large town that has absorbed neighbouring villages over the years, now forming part of the broader Havant borough.

Its position is its main advantage. Waterlooville is close enough to Portsmouth for tenants who work in the city but want more space and better schools, and it is within commuting distance of London (Portsmouth Harbour to Waterloo in around 90 minutes via Havant station). It also sits near the A3, giving good road access to the M25 and London.

The town has seen significant residential development over the last decade. Large housing estates like Berewood and Wecock Farm have added hundreds of new homes, attracting families and commuters.

## Property prices and yields

Waterlooville is more affordable than Portsmouth city centre, which is unusual for a town that offers larger homes and better schools.

Average property prices in Waterlooville sit at roughly GBP 320,000. A three-bedroom semi-detached family home typically costs between GBP 300,000 and GBP 350,000. Two-bedroom terraces and maisonettes can be found for GBP 225,000 to GBP 275,000.

Rental yields range from 4.5% to 6% depending on property type and condition. Standard family lets produce the most consistent returns. HMOs are less common in Waterlooville than in Portsmouth, partly due to the housing stock being more modern and partly because the tenant profile is more family-oriented.

The strength of the Waterlooville market is not headline yields. It is consistent occupancy. Family lets in decent areas of the town rarely sit empty for long, and tenant quality is generally good. Voids of more than two weeks are unusual for well-priced properties.

## Who rents in Waterlooville

The tenant profile is different from Portsmouth or Southampton:

- Young families priced out of home ownership who need three-bedroom homes with gardens
- Professionals working in Portsmouth, Havant, or the broader Solent region
- Commuters who want more space than they can afford closer to the coast
- Military families posted to nearby bases who prefer suburban family housing

This tenant base is stable and tends to stay longer than the average city tenant. Two to three year tenancies are common, which reduces turnover costs and void periods for landlords.

## New developments and supply

Waterlooville has seen substantial new home building. The Berewood development alone is adding over 2,500 homes. This might sound like a concern for existing landlords, but in practice the new supply has been absorbed well. Population growth in the Havant borough has kept pace with building, and the area continues to attract families moving out of Portsmouth and the coastal strip.

For investors, the new developments create two opportunities. First, buy older existing stock in established areas that benefits from the improved local amenities and infrastructure. Second, consider new-build buy-to-let where developers offer incentives and the properties come with modern EPC ratings.

## What to watch for

Waterlooville is not a high-growth market in terms of capital appreciation. Property values here grow steadily rather than spectacularly, and are closely tied to the Portsmouth market. If Portsmouth prices rise, Waterlooville follows, but with a lag.

The local economy depends heavily on Portsmouth and the wider Solent area. There is not a large employment base within Waterlooville itself, so any significant downturn in the regional economy would affect tenant demand.

## Is Waterlooville right for your portfolio

Waterlooville suits investors who want stable, low-hassle family lets with reasonable yields and good tenant quality. It is less suited to those chasing high HMO yields or rapid capital growth. For a balanced portfolio that includes some lower-risk suburban assets, Waterlooville is a strong addition.
