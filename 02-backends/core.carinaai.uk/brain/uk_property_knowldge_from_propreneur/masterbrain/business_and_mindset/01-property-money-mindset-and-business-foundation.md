## 1. Money Mindset - Rewiring Your Relationship With Money

Most people have a broken relationship with money. This isn't about intelligence or ambition - it's about inherited beliefs, conditioning, and fear. Until you fix the mindset, no system, strategy, or tool will work long term.

### The Core Problem

- Money is not the root of evil - the **love of money** is. Money is neutral. It amplifies who you already are.
- Most people treat money as an **end** rather than a **tool**. This inverted relationship drives poor decisions - hoarding, reckless spending, or avoidance.
- The **fear of money** manifests as: "I'm not good with numbers," "Rich people are greedy," "Money doesn't grow on trees," "I'll never have enough."
- These are stories you were told. They are not truths. They must be identified, challenged, and rewritten.

### The Multiplier Instinct

- Wealthy people see money as a **multiplier** - a way to create more value, more time, more impact.
- Scarcity-minded people see money as a **finite resource to protect** - this leads to small thinking, missed opportunities, and chronic financial stress.
- The shift is internal before it's external. You cannot out-earn a broken mindset.

### Saver vs Spender Archetypes

| Archetype | Danger | The Fix |
|-----------|--------|---------|
| **Saver** | Hoards money out of fear. Never invests. Misses opportunities. | Learn to deploy capital. Calculate risk, don't avoid it. A saved pound that never works is a dead pound. |
| **Spender** | Spends everything earned (or more). No reserves. No investment. | Create systems that force saving before spending. Automate allocation. |
| **Avoidant** | Doesn't look at accounts. Doesn't know what they owe or own. | Face the numbers daily for 30 days. Knowledge cures avoidance. |

### Rewiring Money Beliefs - Practical Steps

1. **Identify your money story.** Write down the first 5 things you remember being told about money as a child. Circle which ones are still active in your head.
2. **Challenge each belief.** Ask: Is this objectively true? Does holding this belief help me or hurt me? What would someone believe instead who is financially successful?
3. **Replace with an empowering belief.** Write the new belief. Read it aloud daily for 30 days.
4. **Practice financial exposure.** Review your bank accounts, debts, and assets every day for 30 days. Do not judge. Just look. Desensitisation rewires avoidance.

### The Universe Principle

> "The universe will not give you more of what you cannot look after already."

- If you cannot manage £1,000 responsibly, you will not get £10,000.
- If you cannot manage £10,000, you will not get £100,000.
- Financial capacity must be built before financial volume arrives.
- Proof of stewardship is what unlocks the next level - not wishing, not hoping, not manifesting without action.

## 2. Money Jars System (T. Harv Eker)

Based on the work of T. Harv Eker (*Secrets of the Millionaire Mind*). The principle: your money must be **allocated on arrival** before your brain has a chance to spend it impulsively.

### The Six Accounts

| Account | Purpose | Suggested % | Notes |
|---------|---------|-------------|-------|
| **Day-to-Day Expenses** | Bills, rent/mortgage, groceries, transport, essentials | 50-60% | This is your survival bucket. Must cover life basics. |
| **Savings** | Emergency fund, rainy day, security buffer | 10% | Build 3-6 months of expenses first, then grow. |
| **Investment** | Property deposits, shares, businesses, training that generates returns | 10% | Only deploy this when you have a clear investment thesis. |
| **Education** | Books, courses, coaching, conferences, certifications | 10% | Invest in yourself first. Your earning capacity is your biggest asset. |
| **Fun** | Holidays, dining out, hobbies, entertainment, guilt-free spending | 10% | Critically important. Deprivation kills momentum. This prevents burnout. |
| **Charity/Giving** | Tithing, donations, helping family, community projects | 5-10% | The act of giving signals abundance and rewires scarcity thinking. |

### Implementation Rules

- **The percentage matters less than the habit.** Start with any percentage. The system working imperfectly is better than no system at all.
- **Automate the distribution.** Set up standing orders or direct debits on payday. Money you never see is money you cannot misspend.
- **Open separate accounts** (or use a platform like Monzo/Starling with pots) for each jar. Visibility matters.
- **Review quarterly.** Adjust percentages as income changes. The ratios may shift but the buckets stay.
- **The Fun account must be spent.** If you accumulate fun money but never enjoy it, the system fails. Guilt-free spending is part of the design.

### Example: £3,000/month Income

| Account | % | Amount |
|---------|---|--------|
| Day-to-Day | 55% | £1,650 |
| Savings | 10% | £300 |
| Investment | 10% | £300 |
| Education | 10% | £300 |
| Fun | 10% | £300 |
| Charity | 5% | £150 |

## 3. Profit First Method (Mike Michalowicz)

Based on Mike Michalowicz's *Profit First*. The core insight: traditional accounting is backwards for entrepreneurs. You must invert the formula.

### The Traditional Formula (Broken)

> **Sales − Expenses = Profit**

This leaves profit as whatever is left over. For most entrepreneurs, nothing is left over. Expenses expand to consume all revenue.

### The Profit First Formula (Fixed)

> **Sales − Profit = Expenses**

Profit is taken **off the top**, first. Expenses must fit within what remains. This forces discipline.

### How It Works for Property Entrepreneurs

1. **Set up 4 bank accounts** (or virtual pots):
   - **Income** - all revenue lands here
   - **Profit** - 5-10% of every payment received, transferred immediately
   - **Tax** - 20-30% for HMRC / corporation tax
   - **Operating Expenses** - everything left for running the business

2. **Allocate on receipt.** Every time you get paid from a deal, landlord payment, or fee, split immediately. Do not wait until month end.

3. **The Profit Allocation Rhythm:**
   - Transfer profit percentage to the Profit account on every single receipt
   - Do NOT spend from the Profit account
   - Every 3-4 months, transfer the accumulated profit out to your personal account
   - Spend it on something enjoyable - a trip, a meal, a gift, an experience
   - This rewards your entrepreneurial effort and keeps momentum

4. **The "Smaller Plates" Concept**
   - If your expense account runs out, you cannot spend more - full stop
   - This forces you to cut costs, negotiate harder, and find efficiencies
   - Smaller plates mean you cannot overeat. The constraint is the discipline.

### Why This Matters for Property

- Property deals have irregular cash flow. Profit First creates a buffer between revenue and lifestyle.
- The quarterly profit distribution keeps you motivated through dry spells.
- Tax account prevents the nightmare of a surprise HMRC bill with no funds to cover it.
- It forces capital discipline - essential when managing deposits, renovations, and portfolio growth.

## 4. Practical Money Management

### Credit Report - Check and Fix

- **Where to check:** Check My File (aggregates all four UK credit agencies) or Experian direct.
- **How often:** Quarterly minimum. Before any mortgage application or major finance.
- **What to look for:**
  - Incorrect addresses linked to your file
  - Accounts that are not yours (identity fraud indicator)
  - Late payment markers that are incorrect
  - Old defaults that should have dropped off (6 years in UK)
  - Electoral roll registration status

### Electoral Roll - Non-Negotiable

- Being on the electoral roll at your current address adds significant points to your credit score.
- Lenders use it as a primary identity verification.
- Register at gov.uk/register-to-vote. Takes 5 minutes.
- If you are not on the electoral roll, expect mortgage and finance applications to be declined or delayed.

### Fixing Errors on Credit File

1. Write to the credit agency (Experian, Equifax, TransUnion) with the error details
2. Attach evidence (bank statements, tenancy agreement, utility bill showing correct info)
3. They must investigate and respond within 28 days
4. If they cannot verify the data, it must be removed
5. Keep a copy of all correspondence

### Strategic Credit Card Use

- **Rule 1:** Never exceed 50% utilisation. If your limit is £5,000, never owe more than £2,500 at statement date. High utilisation tanks your credit score even if you pay in full.
- **Rule 2:** Pay the **full statement balance** every month. Never carry revolving debt. The interest rates destroy wealth.
- **Rule 3:** Never cancel old cards. The age of your credit history is a scoring factor. Close a card and you lose that history. Put a small recurring bill on each old card and set up automatic full payment.
- **Rule 4:** Use cards strategically - Section 75 protection on purchases over £100, cashback cards for business expenses, 0% purchase cards for planned larger buys (paid off within the 0% period).
- **Rule 5:** Increase limits strategically - request increases every 6-12 months to improve your utilisation ratio. But never increase your spending to match.

### Negotiating with Creditors

- If you have existing debt you cannot service, do not ignore it. Creditors prefer partial payment over no payment.
- Contact them before you miss a payment. Explain the situation honestly.
- Ask for: payment holiday, interest freeze, reduced monthly payment, or full and final settlement.
- Get everything in writing. Verbal agreements are not enforceable.
- Use a template letter for formal proposals. Keep records of all communications.

### Credit Building for Property Investors

| Action | Impact | Timeline |
|--------|--------|----------|
| Register on electoral roll | High | Immediate |
| Get on the electoral roll at your property address | High | Next election cycle |
| Get a credit builder card (e.g. Vanquis, Aqua) | Medium | 6-12 months |
| Use < 25% of available credit | High | 3-6 months |
| Pay everything on time | High | Ongoing |
| Have 3+ active credit accounts | Medium | 12+ months |
| Check credit report quarterly | Preventative | Ongoing |

## 5. Cost Reduction Mindset - The Ninja Approach

### The Mindset Shift

Most entrepreneurs focus on earning more. Few focus on keeping more. The pound saved is worth more than the pound earned because the saved pound is post-tax.

### Renegotiate Everything

Most bills and supplier costs are negotiable. The default price is rarely the best price. Here is the procedure:

1. **Review every recurring cost** - utilities, insurance, software subscriptions, phone, broadband, cleaning, supplies, marketing tools
2. **Research competitor pricing** - get quotes from at least 3 providers
3. **Call your current provider** and say: "I've been a loyal customer for X months/years. I've received a better quote from [competitor] for [£X]. Can you match or beat it? If not, I'll need to switch."
4. **Be willing to switch.** If they do not match, actually switch. Follow through is what makes negotiating work long term.
5. **Set a calendar reminder** every 6-12 months to repeat the process.

### What to Renegotiate

| Item | Typical Saving | Strategy |
|------|---------------|----------|
| Business energy | 20-40% | Compare via comparison site, switch supplier |
| Business insurance | 15-30% | Get 3 quotes, mention competitors by name |
| Broadband/phone | 20-50% | Loyalty is not rewarded - say you're leaving |
| Software subscriptions | 10-30% | Ask for annual discount or loyalty pricing |
| Office supplies | 10-20% | Bulk order or switch to own-brand |
| Professional services (legal, accounting) | 10-25% | Ask for fixed-fee packages, negotiate scope |
| Landlord insurance | 15-35% | Compare specialist providers (not aggregators) |

### Council Tax to Business Rates Conversion

- If you own a property that is used **wholly or mainly for business purposes**, it may qualify for Business Rates instead of Council Tax.
- Business Rates can be significantly lower than Council Tax, especially with Small Business Rate Relief (properties with Rateable Value under £15,000).
- **Check eligibility:** Does the property have a business use? Is it being marketed or actively used commercially?
- **Process:**
  1. Contact the Valuation Office Agency (VOA)
  2. Request a reassessment of the property
  3. Provide evidence of business use (tenancy agreement, marketing material, business registrations at the address)
  4. If approved, the property moves from Council Tax to Business Rates
- **Caveat:** Mixed-use properties (part residential, part business) may be split rated. Get professional advice before making changes.

### The Apprentice Lesson

> "Don't spend £9,500 on equipment before you've washed a single car."

The principle: start lean. Validate before you invest. Most business failure comes from spending money before proving demand. Property entrepreneurs do this by:

- Buying expensive software before making their first offer
- Renovating a property before securing a tenant or buyer
- Hiring staff before cash flow justifies the overhead
- Taking expensive courses before taking any action

**The Rule:** Every pound of spending must be justified by activity that has already generated income. Invest after proof, not before hope.

### The Ninja Checklist

- [ ] Review all direct debits and standing orders - cancel anything not actively used
- [ ] Call utilities provider - ask for a discount or switch
- [ ] Call insurance provider - ask for a better rate
- [ ] Review software subscriptions - cancel unused licences
- [ ] Check phone/broadband contract - negotiate or switch
- [ ] Check bank fees - switch to a free business account if paying fees
- [ ] Review professional fees - are you paying by the hour when fixed-fee is available?
- [ ] Check Council Tax status on business properties - could it be Business Rates?
- [ ] Review all properties - are you paying for services (cleaning, maintenance) you could do yourself or renegotiate?
- [ ] Check landlord insurance - have you outgrown your current policy?

## 6. The Business Foundation - Property Is a Business, Not a Hobby

### The Hobby Mindset Kills

Most people who "get into property" treat it as a hobby. They:

- Buy properties they personally like
- Renovate to their personal taste
- Do deals when they feel like it
- Take months to respond to tenants
- Have no systems, no metrics, no targets
- Stop when it gets hard

This is not a business. This is an expensive hobby.

### The Business Mindset

A property business operates like any other business:

- It has goals with deadlines
- It has systems and standard operating procedures
- It tracks metrics and reviews performance weekly
- It treats tenants/clients as customers, not annoyances
- It continuously improves and optimises
- It reinvests profit into growth
- It shows up whether you feel like it or not

### The Statistics

- **75% of new businesses fail in year 1** - not because the idea was bad, but because the operator treated it as a hobby
- **~50% survive to year 3**
- **~5% survive to year 5**
- The difference between the 5% and the 95% is **not intelligence, capital, or luck** - it's **systems, discipline, and consistency**

### Income Generating Tasks (IGTs) - The Only Measure That Counts

An Income Generating Task is anything that directly moves money into your business. Examples:

- Making an offer on a property
- Calling a motivated seller lead
- Following up with a prospective tenant
- Sending a proposal to an investor
- Viewing a property for acquisition
- Closing a deal
- Collecting rent
- Marketing a property for sale or let

**Everything else is overhead.** Overhead is necessary but it does not pay the bills. You must spend the majority of your working time on IGTs, especially in the first 2 years.

### The 80/20 Rule for Property Businesses

| 80% of your time on IGTs | 20% on everything else |
|--------------------------|----------------------|
| Deal sourcing | Accounting |
| Lead follow-up | Legal/compliance |
| Viewings and offers | Website updates |
| Tenant/ buyer follow-up | Social media |
| Closing | Learning (limit to 20%) |
| Rent collection | Networking |

### The Hobby vs Business Diagnostic

| Hobby | Business |
|-------|----------|
| "I'll do it when I feel like it" | "I do it every day at 9am" |
| No written goals | SMART goals reviewed weekly |
| No budget | Profit First accounts active |
| Reacts to problems | Prevents problems with systems |
| Works alone | Has a team (even if virtual assistant) |
| Unorganised finances | Clean accounting, tax pot funded |
| No metrics track | Tracks IGTs, leads, conversions, pipeline |
| Stops at resistance | Expects resistance and works through it |

## 7. Commanding a Room - Presence From Day One

### Why Presence Matters in Property

Property is a people business. You negotiate with sellers, lenders, investors, tenants, contractors, solicitors, estate agents, and surveyors. Every single one of them forms an opinion of you within seconds. That opinion determines your deal outcomes.

**Your presence must signal competence before you say a word.**

### Authority Is Built Deliberately

Authority is not something you are born with. It is a set of behaviours you choose to adopt until they become automatic.

| Element | How to Build It |
|---------|-----------------|
| **Eye contact** | Hold eye contact slightly longer than feels comfortable. Look at one eye, then the other, then back. Do not dart around the room. |
| **Dress** | Dress one level above the room. Property meeting at a coffee shop? Wear a jacket. Meeting an investor? Suit. Viewing a property? Smart casual but polished (no trainers, no tracksuits, clean shoes). |
| **Punctuality** | Arrive 5 minutes early. Late arrivals signal disorganisation before you open your mouth. |
| **Follow-through** | If you say you will send something, send it within the hour. If you say you will call, call when you said you would. Reliability is rare and noticed. |
| **Posture** | Stand straight. Shoulders back. Hands out of pockets. Do not slouch, lean, or fidget. |
| **Voice** | Speak clearly. Slower than you think you need to. Pause before important points. Silence signals confidence. |
| **Listening** | Listen more than you speak. Ask questions. Repeat back what you heard. People trust people who feel heard. |

### Trust Is Built Before the Deal Conversation

- Trust is earned in small moments - the returned call, the early arrival, the follow-up email with exactly what you promised
- By the time you sit down to negotiate the deal, trust should already be established
- If you are building trust during the negotiation, you have already lost

### The Property Power Statement

When someone asks what you do, have a prepared response that signals professionalism:

> "I run a property investment business. We acquire and manage residential properties in the [X] area, focusing on [Y strategy - e.g., HMOs, single lets, refurb-to-sell]. We currently manage [Z] units."

This is better than: "I do a bit of property" or "I'm looking to get into property."

### Dealing with Skepticism

You will encounter people who doubt you, especially early on. Respond with:

- **Facts, not feelings.** "I've done X deals this year" beats "I know what I'm doing."
- **Specifics, not generalities.** "I'm looking in the postcode PO4-PO6 area, targeting 3-bed terraces under £200k" beats "I'm looking for properties."
- **Action, not words.** Close deals. Show proof. Let results do the talking.

### Commanding Presence Checklist

- [ ] Prepare your power statement and practise it until natural
- [ ] Check your appearance before every meeting - would you lend this person £500,000?
- [ ] Arrive 5 minutes early to every meeting
- [ ] Make eye contact and shake hands firmly
- [ ] Listen at least 60% of the conversation time
- [ ] Send a follow-up within 2 hours of every meeting
- [ ] Meet every commitment you make, same day if possible
- [ ] Dress one level above the room
- [ ] Don't over-share - you don't need to prove yourself with words
- [ ] Let results speak - the more you do, the less you need to say

## Quick Reference Summary

### The 5 Money Rules

1. Money is a tool, not an end - treat it accordingly
2. Allocate money on arrival (Money Jars / Profit First)
3. Check your credit file quarterly and fix errors immediately
4. Renegotiate every recurring cost annually
5. Spend on proof, not on hope

### The 5 Business Rules

1. Property is a business, treat it like one or it will fail
2. Spend 80% of your time on Income Generating Tasks
3. Build presence and authority deliberately
4. Trust is built before the deal conversation, not during it
5. Systems beat motivation - automate, document, repeat

### The 5 Daily Habits

1. Review your finances (5 minutes)
2. Complete at least one IGT
3. Follow up on one lead or existing contact
4. Read or learn for 15 minutes (education pot at work)
5. Check your credit report quarterly (not daily)
