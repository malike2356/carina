# Student Property Investment on the South Coast

Student housing is one of the most resilient sectors of the UK private rented sector. Tenant demand is predictable, rental income is often guaranteed by purpose-built accommodation providers or parents, and void periods follow a reliable annual cycle. For investors willing to navigate the regulatory requirements, student property on the South Coast offers attractive returns.

## Why the South Coast Works for Student Property

The South Coast is home to several major universities and higher education institutions. The University of Portsmouth enrols approximately 21,000 students, the University of Southampton around 25,000, and the University of Chichester a further 5,000. Bournemouth University and Solent University in Southampton add tens of thousands more. Together, these institutions create sustained demand for private rented accommodation that purpose-built student accommodation (PBSA) cannot fully meet.

Portsmouth is particularly well-suited to student property investment. The main university campus sits in the centre of the city, connected to student-heavy areas of Southsea. Walking distance to campus is a premium feature that students are willing to pay for.

## HMO Licensing and Regulation

Student property investment almost always means operating an HMO (house in multiple occupation). A property let to three or more unrelated tenants who share amenities requires mandatory HMO licensing if it is occupied by five or more people.

Portsmouth City Council operates an additional licensing scheme that covers smaller HMOs (three or four occupiers) in designated areas. The additional licensing scheme in Portsmouth covers much of the city centre, Southsea, and parts of Fratton. The cost of a licence is approximately £600-£1,100 per property per five-year period.

Compliance requirements include:

- Minimum room sizes (6.51 sqm for one person over ten years old)
- Annual gas safety certificate
- Five-year electrical installation condition report
- Fire doors, smoke alarms, and emergency lighting
- Carbon monoxide detectors in rooms with solid fuel appliances
- Adequate kitchen and bathroom facilities for the number of occupants

## Financial Returns on Student Property

Student HMOs typically generate yields of 8-12%, significantly higher than standard buy-to-lets. The trade-off is higher management intensity and seasonal cash flow.

Consider a typical student HMO in Southsea. A three-storey Victorian terrace converted into six en-suite rooms might cost £350,000-£400,000 to purchase. With six rooms let at £550-£650 per month each (44- or 48-week tenancies), the gross annual income is approximately £33,000-£39,000. After mortgage costs, management, bills, and maintenance, the net yield sits comfortably in the 8-10% range.

In contrast, a standard three-bedroom let in the same area might produce £12,000-£14,000 per year in rent on a similar property value, yielding 4-5%.

## Managing the Student Cycle

Student tenancies run on an academic year cycle. Most students require accommodation from September to July, with August being the typical void month. Some landlords offer 48-week tenancies (September to August) to capture summer letting while keeping a buffer for maintenance.

The key risk is the concentration of tenancy start and end dates. If all your students leave in July, you have a narrow window to refurbish rooms and re-let for September. A well-run student portfolio staggers room availability, maintains good relationships with the university accommodation office, and starts marketing rooms in January for the following academic year.

## Universal Credit and Student Tenants

Students are generally not eligible for housing benefit or Universal Credit while studying full-time, except in limited circumstances. This means student rents must be affordable without state support. However, many students have parental guarantees, and the University of Portsmouth operates a rent guarantor scheme for international students.

## Beyond Portsmouth

Southampton's student market centres on the University of Southampton's Highfield campus and Solent University's city centre campus. Properties in Portswood, Highfield, and St Denys are in strong demand. Bournemouth attracts both Bournemouth University and Arts University Bournemouth students, with areas such as Winton, Charminster, and Talbot Woods being popular.

The Isle of Wight has less student demand overall, though the Isle of Wight College and some further education provision creates a modest market in Newport.

## Purpose-Built Student Accommodation vs HMOs

PBSA has grown significantly in Portsmouth, with several large blocks near the university campus. These offer students high-quality en-suite rooms with extensive facilities. However, PBSA is expensive - typically £180-£250 per week - and does not suit all students. HMOs in converted Victorian houses remain popular with students seeking character, space, and a lower rent point (£110-£150 per week).

The key competitive advantage of an HMO over PBSA is flexibility: you can adapt room sizes, offer different price points, and create shared living spaces that many students prefer.

## Getting Started with Student Property

Before buying your first student HMO, research the local licensing scheme, speak to university accommodation officers, and run detailed financial projections that account for the seasonal cash flow pattern. Work with a mortgage broker who understands HMO lending, as not all buy-to-let lenders accept HMO applications.
