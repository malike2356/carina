# Void Rate in Commercial Property: The Cost of Empty Space

Published: Mon, 05 Jul 2027 09:00:00 +0000
Category: Calculations
Slug: void-rate-in-commercial-property-the-cost-of-empty-space

---

Commercial voids are different from residential. A commercial property can sit empty for months or years because the market for commercial tenants is smaller and the decision cycle is longer. A void in a commercial property is expensive.\n\nThe commercial void rate is the percentage of your total lettable space that is currently unoccupied. If a building has 1,000 square metres of space and 100 are empty, the void rate is 10%.\n\nIn the current market, commercial void rates vary significantly by sector. Industrial and logistics properties have low voids, typically 3% to 6%. Retail voids have risen to 8% to 15% in many areas. Office voids depend heavily on location and quality, with prime city centre offices at 5% to 8% and secondary offices at 10% to 20%.\n\nThe cost of a void is not just the lost rent. You still pay business rates on empty property for the first three months, then a reduced rate. You pay insurance and service charges. You may need to spend money on marketing and broker fees to find a new tenant.\n
When underwriting a commercial purchase, assume a vacancy rate of at least 5% even for a fully let property, to account for voids between tenants. For a property with a short WAULT, assume higher voids.

**The formula**

```
Void Rate (%) = (Vacant Area / Total Lettable Area) × 100
```

**Why this matters**

Commercial void rates of 5% to 15% are typical depending on sector. Budget at least 5% even for fully let properties. Commercial voids are expensive because they last longer than residential voids.
