# BMV Discount Percentage: The Number That Tells You If It Is a Real Bargain

Discount amount in pounds is useful. Discount percentage is more revealing because it standardises the comparison across different price ranges. A £20,000 discount on a £80,000 property is 25%. The same £20,000 discount on a £400,000 property is only 5%.\n\nThe discount percentage is your discount amount divided by the market value, multiplied by 100. For a £160,000 purchase of a £200,000 property, it is £40,000 divided by £200,000, which is 20%.\n\nA 20% discount is significant. At that level, you have instant equity. If you can refinance at 75% LTV on the full £200,000 market value, you get £150,000 from the bank. Your purchase was £160,000. You are only £10,000 short of getting all your cash out.\n\nThe discount percentage also affects your exit options. With a 20% discount, you can sell at market value and still make a profit even after costs. With a 10% discount, your profit margin is much thinner.\n
For BRR and BMV strategies, the discount percentage determines whether the deal is viable. A 20% discount on a property that needs refurb could become a 35% discount after refurb, making the BRR numbers work much better.

**The formula**

```
Discount (%) = (Discount Amount / Market Value) × 100
```

**Why this matters**

BMV discount percentage should be at least 20%. Below 15%, the discount is not deep enough for serious equity extraction. Above 25%, you have a potential cash-out opportunity.
