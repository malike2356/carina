# CGT: Capital Gains Tax on Residential Property -- What You Pay When You Sell

Published: Wed, 22 Dec 2027 09:00:00 +0000
Category: Calculations
Slug: cgt-capital-gains-tax-on-residential-property-what-you-pay-when-you-se

---

When you sell a residential property that is not your main home, you pay Capital Gains Tax on the profit. The rate is 18% for basic rate taxpayers and 24% for higher rate taxpayers. The gain is the sale price minus the purchase price minus allowable costs.\n\nAllowable costs include the purchase price, SDLT, legal fees on purchase and sale, estate agent fees, and capital improvements. They do not include ongoing maintenance or refurbishment that was claimed as an expense against rental income.\n\nFor a BTL bought for £200,000 with £15,000 in purchase costs and sold for £300,000 with £6,000 in selling costs, the chargeable gain is £300,000 minus £200,000 minus £15,000 minus £6,000, which equals £79,000. At 24%, the CGT bill is £18,960.\n\nEach individual has an annual CGT allowance (currently £3,000). Married couples have £6,000 between them. If possible, time your sale to use both allowances across two tax years, or transfer ownership to a spouse before selling.

**The formula**

```
Gain = Sale Price - Purchase Price - Allowable Costs (SDLT, legal, improvement costs, agent)
Taxable Gain = Gain - Annual CGT Allowance (£3,000 for 2024-25)
CGT = Taxable Gain × CGT Rate
```

**Why this matters**

CGT on residential property is 18% or 24% depending on your tax band. Plan for it when you sell. Use your annual allowance and consider timing sales across tax years to minimise the bill.
