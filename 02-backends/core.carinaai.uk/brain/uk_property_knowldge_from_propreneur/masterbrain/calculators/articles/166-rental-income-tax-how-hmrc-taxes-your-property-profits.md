# Rental Income Tax: How HMRC Taxes Your Property Profits

Published: Wed, 15 Dec 2027 09:00:00 +0000
Category: Calculations
Slug: rental-income-tax-how-hmrc-taxes-your-property-profits

---

Rental income is taxed as trading profit. Your taxable profit is your gross rental income minus allowable expenses. But since Section 24 was introduced, mortgage interest is no longer an allowable expense. You claim a 20% tax credit on your interest instead.\n\nFor a basic rate taxpayer with £12,000 rent and £4,000 in allowable expenses (management, maintenance, insurance, letting fees), your profit before interest is £8,000. Your mortgage interest is £7,000. You cannot deduct the £7,000. You pay tax on the full £8,000 and then claim a 20% credit on the £7,000 interest, which reduces your tax bill by £1,400.\n\nThe impact of Section 24 is that higher rate taxpayers pay more tax. A 40% taxpayer with the same numbers pays £3,200 in tax minus the £1,400 credit, leaving a net tax bill of £1,800. Before Section 24, the tax would have been 40% of £1,000 (profit after interest), which is £400. The tax bill has more than quadrupled.\n
Understand your tax position before you buy. If you are a higher rate taxpayer, the post-tax return on a BTL is significantly lower than it appears.

**The formula**

```
Taxable Rental Profit = Gross Rental Income - Allowable Expenses (excl. mortgage interest)
```

**Why this matters**

Section 24 means mortgage interest is no longer deductible for individual landlords. Higher rate taxpayers are hit hardest. Calculate your post-tax return before you buy, not after.
