# ADR: Average Daily Rate -- The Core Metric for Every Serviced Accommodation

Published: Wed, 30 Dec 2026 09:00:00 +0000
Category: Calculations
Slug: adr-average-daily-rate-the-core-metric-for-every-serviced-accommodatio

---

ADR is your total room revenue divided by the number of nights you actually sold. It tells you the average price per booked night. For a Portsmouth SA property with £28,105 in annual revenue and 256 booked nights, your ADR is £110.

ADR is the number you control directly. You set your nightly price. You decide when to discount and when to raise rates. A good host actively manages ADR, not just occupancy.

The mistake new SA operators make is lowering prices to fill nights. A lower ADR means you need higher occupancy to achieve the same revenue. Sometimes it is better to have fewer bookings at higher rates than more bookings at lower rates.

In Portsmouth, ADR varies significantly by season and property type. A one-bed city centre apartment might achieve £90 to £140 per night. A three-bed house near the seafront might achieve £150 to £250. Check your competitors on Airbnb to understand the market rate for your property type.

The key is to find the ADR that maximises your total revenue, not just your occupancy rate. Sometimes lowering the price increases occupancy enough to increase total revenue. Sometimes raising the price reduces occupancy but increases total revenue because you are earning more per booking.

Experiment with pricing and track the results. The hosts who optimise ADR consistently outperform those who set and forget.

**The formula**

```
ADR = Total Revenue in Period / Total Nights Sold in Period
```

**Why this matters**

ADR is the most controllable metric in SA. Raise it during peak periods, discount strategically during slow periods. The goal is not maximum occupancy. It is maximum total revenue.
