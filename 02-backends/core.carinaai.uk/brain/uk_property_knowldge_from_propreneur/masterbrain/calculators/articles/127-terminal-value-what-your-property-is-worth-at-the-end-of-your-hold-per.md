# Terminal Value: What Your Property Is Worth at the End of Your Hold Period

Published: Mon, 02 Aug 2027 09:00:00 +0000
Category: Calculations
Slug: terminal-value-what-your-property-is-worth-at-the-end-of-your-hold-per

---

When you sell a property at the end of your hold period, you receive a lump sum. Terminal value estimates what that lump sum will be based on the property's income at that point and expected long-term growth.\n\nThe Gordon Growth Model calculates terminal value as the net income in your final year multiplied by one plus the long-term growth rate, divided by your required return minus the growth rate. If the final year net income is £15,000, growth is 2%, and your required return is 8%, the terminal value is £15,000 times 1.02 divided by 0.08 minus 0.02, which equals £255,000.\n\nTerminal value often accounts for 60% to 80% of a DCF valuation, especially for long hold periods. That means most of your property's value depends on assumptions about what happens at the end of your hold period, not what happens during it.\n\nThis is why terminal value assumptions matter so much. A small change in the growth rate or required return creates a large change in terminal value. Be conservative. Assume lower growth and a higher required return than you expect.

**The formula**

```
Terminal Value = CF(n) × (1 + g) / (r - g)
```

**Why this matters**

Terminal value typically represents 60% to 80% of a property's DCF value. Small changes in growth or return assumptions create large changes in terminal value. Be conservative.
