# Investment Method: Valuing Property Based on Its Income

Published: Mon, 26 Jul 2027 09:00:00 +0000
Category: Calculations
Slug: investment-method-valuing-property-based-on-its-income

---

The investment method values a property based on the income it generates. It is the primary method for commercial property and is increasingly used for residential BTL. The formula is simple: net income divided by the capitalisation rate equals value.\n\nIf a property generates £12,000 in net rental income and the market cap rate for similar properties is 5%, the investment value is £12,000 divided by 0.05, which equals £240,000. That is what the property is worth as an income-producing asset.\n\nThe cap rate is the market's required return for that type of property. A lower cap rate means higher value and lower risk. A higher cap rate means lower value and higher risk. Cap rates vary by property type, location, and market conditions.\n\nThe investment method is useful when comparing BTL properties. If two properties produce the same net income but one is priced higher, the higher-priced one has a lower cap rate. That might be justified by better location or lower risk, or it might mean the property is overpriced.\n
Work backwards from the asking price to calculate the implied cap rate. Compare it to market rates for similar properties. If the implied rate is significantly lower, the property might be overvalued.

**The formula**

```
Value = Net Income / Capitalisation Rate (as decimal)
```

**Why this matters**

The investment method values property by capitalising its net income. It is the standard for commercial and increasingly used for BTL. The implied cap rate at the asking price tells you whether a property is fairly valued.
