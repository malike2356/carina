# Rule of 72: The Simple Trick to Estimate How Long It Takes to Double Your Money

---

The Rule of 72 is a mental shortcut. Divide 72 by your annual return percentage and you get the approximate number of years it takes to double your money. At 6% annual return, it takes 72 divided by 6, which is 12 years to double. At 9%, it takes 8 years. At 3%, it takes 24 years.\n\nFor property investors, the Rule of 72 is useful for quick comparisons. If your property is returning 4% net yield plus 2% capital growth, your total return is 6%. Doubling time is 12 years. If you could earn 8% in an index fund, doubling time is 9 years. The property takes three years longer to double your money.\n\nThe rule is an approximation. It is most accurate for returns between 5% and 12%. Outside that range, the estimate becomes less precise but still useful for quick mental maths.\n
Use the Rule of 72 to compare the doubling time of different investments. If property takes 15 years and stocks take 10, you need a good reason to choose property. Capital growth acceleration, leverage, or portfolio diversification might justify the longer doubling time.

**The formula**

```
Years to Double = 72 / Annual Return %
```

**A worked example**

9% return → 72/9 = 8 years to double.
