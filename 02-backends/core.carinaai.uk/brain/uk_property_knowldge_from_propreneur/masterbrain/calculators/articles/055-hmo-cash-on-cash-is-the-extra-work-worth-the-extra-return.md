# HMO Cash-on-Cash: Is the Extra Work Worth the Extra Return?

Published: Mon, 23 Nov 2026 09:00:00 +0000
Category: Calculations
Slug: hmo-cash-on-cash-is-the-extra-work-worth-the-extra-return

---

Cash-on-cash return is the best way to evaluate whether an HMO is worth the effort. It tells you how much cash you get back each year compared to the cash you put in.

An HMO typically requires more upfront cash than a single let. Higher deposit requirements, HMO licence fees, furnishing costs, and a larger working capital buffer. A £300,000 HMO might require £75,000 deposit plus £15,000 stamp duty plus £15,000 furnishing and setup plus £5,000 legal and survey fees. Total cash: £110,000.

Your annual NOI is £14,684. Your mortgage at 75% LTV and 5.5% is roughly £10,313. Your BTCF is £4,371. Your cash-on-cash return is £4,371 divided by £110,000, which equals 4%.

A single let on the same property with a £70,000 total cash investment and a cashflow of £1,000 a year gives a cash-on-cash return of 1.4%. The HMO gives 4%. That is nearly three times better.

The caveat is that these numbers assume the HMO runs well. If voids increase, costs rise, or rooms rent at lower rates, the HMO cash-on-cash drops faster than a single let because the operational leverage works both ways.

Run the numbers on both scenarios before you decide. If the HMO cash-on-cash is significantly higher, the extra work is justified.

**The formula**

```
CoC (%) = ((NOI - Annual Mortgage) / Total Cash Invested) × 100
```

**Why this matters**

HMO cash-on-cash is typically two to three times higher than a single let. But the operating leverage cuts both ways. If the HMO underperforms, it underperforms harder. Stress-test your assumptions.
