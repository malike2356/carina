# Development Appraisal Shorthand: The RICS Method in Practice

Published: Wed, 02 Jun 2027 09:00:00 +0000

---

The RICS residual method is the standard approach for development appraisals. It works backwards from the GDV, subtracts all costs, and arrives at the residual which is the land value. It is the method lenders, valuers, and professional developers all use.\n\nThe shorthand version works like this. Step one: establish GDV using comparable evidence. Step two: estimate total build costs including contingency. Step three: add professional fees at 10% to 12% of build cost. Step four: add finance costs estimated using the 60% drawdown rule. Step five: add your target profit. Step six: subtract all of the above from GDV. The result is the maximum you can pay for the site.\n\nA quick viability check: if the RLV is less than 20% of GDV, the development is marginal. If the RLV is 25% to 35% of GDV, you have a healthy project. If the RLV is above 35%, the site is attractively priced.\n\nFor a site worth £100,000 on a £560,000 GDV, the RLV to GDV ratio is 17.9%. The project is marginal. You need to reduce costs or increase GDV to make it work.\n\nEvery developer should run this shorthand check before spending money on detailed feasibility studies. If the shorthand numbers do not work, no amount of detail will fix them.

**The formula**

```
RLV = GDV - Total Development Costs (excl. land) - Developer's Profit
Developer's Profit = GDV × Profit % on GDV  (or = Total Costs × Profit % on Cost)
```

**Why this matters**

The RICS residual method is the standard for development appraisals. The shorthand check: RLV should be at least 20% of GDV for a viable project. Below 20%, the project is marginal.
