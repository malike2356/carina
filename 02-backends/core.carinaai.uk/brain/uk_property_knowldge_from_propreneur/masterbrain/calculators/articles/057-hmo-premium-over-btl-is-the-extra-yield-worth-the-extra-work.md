# HMO Premium Over BTL: Is the Extra Yield Worth the Extra Work?

Published: Mon, 30 Nov 2026 09:00:00 +0000
Category: Calculations
Slug: hmo-premium-over-btl-is-the-extra-yield-worth-the-extra-work

---

The final question every HMO investor should answer is simple: how much extra am I earning by running this as an HMO instead of a single let?

The HMO premium is the difference between the HMO gross yield and the BTL gross yield for the same property. If the property as a single let would yield 5.3% and the HMO yields 12%, the premium is 6.7%. That is significant.

A premium of 3% or more justifies the extra management, higher costs, and increased regulatory burden. Below 3%, the premium might not be worth the hassle.

In practice, the premium varies by location and property type. In Portsmouth, where there is strong HMO demand from both students and professionals, the premium is typically 4% to 8%. In some areas, where single let yields are already high, the premium is smaller because there is less rental uplift from converting to HMO.

The premium also depends on the quality of the HMO. A well-furnished, well-managed HMO at full occupancy commands a larger premium than a tired property with high turnover.

Calculate the premium before you convert. If the expected premium is below 4%, consider whether the additional work and risk are justified by the extra return.

**The formula**

```
HMO Premium = HMO Gross Yield - BTL Gross Yield (if single let at market rent)
```

**Why this matters**

The HMO premium should be at least 3% to 4% to justify the extra work. Below that, the maths favours a simpler single let strategy. Above 6%, the HMO is a clear winner.
