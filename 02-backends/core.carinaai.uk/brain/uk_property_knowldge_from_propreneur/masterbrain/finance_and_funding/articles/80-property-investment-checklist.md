# The Ultimate Property Investment Checklist

Every property investment involves dozens of decisions. Miss one, and a good deal can turn expensive quickly. A systematic checklist ensures you consider everything before committing your capital.

This checklist covers the key areas to work through before buying any investment property.

## Before you start: your investment strategy

- [ ] Have you defined your investment goal (cash flow, capital growth, or balanced)?
- [ ] What is your target gross yield (minimum)?
- [ ] What is your budget, including purchase costs (SDLT, legal fees, survey)?
- [ ] Have you decided on property type (single let, HMO, SA, commercial)?
- [ ] Do you know your target area, and have you researched it?
- [ ] Have you established your financing plan (cash, mortgage, private capital)?
- [ ] Is your credit score in good shape?
- [ ] Have you spoken to a mortgage broker to understand your borrowing capacity?

## The property search

- [ ] Have you pre-validated demand in the target area (macro level)?
- [ ] Have you pre-validated demand at neighbourhood level (micro)?
- [ ] Have you identified motivated seller signals (price reductions, days on market, relisting)?
- [ ] Are you using a sourcing agent, or are you sourcing yourself?
- [ ] Have you built a relationship with local estate agents?
- [ ] Are you monitoring auction listings for the area?
- [ ] Have you checked local planning applications that might affect the area?

## Due diligence on the property

- [ ] Has a full building survey been carried out (not just a valuation)?
- [ ] Have you checked the EPC rating (minimum E, with C on the way)?
- [ ] Is the property in a flood risk zone? Have you checked the Environment Agency maps?
- [ ] Have you checked for structural issues (damp, subsidence, roof condition)?
- [ ] Has an electrical installation condition report (EICR) been done?
- [ ] Has a gas safety check been carried out?
- [ ] Is there any Japanese knotweed or other invasive species?
- [ ] Have you checked the title deeds for any restrictions or covenants?
- [ ] Is there a management company or ground rent that affects the property?
- [ ] Have you checked whether the property is listed or in a conservation area?

## Planning and licensing

- [ ] Does the property require planning permission for your intended use?
- [ ] Is permitted development rights relevant for any changes?
- [ ] Does an HMO licence apply? Have you checked the current licensing scheme?
- [ ] Are there any Article 4 directions that affect the area?
- [ ] Have you checked whether selective or additional licensing applies?
- [ ] For student lets: have you checked the council's approach to HMOs?

## Financial analysis

- [ ] Have you calculated the gross yield (annual rent / purchase price)?
- [ ] Have you calculated the net yield (annual rent minus all costs / purchase price)?
- [ ] Have you included all purchase costs: SDLT, legal fees, survey, mortgage fees?
- [ ] Have you included all ongoing costs: mortgage, insurance, management, maintenance?
- [ ] Have you budgeted for refurbishment costs (with 10% to 15% contingency)?
- [ ] Have you included void periods in your cash flow projection (at least one month per year)?
- [ ] Have you included capital expenditure reserves (new boiler, roof, kitchen)?
- [ ] Have you stress-tested the numbers: what if interest rates rise by 2%?
- [ ] What if the property is empty for three months? Can you cover the costs?
- [ ] Have you calculated your return on investment (ROI) and return on capital employed (ROCE)?
- [ ] Have you compared the numbers against your target returns?

## The area market check

- [ ] What is the average rent for similar properties in the area?
- [ ] What are the recent comparable sales prices?
- [ ] What is the average time on market for rental properties in this area?
- [ ] What is the vacancy rate for the area?
- [ ] Who are the main employers and industries driving demand?
- [ ] Are there any major developments planned that could affect property values?
- [ ] What is the local school catchment like (important for family lets)?
- [ ] What are the transport links (train, bus, road)?
- [ ] What is the crime rate and general perception of the area?

## Legal and compliance

- [ ] Have you instructed a solicitor who understands property investment?
- [ ] Have you received the legal pack for the property (if buying at auction)?
- [ ] Are you registered with the ICO for data protection (if managing yourself)?
- [ ] Do you have an AML compliance process in place (if sourcing for others)?
- [ ] Have you checked your mortgage agreement for any restrictions on letting?
- [ ] Is the building insurance in place (landlord-specific, not standard home insurance)?
- [ ] Do you have a tenancy agreement drafted for the intended use?
- [ ] Have you budgeted for gas safety checks, EICR, and EPC?
- [ ] Do you know the deposit protection requirements?

## After purchase: property setup

- [ ] Is an inventory and check-in report prepared?
- [ ] Are all safety certificates in place before tenants move in?
- [ ] Is the property furnished and equipped for the target tenant?
- [ ] Have you set up utilities for the tenant (or arranged for them to do it)?
- [ ] Have you registered the deposit with a protection scheme?
- [ ] Is the property registered for council tax (or exempt if student)?
- [ ] Have you notified your mortgage lender that the property is let?
- [ ] Is the property registered on the right rental portals or with a letting agent?
- [ ] Do you have a process for dealing with tenant enquiries and emergencies?

## Ongoing management

- [ ] Do you have a system for managing rent collection?
- [ ] Have you set up a maintenance fund for repairs?
- [ ] Do you have a gas safety check reminder system (annual)?
- [ ] Do you have an EICR reminder system (every five years, or annually for HMOs)?
- [ ] Do you have a process for responding to tenant complaints?
- [ ] Have you set aside time for regular property inspections?
- [ ] Do you have a process for dealing with tenant turnover (check-out, deposit return, re-letting)?
- [ ] Are you keeping proper financial records for tax purposes?
- [ ] Have you set up separate bank accounts for rental income?
- [ ] Do you have an accountant who understands property?

## Final thought

A methodical approach to property investment separates professionals from amateurs. Work through this checklist for every deal, every time. The few minutes it takes could save you thousands of pounds and months of stress.

Once a checklist becomes habit, you will make better decisions, move faster on good deals, and walk away from bad ones with confidence.
