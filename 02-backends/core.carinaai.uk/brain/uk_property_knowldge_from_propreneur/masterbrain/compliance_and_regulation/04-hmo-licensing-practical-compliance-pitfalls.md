## The Licensing Trap

Councils are increasingly using the licensing period (4-5 years) as leverage to demand ongoing competence, not just initial compliance. Portsmouth is the leading example -- they expect landlords to demonstrate continuous improvement during the licence period, not just hold the paperwork.

Finance kills more HMO deals than bad tenants. For standard single-family homes, lenders ask for proof of income and a valuation. For HMOs, lenders require proof of: valid licence, fire risk assessment, gas safety, EICR, EPC, planning permission (if Article 4 applies), and building regulations approval for any conversions. Missing any one of these can kill the finance.

## The Four Reports That Kill Sales

Every HMO needs four critical reports. Missing any one can cause a sale to collapse, finance to be rejected, or licence renewal to be denied.

### 1. Fire Risk Assessment
The most legally dangerous. The Responsible Person (usually the landlord or managing agent) must have a documented, up-to-date Fire Risk Assessment. Not just a tick-box exercise -- it must be reviewed regularly and updated when circumstances change. Without it, you can be personally fined or imprisoned if a fire causes injury or death.

Real example: A Hackney 25-bed HMO was delayed 8 months because the Fire Risk Assessment was missing.

### 2. Asbestos Survey
Required for any property built before 2000. The lender will ask for it. If you don't have one, they may freeze the finance. The survey must be a Management Survey (type 2) or Refurbishment/Demolition Survey (type 3) depending on planned works.

Real example: An Oxford serviced accommodation deal collapsed because the asbestos survey was missing.

### 3. Legionella Risk Assessment
Often treated as a "tick box" but can kill a sale. Required for all rented properties with water systems. Must be a written assessment, not just verbal assurance. Needs regular reviews and temperature monitoring records.

Real example: A landlord's sale collapsed two weeks before exchange because the Legionella assessment couldn't be produced.

### 4. Gas Safety Certificate (CP12) + EICR
Gas Safety Certificate must be renewed annually. EICR (Electrical Installation Condition Report) is required every 5 years (or on change of tenancy). These are the most commonly checked documents by councils and lenders, yet landlords still miss renewal dates.

## The Kitchenette Deception

One of the most expensive mistakes in HMO development:

**The rule:** If you put a kitchenette inside a bedroom that already has an ensuite bathroom, you have created a self-contained flat in the eyes of planning, building control, and increasingly, lenders.

This can trigger:
- Requirement for planning permission (which you don't have).
- Building regulations for a change of use (which you haven't applied for).
- Refusal to refinance by lenders.
- Enforcement action by the council.

If a council decides you've created self-contained flats, you may need to:
1. Remove the kitchenettes at your own cost (£20k+ refurb wasted).
2. Apply for retrospective planning permission.
3. Face enforcement notices and fines.

The safe approach: provide communal kitchen/dining facilities and keep bedrooms as bedrooms. If you must put a kitchenette in a bedroom, consult a planning officer BEFORE doing any work.

## Fire Safety as Managerial Responsibility

The Manchester case (public record, now used to train enforcement officers nationally): a landlord was fined per fire door for non-compliance. The key principle is that fire safety is no longer just technical -- it's managerial. You are the Responsible Person.

**What this means practically:**
- You must have documented evidence of all reasonably practicable steps to prevent fire and protect residents.
- "I didn't know" is not a defence.
- Competency requirements are coming -- councils will expect landlords to have formal training in fire safety management.
- The courts will ask about your personal liability, not just your company's.
