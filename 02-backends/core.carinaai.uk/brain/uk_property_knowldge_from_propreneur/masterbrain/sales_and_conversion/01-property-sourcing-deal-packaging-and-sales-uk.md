# Property sourcing: deal packaging, sales, and payment controls

**Updated:** 2026-05-03  
**Kind:** External practice guide (Carina RAG).  
**Source notes:** Property Sourcing Manual (pages 88-100, 119-130). Not legal or tax advice.

## 1. Bespoke sourcing (retainer model)

- Charge an upfront fee (example £1,000 + VAT).
- Contract to deliver a fixed number of on-brief properties (example six) matching investor criteria on location, price, refurb tolerance, strategy.
- If none purchased, contract may still be fulfilled by delivery of qualified opportunities; clarify in writing.
- Finder's fee on completion is commonly **additional** to retainer when a purchase proceeds.

## 2. Core packaged deal workflow

1. Source, view, underwrite, negotiate, secure acceptance in principle where possible.
2. Package into a professional memorandum.
3. **Do not disclose full address or postcode** until contract and fee terms are agreed and documented.

## 3. Example offer email structure

**Subject line pattern:** `[Beds] bed [strategy] in [city] - [price]`

Body elements often include:

- Short condition summary and strategy fit.
- Planning status if relevant; headline refurb estimate and main work packages.
- Achievable rent post-works (pcm).
- GDV or post-refurb value band if applicable.
- Location drivers (transport, employment, amenities).
- Management fee percentage if relevant.
- **Sourcing fee:** £X,XXX + VAT (example phrasing).

Principle: withhold identifying detail that allows circumvention.

## 4. Sales call discipline

- Add value first; solve problems; ask questions.
- **Qualification examples:** current portfolio, financing route, success criteria, timeline, experience with refurbs or HMO licensing.
- You choose counterparties; walk away from misaligned or high-risk buyers.

## 5. Getting paid (controls)

1. **Address release gate:** Finder's fee agreement and terms signed and fee cleared or secured per your policy before full identification data is released.
2. **Client money:** If holding purchaser funds (for example cooling-off holding), use a **designated client account**, not the trading account.
3. **Invoicing:** Use accounting software (examples cited: Xero, QuickBooks, Sage) rather than ad-hoc Word/Excel invoices.
4. **AML and source of funds:**
   - **Cash purchaser:** Recent bank statements; query large unexplained credits with evidence trail (inheritance, asset sale, savings build-up).
   - **Mortgaged purchaser:** Decision in principle or mortgage promise from appropriate lender channel.
   - Verify **origin** of funds, not only presence of balance.

## 6. Upsells after purchase

Existing buyers convert more easily:

- Furnishing and staging packages.
- Tenant find and lettings management.
- Refurbishment project management.

Training material cites illustrative staging margins; treat as anecdotal unless locally costed.

## 7. Training Your Investors - Deal Reservation Rules

Set explicit rules for how investors interact with you and enforce them consistently.

**The text-only deal reservation rule:**
- When a deal email goes out, investors who wish to reserve must TEXT (not call, not email)
- Calls and emails about deal reservations are redirected: "Follow the process in the email"
- Call investors back in the order they texted
- This rule keeps you in control of the process and prevents queue-jumping

**Why this matters:**
- You control your business, not the investor
- Weak sourcers reward bad behaviour (answering calls, ignoring their own rules) - this trains investors to bypass the process
- Consistent enforcement filters time-wasters and rewards serious investors who follow instructions

**The address disclosure gate:**
- Never disclose the full address or postcode before: (1) T&Cs are signed, and (2) reservation fee is received
- This prevents investors from cutting you out by going directly to the estate agent
- An NDA is insufficient protection - it is not worth enforcing and is difficult to prove breach

**The only ways to buy deals from a professional sourcer:**
1. VIP list (paid monthly subscription - first access)
2. Bespoke sourcing (upfront instruction fee, specific search)
3. Standard deal (via the email database, text to reserve process)

**The 7-interaction rule applied to investor training:**
Before an investor buys, they will have engaged with you multiple times. Use the CRM to ensure all new database members go through the Know-Like-Trust sequence automatically before being presented with deals.
