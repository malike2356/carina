# Property sourcing: legal and compliance templates (UK)

**Updated:** 2026-05-03  
**Kind:** External practice guide (Carina RAG).  
**Source notes:** Template agreements and AML pack samples. **Templates are not legal advice.** Have all customer-facing documents reviewed by a solicitor and MLRO before use. Money Laundering Regulations and HMRC supervised-business rules change; follow current guidance.

## 1. Bespoke sourcing reservation agreement (themes)

- **Instruction fee:** often non-refundable (example **£1,200**).
- **Deliverable:** fixed number of opportunities matching agreed criteria (example **six**).
- If buyer completes none, contract may still be fulfilled by delivery of qualified opportunities depending on drafting.

**Buyer obligations (typical themes):** proceed in good time; supply documents promptly; instruct solicitor within agreed days; obtain independent mortgage advice if needed; comply with CDD.

**Company obligations (typical themes):** suitable diligence; physical inspection where promised; rental demand checks; GDPR-aligned handling; disclosure timing tied to contract; refund policy if stated.

**Termination:** cooling-off style window (example **14 days**) with admin deduction (example **£250**); written notice requirements.

## 2. Standard property sourcing reservation agreement (themes)

- Property address may be withheld as "to follow" until fee stage cleared per your policy.
- Agreed purchase price and reservation fee including VAT (example **£2,000**).
- **Due diligence period** (example **14 days**); non-refundable after expiry except where contract allows.
- Refund requests in writing; admin fee (example **£250**) may apply.

**Replacement property triggers (example list):** vendor withdrawal; survey kills viability; legal defect with major cost or delay; RICS down-valuation beyond threshold (example **10%** below agreed).

**AML clause themes:** verify identity and address; third-party electronic checks may leave soft footprint; **Proceeds of Crime Act 2002** reporting duties to the **National Crime Agency** where suspicion exists.

## 3. Anti-money laundering policy (core themes)

**Legal references (non-exhaustive):** Estate Agents Act 1979; **Money Laundering, Terrorist Financing and Transfer of Funds Regulations** (current UK MLRs as amended); HMRC registration for relevant supervised roles.

**Staff vigilance examples:** unusually large cash use; weak source of funds; reluctance to provide ID; complex layering.

**Criminal offence:** failing to report knowledge or suspicion where the statutory tests are met (get training).

**Identification (face-to-face baseline):** one photo ID (passport or driving licence); one proof of address (utility or bank statement within typical **three-month** window); screening against fraud, deceased, sanctions, and PEP databases where proportionate.

**Identification (remote):** certified or high-assurance digital checks per risk; additional address history; overseas ID may need **PRADO**-style authenticity checks where applicable.

**Enhanced due diligence (EDD) triggers (examples):** high-risk client profile; non-face-to-face onboarding; client or funds linked to **high-risk jurisdictions** per UK government, EU delegated lists, FATF public statements, or HM Treasury sanctions and related guidance (lists change; use current official sources); false or stolen documents (SAR pathway); **PEP** exposure; complex or unusual transaction shapes.

**Record keeping:** typically **five years** from end of relationship or transaction completion (confirm current rule set).

**Training:** on joining and at least biennial refreshers are common policies (align to MLRO handbook).

## 4. Risk assessment matrix (guidance style)

| Risk theme | Typical probability band | Example controls |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Customer types (individual vs company) | Medium | CDD depth scaled to risk; Companies House checks |
| Sector attractiveness to fraud | Medium | Vendor-side checks where you touch both parties |
| Overseas clients | Low to medium | Extra ID and address proof; video interview; embassy or professional certification where justified |
| Regulatory change | Low | Annual policy review; MLRO sign-off |
| Property as laundering vehicle | Medium | Source of funds and wealth statements; file notes |

## 5. In-house complaints procedure (themes)

1. Written complaint logged.  
2. Acknowledgement within **three working days** with procedure copy.  
3. Investigation and substantive response within **15 working days** where feasible.  
4. Remedy or rejection with reasoning.  
5. Final viewpoint within **12 working days** of any escalation step if used.  
6. Redress scheme referral window (example **six months** from final letter) if still dissatisfied.

Estate agency work often requires a **redress scheme** membership by law.

## 7. Website privacy policy (GDPR / UK GDPR themes)

Cover at minimum:

- **Collection:** device information and order or enquiry information you actually process.  
- **Use:** fulfil orders or services, communicate with customers, screen for fraud or credit risk where lawful, send marketing only with consent or soft opt-in where valid.  
- **Sharing:** which processor categories (for example payments, analytics) and why.  
- **Analytics:** if you use Google Analytics or similar, name them and link opt-out routes where relevant.  
- **Cookies:** what they do and how to control them.  
- **Behavioural ads:** if used, how to opt out.  
- **Retention:** how long classes of data are kept.  
- **Contact:** how to reach your data controller or DPO if appointed.  
- **European or UK data subjects:** access, correction, update, erasure, objection, restriction, portability, and complaint to supervisory authority (wording must match your legal advice).

## 8. Client onboarding (individual) data points

- Electronic verification reference if used  
- Title, full legal name, phone, nationality, date of birth  
- Home address; own or rent  
- UK resident flag  
- PEP links yes or no  
- Employment block: employer name, address, contact, income band  
- Solicitor and broker contacts  
- Mortgage in principle or cash buyer declaration  
- Signatures for all applicants  

## 9. Client onboarding (corporate entity) data points

- Registered name and trading names  
- Phones  
- Structure: partnership, LLP, limited company  
- SIC codes  
- Country and company number  
- Registered and trading addresses  
- Overseas flag  
- Intended relationship nature  
- Source of funds bank and branch  
- Professional memberships (examples cited: FCA, RICS, ARLA, NAEA, TPO)  
- **UBOs 25%+:** name, DOB, address, role  
- Broker details  
- Mortgage, bridging, or cash flags

## 14. Company client risk assessment (themes)

Assess: introduction route; verification depth; business and sector risks; geography; operational anomalies; client cooperation.

**Overall rating:** LOW, MEDIUM, HIGH (definitions internal).

**Common rule:** relationship under **12 months** may not qualify as LOW in some internal policies.

**HIGH at any stage:** escalate to **MLRO** before proceeding.
