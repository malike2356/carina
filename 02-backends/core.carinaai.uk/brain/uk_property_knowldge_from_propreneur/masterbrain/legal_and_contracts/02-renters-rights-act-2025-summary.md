# Renters' Rights Act 2025 -- Summary for Landlords

**Source:** UK Government Information Sheet 2026
**Effective Date:** 1 May 2026
**Purpose:** Key changes to landlord-tenant law that every UK property professional must know
**Audience:** Carina can advise landlords on how the Renters' Rights Act affects their tenancies, evictions, rent increases, and pet policies

---

## Overview

The Renters' Rights Act 2025 came into effect on 1 May 2026. It introduces significant changes to the private rented sector in England. This document covers only assured and assured shorthold tenancies. It does not apply to social housing or lodgers. The rules apply automatically -- landlords do not need to update tenancy agreements.

If a tenant has no written tenancy agreement or written record of terms, the landlord must provide certain written information on or before 31 May 2026.

## Section 21 Abolished

From 1 May 2026, section 21 ("no fault") evictions are abolished. Landlords can no longer serve a section 21 notice. To evict a tenant, the landlord must have a valid legal reason (a "ground for possession") and use the section 8 process.

If a landlord served a section 8 or section 21 notice BEFORE 1 May 2026, the old rules may still apply to those proceedings.

## Assured Shorthold Tenancies Renamed

ASTs are abolished from 1 May 2026. All existing ASTs automatically become **Assured Periodic Tenancies**. The tenancy does not end because of this change. All tenancies become rolling (periodic) -- fixed terms and set end dates no longer apply.

## All Tenancies Become Periodic

From 1 May 2026, it is not possible for assured tenancy agreements to have a fixed term or set end date. Tenancies continue on a rolling basis (usually monthly). The tenancy continues until:
- Both landlord and tenant agree to end it.
- The tenant gives notice to end it (minimum 2 months, in writing, ending on or before the rent due date).
- The landlord has a valid legal reason under section 8.

## Rent Increases

Rent review clauses in existing tenancy agreements cannot be used for new rent increases after 1 May 2026. Landlords must use the section 13 process:
- Maximum one rent increase per year.
- Minimum 2 months' written notice using Form 4A.
- Increase must be no higher than the open market rent.
- Tenant can challenge above-market increases at the First-tier Tribunal.

## Grounds for Possession (Section 8)

Landlords now need a valid legal reason to evict. Key grounds include:
- Rent arrears (non-payment).
- Antisocial behaviour (by tenant, occupants, or visitors).
- Property not cared for properly (neglect/damage).
- Landlord or family member wants to move in.
- Landlord intends to sell the property.
- Employment-related tenancy ending.
- Temporary or supported accommodation ending.
- Student accommodation (ground 4A -- see below).

Some grounds cannot be used in the first 12 months of a tenancy (e.g. landlord moving in, selling).

At court, the landlord must provide evidence of the valid ground. The tenant can challenge. Free legal advice is available through the Housing Loss Prevention Advice Service.

## Student Tenancies (Ground 4A)

Landlords can evict full-time students at the end of the academic year using ground 4A. Requirements:
- 4 months' notice.
- Notice period must end between 1 June and 30 September.
- Landlord must have previously given written notice that this ground may be used (must be given by 31 May 2026 in most cases).
- The information sheet itself does not count as that written notice.
- For 2025/26 academic year evictions: notice can be served between 1 May and 30 July 2026 with at least 2 months' notice.

## Pet Requests

From 1 May 2026, tenants have the right to request to keep a pet. The landlord:
- Cannot unreasonably refuse.
- Must inform the tenant in writing if they refuse, with reasons.
- Must consider each request on a case-by-case basis.
- The tenant can challenge an unreasonable refusal in court.

## Transitional Provisions

If a notice seeking possession was served before 1 May 2026, the old rules may still apply to that case. Tenants who receive such notices should seek legal advice.

## Key Practical Takeaways for Landlords

1. **No more section 21.** You need a valid legal ground for every eviction. Keep thorough records of rent payments, communications, and any issues.
2. **No more fixed terms.** All tenancies are now rolling/periodic. Plan your portfolio strategy accordingly.
3. **Rent increases require Form 4A and section 13 process.** Rent review clauses in existing contracts are no longer valid.
4. **Document everything.** At court, you must prove the ground for possession. Good record-keeping is essential.
5. **Respond to pet requests properly.** Unreasonable refusal can be challenged in court. Have a clear policy.
6. **Student landlords:** serve the ground 4A written notice by 31 May 2026 if you intend to use it.
7. **Update tenancy agreements** for new tenancies to reflect the new legal framework.
