# Example deal analysis: BRRR HMO case study (Bedford)

**Updated:** 2026-05-03  
**Kind:** External practice guide (Carina RAG).  
**Source notes:** Property Sourcing Manual (pages 94-104). Illustrative numbers from a third-party training narrative; not a live Propreneur deal. Tax positions (for example SDLT) depend on facts at the time; always use qualified advisers.

## Summary

Worked example of a **buy, refurbish, refinance, rent** journey on a **five-bed HMO** created from a semi-detached house. Shows how fear-inducing vendor disclosures, funding stack complexity, and planning context can change outcomes versus a naive spreadsheet.

## Property and negotiation

- **Address (as in source material):** 5 Caister Road, Bedford MK41 0DF.
- **Product:** Semi-detached converted to licensed 5-bed HMO (high finish positioning in narrative).
- **Asking:** £220,000.
- **Agreed purchase:** £210,000 (lower offer £180,000 rejected in story).
- **Vendor scare tactic:** Large contractor quote (cited **£29,805 + VAT**) for structural rectification from prior tenant damage; many viewers walked away.
- **Lesson:** Second opinion from structural surveyor vs accepting a single inflated quote; professional judgement on scope and pricing.

## Purchase and finance stack (training figures)

| Cost item | Amount (narrative) |

| Purchase price | £210,000 |

**Funding mix described:**

- Bridging gross £117,000; day-one net after deductions about **£100,149**.
- Private lender **£45,000** at **1% per month**.
- Internal group loan **£66,876**.
- **Total cash/debt to complete purchase** about **£212,025** in narrative.

## Refurbishment

- **Budget:** £60,000 planned.
- **Actual:** £68,000.
- **Overrun drivers:** Material inflation period (COVID-era reference in source) and a contractor liquidation mid-project.
- **Lesson:** Contingency line and replacement contractor plan are core risk controls.

## Outcome vs initial thesis

- **Money in:** About £212k purchase stack + £68k refurb ≈ **£280k** deployed.
- **Initial GDV expectation:** About £320k to £350k (narrative says "not amazing on paper").
- **RICS refinance valuation achieved:** **£400,000** in story.
- **Value driver cited:** High-spec licensed 5-bed HMO in patch where **Article 4** direction had recently constrained new HMO supply, supporting existing asset pricing.
- **Rent:** Five rooms at **£650 pcm** each ≈ **£3,250 pcm** gross.
- **Refinance:** Lender offered **75% LTV** on a **£350,000** valuation in the narrative (investor chose lower leverage than maximum valuation), releasing about **£260,000** after fees to repay bridge and private loan.
- **Residual story claims:** About **£2,000 pcm** net after costs and roughly **£90,000** equity left in the deal.

## Sourcer fee band (illustrative)

Training material suggests a sourcer could charge roughly **£4,000 to £6,000** for introducing such a deal, compared to investor cash flow and equity created.

## Takeaways for Carina-style reasoning

- Paper thin margin deals can work when **planning moats**, **professional verification**, and **execution quality** shift value.
- Always separate **vendor-provided quotes** from **independent survey and tendered pricing**.
- Funding stacks compound risk (rate, fees, exit timing); stress-test refinance exit before commitment.
