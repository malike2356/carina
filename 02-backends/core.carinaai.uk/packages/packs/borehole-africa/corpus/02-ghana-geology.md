# Ghana Geology for Borehole Drilling

## Major Geological Provinces

### Birimian Belt (Ashanti, Western, Eastern, Brong-Ahafo)

Metamorphic rocks: greenstone, schist, phyllite, granite intrusions.
Typical drilling: hard to very hard; slow penetration in fresh granite.
Water strikes often in fractured zones and weathered regolith (saprolite).
Typical depths to water: 25-80m depending on topography.

### Voltaian Basin (Northern, Upper East, Upper West)

Sedimentary sandstones, shales, mudstones.
Generally easier drilling than Birimian; some sticky shale sections.
Water quality can include iron and manganese from reducing conditions.

### Coastal Plain (Greater Accra, Central, parts of Western)

Sedimentary and unconsolidated deposits; laterite cap common.
Shallow water table in some areas; saline intrusion risk near coast.
Laterite is abrasive on bits; casing required through unconsolidated layers.

### Togo Series (Volta Region)

Metamorphic and volcanic rocks along eastern border.
Mixed drilling conditions; fault zones may carry groundwater.

## Common Formation Codes (ABBIS)

- `laterite`: red-brown iron-rich clay; upper 5-15m common
- `alluvial`: river valley sand and gravel; good yield potential
- `granite`: hard crystalline; slow drilling; fracture water
- `schist`: foliated metamorphic; moderate hardness
- `phyllite`: softer than schist; can swell when wet
- `sandstone`: Voltaian; may be porous aquifer

## Depth Guidelines

Community boreholes in Ghana typically target 45-90m total depth.
CWSA projects often specify minimum 45m with productive aquifer interval.
