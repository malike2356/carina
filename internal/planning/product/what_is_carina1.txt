What is Carina?
The full picture.
Everything built.
Everything live.
Carina is not an AI agent.
Calling Carina an AI agent
is like calling an aircraft carrier a boat.
The word is
technically correct and entirely misses
the point.
Hermes is an AI agent.
Openclaw is an AI agent.
Odysseus is an AI
agent.
They are excellent at what they do.
They take prompts, they run tools,
they produce output.
They live in a terminal.
They are tools for builders.
Carina is something different.
Carina is the first
autonomous agent platform.
Not an agent.
A platform upon which
agents operate, governed by
security infrastructure that no other
agent possesses, connected to
a standalone security product that
monitors everything those agents do.
Backed by a forensics pipeline
with cryptographic chain of custody,
surrounded by 72 integrated
offensive and defensive security
tools, observable through a web dashboard,
controllable through a
TUI and CLI, accessible
from desktop and mobile, extensible
through a public skill hub,
deployable in one command.
Via Docker and importable
from every competing ecosystem in
30 seconds with zero data loss.
That is not an AI agent.
That is a category.
When Carina is
fully implemented, a developer
types Carina import from Hermes
and watches as 2 years of chat history,
51 custom skills,
12 cron jobs, and
every provider configuration migrate
into Carina in the time it takes to
drink a coffee.
Nothing is lost.
The Hermes terminal they have known for years
is now running inside Carina.
But now, when that developer
asks Carina to scan a domain,
they do not get a text summary.
They get a map running
57 service detection probes
across 6,000 ports while Nuclei
simultaneously fires
2,000 CVE templates at every
discovered endpoint.
The results do not scroll past in a
terminal window.
They materialize as structured findings
in a web dashboard with CVSS
scores, exploit availability
indicators, and one-click investigation
buttons.
That developer has just learned what Carina
is. But they have
not seen Scout.
Scout is running continuously in the background.
It has been
scanning their domains every week since
they connected them.
It discovered 3 subdomains they did not
know existed.
It flagged a critical CVE on their
production Jinx server.
It sent an alert to their phone.
When they click investigate,
Carina opens a case in their obsidian
vault. The investigation
note has YAML front matter with
the CVE ID, CVSS
score, affected targets, and
a blank timeline waiting to be filled.
Carina suggests
the exploit module.
The operator approves.
Metasploit fires.
The exploit succeeds.
Carina captures
the meterpreter session, dumps the
evidence to the case folder, crosslinks
the target profile, appends
the timeline, and updates the Scout
dashboard. The finding
moves from open to confirmed to
remediated. The operator
did not write a single case note.
The vault wrote itself.
Now imagine that
same workflow running for a CISO at a
company with 40 developers,
all using different AI agents.
Some use Hermes.
Some use Openclaw.
One team built their own
wrapper around a raw API.
The CISO does not care
which agent anyone uses.
Scout monitors all of them.
Every domain.
Every deployment.
Every exposed port.
Every CVE.
The CISO opens their
Scout dashboard and sees a single
number. The Scout score.
92.
It was 95
last week.
Three new critical findings dropped it.
They click through to see
which team is responsible.
They generate a compliance report
mapped to SOC2 and ISO
27001 controls.
The report
is 70 pages of automated evidence,
every finding time-stamped
with a cryptographic hash, every
action logged in an immutable audit trail.
The auditors will love it.
The CISO did
not run a single command.
Scout ran 25 automated
tools this week.
The CISO just read the output.
That is Scout.
That is what no other agent
has.
