# 91 - UK Property Investor Compliance and HMRC

## Purpose
A practical guide to the compliance obligations that UK property investors must meet. Covers HMRC filing, Making Tax Digital, company secretarial obligations, director loan tax, and HMRC's Connect system. Written for Carina to help investors understand their obligations and take appropriate action. Not tax advice - Carina should recommend a qualified accountant for specific guidance.

---

## 1. Why Compliance Is Non-Negotiable

The compliance environment for UK property investors is tightening every year. HMRC has more tools, more data, and more enforcement resources than ever before. The response to this is not panic - it is to run your property business properly, file everything correctly and on time, and treat it as the business it is.

Getting compliance right from the start costs far less than fixing it later. Penalties, interest, back-taxes, and professional corrective fees can easily exceed the cost of doing it properly for a decade.

---

## 2. HMRC Connect - The Data Integration System

**What it is:**
HMRC Connect is a large-scale data integration system that collects information from multiple sources, links it together, and flags inconsistencies with taxpayer records.

**What it collects:**
- Bank accounts and transactions (via banking data requirements)
- Credit card transactions
- Letting agent records (agencies must report landlord income to HMRC)
- Estate agent records
- Land Registry (every property transaction is visible to HMRC)
- Companies House data (directors, shareholders, registered office)
- International data sharing (under Common Reporting Standard agreements)
- Social media scraping (lifestyle signals, asset ownership signals)

**What Connect looks for:**
- Property ownership at Land Registry with no corresponding rental income declaration
- Lifestyle (visible assets, travel, spending) inconsistent with declared income
- Director shareholder loan balances
- Transactions between connected companies
- Non-filing landlords

**Real-world consequence:**
A landlord who had not filed tax returns on rental properties for nearly a decade was identified by Connect matching their Land Registry ownership records against self-assessment returns. HMRC can look back up to 20 years in cases of deliberate non-disclosure. The penalties for non-disclosure can be 100% of the tax owed in addition to the tax itself.

**The right response:**
File everything, file it correctly, and file it on time. If you have historic non-filing, engage a specialist accountant to make a voluntary disclosure. Voluntary disclosures attract lower penalties than HMRC-initiated investigations.

## 3. Self-Assessment - Personal Landlords

Every individual who receives rental income must file a self-assessment tax return, even if no tax is due.

**Registration:** If you receive rental income and are not already in self-assessment, you must register with HMRC by 5 October following the end of the tax year in which income was first received. Failure to register is itself a penalty risk.

**Annual deadlines:**
- 31 October: paper return deadline
- 31 January: online return deadline
- 31 January: payment of tax due

**What to declare:**
- Gross rental income (all rent received)
- Allowable expenses (agent fees, repairs, insurance, mortgage interest credit under Section 24, utilities if included in rent, professional fees, travel directly related to the rental business)
- Any capital gains on disposal of properties (separate CGT computation)

## 4. Section 24 - Mortgage Interest Relief Restriction

**What it does:**
Personal landlords cannot deduct mortgage interest as a business expense from rental income. Instead, they receive a 20% basic rate tax credit on interest paid.

**Full implementation since April 2020.**

**Impact by tax bracket:**

| Tax bracket | Old treatment | Section 24 treatment | Annual difference on £12,000 interest |
|---|---|---|---|
| Basic rate (20%) | £2,400 deduction | £2,400 credit | £0 - no change |
| Higher rate (40%) | £4,800 deduction | £2,400 credit | £2,400 extra tax |
| Additional rate (45%) | £5,400 deduction | £2,400 credit | £3,000 extra tax |

**Note:** Section 24 does not apply to limited companies - they can still fully deduct mortgage interest as a business expense. This is one of the primary drivers of the shift toward limited company ownership for higher rate taxpayer landlords.

## 5. Making Tax Digital (MTD)

**What it is:**
MTD is a government programme requiring digital record-keeping and digital submission of tax information to HMRC.

**MTD for Income Tax Self-Assessment (ITSA):**
- April 2026: mandatory for individuals with total gross income (from self-employment and/or property) over £50,000
- April 2027: mandatory for individuals with total gross income over £30,000
- Landlords below these thresholds: voluntary for now, mandatory dates to be confirmed

**What MTD requires:**
- Keep digital records (in HMRC-compatible accounting software or spreadsheet with bridging software)
- Submit quarterly updates to HMRC (not quarterly payment - just digital updates of income and expenses)
- Submit an end-of-period statement
- Submit a final declaration (replacing the annual self-assessment return)

**HMRC's long-term direction:**
HMRC's stated objective is to move from a "push" system (you send data to HMRC) toward a "pull" system where HMRC can interrogate your records directly. While this is not yet law, getting digital accounting systems set up now is the correct response.

**Practical implication:**
Property investors should be using digital accounting software (Xero, QuickBooks, FreeAgent, or similar) rather than spreadsheets or paper records, ahead of mandatory MTD implementation.

## 6. Limited Company - Annual Compliance Obligations

Every limited company must meet these annual requirements regardless of turnover:

| Obligation | Deadline | Who files | Cost |
|---|---|---|---|
| Corporation Tax return (CT600) | 12 months after accounting period end | Accountant + HMRC online | Part of accountancy fee |
| Corporation Tax payment | 9 months and 1 day after accounting period end | Director or accountant | Tax due |
| Annual accounts (Companies House) | 9 months after accounting period end (private companies) | Accountant | Part of accountancy fee |
| Confirmation statement | Within 14 days of anniversary of incorporation | Director | £34 HMRC fee |
| Self-assessment for director | 31 January annually | Director (personal) | Part of accountancy fee |

**Estimated annual accountancy cost:** £800-£2,500 for a straightforward property holding company, depending on transaction volume and accountant.

## 7. Company Secretarial - Articles of Association

**The Model Articles problem:**
Most companies are set up using government template "model articles of association." These contain a provision that the minimum number of directors needed to form a quorum for decision-making is one. However, separate case law (notably a 2022 case, and a specific 2024 case in a narrow area) has found that a sole director using model articles acted in an "ultra vires" manner - i.e., made decisions they were not legally empowered to make as a sole director under model articles.

**The practical consequence:**
At least one major mortgage lender has refused to proceed with a loan shortly before completion because the borrower's company had a sole director and unmodified model articles. The lender required the articles to be amended before drawdown.

**The fix:**
Amend the articles of association to explicitly permit a sole director to make all decisions. This is a legal document change that requires specialist drafting and filing at Companies House. Cost: £300-£800 depending on the firm. Any property investor operating as a sole director in a company using model articles should get this done.

**Group structure articles:**
If operating a group structure, each company needs articles drafted to reflect the group relationship and appropriate shareholder/director decision-making rights. Standard templates are not suitable.

## 8. Director Loans and Section 455 Tax

When a director takes money out of a limited company without going through payroll or declaring a dividend, it creates a director's loan. This is a loan from the company to the director.

**Section 455 tax:**
If a director's loan balance is outstanding more than 9 months after the end of the company's accounting period, the company must pay Section 455 tax equal to 33.75% of the outstanding balance to HMRC. This is a temporary tax - it is refunded when the loan is eventually repaid - but it creates a significant cash flow burden.

**Common situation:**
A director takes money from the company informally throughout the year (transfers to personal account, company pays personal bills). At year end, the accountant finds the director's loan account is overdrawn. If not resolved, Section 455 tax is due.

**The fix:**
Pay the money back to the company before the 9-month deadline, or declare a dividend (if there are sufficient distributable reserves) to convert the loan to a dividend - noting dividend tax will then apply.

**HMRC scrutiny:**
Director/company transactions are now a specific section in corporation tax returns and are being closely monitored. Keep clean records of everything the company pays for or receives on behalf of a director.

## 9. Capital Gains Tax (CGT) on Disposal

When you sell an investment property (not your main home) you must pay Capital Gains Tax on the profit.

**2025 rates:**
- Basic rate taxpayers: 18% on residential property gains
- Higher rate taxpayers: 24% on residential property gains
- Annual exempt amount: £3,000 (down from £12,300 in 2022)

**Reporting:**
- Residential property CGT must be reported and paid within 60 days of completion (not the annual self-assessment return)
- Use the HMRC UK Property account online service

**Companies:**
Companies pay Corporation Tax (not CGT) on gains from property disposals. No annual exempt amount. Indexation allowance (which tracked inflation) was abolished for new periods from January 2018.

## 10. SDLT - Stamp Duty Land Tax

See knowledge file 87 for the full SDLT table. Key compliance points:

- SDLT must be reported and paid within 14 days of completion
- Your solicitor typically handles this, but ultimate legal responsibility is yours
- Investment property (second or additional property) incurs a 5% surcharge (increased from 3% in October 2024) on top of standard rates
- First-time buyers buying personally: no surcharge; standard residential rates apply (check current thresholds as these change with Budgets)
- Companies buying: pay SDLT at standard rates plus the 5% surcharge - no first-time buyer relief
- Reliefs available: multiple dwellings relief (MDR), group relief, charity relief - get these checked at the point of purchase, not after

## 11. Non-Resident Landlord Scheme

Non-UK residents receiving UK rental income must register for the Non-Resident Landlords (NRL) Scheme.

**Without NRL registration:**
Letting agents must deduct 20% basic rate tax from rent before paying it to the non-resident landlord.

**With NRL approval:**
HMRC authorises the letting agent to pay rent gross; the non-resident landlord then declares the income in a UK self-assessment return.

**Non-resident CGT:**
Since 2015 for residential property and 2019 for commercial property, non-residents pay UK CGT on disposals of UK property. Must be reported within 60 days of completion.

**Statutory Residence Test:**
Days spent in the UK count toward residency status under a complex multi-factor test. Even transit stops overnight can count. Anyone aiming for non-resident status must take specialist advice before spending any nights in the UK above the permitted thresholds.

## 12. Building a Compliant Property Business

Practical steps:
1. Register for self-assessment with HMRC from the first year of rental income
2. Use digital accounting software from day one (Xero, QuickBooks, FreeAgent)
3. Keep all receipts and invoices (digitally where possible)
4. Engage a qualified property accountant - not just any accountant; one who specialises in property
5. If operating a limited company: keep director/company transactions clean, avoid informal withdrawals, declare dividends properly with board minutes
6. Get articles of association reviewed if operating as a sole director under model articles
7. Have a corporation tax return AND a personal self-assessment filed every year
8. Monitor the MTD mandatory threshold and prepare before the deadline
9. Report CGT within 60 days of any property disposal; do not wait for the annual return
