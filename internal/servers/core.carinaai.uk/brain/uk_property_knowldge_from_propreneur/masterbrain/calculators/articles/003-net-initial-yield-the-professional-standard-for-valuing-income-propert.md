# Net Initial Yield: The Professional Standard for Valuing Income Property

Published: Mon, 25 May 2026 09:00:00 +0000
Category: Calculations
Slug: net-initial-yield-the-professional-standard-for-valuing-income-propert

---

If you have ever looked at a commercial property listing, you will have seen something called the Net Initial Yield, or NIY. It looks similar to the residential net yield. But it is not quite the same thing.

NIY is the RICS-approved way of measuring the income return on a commercial property. It uses the current passing rent -- the rent the tenant is actually paying right now -- not the estimated rental value or what you hope to achieve after refurbishment.

The calculation is simple enough: take the passing rent, subtract the purchaser's costs (stamp duty, legal fees, agent fees), divide by the purchase price, and multiply by 100. The inclusion of purchaser's costs makes it more conservative than a standard net yield.

Here is the important difference. In residential property, yield is a quick comparison tool. In commercial property, NIY is a valuation method. A valuer will look at a property with a passing rent of £50,000 and say, the market NIY for this type of asset in this location is 7%, so the capital value is roughly £714,000.

Commercial yields move inversely to capital values. When NIY goes down, capital values go up. Over the past decade, commercial NIYs have compressed significantly as investors chased income. A multi-let industrial estate that might have traded at 9% ten years ago might trade at 6% today.

If you are moving into commercial property, learn to think in NIY terms. It changes how you value buildings and how you negotiate.

**The formula**

```
Net Initial Yield (NIY) (%) = ((Passing Rent - Operating Costs) / Capital Value) × 100
```

**Why this matters**

NIY is the language of commercial property. If you cannot calculate it, you cannot value a commercial building, and you cannot negotiate with a commercial seller. It is one of the first metrics any commercial agent or lender will quote.
