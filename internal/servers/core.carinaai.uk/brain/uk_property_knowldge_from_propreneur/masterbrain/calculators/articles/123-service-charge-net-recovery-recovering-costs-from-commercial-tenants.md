# Service Charge Net Recovery: Recovering Costs from Commercial Tenants

Published: Mon, 19 Jul 2027 09:00:00 +0000
Category: Calculations
Slug: service-charge-net-recovery-recovering-costs-from-commercial-tenants

---

In commercial property, the landlord typically provides services like cleaning, security, heating, and maintenance to common areas. The cost of these services is recovered from tenants through the service charge. The net recovery rate measures how much of the cost is actually collected.\n\nFor a fully let building with a £100,000 annual service charge budget and 100% occupancy, the service charge is fully recovered from tenants. If one tenant is delinquent or the property has 10% void space, only 90% of the service charge is recovered. The landlord pays the remaining 10%.\n\nThe net recovery rate is the amount actually collected divided by the total service charge cost. A 100% recovery rate means the tenants cover all costs. Anything below 90% means the landlord is subsidising the building's operating costs.\n\nThe service charge is not profit. It is a cost recovery mechanism. But if you under-estimate the service charge budget, you will under-recover and the shortfall comes out of your net income.\n
When underwriting a commercial purchase, review the historical service charge accounts. If the budget has been consistently under-estimated, the shortfall has been coming out of the landlord's pocket. Adjust your income projections accordingly.

**The formula**

```
Net Service Charge = Gross Service Charge Expenditure - Tenant Recoveries
```

**Why this matters**

Service charge net recovery should ideally be 100% for fully let properties. If the recovery rate is below 90%, the landlord is subsidising operating costs. Review historical service charge accounts before buying.
