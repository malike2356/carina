# Commercial Gross Yield: How to Calculate Returns on Commercial Property

Published: Mon, 07 Jun 2027 09:00:00 +0000
Category: Calculations
Slug: commercial-gross-yield-how-to-calculate-returns-on-commercial-property

---

Commercial property yields are calculated differently from residential. The numbers are lower but the income is more secure because commercial tenants sign longer leases. A shop with a ten-year lease is worth more than a residential tenant on a rolling tenancy.\n\nCommercial gross yield is the passing rent divided by the purchase price. If a retail unit in Portsmouth has a passing rent of £25,000 and costs £417,000, the gross yield is 6%. That is competitive for the South Coast.\n\nThe difference between commercial and residential yield is the risk premium. A commercial property yielding 6% with a strong tenant on a ten-year lease is generally safer than a residential BTL yielding 6% with rolling tenancies. The yield is the same but the risk is lower.\n\nCommercial yields vary by sector and location. Prime London offices might yield 3.5% to 4.5%. Regional offices might yield 6% to 8%. Industrial estates might yield 5% to 7%. Retail depends heavily on location, with prime high street at 4% to 6% and secondary retail at 7% to 10%.\n
On the South Coast, typical commercial yields range from 4.5% for prime Portsmouth city centre to 8% for secondary industrial estates. Know the market before you buy.

**The formula**

```
Gross Yield (%) = (Passing Rent Annual / Purchase Price) × 100
```

**Why this matters**

Commercial gross yield of 4.5% to 8% is typical on the South Coast. The yield reflects the risk and lease length. A lower yield with a strong tenant on a long lease can be better value than a higher yield with risk.
