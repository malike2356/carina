# BMV Discount Amount: How Much Below Market Value Are You Actually Getting?

Below Market Value is one of the most misused terms in property. A property is only BMV if it is genuinely priced below what similar properties have recently sold for, not what the agent hopes it will sell for.\n\nThe discount amount is the difference between the market value and your purchase price. If a property is worth £200,000 and you buy it for £160,000, your discount amount is £40,000.\n\nThe critical question is: how do you know the market value? Relying on the estate agent's valuation is dangerous because agents often overvalue to win instructions. You need comparable recent sales of similar properties in the same area.\n\nIn Portsmouth, a genuine BMV property might offer a £20,000 to £50,000 discount depending on the seller's motivation. A typical BMV discount is 15% to 25% below market value. Below 15%, it is a good deal but not a BMV play. Above 25%, you have found something special.\n
The discount amount determines what is possible. A £40,000 discount gives you room to refinance and pull cash out. A £15,000 discount might still be a good purchase but will not support a no-money-down strategy.

**The formula**

```
Discount Amount = Market Value - Purchase Price
```

**Why this matters**

BMV discount should be at least 15% to 20% below genuine market value. Below 15%, it is a good deal but not a true BMV. Above 25%, you have a potential no-money-down opportunity.
