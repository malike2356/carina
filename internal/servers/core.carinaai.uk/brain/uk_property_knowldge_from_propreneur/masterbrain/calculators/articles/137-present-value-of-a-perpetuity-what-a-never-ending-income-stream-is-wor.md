# Present Value of a Perpetuity: What a Never-Ending Income Stream Is Worth

A perpetuity is an income stream that continues forever. In property, a perpetuity approximates a property that you hold indefinitely and never sell, collecting rent year after year. The present value of a perpetuity is the annual income divided by the discount rate.\n\nIf a property generates £12,000 in net income and your required return is 6%, the present value of the perpetual income stream is £12,000 divided by 0.06, which equals £200,000. That is what the property is worth if you hold it forever and never sell.\n\nThe perpetuity formula is the foundation of the investment method. Commercial valuations use it implicitly. The capitalisation rate is the discount rate applied to a perpetual income stream.\n\nThe limitation is that properties do not generate constant income forever. Rents change, costs change, and buildings eventually need major refurbishment. But as a simplifying assumption, the perpetuity formula gives a quick approximate value.\n
For a quick valuation, use the perpetuity formula. It gives you the investment value assuming constant income. Adjust up or down based on whether you expect income to grow or decline.

**The formula**

```
PV = CF / r
```

**Why this matters**

The perpetuity formula is the basis of the investment method. Income divided by discount rate equals value. It provides a quick approximate value assuming constant income forever.
