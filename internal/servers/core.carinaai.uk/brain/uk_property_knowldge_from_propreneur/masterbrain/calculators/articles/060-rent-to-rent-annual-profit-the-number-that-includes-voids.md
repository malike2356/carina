# Rent-to-Rent Annual Profit: The Number That Includes Voids

Published: Wed, 09 Dec 2026 09:00:00 +0000
Category: Calculations
Slug: rent-to-rent-annual-profit-the-number-that-includes-voids

---

Monthly profit assumes every room is full every month. Annual profit adjusts for voids. And in rent-to-rent, voids are your biggest risk because you still pay the landlord even when rooms are empty.

If your monthly profit is £350 and you have two months with one empty room (and that room represents 25% of your tenant income), your annual profit is (10 months at £350) plus (2 months at £162.50). That is £3,500 plus £325, which equals £3,825.

But if you have two months where two rooms are empty (50% of income lost), your calculation changes significantly. This is the risk of rent-to-rent. When the property is full, it looks great. When rooms are empty, the losses mount quickly because your landlord cost is fixed.

The solution is to build a void buffer. Set aside one to two months of your landlord rent as a reserve before you take any profit. This covers you during void periods when you are paying the landlord from your own pocket.

Professional R2R operators budget for 10% to 15% voids annually. If your actual voids exceed 15%, your business model has a structural problem. Either the property is not in demand, or your pricing is wrong.

**The formula**

```
Annual Profit = (Monthly Profit × 12) - (Subtenant Income g × Void Months)
```

**Why this matters**

Annual rent-to-rent profit must account for voids. Budget 10% to 15% vacancy rate. If voids exceed 15%, your business model has a problem. Build a void reserve before you take any profit.
