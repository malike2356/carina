# Development Finance: Funding Property Development Projects

If you are planning to build new homes, convert a property into multiple units, or undertake significant renovation work, standard buy-to-let mortgages will not be suitable. Development finance is designed for these projects.

This guide covers the types of development finance available, how they work, and what lenders look for.

## What is development finance

Development finance is funding secured against a property or land for the purpose of building, converting, or substantially renovating. It differs from standard mortgages in several important ways:

- Funds are released in stages (drawdowns) as the project progresses
- Interest is typically rolled up and added to the loan, rather than paid monthly
- The loan term matches the project timeline, usually 6 to 24 months
- The lender assesses the project viability, not just the borrower's income

Development finance is more expensive than standard mortgages because the risk is higher. The lender is relying on the project completing successfully to repay the loan.

## Types of development finance

### Ground-up development finance

This funds building new homes on land you own or are purchasing. The lender typically advances up to 60% to 70% of the land value and up to 100% of the build costs, subject to a maximum loan-to-cost (LTC) ratio of around 65% to 75%.

Funds are drawn down in stages aligned with the construction milestones: foundations, wall plate, roofing, first fix, second fix, and completion. A surveyor visits the site at each stage to verify progress before the next drawdown is released.

### Conversion finance

This covers projects where you convert an existing building into residential units. Examples include converting a commercial property into flats, subdividing a large house into multiple units, or converting a pub into homes.

The LTC is typically 65% to 75%, and the loan structure mirrors ground-up development with staged drawdowns.

### Refurbishment finance

This is similar to development finance but for lighter refurbishment projects. If you are buying a property that needs significant but not structural work, refurbishment finance can fund the purchase and the works.

These loans are often structured as bridging loans with a refurbishment element. The key difference from a standard bridging loan is that the lender releases additional funds for the works as they progress.

### Ground-up vs conversion vs refurbishment

The key distinction is the level of complexity:

- **Ground-up development:** Highest complexity, longest timeline, highest returns but highest risk
- **Conversion:** Medium complexity, requires planning permission and building regulations compliance
- **Refurbishment:** Lower complexity, faster timeline, lower returns but lower risk

## How lenders assess development projects

Development lenders focus on three things:

### The project viability

The lender will commission a detailed appraisal of the project, including:

- Gross development value (GDV): the estimated value of the completed scheme
- Total development costs: land, construction, fees, finance costs
- Profit margin: the GDV minus total costs, expressed as a percentage

Most lenders want to see a profit margin of at least 15% to 20% of GDV, or 20% to 25% of total costs. If the margin is lower than this, the project does not leave enough safety buffer if costs overrun or the sales value drops.

### The experience level

Lenders prefer borrowers with a track record. If this is your first development project, you may need to:

- Use a contractor with a proven track record
- Bring in an experienced development partner
- Provide a larger equity contribution (30% to 40% rather than 25%)
- Accept a higher interest rate

The lender wants confidence that you can manage the project to completion.

### The exit strategy

How will the loan be repaid? Common exits include:

- Sale of the completed units (most common)
- Refinancing onto a standard mortgage or portfolio facility
- A combination of both (sell some units, refinance others)

The lender needs to be convinced the exit is realistic and achievable within the loan term.

## Costs of development finance

Development finance is more expensive than standard mortgages. Typical costs include:

- Interest rate: 0.6% to 1.2% per month
- Arrangement fee: 1% to 2% of the loan amount
- Monitoring surveyor fees: GBP 500 to GBP 1,500 per visit
- Legal fees: GBP 1,000 to GBP 3,000
- Exit fee: sometimes 0.5% to 1% of the loan

For a 12-month development loan of GBP 500,000 at 0.8% per month, the total costs could be:

- Interest: GBP 48,000 (rolled up)
- Arrangement fee: GBP 7,500
- Surveyor and legal fees: GBP 4,000
- Total: approximately GBP 59,500

This is why developers need a sufficiently large profit margin to cover the finance costs.

## Equity and mezzanine finance

For larger projects, you may need additional funding beyond what a senior development lender will provide. Mezzanine finance fills the gap between the senior debt and your equity.

Mezzanine lenders charge higher rates (1.2% to 2% per month) and typically take a secondary charge on the property. The combined senior and mezzanine debt usually cannot exceed 80% to 85% of the project cost.

## Common mistakes

- **Underestimating costs:** Always add a 10% to 15% contingency to your budget.
- **Overestimating sales values:** The market moves during a development. Stress-test your numbers with lower sales prices.
- **Delaying the timeline:** Every month of delay adds finance costs. Build realistic timelines and stick to them.
- **Ignoring planning risk:** Do not start a development without planning permission in place, or at least a clear path to it.

## Final thought

Development finance is a powerful tool for investors who want to move beyond buying standing stock. The key is to underwrite conservatively, build realistic timelines, and work with lenders who understand development rather than those who just understand property.

A well-structured development project with proper finance can deliver significantly higher returns than standard buy-to-let. But it needs careful planning and professional advice.
