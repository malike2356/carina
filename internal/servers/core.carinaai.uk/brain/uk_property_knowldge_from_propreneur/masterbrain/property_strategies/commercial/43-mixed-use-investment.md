# Mixed-Use Property Investment: Retail Below, Residential Above

One of the most interesting opportunities in commercial property investment is the mixed-use building: retail or commercial space on the ground floor with residential accommodation above. These properties are common on high streets across Portsmouth, Southampton, and the Isle of Wight, yet many investors overlook them because they fall between the residential and commercial categories.

Mixed-use can offer the best of both worlds. But it must be approached with the right underwriting discipline.

## The structure of mixed-use deals

A typical mixed-use building has a shop, office, or restaurant at ground level with one or more flats on the upper floors. The income comes from both the commercial unit and the residential units, creating a diversified income stream from a single asset.

The total yield is a blend of the commercial and residential returns. A commercial unit yielding 8% combined with residential flats yielding 5% produces an overall return somewhere between the two, depending on the proportion of each.

For example, a building in Southsea with a ground-floor retail unit generating GBP 15,000 per annum and two one-bedroom flats above generating GBP 12,000 each gives a total income of GBP 39,000. At a purchase price of GBP 500,000, the blended yield is 7.8%.

## Underwrite on the commercial income first

This is the most important principle in mixed-use investment. Always underwrite the deal on the commercial income alone. The residential income is your bonus, not your primary justification.

Why? Because planning risk exists. You may intend to convert the upper floors to residential, but planning permission could be denied, restricted, or delayed. If that happens, the commercial income must be sufficient to make the investment worthwhile.

A deal where the commercial income alone covers your target yield is a safe deal. One that relies on unrealised residential potential is speculative.

This principle is particularly relevant on the South Coast, where local planning authorities have different attitudes to change of use. Portsmouth City Council has supported residential conversions in principle, but each application is assessed on its merits.

## What to look for in a mixed-use building

The best mixed-use opportunities share certain characteristics:

- A strong commercial tenant on a long lease provides stable base income
- The upper floors are currently unused or underused (many are used for storage or are vacant)
- The building is in a location with proven residential demand
- The residential units can be accessed separately from the commercial space
- There is parking or proximity to public transport

We recently evaluated a mixed-use property in Portsmouth where the upper floors were being used for storage by the ground-floor commercial tenant. By negotiating a partial lease surrender, the owner could access that space for conversion to two one-bedroom flats worth GBP 950 per month each in rental income.

## Planning considerations

Permitted development rights for commercial to residential conversion have been expanded in recent years. However, these rights do not apply everywhere, and certain conditions must be met.

Key considerations include:

- Whether the property is in a conservation area (many in Old Portsmouth and Southsea are)
- Whether the upper floors have independent access
- Fire safety requirements for residential use above commercial premises
- Sound insulation and separation between commercial and residential areas

Always have a planning assessment done before committing. A planning consultant familiar with the South Coast area, like those in Portsmouth or Southampton, can give you a realistic view of what is achievable.

## The financing question

Mixed-use properties can be more difficult to finance than purely residential or purely commercial assets. High street lenders often classify them as commercial, meaning you need a commercial mortgage with a larger deposit.

Specialist lenders and bridging finance are common options. Expect to put down 25% to 35% deposit on a mixed-use acquisition.

## Final thought

Mixed-use properties are undervalued and underappreciated. They offer income diversification, value-add potential through upper-floor conversion, and exposure to both the residential and commercial markets. The key discipline is underwriting on the commercial income first and treating the residential upside as the bonus.
