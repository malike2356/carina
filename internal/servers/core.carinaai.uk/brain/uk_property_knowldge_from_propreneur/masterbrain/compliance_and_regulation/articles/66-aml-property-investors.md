# What Property Investors Need to Know About Anti-Money Laundering Compliance

Anti-money laundering (AML) regulations apply to more property transactions in the UK than many investors realise. If you are buying, selling, or letting property, you may have legal obligations that carry significant penalties if ignored.

This guide covers what property investors need to know about AML compliance in 2026.

## Who is regulated

Under the Money Laundering Regulations 2017 (as amended), the following property-related activities are regulated:

- Estate agents and property brokers
- Letting agents (where rent exceeds EUR 10,000 per month)
- Property sourcers and introducers
- Anyone involved in the buying or selling of property on behalf of others
- Solicitors and conveyancers handling property transactions
- Accountants advising on property transactions

The key threshold for letting agents is important. Most residential tenancies in the UK fall below the EUR 10,000 per month threshold, so standard letting agents are often not caught by AML regulation. However, corporate lets, high-value short lets, and serviced accommodation may exceed this threshold.

If you are an investor who sources deals for other investors or acts as an intermediary, you are likely within scope regardless of the transaction value.

## What the obligations look like

If you are a regulated person or business, the main obligations are:

### Customer Due Diligence

You must verify the identity of your clients before proceeding with a transaction. For property transactions, this means:

- Obtaining and verifying photographic ID (passport or driving licence)
- Confirming proof of address (utility bill, bank statement, council tax bill)
- Understanding the source of funds for the transaction
- Identifying the beneficial owner if the client is a company or trust

For property purchases, source of funds checks are critical. You need to understand where the money is coming from. A simple statement from the buyer saying "I have saved it" is not sufficient. You should see evidence: savings statements, property sale completion statements, inheritance documentation, or investment withdrawal confirmations.

### Risk Assessment

Your business must have a written risk assessment that identifies the money laundering risks relevant to your activities. This is not a tick-box exercise. The regulator expects you to have thought about how your specific business could be used for money laundering and what you do to prevent it.

### Policies, Controls, and Procedures

You need documented policies covering:

- How you carry out customer due diligence
- How you monitor transactions
- How you report suspicious activity
- How you train your staff
- How you record and retain documents

### Record Keeping

You must keep copies of identification and due diligence documents for at least five years after the end of your business relationship with the client. This applies even if the transaction did not proceed to completion.

## What happens if you get it wrong

The penalties for non-compliance are severe. HMRC, which supervises estate and letting agents, can:

- Issue fines of up to GBP 5,000 for minor breaches
- Impose unlimited fines for serious breaches through criminal prosecution
- Issue public censure that can damage your reputation
- Disqualify individuals from acting as a company director

In addition to regulatory penalties, failing to carry out proper due diligence can leave you exposed if a property transaction is linked to criminal activity. You could face legal action from buyers, sellers, or law enforcement.

The reality is that HMRC has been increasing enforcement activity in the property sector. Estate agents and property professionals are now regularly audited.

## Practical steps for investors

### If you buy properties directly (no intermediary)

You are not required to verify your own identity to yourself, but you should ensure that your solicitor or conveyancer carries out proper AML checks on you. If they ask for identification, this is normal. Cooperate fully.

### If you use a sourcing agent or introducer

The sourcing agent should carry out AML checks on you as their client. Ask them about their AML process. If they do not have one, that is a red flag. A professional sourcing company like Xelox Properties treats AML compliance as a core part of its service.

### If you source deals for others

You are likely a regulated person under the regulations. You need to register with HMRC for AML supervision, implement the policies described above, and ensure you are checking your investors properly.

### If you let property through an agent

The letting agent should carry out checks on the tenant. You should ask to see that these have been done. If you self-manage, you should check tenant identity yourself and keep records.

## Common mistakes

- Assuming AML only applies to cash transactions: The regulations cover all property transactions regardless of payment method.
- Thinking you are too small to be regulated: There is no size exemption. A sole trader sourcing one deal a year is as regulated as a national agency.
- Relying on your solicitor to handle everything: If you are the regulated person, the obligation is yours. You cannot delegate it away.
- Not keeping records: If HMRC audited you tomorrow, could you produce ID checks for every client you have worked with in the last five years?

## Final thought

AML compliance is not optional. It is a legal requirement that applies to more property investors than most realise. The cost of getting it wrong is far higher than the cost of doing it properly.
