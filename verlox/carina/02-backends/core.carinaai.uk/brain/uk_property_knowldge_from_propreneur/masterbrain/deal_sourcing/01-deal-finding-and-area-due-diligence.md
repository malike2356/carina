# Property sourcing: area due diligence and deal finding

**Updated:** 2026-05-03  
**Kind:** External practice guide (Carina RAG).  
**Source notes:** Area due diligence notes; Property Sourcing Manual extracts. Third-party tools named for education only.

## 1. Goldmine investment patch

Choose a primary geography you can visit and research repeatedly. Training material often suggests about **one hour** travel from base.

**Illustrative city narrative (pre-2017 training example):** A formerly depressed post-industrial city with port employment, culture-led regeneration narrative, and relatively low entry prices. Use as a pattern only: every patch has a different risk stack.

**Principle:** Every area suits some strategies; not every strategy suits every area (for example rent-to-rent may work in one city and underperform in another; serviced accommodation demand differs by micro-market).

## 2. Using PropertyData-style research

**Site example:** `www.propertydata.co.uk`

**Workflow:**

1. Open Research then Local Data (or equivalent menu at time of use).
2. Enter a postcode seed.
3. Expand sample to roughly **2,000 to 3,000** data points rather than an oversized default that dilutes signal.
4. Read dashboards and exports together.

**Metrics to review:**

| Theme | What it signals |
| --- | --- |
| Population and households | Renter pool depth |
| Asking prices by beds | Current seller ambition |
| Sold prices and £/sq ft | Transaction proof points |
| Rents by bed or room | Income underwriting |
| HMO room rates | Double ensuite vs standard room gaps |
| Gross yields | Often cited band around 6% to 7% for vanilla context (market dependent) |
| Price trend | Momentum, mean reversion risk |
| Sales per month | Liquidity; very low counts can aid buyers in negotiation (not always) |
| Demographics | Deprivation, employment, travel, age mix |
| Planning map | Pipeline supply (student blocks, towers) can cap rents or add demand |

**Trend example (illustrative numbers from training):** £/sq ft trough then peak then partial pullback; best buy zones are debated (do not treat a single chart point as automatic timing).

**Sales activity example:** A spike during a stamp duty holiday style event vs a stabilised band (for example 80 to 100 sales per month) vs a thin month (for example 20s) interpreted as buyer leverage opportunity only when combined with stock and seller motivation.

**Planning overlay:** Many small applications vs few very large schemes; high-value schemes may add competing beds for HMO operators.

## 3. Offline immersion

- Letting and sales agent calls.
- Networking and Chamber-style business events.
- Council public sessions where useful.
- Multi-day street time: visible construction, relocations, void commercial, derelict stock.

## 4. Strategy popularity and price bands (training table)

| Strategy | Typical price band cited | Popularity in material |
| --- | --- | --- |
| BRRR / flip | roughly £80k to £200k | Highest |
| HMO | roughly £100k to £300k | Next |
| Vanilla BTL | yield-led rather than fixed band | Steadier, less hype |

**BTL:** training example targets roughly **15% ROI** rule-of-thumb before finance (clarify gross vs net in your own model); prefer employment and amenity drivers (hospitals, schools, industry, retail).

**BRRR:** seek heavy cosmetic obsolescence; dated wallpaper patterns used as a quick visual heuristic for long-unmodernised stock.

**HMO:** universities, hospitals, industrial employment; always check **Article 4** and planning path (`[area] article 4 HMO` style searches).
