# Understanding Property Market Cycles in 2026

Property markets do not move in straight lines. They cycle through phases of growth, peak, decline, and recovery. Understanding where we are in the cycle is one of the most valuable skills for an investor. It informs when to buy, when to sell, when to refinance, and when to hold cash. This article explains the property cycle framework and examines where the UK market - particularly the South Coast - sits in 2026.

## The Four Phases of the Property Cycle

Property markets follow a broadly predictable four-phase cycle, typically spanning 10-18 years in the UK.

**Phase 1: Recovery.** This phase follows a downturn. Prices are stable or rising slowly. Transaction volumes are low. Seller expectations have adjusted to lower values, and buyers are beginning to return. This is historically the best time to buy - competition is low, and prices are at or near the bottom of the cycle.

**Phase 2: Expansion (or Boom).** Prices rise steadily, then accelerate. Transaction volumes increase. Confidence returns, and more buyers enter the market. Lending becomes more available. This phase sees increasing competition for properties, and the gap between asking and selling prices narrows.

**Phase 3: Hypersupply (or Peak).** Price growth continues but at a slowing rate. Supply begins to outstrip demand as more sellers list properties to capitalise on high prices. Buyer fatigue sets in. This is the most dangerous time for inexperienced investors - it is tempting to buy at the peak, believing the growth will continue indefinitely.

**Phase 4: Recession (or Correction).** Prices begin to fall. Transaction volumes drop sharply. Negative sentiment dominates. Lending tightens. This phase can last several years, and it is psychologically difficult for investors who bought near the peak.

## Where We Are in 2026

The UK property market experienced a strong boom phase from 2020 to mid-2022, driven by the stamp duty holiday, low interest rates, and the race for space following the pandemic. By late 2022, rising interest rates began to slow the market. 2023 saw price corrections across much of the UK, with some areas experiencing 5-10% falls from peak.

Through 2024 and 2025, the market has been in a recovery phase. Prices have stabilised, transaction volumes are slowly returning, and interest rates have edged down from their peak. As of early 2026, the market appears to be transitioning from recovery towards early expansion. This is generally a favourable time for investors - prices are still below peak in many areas, competition is manageable, and confidence is building.

## Regional Variations: The South Coast

Property cycles are not uniform across the country. The South Coast has its own dynamics influenced by local economic conditions, housing supply, and demographic trends.

**Portsmouth.** The city has seen steady recovery since the 2023 correction. Prices in late 2025 averaged roughly 5% below the 2022 peak in real terms. Areas such as Southsea, which benefited most from the pandemic boom, experienced a sharper correction and are now showing signs of renewed buyer interest. Affordable terraced houses in Fratton and Buckland have held their value better due to strong rental demand from the universities and naval base.

**Southampton.** A stronger economic base with two universities, a major port, and growing technology and financial services sectors. The market corrected by 3-5% and has been recovering more quickly than Portsmouth. Family homes in the suburbs and city-centre apartments have both seen renewed demand.

**Isle of Wight.** The island experienced a pronounced pandemic boom as buyers sought coastal properties and rural space. The correction from 2023 was more significant, with some properties falling 10-15% from peak prices. Recovery has been slower, partly due to the practical challenges of island living (ferry costs, limited employment base). For investors, this means there are genuine bargains available for those with a long-term view. A period cottage in Ryde or Ventnor bought now at 2020-2021 price levels may prove an excellent entry point when the next expansion phase gains momentum.

## What the Cycle Means for Investors in 2026

**For buyers.** The current recovery phase is a good time to buy, particularly in areas that have had a more pronounced correction. Interest rates are still elevated compared to the 2010s but have stabilised, and mortgage rates of 4-5% are manageable against rental yields of 5-7% in good locations. The window before full expansion takes hold may be limited.

**For sellers.** If you are selling to reinvest, the market is liquid enough to achieve a fair price. You are unlikely to achieve 2022 peak prices in most areas, but a sensible asking price with good presentation will attract buyers.

**For portfolio holders.** Refinancing opportunities are improving as valuations recover and lenders compete for business. Fixed-rate products are available at increasingly competitive rates. Consider fixing for two to five years to lock in current rates before the expansion phase inevitably pushes interest rates up again.

## The Long View

The property cycle is not something to fear. It is something to understand and use. The most successful investors buy during recovery, hold through expansion, sell selectively at the peak, and use downturns to acquire bargains from distressed sellers.
