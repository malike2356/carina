# BRR Monthly Cashflow: What the Refinanced Property Pays You

After the BRR refinance, you have a smaller mortgage on a refurbished property. The monthly cashflow should be better than a standard BTL because you have less debt.\n\nThe mortgage is based on the refinance amount of £165,000. At 5% interest-only, that is £687.50 a month. Your gross rent is £1,100. Operating costs at 25% of rent: £275. Your monthly cashflow is £1,100 minus £687.50 minus £275, which equals £137.50.\n\nThat is £1,650 a year. On your cash left in of £31,950, that is a 5.2% cash-on-cash return. And you got 84% of your original capital back to do the next deal.\n\nThe cashflow is positive but modest. That is typical for BRR on the South Coast. The real return comes from the capital recycling and the equity growth, not the monthly cashflow.\n\nIf BRR cashflow is your primary goal, look for areas with higher yields. The North and Midlands offer better BRR cashflow because the purchase prices are lower relative to rents. You can BRR a £80,000 property in the North, refinance at £120,000, and achieve £150 to £200 a month cashflow.

**The formula**

```
Monthly Mortgage (IO) = Refinance Proceeds × Annual Rate / 12
Monthly Cashflow = Monthly Rent - Monthly Mortgage - Management - Maintenance
```

**Why this matters**

BRR monthly cashflow on the South Coast is typically £100 to £200 positive. Higher yields elsewhere deliver better cashflow. The real BRR return comes from capital recycling, not monthly income.
