# Flip ROI: How Well Did Your Money Work in That Project?

Profit margin measures the deal. ROI measures your money. And because you usually do not finance a flip the same way as a BTL, the ROI can look very different.\n\nIf you put £70,000 of your own cash into the flip (deposit plus refurb plus costs) and made £16,120 net profit, your ROI is £16,120 divided by £70,000, which equals 23%. That is a good return for a six-month project.\n\nBut if you used a joint venture partner who put up £35,000 and you split the profit 50/50, your cash invested might be £35,000 and your share of the profit is £8,060. Your ROI is still 23%. The numbers scale proportionally.\n\nThe magic of flip ROI comes when you use bridging finance. If you borrow most of the purchase price and only put in £30,000 of your own cash, your ROI can look much higher. A £16,120 profit on £30,000 cash is a 54% ROI.\n\nBut higher ROI with more leverage is riskier. If the sale is delayed by two months, the bridging interest eats into your profit. If the market drops, you might sell at a loss. The ROI looks good on paper but the risk is real.\n
Calculate ROI both ways: with your actual cash and with different leverage scenarios. The right approach depends on your risk tolerance and available capital.

**The formula**

```
ROI (%) = (Net Profit / Total Cost) × 100
```

**Why this matters**

Flip ROI of 20% to 50% is achievable depending on leverage. Higher leverage means higher ROI but higher risk. Calculate ROI for each scenario before you commit.
