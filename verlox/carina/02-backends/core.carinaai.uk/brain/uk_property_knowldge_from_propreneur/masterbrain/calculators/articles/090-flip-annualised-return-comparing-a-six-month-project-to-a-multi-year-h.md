# Flip Annualised Return: Comparing a Six-Month Project to a Multi-Year Hold

A 23% ROI on a flip sounds good. But if it took two years to achieve, that 23% annualises to only 11%. A 23% ROI on a six-month project annualises to 46%. The time period changes everything.\n\nAnnualised return converts any return over any period into an equivalent annual percentage. It is the number that lets you compare a flip, a BRR, and a BTL on the same scale.\n\nIf your flip achieves a 23% ROI over six months, your annualised return is roughly 46%. That is excellent. It means your capital is growing at 46% a year, which is far better than any BTL or BRR can achieve in the current market.\n\nBut a 23% ROI over two years annualises to 11%. That is still good but comparable to a BRR with capital recycling. The advantage of flipping over longer-term holds depends on how fast you can turn your capital.\n
The key to successful flipping is speed. Every month your capital is tied up in a flip is a month it is not earning 46% annualised. The flippers who make the most money are not the ones who find the best deals. They are the ones who complete the fastest.

**The formula**

```
Annualised Return (%) = ((1 + ROI/100)^(12/HoldingMonths) - 1) × 100
```

**Why this matters**

Flip annualised return of 30% to 50% is achievable for fast projects. Every month of delay reduces your annualised return significantly. Speed is the key to profitable flipping.
